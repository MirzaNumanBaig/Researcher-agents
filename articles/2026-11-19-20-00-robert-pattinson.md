## Robert Pattinson at Rikers Island: what happened

The [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson) Rikers Island story, from the early days of his post-*Twilight* career, is back in circulation as he prepares to play 007. [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/robert-pattinson-rikers-good-time-james-bond-safdie-prison-1236759112/) revisited the time he got himself into Rikers Island, the large New York jail complex, while preparing for *Good Time*.

It was the middle of 2016. Pattinson was getting ready to play Connie Nikas, a man on the run, in the Safdie brothers' crime drama. The filmmakers knew some people held at Rikers, among them their friend [Buddy Duress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddy_Duress), who later appeared in the film. According to the outlet, Pattinson pushed Josh Safdie to get him inside, and the director agreed.

## Makeup, wardens and a scared-straight guess

By Pattinson's own account in a 2017 interview, the two men "just kind of went everywhere" and did not ask for permission. They spoke with wardens and inmates about life inside.

One exchange stayed with him. He had put on a lot of makeup so nobody would work out who he was, and he believed he looked like he belonged. Then an inmate asked what he was doing there and whether it was for a scared-straight program, the type of scheme aimed at steering at-risk teenagers away from crime. "I wasn't convincing anyone," Pattinson said.

The Hollywood Reporter notes that Pattinson is known for telling tall tales in interviews, but says this one is true because Josh Safdie confirmed it.

## How he talked his way into Good Time

The article places the visit inside a longer pattern of persistence. After *Twilight*, Pattinson reached out to the Safdies after seeing a promotional photo for their earlier film, *Daddy Longlegs*. He later read the script that became [*Uncut Gems*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncut_Gems) and asked to be in it.

According to The Hollywood Reporter, the brothers did not see him in any of the major parts, including the lead hustler eventually played by [Adam Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Sandler). They were taken by how hard he pushed, though, and wrote a whole chase drama for him. That film became *Good Time*.

## Why it is being linked to Bond

The outlet is open that the story cuts both ways. On one hand, it says, a failed disguise adds to the doubts of those who think Pattinson is too boyish for a weathered 007, and who dislike that the casting was seen as a corporate decision. On the other, it asks what is more Bond than sneaking around Rikers and walking out unscathed. It also jokes that persuading powerful people to do something they were not ready to do is something M would admire.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Robert Pattinson really go to Rikers Island?

Yes, according to The Hollywood Reporter. Josh Safdie confirmed the account, and Pattinson described the visit in a 2017 interview.

### Why did he visit?

He was researching the role of Connie Nikas, a man on the run, for the Safdie brothers' *Good Time*.

### Did the inmates recognise him?

No. By his own account he wore makeup to avoid being recognised, but one inmate saw through his act and asked if he was there for a scared-straight program.
