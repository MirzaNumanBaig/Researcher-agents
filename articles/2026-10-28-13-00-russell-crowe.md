**Quick summary**

- The 1995 sci-fi thriller *Virtuosity*, starring [Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe) and [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington), joins Paramount+ on 1 November.
- It is part of the service's November slate of licensed titles, which also includes the Rambo trilogy, *Gone Girl* and *Footloose*, according to [Comic Book Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources).
- The film was a critical and commercial miss in 1995, with a 28% critic score on Rotten Tomatoes.
- Roger Ebert was a notable exception and gave it three out of four stars.
- The report does not say whether the film arrives on the Australian version of Paramount+.

A film that critics and cinema audiences largely rejected more than three decades ago is getting a second life on streaming. *Virtuosity*, the 1995 thriller that pairs Russell Crowe with Denzel Washington, begins streaming on Paramount+ on 1 November, [Comic Book Resources reports](https://www.cbr.com/virtuosity-sci-fi-thriller-coming-to-paramount-plus/). The report is written for US readers, so Australian viewers should check their local Paramount+ listing for availability.

## What Virtuosity is about

Washington plays Parker Barnes, a former LAPD officer jailed for killing the man who murdered his family. Police recruit him to test a virtual-reality program called SID 6.7, played by Crowe. SID, short for Sadistic, Intelligent, Dangerous, is a training tool built from the traits of hundreds of history's worst serial killers.

The test goes wrong when SID overloads the neural link and kills an inmate taking part, and the project is shut down. Its creator refuses to let it die, though. He moves SID's artificial intelligence into a synthetic android body that can regenerate, and SID escapes into the real world.

From there, according to Comic Book Resources, the character goes on a killing spree across Los Angeles. Barnes is given a conditional release to hunt down and destroy it.

## Who made it

The film was directed by [Brett Leonard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Leonard) from a script by [Eric Bernt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Bernt). The supporting cast includes [Kelly Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Lynch), [Stephen Spinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Spinella), [William Forsythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Forsythe_(actor)) and [Louise Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Fletcher).

There is a neat footnote for fans of the two leads. Crowe and Washington reunited in 2007 for *American Gangster*, and the roles were reversed: Washington played the villain and Crowe played the hero, as the report points out.

## A poor reception, with one big defender

*Virtuosity* did not impress critics on release. [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) currently lists a 28% critic score and a 32% audience score. The San Francisco Examiner complained of "a tiredness" in its action sequences, and Rolling Stone called the film "stillborn", according to the Comic Book Resources piece.

The box office told a similar story. The film took $37 million worldwide against a $30 million budget, which is a thin return once marketing costs are counted.

[Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert) went the other way. Writing in the Chicago Sun-Times, he gave it three out of four stars and described it as a film caught between formula and invention, "filled with bright ideas and fresh thinking". That split is part of why the film still gets rediscovered.
