- Fox also interviewed Lloyd for Interview magazine, in a conversation held on 1 September 2026.

- The Michael J. Fox Foundation held its Parkinson's Disease Therapeutics Conference in New York on 14 and 15 October.

[Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) has spent the week celebrating his Back to the Future co-star. After [Christopher Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Lloyd) turned 88 on 22 October, Fox answered his birthday post with a line from the films, and the pair are about to share the spotlight again as Lloyd's memoir reaches shelves on 27 October.

## How did Michael J. Fox respond to Lloyd's 88th birthday?

Lloyd's Instagram video carried the caption "I told you, when this baby turns 88...", a nod to Doc Brown's rule that the DeLorean must reach 88 miles per hour to time travel, AOL reported. His post on X ended with a reference to "The Power of Love", the Huey Lewis and the News song from the soundtrack, as Page Six noted.

Fox posted a photo of the two smiling at a restaurant table. People noted the image had first appeared on Instagram in March. "Great Scott you're 88! That's some serious shit, love you!" Fox wrote. Lloyd replied in the comments: "We truly are timeless. Love you friend."

The birthday landed one day after Back to the Future Day on 21 October, the date the characters visit in 2015 in the second film. Lloyd first played Emmett "Doc" Brown opposite Fox's Marty McFly in 1985, and the trilogy wrapped in 1990.

So how well did the two actually know each other? Fox says it took a while.

## A friendship that grew on the third film

In a 2023 Variety Studio interview at Sundance, quoted by CinemaBlend, Fox said their bond deepened during Back to the Future Part III. He recalled talking with Lloyd about acting, Shakespeare and King Lear, and admired how much story Lloyd could convey in a short performance. "He's full of surprises," Fox said.

Lloyd's memory goes back further. In an excerpt from his memoir, he recalls meeting Fox in a car park during reshoots of scenes first filmed with Eric Stoltz. Fox was juggling the film with Family Ties, and Lloyd admired how he coped with the schedule. Lloyd also admits he worried about redoing weeks of work, until Fox's comic energy helped him settle in.

## Fox interviews Lloyd for Interview magazine

The two have now swapped roles. In a conversation for [Interview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_(magazine)) magazine, held over Zoom on 1 September, Fox interviewed Lloyd about In My Own Time. The magazine describes Lloyd as an actor who has never liked talking about himself, and says it took his old friend to get him going.

Fox told him he had known him for "100 years" without knowing his story. Lloyd said he finally agreed to write the book after years of people suggesting it, and once his wife, Lisa Loiacono, pushed him too. He told Fox he grew up isolated, with little conversation at home.

Fox asked about Waveny, the Connecticut estate where Lloyd lived from the age of five. Lloyd said the grounds were about 400 acres when his grandfather bought them and about 350 when he lived there, with a pond, a canoe and a lot of snakes. Fox, who grew up on army bases, said his own childhood adventures were similar.

## What is in In My Own Time?

According to AOL, the 256-page memoir covers Lloyd's childhood at Waveny, his family relationships and his early stage work in New York. In an excerpt released by Hachette Book Group, Lloyd says he was filming in Mexico City and thinking about returning to the theatre when his agents sent him the Back to the Future script. He skimmed it and binned it, until a friend challenged him to read it properly. He then flew to Los Angeles to meet director Robert Zemeckis.

He also credits conductor Leopold Stokowski as an influence on Doc Brown. A photo of Stokowski, with raised arms and wild white hair, on a recording of Holst's The Planets helped Lloyd picture the scientist.

## Fox's foundation keeps working

Away from the film nostalgia, the Michael J. Fox Foundation held its annual Parkinson's Disease Therapeutics Conference in New York on 14 and 15 October, Parkinson's Europe reported. Chief Scientist Mark Frasier told delegates: "We want to go out of business and have a world without Parkinson's."

The agenda focused on drug development and on defining the biological root of Parkinson's so treatments can target it. A session covered the Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative (PPMI), an observational study launched by the foundation with global partners in 2010 and now 16 years old. Its aim is an open-access data set and biosample library for researchers.

## What happens next
