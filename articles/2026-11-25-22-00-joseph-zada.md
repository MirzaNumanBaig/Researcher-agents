- [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) plays a younger Haymitch Abernathy in *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, a prequel set around the 50th Hunger Games.

- The part of Haymitch was played in the original films by [Woody Harrelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Harrelson).

Australian actor [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) is about to take on his biggest part yet as a young Haymitch Abernathy in the Hunger Games prequel, and a new profile from Instinct Magazine argues that the audience's first look at him came well before the arena. The piece, published on 24 November 2026, revisits the two television roles that made him a name.

## From Geraldton to Panem

According to [Instinct Magazine](https://instinctmagazine.com/joseph-zad-joseph-zada/), Zada is preparing to step into *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, the prequel built around the 50th Hunger Games. The character of Haymitch is already well known to fans of the franchise, because [Woody Harrelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Harrelson) played the older version on screen.

Instinct calls this "no small assignment", and the point is fair. Playing the younger self of a character an audience already loves means every choice is measured against a familiar performance. The magazine's view is that Zada's recent work shows he can handle the weight. It is an opinion piece, not a review of the film, and the film itself has not been judged in the article.

## Invisible Boys came first

The profile spends most of its time on [*Invisible Boys*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_Boys), the Australian drama based on Holden Sheppard's novel of the same name. The series follows four young gay men in Geraldton, a coastal town in Western Australia, as they deal with identity, relationships and the pressure of small-town life.

Zada plays Charlie Roth, described as a quiet, punk-loving teenager. His carefully guarded private life falls apart when a secret hookup with a married man is exposed on social media. The fallout reaches the other three leads too, each of whom is dealing with growing up gay in a place where being openly queer is not always easy.

Instinct says the series does not treat adolescence as a tidy coming-of-age story. It points to the show's focus on desire, heartbreak and the search for acceptance, and says it does not avoid intimate scenes. For the magazine's LGBTQ+ readers, that role is what Zada is best remembered for.

## We Were Liars widened the audience

His next major credit was *We Were Liars*, Prime Video's adaptation of E. Lockhart's bestselling novel. Zada plays Johnny Sinclair Dennis, cousin to Cadence and one of the younger members of the wealthy Sinclair family.

Instinct describes the series as a step up for his career because it put him in front of a wider international audience. It also notes that some viewers have debated Johnny's dynamic with another character, Blake. The magazine is careful to say those readings come from fans and are not confirmed details of the character, and that caution is worth repeating here.

The article also quotes a fan post on X that jokes about people being surprised by Zada's roles, noting that his first two main parts were these two series and that he was once known mainly for playing gay characters. It is a social media comment, not a statement from the actor.

## Why the timing matters

The profile frames the franchise as a turning point. Mainstream audiences, it says, are about to meet an actor that a smaller group of viewers has followed for some time. That fits a pattern in screen careers, where a streaming or festival drama builds a following before a studio release brings in the wider crowd. For Zada, the shift is from Australian and streaming television to one of the best-known young adult film series.
