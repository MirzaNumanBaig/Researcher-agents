**Quick summary**

- Page Six reported on 24 October that Nicole Kidman is working across TV, film and fashion as she approaches her 60th birthday next June.
- She stars in season three of Lioness, is filming the thriller Discretion and will return in Nine Perfect Strangers, according to Page Six.
- An unnamed industry source told Page Six that trade coverage of Practical Magic 2's $30 million opening was unfair.
- The film has made $105 million worldwide, against $156 million for Brad Pitt's Heart of the Beast, Page Six says.

[Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman) has a crowded calendar. According to [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/10/24/celebrity-news/inside-nicole-kidmans-red-hot-renaissance-at-age-59/), the Australian Oscar winner is starring in a third season of Paramount+'s Lioness, filming a new thriller called Discretion and fronting Chanel, all while her latest cinema release is still being argued over.

That argument is the more interesting part of the story. It centres on how a $30 million opening weekend gets judged when the film is led by women.

## What is Nicole Kidman working on now?

Page Six lists a long run of projects. In Lioness she plays Kaitlyn Meade, a senior CIA official who oversees the Lioness Engagement Team, which is led by Zoe Saldaña's character. The outlet says a fourth season is planned.

She has also finished a second season of the Amazon Prime show Scarpetta, according to Page Six, and will reprise her role in the HBO series [Nine Perfect Strangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Perfect_Strangers). Page Six also notes she appeared at this month's Chanel show.

More of her TV work is covered in our [TV & Dramas section](https://moduscarry.com/category/tv-dramas/).

## Was Practical Magic 2 a flop?

Not according to a source who spoke to Page Six. [Practical Magic 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_Magic) reunites Kidman with [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock) and opened to $30 million. The source said the trades "slated it" for that figure.

The following week, the source said, Brad Pitt's Heart of the Beast opened at $20 million and was celebrated. Page Six adds that Tom Cruise's Digger made $8 million on its opening weekend on 2 October.

The numbers have since moved. Page Six concedes that Heart of the Beast now leads, with $156 million worldwide to Practical Magic 2's $105 million. The source's broader point is that Hollywood still struggles to take female-led films and their audiences seriously. That is one person's view, and Page Six does not name them.

## A pledge that predates the debate

Kidman has been public about this for years. At the Cannes Film Festival in 2017 she promised to work with a female director at least once every 18 months. Page Six says she went on to work with 19 female filmmakers over eight years.

Industry insiders told the outlet the total is now 67 collaborations with women directors across TV, film and shorts, in roles as actor, producer or executive producer. That count is Page Six's, from unnamed insiders, and we have not seen it independently confirmed.

At a Variety event in 2018 Kidman said that anyone with a little power to get something greenlit can ask for a woman to direct it, and that doing so helps.

## What her collaborators and commentators say

[Susanne Bier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanne_Bier), who directed Practical Magic 2 and has worked with Kidman often, told British Vogue that Kidman is "the ultimate seducer", adding that she didn't mean it in a sexual way. Bier described an energy that draws people into her orbit.

Ethan Alter, deputy editor of Gold Derby, told Page Six that Kidman understands modern celebrity as a bigger job than movie stardom. He pointed to her television work and her ease with social media. He also credited her AMC cinema ads with bringing audiences back after the pandemic.

Then there is the pop-culture footnote. Page Six says Adéla's song "Nicole Kidman", released in September, reached the Billboard Hot 100 top 10 and number one on US Spotify.
