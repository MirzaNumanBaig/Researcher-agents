**Quick summary**

- [Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger) turned down the lead in Oliver Stone's $155 million epic Alexander in 2002, according to Far Out Magazine.
- Colin Farrell took the part, and the film lost heavily and drew six Razzie nominations.
- Ledger later earned Best Actor nominations at the Oscars and Golden Globes for Brokeback Mountain.
- SBS News reports that Timothée Chalamet looked to Ledger's Joker, and to Marlon Brando, for Dune: Part Three, due in cinemas on 16 December.

Two separate stories this week show how far Heath Ledger's choices still reach. One looks back at the role he refused in 2002. The other points to the performance he left behind in The Dark Knight, now cited as inspiration for one of Hollywood's biggest stars.

## The $155 million role he refused

By 2001 Ledger could choose his work. According to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/heath-ledger-turned-down-movie-role-lost-fortune-six-razzie-nominations/), he had moved from Australian soap opera to Hollywood within five years, via 10 Things I Hate About You with [Julia Stiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Stiles) in 1999 and The Patriot with [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson) in 2000. A Knight's Tale and Monster's Ball, the drama with [Halle Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_Berry) and [Billy Bob Thornton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Bob_Thornton), followed in 2001.

Then [Oliver Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Stone) came calling with Alexander, a historical epic about Alexander the Great budgeted at $155 million. The outlet reports that Stone believed he had found his lead. Ledger declined, saying he was young and wanted to pace his career.

## What happened to Alexander

[Colin Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Farrell) took the role instead. Far Out describes the film as a bad struggle on release, one that lost a fortune and was shortlisted for six Razzies. They included Worst Picture, Worst Director for Stone and Worst Actor for Farrell. The outlet adds that Farrell has spent much of the past two decades apologising for his involvement.

## A different path

Ledger kept to smaller, harder projects rather than blockbusters. Far Out points to 2005, when he made Brokeback Mountain with [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal) for Ang Lee. The performance brought Ledger Best Actor nominations at the Oscars and Golden Globes. The piece is a retrospective, and it rests on one outlet's account, so treat the finer details as its telling of the story.

## Ledger's Joker as a reference point

Ledger's work also turns up in a very current film. SBS News, in a report translated from Korean by AI, says Timothée Chalamet drew on two performances for the final stretch of his character Paul in Dune: Part Three. One is [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando) as Colonel Kurtz in Apocalypse Now, shown through a buzzcut and low-toned delivery. The other is Ledger's Joker in The Dark Knight, with its unpredictable acting and grotesque clown makeup.

SBS reports that Chalamet shaved his trademark wavy hair into a buzzcut for the part. The film returns to Paul 19 years after the previous instalment, with Paul now a tyrant at war with Zendaya's Chani. The report is a promotional piece, so the exact influence is described at second hand rather than in Chalamet's own words.

## What happens next

Dune: Part Three is set to open in cinemas on 16 December, according to SBS, which says it will premiere first in South Korea. Australian release details were not in the reports we read.
