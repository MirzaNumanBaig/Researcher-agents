**Quick summary**

- Director Todd Haynes told French magazine Le Point that his next film will adapt Jean Genet's play The Maids.
- Haynes said he thinks Cate Blanchett will be in it. Nothing has been formally announced.
- It would be their third collaboration, after I'm Not There and Carol.
- Separately, Blanchett's Displacement Film Fund will release five short films with The New Yorker, starting with a documentary on Nov. 9.

[Todd Haynes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Haynes) has revealed what he plans to make after his current film, and it brings him back to [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett). Speaking to Le Point, the director said his next project is an adaptation of Jean Genet's play The Maids, and that he believes Blanchett will star. The news, reported on 23 October, came as Blanchett also announced a new partnership for her Displacement Film Fund.

## What Haynes said about The Maids

According to [World of Reel](https://www.worldofreel.com/blog/2026/10/23/todd-haynes-to-direct-the-maids-with-cate-blanchett), which quoted the Le Point interview, Haynes described the film as coming after De Noche, the picture he is finishing. He said he has "a very particular approach" to the story and could not say more, adding that he had not seen this approach anywhere else.

His wording on casting was cautious. Haynes said he thinks Blanchett, who played the work on stage with [Isabelle Huppert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Huppert), "will be in" the film. [The Film Stage](https://thefilmstage.com/todd-haynes-and-cate-blanchett-plan-reunion-with-adaptation-of-jean-genets-the-maids/) reports that he called Genet "an absolute genius" who goes "into the raw, into the political." Neither Blanchett nor a studio has confirmed the project, so it should be treated as a plan rather than a greenlit production.

## Why the material suits both of them

Genet's 1947 play follows two sisters, Claire and Solange, who work as maids for a wealthy Madame. World of Reel notes they spend their evenings acting out her humiliation and murder, and that the play was loosely inspired by the 1933 case of the Papin sisters, two French maids who killed their employer and her daughter.

Blanchett knows it well. She played Claire opposite Huppert's Solange in Benedict Andrews' production for Sydney Theatre Company in 2013, which then moved to New York's Lincoln Center Festival in 2014, according to World of Reel. Haynes' remark leaves open whether she would return to Claire or take on Madame. The Film Stage adds that the play has had few screen versions, the best known being Christopher Miles' 1974 feature with Glenda Jackson, Susannah York and Vivien Merchant.

The pairing has history. Haynes and Blanchett have already worked together on I'm Not There and Carol, which World of Reel calls one of the best films of the 2010s. Whether Huppert returns for the screen version is, as that outlet puts it, the obvious next question.

## The film that comes first

De Noche has had a troubled path. World of Reel reports the 1930s gay noir fell apart in 2024 when Joaquin Phoenix left five days before production was due to start. Pedro Pascal was later set to play a detective opposite Danny Ramirez, and filming wrapped in Mexico in April 2026. The same outlet, citing El País, says a 2027 release is planned. The Film Stage expects more news about The Maids when De Noche premieres, which it guesses could be around May 2027.

## Blanchett's Displacement Film Fund goes to The New Yorker

On the same day, [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/cate-blanchett-displacement-film-fund-new-yorker-1236922000/) reported exclusively that the Displacement Film Fund will release five short films with The New Yorker. The inaugural slate comes from Mohammad Rasoulof, Shahrbanoo Sadat, Mo Harawe, Maryna Er Gorbach and Hasan Kattan, and explores exile, identity and belonging.

The films will arrive weekly on The New Yorker's website and YouTube channel, beginning with Kattan's documentary "Allies in Exile" on Nov. 9. Variety says Film Forum in New York will screen the slate on Nov. 11, with Blanchett in conversation with a New Yorker editor, alongside Er Gorbach and Harawe.

The fund was established last year, and each of the five projects received a €100,000 production grant. Blanchett said that with one in 70 people on earth displaced by conflict, war and persecution, it is vital to create opportunities for audiences to connect with experiences beyond their own. A second cohort, including Mohammed Amer, Annemarie Jacir, Akuol de Mabior, Bao Nguyen and Rithy Panh, will premiere at International Film Festival Rotterdam in January.

## What happens next

Expect no formal word on The Maids until De Noche has been shown, and possibly not until 2027. In the meantime, the first of the New Yorker shorts is due on Nov. 9, with the Film Forum event two days later. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is Cate Blanchett confirmed for The Maids?

Not formally. Todd Haynes told Le Point he thinks she will be in the film, but no studio or cast announcement has been made.

### Who is directing The Maids?

Todd Haynes, according to his own comments to Le Point as reported by World of Reel and The Film Stage. He has given no plot details beyond saying his approach is unusual.

### When does the Displacement Film Fund series start?

Variety reports the first short, "Allies in Exile", premieres on Nov. 9 on The New Yorker's website and YouTube channel, with one film following each week.
