**Quick summary**

- The woven Wicker Husband look on [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) took about 18 months to develop, according to Wealth of Geeks.
- The transformation used around 50 body pieces and 10 facial appliances, reduced to about 90 minutes of daily application.
- New Zealand's Wētā Workshop built the costume and prosthetics, mostly without computer-generated effects.
- Separately, ScreenRant reports his series [True Blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blood) leaves Hulu on 31 December 2026 (a date it attributes to unconfirmed sources).

The Wicker Husband that has fans photographing cinema displays was built by hand and by 3D printer, and it took about 18 months to get right. Details published this week by Wealth of Geeks, via NewsBreak, show how Wētā Workshop turned Alexander Skarsgård into a living basket for the film *Wicker*, which is now in cinemas through Black Bear.

## A woven face that still had to smile

In the film, Olivia Colman's Fisherwoman asks the Basketmaker, played by Peter Dinklage, to build her a husband. Skarsgård plays the result. [Olivia Colman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Colman) has said her character is self-sufficient and surprised to fall in love.

The challenge, as makeup designer Joe Dunckley and application lead Sarah Rubano explained to The World of Interiors, was that individual strands had to move with Skarsgård's face. Traditional moulding struggled with the geometry. So weavers worked beside digital sculptors, and the designs were 3D-printed.

Master weaver Jasmin van Lith made physical samples so the digital team kept believable basketmaking techniques. Dunckley said the look also had to suggest a Basketmaker who had studied weaving traditions from different cultures. The finished makeup had to hold its shape while still showing sadness, affection and jealousy.

## The numbers behind the transformation

According to Allure, as relayed in the same report, the look involved around 50 interlocking body pieces and 10 facial appliances. The team eventually brought daily application down to about 90 minutes. Hair, beard and eyelashes came from muka, a fibre taken from New Zealand flax.

Most shots used the physical creation with little or no computer-generated work. Light shining through the weave, to suggest a hollow body, was among the few finishing touches added digitally.

Lunch needed its own routine. Artists removed his lip pieces and eyelashes during breaks and put a bib over the wicker beard, so most of the makeup survived to the afternoon.

## What it was like inside the suit

Skarsgård told Asian Journal that the suit's rigidity affected how he moved. Colman said she valued having a physical character to touch. Rubano told The World of Interiors that a small smirk on screen sometimes meant smiling broadly underneath the prosthetics.

Skarsgård also said the innocence of the role appealed after darker work in *Pillion*, *The Northman* and *Succession*. ## True Blood is reportedly leaving Hulu

In other Skarsgård news, ScreenRant reports that *True Blood* will leave Hulu on 31 December 2026, nearly four years after it arrived in January 2023. ScreenRant says the date comes from "some sources" and an Instagram post, so treat it as unconfirmed for now. This is a US streaming change, and nothing in the report concerns Australian services.

The HBO series, based on [Charlaine Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlaine_Harris)'s Southern Vampire Mysteries novels, premiered in 2008. All seven seasons and 80 episodes are on Hulu, each running 45 to 64 minutes. ScreenRant says it should stay on HBO Max through 2027.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long did Alexander Skarsgård's Wicker makeup take to apply?
About 90 minutes a day, once the team had streamlined it, according to Allure as cited by Wealth of Geeks. It used roughly 50 body pieces and 10 facial appliances.

### Who made the Wicker Husband costume?
New Zealand's Wētā Workshop built the costume and facial prosthetics. Joe Dunckley designed the makeup and Sarah Rubano led its application.
