**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) is the face of Sephora's new holiday campaign, "We All Sephora", according to ADWEEK.
- The hero ad was directed by [Jason Woliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Woliner) and developed by agency Gut New York.
- The campaign rolls out on 29 October across connected TV, linear TV, streaming and social in the US and Canada.
- Sephora teased it by renaming its store at The Grove in Los Angeles "Sethphora".

Seth Rogen has been named the lead of Sephora's holiday campaign, and the retailer started the promotion with a joke: it renamed its Los Angeles store at The Grove "Sethphora" over the weekend. The full "We All Sephora" campaign launches on 29 October in the US and Canada, ADWEEK reports.

## What is the Seth Rogen Sephora campaign?

"We All Sephora" is the retailer's holiday push, built around the idea that anyone can give the gift of beauty. ADWEEK says the brand tapped Rogen, the star of *The Studio*, to front it.

The centrepiece is a hero ad that follows Rogen into a Sephora store with a gift list. With help from the staff, he walks out with setting spray for his producer, a fragrance for his cousin and a red-light therapy mask for his mum (and, ADWEEK notes, for himself). BeautyNewsDaily describes the premise as a playful nod to anyone who finds beauty shopping a little intimidating.

Shoppers will be able to browse Rogen's curated holiday picks on Sephora.com and in the Sephora app.

## Who made the ad?

The spot was developed by Gut New York and directed by Jason Woliner, whom ADWEEK identifies as the creator of the sketch comedy show *Human Giant*. Both ADWEEK and BeautyNewsDaily credit Woliner with the direction.

Sephora's chief marketing officer, Zena Arnold, said in a statement that the best gifts create moments of joy, "especially when it comes in a black-and-white striped bag", but that finding the right one isn't always easy. She said the brand wanted gifting to feel effortless, and that it used Rogen's comedic lens to make beauty feel approachable and fun.

Rogen also gave a statement. "Holiday gifting can be stressful, so anything that makes it easier is a win in my book," he said.

## Why Sephora chose a comedian

The casting speaks to a gap the retailer wants to close. ADWEEK cites Ipsos data showing that 42 per cent of US men aged 18 to 34 said they were very comfortable buying cosmetics in person. Among US men aged 51 and older, the figure was 15 per cent.

That is a wide difference between age groups, and it explains why a campaign about hesitant shoppers would lean on humour rather than a glossy product line-up.

The timing also suits the company. ADWEEK reports that Sephora remains a bright spot for its parent, LVMH, with sales in the Selective Retailing division, which includes Sephora, up 8 per cent in the third quarter of 2026.

## What happens next

The campaign officially starts on 29 October, with ads running across connected TV, linear television, streaming and social channels, according to ADWEEK. The sources describe the rollout for the US and Canada only, and neither mentions an Australian release.
