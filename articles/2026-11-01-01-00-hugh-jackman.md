**Quick summary**

- *The Wolverine*, starring [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman), becomes available to HBO Max subscribers on 1 November, according to [Comic Book Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources).
- The film grossed more than US$415 million worldwide, over three times its production budget.
- It holds roughly a 70 per cent rating on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) and 6.7/10 on IMDb.
- The report does not say which countries get the title, so Australian viewers should check their own HBO Max listing.

*The Wolverine*, the 2013 standalone film that sent Hugh Jackman's clawed mutant to Japan, joins the HBO Max catalogue on 1 November. Comic Book Resources reported the move on 31 October, calling the film an underrated entry in Jackman's long run as the character.

## What the film is about

The official synopsis, quoted by Comic Book Resources, has a century-old Wolverine lured to a Japan he has not seen since World War II. There he lands in a world of yakuza and samurai and is forced to run with a powerful industrialist's daughter, played by [Tao Okamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Okamoto). For the first time, the synopsis says, he faces the prospect of death.

The cast also includes [Famke Janssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famke_Janssen), returning as Jean Grey, and [Hiroyuki Sanada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroyuki_Sanada), who plays Shingen Harada. Comic Book Resources says Janssen's presence was the main thread tying the film to the wider X-Men universe.

## The numbers behind it

According to the outlet, the film took more than US$415 million at the global box office, more than triple its production budget. It also reports a 70 per cent approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and a 6.7/10 score on IMDb. Comic Book Resources describes it as one of the highest-grossing films of Jackman's career.

For context, the same piece notes that Jackman later starred in *Logan* and returned to the role in the record-setting *Deadpool & Wolverine* in 2024. Our own coverage looked at [what that film cost Disney](https://moduscarry.com/hugh-jackmans-deadpool-wolverine-cost-disney-nearly-534m/). The article also notes that [Dafne Keen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafne_Keen), who debuted in *Logan*, reprised her role in that film.

## What Jackman said at the time

The writer notes that Jackman spoke about the project to Collider when it was released. He said a clear approach to the story was key to signing on, and that he was thrilled when the studio agreed to title it *The Wolverine* rather than *Wolverine 2*.

Jackman described it as a standalone story set well after *X-Men Origins*, in a new location and under director James Mangold, with a visual style different from the earlier X-Men films. "I think it's going to feel very fresh," he said, according to the report.

Comic Book Resources argues that Jackman's Marvel career is rivalled only by [Robert Downey Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Downey_Jr.) and [Chris Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evans_(actor)). That is the outlet's opinion, not a measured ranking.

## What happens next

The film is available from 1 November. The report frames it as a good time for a rewatch, 13 years after release. No new announcement about Jackman's Wolverine future came with the news.
