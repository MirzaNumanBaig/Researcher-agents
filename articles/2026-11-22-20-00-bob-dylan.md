**Quick summary**

- Sotheby's in New York will auction rare [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) manuscripts in December.
- Handwritten pages for "Subterranean Homesick Blues" are estimated at US$400,000 to US$600,000.
- Two pages of verses from "Masters of War" carry an estimate of US$350,000 to US$400,000.
- The "McKenzie Tape", a 1963 private concert recording, is estimated at US$15,000 to US$20,000.
- It is not known who owns the items or who will profit from the sale.

The Bob Dylan Sotheby's auction will put some of the singer-songwriter's earliest handwritten work in front of collectors this December, according to a report carried by [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/new-albums/articles/bob-dylan-manuscripts-mckenzie-tape-081828186.html). The New York auction house is offering lyric manuscripts and an unreleased recording from the start of his career.

## What is being sold

The headline lot is a set of three pages in black ink holding handwritten notes for "[Subterranean Homesick Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subterranean_Homesick_Blues)", written in the mid-1960s. Sotheby's estimates they will sell for between US$400,000 and US$600,000.

Two further pages carry verses from the anti-war song "Masters of War". Their estimate is US$350,000 to US$400,000. Together, the two song manuscripts could bring in between roughly US$750,000 and US$1 million on estimates alone.

The cheapest lot may be the most unusual. The "McKenzie Tape" is an unpublished recording of a private concert Dylan gave at the apartment of Eve and Mac McKenzie. The couple recorded him during his first years in New York, and this recording dates from 1963. It carries an estimate of US$15,000 to US$20,000, a small fraction of the manuscript prices.

## Who is selling?

That is still unclear. According to the report, it is not known who currently owns the documents or who will benefit from the sale. Auction houses often keep consignors confidential, so an answer may not emerge before the hammer falls.

## Dylan on stage, and a meeting at the Vatican

The auction lands while Dylan is still working. The report says he is in his mid-80s and still touring, with a Frankfurt show on Sunday marking the last of seven concerts in Germany on the current run.

Earlier this month in Rome, he stepped away from the tour to meet Pope Leo XIV at the Vatican. According to the report, the two US-born men had a short conversation and swapped gifts.

## An old song he wishes he had left alone

A separate piece from Men's Journal, republished by [AOL](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/reason-bob-dylan-now-wishes-161756000.html), revisits one of his early songs that he regrets. "Ballad in Plain D", from his fourth album, *Another Side of Bob Dylan*, runs 13 verses and just over eight minutes. It recounts the painful end of his relationship with [Suze Rotolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suze_Rotolo), whom he dated from 1961 to 1964, including a bitter row with her sister Carla.

Dylan told [Bill Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Flanagan) in 1985 that he must have been "a real schmuck" to write it and that it was perhaps the one song he could have left alone. Rotolo took a gentler view. As quoted by the Financial Times, she said she never felt hurt and understood that his art was his way of working through the breakup. The report adds that [American Songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Songwriter) links "Don't Think Twice, It's Alright" and "It Ain't Me, Babe" to her as well.

## What happens next

The sale is set for December at Sotheby's in New York. Final prices will show how collectors value Dylan's handwriting against a rare early recording.
