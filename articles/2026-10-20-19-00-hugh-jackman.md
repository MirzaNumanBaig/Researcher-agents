**Quick summary**

- [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) plays an ageing, remorseful Robin Hood in *The Death of Robin Hood*, now streaming on HBO Max.
- The film is directed by Michael Sarnoski, who also made *Pig* and *A Quiet Place: Day One*.
- Reviewer John Serba, writing for Yahoo, gave it a "Stream It" verdict while warning it is slow and violent.
- The cast includes [Jodie Comer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodie_Comer), Bill Skarsgård, [Murray Bartlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Bartlett) and [Noah Jupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Jupe).

Hugh Jackman has traded the swagger of past screen outlaws for something far bleaker. In *The Death of Robin Hood*, now on HBO Max, the Australian star plays the folk hero as a scarred killer haunted by his past, according to a [Yahoo review](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/stream-skip-death-robin-hood-190000288.html) by critic John Serba.

## A Robin Hood who is no hero

Serba opens by saying this is not the Robin Hood of [Errol Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errol_Flynn), or even of [Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe). Director Michael Sarnoski reworks the legend so that the man who robbed the rich is closer to an outlaw weighed down by guilt. Jackman, the review says, plays him with a mind "teeming" with pain and regret.

The story is set in 1247. Robin has killed many people, and relatives of the dead want revenge. Serba writes that the film strips away the myth of his virtue, treating it as cover for killing that was often pitiless.

## What happens in the film

Robin is first seen crossing a bitter, windswept landscape. He is reunited with Little John, played by Bill Skarsgård, whose wife and daughter are being held by people the two men wronged earlier. A violent sequence follows, and Robin is badly hurt.

Little John leaves him at a priory on an island. There Sister Brigid, played by Jodie Comer, nurses him back to health without knowing who he is. He gives his name as Randolph and builds friendships with her and with a bandaged man known as The Leper, played by Murray Bartlett.

The calm is short. Little John's young daughter Margaret arrives in shock, followed by a young man, played by Noah Jupe, who has lost an eye. Serba notes that viewers can guess what Robin does not: the newcomer may be after revenge.

## How the reviewer rates it

Serba compares the film to a mix of *The Northman* and Clint Eastwood's *Unforgiven*, calling the latter a major reference point. He describes the pacing as slow but deliberate, and praises Pat Scola's cinematography and the rugged island setting. Comer, he writes, is excellent in a somewhat thankless role.

On Jackman, the critic says the performance is not especially complex but is highly watchable. He links it to the intense brooding of *Logan*, and notes it follows the actor's livelier turn in *Song Sung Blue*.

Serba is candid about the limits. He says some viewers will not want a two-hour "mopefest" with unflinching slaughter, and that Sarnoski adds little to *Unforgiven*'s view of cruelty. The film also holds back the bloody payoff its early violence promises. His verdict is "Stream It", on the grounds that it may hold viewers in a pensive grip even if it will not lift their mood.

The review carries a content warning: it mentions graphic violence and brief nudity, so it is not family viewing.
