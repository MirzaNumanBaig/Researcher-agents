**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) has joined the judging panel for the 2027 Women's Prize for Playwriting.
- Submissions open on Thursday 26 November 2026 and close on Wednesday 31 March 2027.
- The winner receives £20,000, making it the largest award for female and non-binary playwrights in the UK and Ireland, according to WhatsOnStage.
- The prize is adding residencies for finalists and more support for longlisted writers.

Oscar winner Cate Blanchett has been named as a judge for the 2027 Women's Prize for Playwriting, as the award opens submissions today for what its organisers call an expanded offer. [WhatsOnStage reports](https://www.whatsonstage.com/news/womens-prize-for-playwriting-opens-submissions-for-2027-with-cate-blanchett-joining-judging-panel_1738324/) that writers have until 31 March 2027 to enter, and that Blanchett will sit on a panel chaired once again by [Indhu Rubasingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indhu_Rubasingham), director and co-chief executive of the National Theatre.

## Who is on the judging panel

Alongside Blanchett, the panel includes playwright and director [Lynette Linton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynette_Linton), literary agent Mel Kenyon, writer [Abi Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abi_Morgan), writer Kamila Shamsie, the National Theatre's head of new work Nina Steiger and Guardian editor-in-chief Katharine Viner. Rubasingham has judged the prize since it began and says she is moved each year by the ambition and range of the plays that reach the panel.

The Stage also reported Blanchett's appointment, describing the prize as "expanded". The sources do not say how involved Blanchett will be beyond serving as a judge.

## What the prize offers

The prize was founded in 2019 by [Ellie Keel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellie_Keel) and [Paines Plough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paines_Plough). It is open to full-length plays by female or non-binary writers, over 60 minutes and written in English.

The winner receives £20,000. The winning play can also be optioned by the prize, Paines Plough and Sheffield Theatres, and published by Concord Theatricals.

By the organisers' figures, the prize has so far received 4,295 submissions. It has awarded £68,000 to five winners and co-produced five productions seen by more than 50,000 people.

## What is new for 2027

The expansion is mostly about what happens after a play is recognised. Through a partnership with the Arvon Foundation, three of the final five playwrights will be offered a residential week at Lumb Bank, Arvon's Yorkshire base. In collaboration with the University of Oxford's Humanities Division, all five finalists will be offered a short residency to work with researchers on developing a new play.

The talent development programme Future Light, launched last year, returns to support every longlisted writer. The longlist represents the top three per cent of submissions. The programme pairs writers with an experienced dramaturg, runs writing courses led by UK playwrights and includes an industry day of panels. The prize has also launched a new website with a hub for workshops and opportunities, and partner organisations including Clean Break, the Abbey Theatre, Sheffield Theatres and Theatr Clwyd will host events during the submission window.

Keel said the prize has "always been about more than a single winner" and that the aim is for recognition to become "the start of a sustained creative relationship". She added that although the prize has contributed to more plays by women and non-binary writers reaching major stages, "there's still a long way to go" on equality.

## Where past winners are now

Georgina Duncan's 2025 winner, Sapling, will have its world premiere at the Orange Tree Theatre and Sheffield Theatres in June and July 2027. Earlier winners include Sarah Grochala, whose play Intelligence is in development, Amy Trigg, Ahlam and Karis Kelly.

## What happens next

Writers can submit from today until 31 March 2027. The longlist, final five and winner will follow, though the sources do not give those dates.
