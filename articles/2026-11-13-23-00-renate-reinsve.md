**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) says the strength of the anger over *Fjord* has been "surprising", but she is proud the film divides people.
- She and co-star [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) spoke to Radio Times at the London Film Festival.
- *Fjord* won the Palme d'Or at Cannes this year and is being tipped for several major Oscar categories, according to Radio Times.
- Stan says the film does not tell viewers what to think, and that cinema needs to provoke a reaction.

Renate Reinsve has described the fierce reaction to *Fjord* as surprising, while insisting she is glad the film is splitting audiences. The Norwegian actress and her co-star Sebastian Stan discussed the debate in an exclusive interview with [Radio Times](https://www.radiotimes.com/movies/sebastian-stan-renate-reinsve-fjord-response-exclusive-newsupdate/) at the London Film Festival.

## What Reinsve said about the anger

Reinsve told the magazine that the "really big emotions" around the film, and how much some viewers have struggled with parts of it, took her by surprise. She added that she finds it exciting when a film sets off so much disagreement.

"I really love when movies do that," she said, adding that she is very proud to be part of one that does. Her view is a notable one, given that Radio Times describes the response to *Fjord* as "very mixed" since its Cannes premiere.

According to the outlet, some critics have called the film anti-progressive. Others argue it bravely exposes flaws in rigid institutions. Radio Times gave the film four stars, calling it "perfectly judged" and unsettling without tipping into the absurd.

## Stan on a film that refuses to give answers

Stan said winning the Palme d'Or was a special experience and that the attention matters because "this is a movie that needs it". He then explained why he thinks the split reaction is healthy.

In his words, the film is not giving viewers the answer or telling them how to think. Audiences have to decide for themselves about the people on screen, and about what was right and wrong. "We need movies that bring some reaction," he said.

He went further, calling cinema "one of the last places" that offers the privacy to think for yourself. People can keep their opinions to themselves or share them, he said, but the space to form them is what counts.

## The film and its makers

Radio Times describes *Fjord* as a legal drama loosely inspired by true events. Reinsve and Stan play Lisbet and Mihai Gheorghiu, a conservative couple whose parenting methods come under scrutiny after they move with their five children to Lisbet's remote Norwegian hometown.

It is directed by Romanian filmmaker [Cristian Mungiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Mungiu), who has been here before. The magazine notes that his 2007 drama *4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days* also won the Palme d'Or and also drew controversy, in that case over censorship and the abortion debate.

The supporting cast includes [Lisa Carlehed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Carlehed), Markus Tønseth and Henrikke Lund-Olsen as the family's neighbours Mia, Mats and Noora Halberg. Vanessa Ceban and Jonathan Ciprian Breazu play the two eldest Gheorghiu children.

## What happens next

Radio Times reports that *Fjord* is being tipped for nominations in several major categories at next year's Academy Awards, with Reinsve and Stan often named as standouts. Both have been nominated before: Reinsve for [Sentimental Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentimental_Value) and Stan for *The Apprentice*, according to the outlet.
