**Quick summary**

- [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman) says her 2010 role in *Rabbit Hole* disturbed her "on a deep level".
- She spoke about it on BBC Radio 4's *Front Row*, as reported by Far Out Magazine.
- She played Becca, a mother grieving her four-year-old son, and also co-produced the film.
- The performance earned her an Oscar nomination for Best Actress.

Nicole Kidman has played many frightening characters, but the one that unsettled her most was an ordinary woman in mourning. Speaking on BBC Radio 4's *Front Row*, the Australian actor said her part in *Rabbit Hole* in 2010 hurt more than she had expected, according to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/dangerous-2010-role-made-nicole-kidman-confront-her-fears/).

## Why Nicole Kidman called the role dangerous

Kidman played Becca, who is grieving the death of her four-year-old son with her husband Howie, played by [Aaron Eckhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Eckhart). Far Out notes that nothing in the story is supernatural or sinister. The fear comes from the subject itself.

Kidman told *Front Row* she knew the part was risky before she took it. "I knew before going in that it was dangerous, but I couldn't foresee the way it was going to disturb me on such a deep level," she said, as quoted by Far Out.

She also described it as "walking through something that is probably one of my biggest fears", adding that this was "not necessarily a bad thing". Far Out points out that she is a mother herself, which made the weeks of filming emotionally heavy.

## A different kind of dark role

Kidman has built a career on uncomfortable material, moving between horror and drama. Far Out argues that *Rabbit Hole* was harder to shake off than her earlier psychological films, because there was no ghost or twist to explain the distress away. She had to stay in Becca's headspace day after day.

She did not only act in it. According to the same report, Kidman co-produced the film, which is based on a Pulitzer Prize-winning play by [David Lindsay-Abaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lindsay-Abaire).

## How the film shows grief

Far Out says the film avoids neat, tearful grief. Becca lashes out at strangers, struggles with her sister's pregnancy and pushes her husband away. The outlet describes the portrayal as unfiltered, with Kidman recoiling at reminders of motherhood.

The outlet adds that the film's closing idea is hopeful: grief never goes away, but it becomes easier to carry. It suggests that facing the story helped Kidman build resilience against her greatest fear, even if the process was painful.

## Recognition and a very different co-star approach

The performance earned Kidman an Oscar nomination for [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actress), Far Out reports. Her co-star took another route. Eckhart, who has no children, attended bereavement groups without telling the other members he was researching a role, the outlet says, and it calls this one of the more unsettling method-acting choices.
