**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) asked audiences at London's National Theatre to stop filming her play *Electra/Persona*, according to Variety and The A.V. Club.
- She made the request in an address after a curtain call, in a video that circulated on social media from 19 September.
- She said filming "spoils it for the next group of people".
- The production was due to run at the Lyttelton Theatre until 10 October. The remark was still prompting commentary in mid-October, including an Irish Examiner column.

Cate Blanchett has asked theatregoers to stop recording her on their phones, and the plea has kept travelling. The Australian actor addressed the audience after a performance of *Electra/Persona* at the National Theatre's Lyttelton Theatre in London, and a video of her remarks spread online in the days that followed.

## What did Cate Blanchett say to the audience?

According to [The A.V. Club](https://avclub.com/cate-blanchett-asks-electra-persona-audience-not-to-record), Blanchett spoke after the curtain call and asked people not to film the show. "There are few places on this planet where we can simply be together and be present," she said.

[Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/more/news/cate-blanchett-tells-audience-stop-recording-40516/) reported that she also called the filming "kind of flattering in a way", but said recording the performance "spoils it for the next group of people". Variety said the video was shared on Instagram.

The tone, by both accounts, was polite rather than scolding. That matters, because the request landed on a night when she was still on stage in front of the people she was asking.

## What is Electra/Persona?

The play, written and directed by Benedict Andrews, blends Sophocles' *Electra* with Ingmar Bergman's 1966 film *Persona*, according to Variety and The A.V. Club. Blanchett shares the stage with Nina Hoss. Both outlets report that the two actors swap roles, with a coin toss deciding who plays which part each night.

Variety says the run continued through 10 October. (Variety's article dates Bergman's film to 1996, an apparent typo; The A.V. Club gives 1966.)

## Why the remark has lasted

Reaction has been wide. The A.V. Club described the phone use as rude. On 19 October the Irish Examiner ran a farming column by Denis Lehane that borrowed her complaint to make a point about cattle marts, where he says online bidding has replaced the hand signals and cap-twitching of old.

It is a light piece, and it is a column rather than news. But it shows how far one curtain-call comment has gone, from a London theatre to a rural Irish mart ring.

Blanchett's own stage history has an Australian chapter. A Sydney Morning Herald column on Surry Hills, published on 19 October, mentions in passing that the Belvoir St Theatre is where she "once plied her trade". Coverage of the suburb's pubs and cafes is otherwise unrelated to her.

## What happens next

*Electra/Persona* was due to finish at the Lyttelton Theatre on 10 October, according to Variety, so the run has now ended. None of the sources report whether the production has plans beyond it, and neither the theatre nor Blanchett has been quoted on any change to its phone policy.
