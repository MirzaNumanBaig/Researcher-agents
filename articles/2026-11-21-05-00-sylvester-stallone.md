**Quick summary**

- *I Play Rocky*, directed by Peter Farrelly, opened in cinemas on 20 November 2026 and dramatises how Sylvester Stallone got *Rocky* made.
- Anthony Ippolito plays Stallone. He put on roughly 30 to 40 pounds, depending on the outlet, to match the actor's build.
- Stallone told The Hollywood Reporter he first had "mixed" feelings about the film, then was won over by Ippolito's audition video.
- Critics describe the film as funny and warm, a companion piece to the 1976 original.

[Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) did not walk into *I Play Rocky* with a Hollywood résumé behind him. He recorded an audition tape in his grandmother's basement on Long Island, sent it in unasked, and ended up playing [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) in the film that reached cinemas on 20 November. The transformation behind that casting is now the main talking point around the release.

## An unsolicited tape that ended the search

According to [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/article/how-did-i-play-rocky-star-anthony-ippolito-transform-into-sylvester-stallone-raw-eggs-a-40-pound-weight-gain-and-some-x-rated-homework-171914757.html), Farrelly had planned to search the world for his Stallone. Then he watched Ippolito's tape and stopped looking.

Ippolito had practice at this kind of role. He played a young Al Pacino in *The Offer*, the series about the making of *The Godfather*, and Yahoo notes that Stallone at a similar career stage sent him to some less obvious places. He watched Stallone in *Kojak* and *The Lords of Flatbush*, and he admitted to sampling an early softcore film from 1970, which he found tamer than expected.

## Weight, food and a delayed shoot

Ippolito expected about six months to get into shape. A production delay stretched that to closer to a year and a half, and he told Yahoo he was glad of it. He gained about 40 pounds by eating as much protein as he could each day.

[Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/i-play-rocky-star-anthony-ippolito-packed-weight-entire-lifestyle-shift-play-sylvester-stallone) puts the gain at more than 30 pounds, so the exact figure depends on who is counting. Ippolito described it to Fox as "an entire lifestyle shift". He pointed out that Stallone started lifting weights young and was a fan of bodybuilder Steve Reeves, so the physique had to look right for the part.

Trainer Grant Roberts, who had worked with Stallone, joined about six months before shooting to manage the diet and the lifting. Add sessions with boxing choreographer Bobby Beckles and Ippolito was training twice a day near the end of pre-production, Yahoo reports.

## Learning the voice

The physical work was only half of it. Ippolito said he isolated Stallone's voice and simply listened, trying to hear how the actor sounded before decades of impressions. He paid attention to how the facial paralysis Stallone has had since birth shapes his speech, until he found what he called a specific rhythm.

He was nervous when they finally met. Stallone gave him advice, some of which Ippolito said he cannot repeat, and having the actor in his corner "really did mean the world".

## What the film covers

The story follows a struggling Stallone who writes the script for *Rocky* and refuses to sell it unless he plays the lead. [America Magazine](https://www.americamagazine.org/film/2026/11/20/i-play-rocky-documentary-sylvester-stallone/) says the film also features AnnaSophia Robb as his wife Sasha, [Toby Kebbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toby_Kebbell) as producer [Robert Chartoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Chartoff) and Tracy Letts as a studio executive who warns the film will flop without a star.

The studio's conditions were harsh. If production fell even one day behind schedule, Stallone would be replaced and the movie reshot. *Highbrow Magazine* reports that he turned down a $350,000 offer to let someone else play the part, and says the title is "not a brag" but a demand. Fox puts the original film's budget at $1 million, which gives a sense of how little room the crew had.

Reviewers have been warm. *Highbrow* critic Ulises Duenas calls the film funnier than he expected and says it makes you want to rewatch the real thing. America Magazine, writing from Philadelphia, reads it as a story of faith in your own work. For more on the response, see our [I Play Rocky review roundup](https://moduscarry.com/i-play-rocky-review-stallone-biopic/).

## Stallone's own verdict

Stallone has been open about his doubts. Fox cites a Hollywood Reporter interview in which he said he felt "mixed" at first, joking that he was not dead yet and the film might be jumping the gun. Then he saw Ippolito's video and, by his own account, briefly thought it was himself. Ippolito called the compliment "really, really unbelievable, very surreal".

## Frequently asked questions

### Who plays Sylvester Stallone in I Play Rocky?

Anthony Ippolito plays Stallone. He won the role after sending Peter Farrelly an unsolicited audition tape recorded in his grandmother's basement.

### How much weight did Anthony Ippolito gain?

Yahoo reports about 40 pounds, while Fox News says more than 30 pounds. Both describe a long programme of eating, weightlifting and boxing training.

### When was I Play Rocky released?
