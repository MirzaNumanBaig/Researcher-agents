- [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) voices the Easter Bunny.
- Until now, the only no-purchase option has been YouTube, free with ads, ScreenRant reports.
- The film holds a 75% Certified Fresh score on Rotten Tomatoes.
- It earned $306.9 million worldwide but was still reported to have lost money, which ended sequel plans.

Rise of the Guardians, the 2012 animated fantasy in which Hugh Jackman voices the Easter Bunny, will stream on HBO Max from 1 November 2026, the service has announced, as reported by [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/hugh-jackman-rise-of-the-guardians-hbo-max-streaming-release-november-2026/). It is a quiet return for a film that never got the franchise it was built for.

## When does Rise of the Guardians arrive on HBO Max?

The film lands on HBO Max on 1 November 2026, timed for the approaching holiday season. ScreenRant says it has spent a long stretch without a home on any streaming service. At the time of writing, the only way to watch it without buying or renting was the free, ad-supported version on YouTube.

That makes the HBO Max listing the first convenient subscription option in some time, 14 years after the film's cinema release.

## Who is in the cast?

The story follows a team of fairy-tale figures who protect children's dreams. ScreenRant lists Santa Claus (voiced by Alec Baldwin), the Tooth Fairy ([Isla Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Fisher)), the Easter Bunny (Jackman) and Sandman as the core four.

They recruit Jack Frost ([Chris Pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pine)) and the Boogeyman ([Jude Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_Law)) to face a dark force threatening the world. Jack is the main protagonist, though the rest of the Guardians matter to the plot. The film is based on a book series by William Joyce, which ScreenRant describes as an eight-novel franchise.

Jackman is better known for playing Wolverine in the X-Men films, but ScreenRant points to his other fantasy work, including Van Helsing and The Fountain. For more on the latter, see our story on [Hugh Jackman and The Fountain](https://moduscarry.com/hugh-jackman-plays-coy-on-secret-wars-as-the-fountain-is-revisited/).

## How did the film perform?

Reviews were generally positive. ScreenRant notes a Certified Fresh 75% on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), with praise for the fast pace, an Avengers-style feel and striking animation. The story drew most of the criticism.

The money was less kind. According to ScreenRant, the film took $306.9 million against a $146 million budget, yet marketing and distribution costs left it about $87 million in the red by the end of its theatrical run. That result shelved a planned sequel and the hope of a longer animated franchise.
