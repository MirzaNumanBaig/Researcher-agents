Pamela Anderson is best known for the spotlight, but [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/life-style/home-garden/pamela-andersons-garden-at-her-childhood-estate-in-vancouver-is-a-rustic-vintage-cottage-straight-out-of-a-storybook-from-embracing-imperfection-to-consistency-gardening-rules-beginners-can-follow/articleshow/135291470.cms) reports that she is just as content with soil on her hands. The publication describes her kitchen garden at her childhood home on Vancouver Island as a rustic, vintage cottage plot straight out of a children's storybook, and says it has become a favourite with her gardening followers online.

## What the garden looks like

According to the report, the garden is built from rows of raised wooden beds filled with vegetables, herbs and flowers that grow almost side by side. Willow obelisks and trellises add height, and an archway covered in squash leads into the different growing areas. Tall sunflowers, soft pink roses and a small tabletop greenhouse complete the scene.

The Times of India says Anderson posts regularly on Instagram and X, sharing pictures and videos of the beds and the plants climbing above them. It points to one Instagram video from her childhood home as the moment the relaxed, vintage look caught the eye of gardening fans.

## Seven rules from the garden

In 2025, the report says, Anderson teamed up with her skincare brand, Sonsie, to share a garden tour and seven rules she follows in the garden and in life. The outlet describes them as short and easy to remember, which makes them useful for anyone who has wanted to grow a few tomatoes but felt nervous about starting. The article draws out several of them for beginners.

## Tips beginners can borrow

**Mix vegetables and flowers.** Lavender grows beside onions, nasturtiums spill over the edges of the beds and roses bloom among the vegetables. The Times of India says this mix improves the look of a vegetable patch and attracts pollinators that help plants grow and produce well.

**Use raised beds and grow upwards.** The beds come in different sizes and heights, which gives the garden layers and movement instead of formal straight rows. The squash-covered arch, willow obelisks and trellises hold climbing crops and flowering vines, so small spaces such as a balcony can use the same idea with pots.

**Do not hover, be gentle and stay consistent.** The report groups three of her rules together. Give plants what they need and then leave them alone, because, as Anderson puts it, "Hovering never helped anything grow." Handle everything with love, since plants remember how they are treated. And be consistent, because gardens cannot be rushed and lasting results come from regular care.

**Forget perfection.** Anderson advises welcoming the natural messiness of a garden, paying attention to the roots below the surface, and letting the space evolve instead of forcing everything into place. One of her Instagram posts, quoted by the outlet, sums it up: "Life is like a garden… tend to it kindly, forgive the weeds, and cherish what blooms."
