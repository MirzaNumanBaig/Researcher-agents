**Quick summary**

- The Observer has reviewed *Eurotrash* at the Young Vic in London and says [Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw) and Kathryn Hunter cannot rescue it.
- The play adapts Christian Kracht's 2021 novel and is directed by Walter Meierjohann.
- The reviewer finds the production flashy and maximalist, but its message about obscene wealth stays unclear.
- *Eurotrash* runs at the Young Vic until 9 January.

The Observer has given a cool verdict on *Eurotrash*, the new Young Vic play starring [Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw) and [Kathryn Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Hunter). In its review, published on 27 November, the paper says the pair cannot save a production whose satire of grotesque wealth "makes no statement at all". It is a sharper note than some of the earlier notices for the show, which you can follow on our [Ben Whishaw](https://moduscarry.com/tag/ben-whishaw/) page.

## What Eurotrash is about

The play is adapted by [Colin Teevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Teevan) from the novel by [Christian Kracht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Kracht). According to The Observer, the book was shortlisted for the German and Swiss book prizes when it came out in 2021, and was longlisted for the 2025 International Booker prize in its English translation by Daniel Bowles.

The story is an absurdist, autofictional travelogue. A writer who shares the author's name takes a road trip across Switzerland with his 80-year-old mother, who is an alcoholic. She has spent time in a psychiatric institution, and the family's money has Nazi-linked roots and was recently boosted by her investments in arms, the review says. This time she has summoned him to Zurich because she wants help giving the money away.

They collect hundreds of thousands of francs in cash from the bank, carry it in a bag, and set off by taxi to find people willing to take it.

## Whishaw and Hunter on stage

Whishaw plays Christian as a loping figure in an oversized Barbour jacket and large headphones, and he narrates the story directly to the audience. Hunter plays Mama in Bulgari sunglasses, hunched and sharp-tongued. The Observer says the part is made for her and points to her turn in Complicité's 2022 *Drive Your Plow Over the Bones of the Dead* as part of a long run of acclaimed work. Glyn Pritchard completes the small cast, playing a janitor, a taxi driver and a waiter.

The reviewer saves the warmest words for the pair's dynamic. Mama has complaints about her son's neglect, while he sees her as a drag on his stalled creative life. Still, there is tenderness in her asking him to brush her hair or tell her a story. Hunter's physical comedy, including a slip on an icy mountain that ends in the splits, is singled out as a highlight.

## Where the review finds fault

The Observer is less taken with the rest. Some of the slapstick is described as crass, and one scene involving a burst stoma bag means front-row audience members are handed rain ponchos beforehand. Mama's disclosure of a childhood trauma is called heavy-handed, "a sob story crammed in and then deserted" that tells us little about her.

The design draws mixed comment. The word "Switzerland" is lit across the top of the set like an airport sign, and the stage ends up swamped with black rubbish bags. A lone musician, dressed as an airport customer service worker, plays violin and keyboard and sings David Bowie numbers from a glass booth at the side. The reviewer reads the booth as keeping her contained from the messy frivolity outside, and finds the overall effect strangely stark.

The verdict: for all the flashiness, the production's purpose remains obscure.

## What happens next

*Eurotrash* plays at the Young Vic in London until 9 January, so audiences have about six weeks to judge it for themselves. More notices may follow as the run continues.
