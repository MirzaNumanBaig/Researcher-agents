**Quick summary**

- The New South Wales Supreme Court dismissed a $15 million claim by UK production firm AI Film against [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson).
- Wilson's company, Camp Sugar Productions, was awarded $359,000 in unpaid producer fees plus interest, and Wilson's legal costs.
- AI Film's subsidiary, AI Films, received only $10 in nominal damages for technical breaches of a social media contract.
- A full written judgment is still to come, and related cases continue in Australia and California.

Rebel Wilson has won a second court victory over her directorial debut, *The Deb*, after the New South Wales Supreme Court threw out most of a $15 million case against her and ordered that her company be paid $359,000. According to [The Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2636498/rebel-wilson-wins-359000-in-legal-battle-over-the-deb-as-producers-receive-just-10?amp=1), the opposing side was awarded just $10.

## What the court decided

UK firm AI Film had argued that Wilson's social media posts and a website aimed at producer [Amanda Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Ghost) damaged the commercial prospects of the film. Justice Elisabeth Peden found the company had not shown a causal link between those statements and any financial loss, the Express Tribune reported.

AI Films, a subsidiary, was given $10 in nominal damages for technical breaches of a social media contract. Camp Sugar Productions, Wilson's company, was awarded $359,000 in unpaid producer fees with interest, and the other side was ordered to pay Wilson's legal costs.

The $10 figure is the headline, but it sits against a much larger gap. AI Film had sought $15 million. The result is roughly 1,500 times smaller for the claimants than the sum they asked for, even before counting the fees owed to Wilson's company.

## The finding against Wilson

The ruling was not a clean sweep. Citing ABC News, [Oz Arab Media](https://ozarab.media/rebel-wilson-awarded-359000-in-the-deb-case/) reported that Justice Peden found Wilson had asked for a malicious website to be created. The judgment said statements on the site amandaghostsucks.com were "actuated with malice on Ms Wilson's part".

The judge also noted that none of the people named on the site were AI Film producers. That detail, together with the lack of proven financial harm, is why the malice finding did not turn into a large damages award.

## A troubled release

The court also heard evidence about the film's reception behind the scenes. Emails from October 2025 showed international distributors had concerns that the movie felt "small and distinctly Australian", both outlets reported.

*The Deb* is a musical comedy in which Wilson directed, starred and produced. The Express Tribune put its reported budget at $22 million, against roughly $335,000 at the Australian box office. That is about 1.5 per cent of the cost recovered at home, which helps explain why the dispute over who is to blame has been so bitter.

## Wilson's response

Wilson marked the result on Instagram with a photo of herself in a graduation gown holding her University of New South Wales degree. "This girl never knew how much she was going to need that law degree!!" she wrote.

She also said defending herself had come at "an enormous personal, emotional and financial cost", but that she was proud to have stood her ground "for myself and for others I was trying to protect".

## What happens next

The case is one of several. Earlier in 2026 the Federal Court of Australia ruled in Wilson's favour in a defamation action brought by the film's lead actor, Charlotte MacInnes, who argued that Wilson's posts damaged her reputation. The court found the posts were not defamatory, and MacInnes has lodged an appeal. In the United States, the film's producers are pursuing separate defamation proceedings against Wilson in California. Both outlets say the full written reasons for the latest decision are still expected. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much was Rebel Wilson awarded?

Her company, Camp Sugar Productions, was awarded $359,000 in unpaid producer fees plus interest, and the court ordered the other side to pay her legal costs.

### How much did AI Film receive?

AI Film's subsidiary, AI Films, received $10 in nominal damages for technical breaches of a social media contract. The parent firm had sought $15 million.

### Did the judge find Wilson did nothing wrong?

No. The judge found Wilson had acted maliciously in connection with a website targeting Amanda Ghost, but the claimants did not prove it caused them financial loss.

### Is the dispute over?

Not yet. Charlotte MacInnes has appealed the earlier defamation ruling, the film's producers are pursuing a separate defamation case in California, and a full written judgment is still to come.
