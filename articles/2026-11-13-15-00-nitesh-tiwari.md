**Quick summary**

- Actor Saurabh Sachdeva, who plays Maricha, says director Nitesh Tiwari kept the cast on one interpretation of the epic, according to The Times of India.
- Sachdeva described Tiwari as "our North Star" in an interview with Variety, as reported on 13 November 2026.
- The New Indian Express reports that Assam artisans handmade the armour worn by Rama and Lakshman using cane, jute and local wood.
- Assam minister Nilima Devi called the credit a proud moment for the state.

[Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari)'s *Ramayana: Rise of a Legend* is still drawing attention after release, and two new reports on 13 November add detail from both sides of the camera. One is an actor praising the director's clarity. The other credits craftspeople in Assam for the film's armour.

## Nitesh Tiwari Ramayana: why the cast followed one vision

[Saurabh Sachdeva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saurabh_Sachdeva) plays Maricha in the mythological drama, which stars [Ranbir Kapoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranbir_Kapoor), [Sai Pallavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Pallavi) and Yash. In an interview with Variety, summarised by [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/bollywood/news/saurabh-sachdeva-who-plays-maricha-in-the-film-opens-up-on-ramayana-rise-of-a-legend-nitesh-tiwari-was-like-our-north-star-guiding-us/articleshow/135788394.cms), he said casting director [Mukesh Chhabra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukesh_Chhabra) auditioned him for the part and that he was thrilled to be chosen.

Sachdeva said he read several versions of the epic while preparing and saw that Maricha could be played in more than one way. Tiwari, he said, told him the character had to be played the way the director wanted. Sachdeva's reasoning was that if every actor stuck to a personal reading, the finished film could look very different.

"He was like our North Star, guiding us," Sachdeva said, according to the report. He also acknowledged that the film arrived to mixed reviews, saying the team expected debate because the story exists in many versions and carries deep faith for audiences. For readers wanting the critical view, our [review of the film](https://moduscarry.com/ramayana-rise-of-a-legend-review/) covers the spectacle in more detail.

## The hardest roles, and the heaviest costumes

Sachdeva said playing Rama and Sita was the toughest job, because audiences already hold strong images of those figures. By comparison, he could change his body, voice and attitude as Maricha. He said Kapoor and Pallavi carried the harder responsibility.

The shoot itself was physical. Sachdeva described the costumes as very heavy, with jewellery made from real materials to keep the look authentic. Pieces stored in air-conditioned rooms were cold to wear, which he called the hardest part of the shoot. He described Yash as energetic, disciplined and humble.

Looking ahead, he said he would like to play Duryodhan in a screen version of the *Mahabharata*, citing his training in mask work and body language.

## Assam artisans behind the armour

The second story is about craft. [The New Indian Express](https://www.newindianexpress.com/states/assam/2026/Nov/13/guwahati-diary-assam-artisans-showcase-indigenous-craftsmanship-in-nitesh-tiwaris-ramayana) reports that artisans in Assam handcrafted the armour for Rama and Lakshman using indigenous cane, jute and local wood elements.

Assam minister [Nilima Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilima_Devi) said the film's costume designer, Ajay Kumar, travelled to Assam to learn about these crafts and, with his team, brought the pieces to life. She called the recognition on a global platform a matter of pride for every Assamese. Fellow minister Kaushik Roy linked the film to the state's long relationship with the epic, pointing to the 14th-century [Saptakanda Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saptakanda_Ramayana) by [Madhava Kandali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhava_Kandali).
