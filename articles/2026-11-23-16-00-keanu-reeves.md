**Quick summary**

- Constantine, the 2005 DC film starring [Keanu Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keanu_Reeves), is available on HBO Max, according to Collider.
- Critics gave it 46% on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), but Collider says 40% of Letterboxd viewers rated it four stars or higher.
- Collider reports that Reeves and director [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) have confirmed a sequel is on its way.
- No release date or cast details for the sequel appear in the source material.

A film that critics shrugged at in 2005 is getting a second look. Constantine, the supernatural thriller in which Keanu Reeves plays DC Comics' John Constantine, is now streaming on HBO Max, and Collider argues in a new piece that it is one of the best-looking superhero films ever made. The timing matters, because a sequel has been confirmed by Reeves and Lawrence, according to the same report.

## Why Constantine is being rediscovered

Collider describes the film as a cult favourite that has gained a second life among fans of Reeves and of 1990s gothic style. It did well at the box office, the outlet notes, yet it seemed to fade from the cultural conversation once it left cinemas.

The numbers show a split. Collider says many critics at the time marked the film down for its plotting and heavy use of visual effects, which left it with a 46% Rotten Tomatoes score. Among viewers on Letterboxd, the picture is warmer: Collider puts the share rating it four stars or above at 40%.

## What the film looks like

Lawrence, who later directed films in [The Hunger Games](https://moduscarry.com/category/movies/) series and the Stephen King adaptation The Long Walk, used Constantine as a calling card, Collider writes. The outlet says none of his later work matches it for sheer visual spectacle.

Collider describes a film that opens somewhere between an [Indiana Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Jones) adventure and The Exorcist before settling into demonic noir. Exteriors and interiors are washed in greens, yellows and browns, and the camera tilts and looks down from above whenever it can.

Its version of Hell is Los Angeles frozen in the middle of an atomic blast, with sinners and demons writhing beneath the surface. [Tilda Swinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilda_Swinton) plays the angel Gabriel, and Collider singles out Peter Stormare's single five-minute scene as Satan, in a white suit and dripping tar, as the highlight. [Djimon Hounsou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djimon_Hounsou) also appears in the cast.

## Reeves carries the film

Reeves was already established through The Matrix trilogy when Constantine arrived, but he had not yet picked up the pistols of John Wick. Collider says that Wick-style energy is visible anyway: the angry "Yeahs" aimed at demons and angels, the constant lighting up, and a rude gesture for the devil.

The outlet also credits him with real emotion in quieter moments, including a scene where oily black tumours are drawn from his chest.

## What happens next

Collider says Reeves and Lawrence have talked about sequel ideas for years, including versions that would lean harder into an R rating, and that a sequel is now confirmed. The piece gives no start date, release window or cast list, so those details remain unknown. For now, the original is the easiest way to catch up.
