**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) and [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison) wrote together over Thanksgiving weekend in 1968, at Dylan's home near Woodstock, New York.
- One result, "I'd Have You Anytime", opens Harrison's solo album *All Things Must Pass*.
- The other, "Nowhere to Go" (first called "Everybody Comes to Town"), was never officially released, according to American Songwriter.
- A separate American Songwriter list this week revisits three Dylan songs that became big hits for other artists.

## A weekend that produced two songs

Men's Journal this week returned to a quiet piece of rock history: in late November 1968, Bob Dylan and George Harrison sat down over the Thanksgiving weekend and wrote a song that the public has never been able to buy. The Men's Journal page was not available to us, so the detail below comes from American Songwriter's coverage of the same story.

According to [American Songwriter](https://americansongwriter.com/the-lost-george-harrison-and-bob-dylan-collaboration-written-over-thanksgiving-weekend-in-1968), the sessions took place at Dylan's home in Bearsville, near Woodstock. The song was first called "Everybody Comes to Town" and later became "Nowhere to Go". The magazine says it was meant for Harrison's solo album *All Things Must Pass* but never received an official release.

## The song that did make it

The same weekend produced something that did reach listeners. American Songwriter's [Behind the Song feature](https://americansongwriter.com/behind-the-song-id-have-you-anytime-by-george-harrison-bob-dylan) says "I'd Have You Anytime" was written on 20 November 1968 and became the opening track of *All Things Must Pass*, Harrison's first solo album after the Beatles, produced by Phil Spector.

The way it came together is a good picture of the two men's working relationship. Harrison showed Dylan a set of chords, and Dylan wrote the words. Harrison later said the bridge grew out of Dylan's own song "I Threw It All Away". He also recalled that the opening moves from a Gmaj7 chord up four frets to Bbmaj7, a shape Dylan had little experience with at the time.

On the record, Harrison sings lead and harmony and plays acoustic guitar. Eric Clapton plays electric guitar and the solo, Klaus Voormann is on bass and Alan White on drums. Harrison said he put the song first partly because Dylan had helped write it and he felt he needed that support.

## Why the second song stayed in the drawer

American Songwriter says the lyrics of "Nowhere to Go" reflect Harrison's frustration at being seen only as a Beatle and his wish to do more. Its account of the weekend also points to Dylan's state of mind. He was still easing back after his 1966 motorcycle accident and did not tour again until 1974. Harrison wrote in his 1982 memoir *I, Me, Mine* that Dylan seemed very nervous during the visit.

The magazine adds that by 1970 the two met again at Dylan's place in Greenwich Village, where Harrison recorded a demo of the song. Even so, no official version followed. We could not confirm from the sources we read why it was left off the album, so that part of the story remains open.

Sources also differ on small details, such as the exact location of the 1968 visit and whether the writing began before or after Thanksgiving Day itself. The broad outline is consistent: one weekend, two songs, and only one of them released.

## Dylan songs that thrived in other hands

The collaboration story landed on the same day as another reminder of Dylan's reach. A separate [American Songwriter list](https://americansongwriter.com/3-songs-written-by-bob-dylan-that-became-incredible-hits-for-other-artists/) picks out three of his songs that became hits for other artists.

- "Blowin' in the Wind" was a five-week No. 1 single for Peter, Paul and Mary. Dylan had first put it out on *The Freewheelin' Bob Dylan*.
- "Mr. Tambourine Man" gave The Byrds their first No. 1. Dylan's own version, on *Bringing It All Back Home* in 1965, includes more verses than the group's recording.
- "Wagon Wheel" was released by Old Crow Medicine Show in 2004. Dylan made the chorus in 1973, and [Ketch Secor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketch_Secor) later added the verses. In 2013 [Darius Rucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_Rucker) took it to No. 1.

## What happens next

There is no announcement of an official release for "Nowhere to Go", and nothing in the coverage we read suggests one is planned. For now the song lives in the retelling, while "I'd Have You Anytime" remains the audible proof of that weekend in 1968.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Bob Dylan and George Harrison write a song together?

Yes. According to American Songwriter, they wrote "I'd Have You Anytime" in November 1968. It opens Harrison's album *All Things Must Pass*.

### What was the unreleased Dylan and Harrison song?

It was "Nowhere to Go", first titled "Everybody Comes to Town". American Songwriter says it was written the same Thanksgiving weekend and never officially released.

### Which Dylan songs became hits for other artists?

American Songwriter highlights "Blowin' in the Wind" (Peter, Paul and Mary), "Mr. Tambourine Man" (The Byrds) and "Wagon Wheel" (Old Crow Medicine Show, later Darius Rucker).
