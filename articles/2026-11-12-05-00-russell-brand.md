**Quick summary**

- A London museum of curiosities has taken a Russell Brand exhibit off display, [Variety reported](https://variety.com/2026/global/news/russell-brands-pubic-hair-london-museum-removed-1236945231/) on Wednesday 11 November.
- The curator, identified as Wynd, said the item was removed straight away and that Brand "has no place in my museum of horrors".
- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), 51, was sentenced to 10 years and six months on Monday 9 November at Southwark Crown Court.
- A jury convicted him of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, for offences between 2004 and 2009.

A London museum has pulled a Russell Brand exhibit from public view just days after the comedian and actor was jailed. Variety reports that the curator, who goes by Wynd, took the item down after the sentencing, saying Brand has no place in the collection. The move is small in scale, but it shows how far the fallout from the case is spreading beyond the courtroom.

## What the Russell Brand museum removal involved

The item was a sample of pubic hair that had sat among the more notorious pieces in Wynd's collection for more than a decade. According to a 2016 account of the museum cited by Variety, it was originally posted to the curator by a woman who said she had shaved Brand at a Camden Town salon, along with a handwritten letter. Variety notes that the hair's authenticity has never been independently verified.

Wynd told the outlet on Wednesday afternoon: "I took them straight off display and have no desire to hear his name again." The museum opened in 2014 on Mare Street in Hackney and is modelled on the traditional Wunderkammer, or cabinet of curiosities. Its collection includes shrunken heads, two-headed animals, dodo bones, occult objects and a lock of Elvis Presley's hair. An absinthe parlour and cocktail bar run by the Last Tuesday Society also operates in the building.

The removal follows a pattern. A week that began with a courtroom verdict is now producing a steady stream of reactions from institutions, commentators and public figures.

## The verdict behind the decision

Variety says a jury found Brand guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, involving four women. The offences took place between 2004 and 2009, while he was rising to fame in British television and Hollywood. He was acquitted of two further rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault. He received a sentence of 10 years and six months.

In his sentencing remarks, Mr Justice Bennathan said women keep the right to say no and to seek justice "whatever women wear, wherever they go, indeed whatever they drink", as quoted by [The Guardian's Rhiannon Lucy Cosslett](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/11/russell-brand-david-walliams-noughties-culture-girls-women). Her column argues the early 2000s "lad culture" made abuse seem normal to many young women, and she links the reaction to Brand with the separate Guardian and ITV investigation into David Walliams. Walliams has denied the allegations against him.

## Debate over rape myths in court

The trial has also prompted a wider policy conversation. [The Guardian's letters page](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2026/nov/11/russell-brand-trial-myths-and-a-questionable-faith) noted that the government has pledged to examine calls to crack down on "rape myths" in trials. Andy Burnham told Times Radio that changes should ensure trials "do not leave the woman feeling like she is on trial in court", according to one letter writer.

Katie Kempen, chief executive of Victim Support, wrote that the defence's use of such stereotypes in this case was "disgraceful" and that the case must be "a catalyst for meaningful action". Other readers questioned Brand's public conversion to Christianity. These are opinions from letter writers, not findings, but they show where the public discussion is heading.

## Old comments resurface online

Brand's past is also being revisited on social media. [Mezha.net](https://mezha.net/eng/news/mezha-2db6abdf8835fa78a28d6059_katy_perry-s_-he/) and [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/katy-perrys-comment-ex-should-jail-resurfaces-after-russell-brands-prison-sentencing) both report that remarks by his former wife, Katy Perry, have circulated again. In a 2013 Vogue interview she described his behaviour during their marriage as "controlling". The pair met filming "Get Him to the Greek" in 2009 and married in India on 23 October 2010. The marriage lasted about 14 months.

At a July concert at Blenheim Palace, Perry said during an audience game: "I hope my ex isn't here. He should be in jail," according to The Mirror, as relayed by both outlets. She did not name Brand, so the line should not be read as a statement about him.

The same reports say his wife, Laura Brand, testified in his defence at the trial, describing him as a caring father and attentive husband. That evidence sat alongside the jury's verdicts.

## What happens next
