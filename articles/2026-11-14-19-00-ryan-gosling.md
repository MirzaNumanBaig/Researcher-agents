**Quick summary**

- Cinematographer Linus Sandgren says Marvel's Ghost Rider will have an emotional, grounded visual approach, according to Business Upturn.
- He spoke at the Hamilton Behind the Camera Awards on 13 November, where he was honoured for Dune: Part Three.
- [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) stars, [Shawn Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Levy) directs, and filming is expected to start in New York in 2027.
- Marvel has set a theatrical release of 28 July 2028.

[Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling)'s Marvel debut has its cinematographer. Linus Sandgren, who shot [La La Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_La_Land), told an awards audience on 13 November that he wants Ghost Rider to feel emotional and grounded rather than purely spectacle, Business Upturn reported on 14 November. The film is due in cinemas on 28 July 2028.

## What Linus Sandgren said about Ghost Rider

According to [Business Upturn](https://businessupturn.com/entertainment/hollywood/hollywood-movies/la-la-land-cinematographer-linus-sandgren-promises-an-emotional-grounded-visual-approach-for-ryan-goslings-ghost-rider/), Sandgren described his plans at the Hamilton Behind the Camera Awards, where he was recognised for his work on Dune: Part Three. The outlet describes him as an Oscar-winning artist known for La La Land and says he wants the supernatural story to rest on a strong emotional core while still giving the character a distinctive cinematic flair.

That is a notable choice for a character built around a flaming skull and a haunted bargain. A grounded treatment suggests the film will lean on the person underneath the fire, which fits the emotional angle Sandgren stressed.

## How Ghost Rider will be shot

Business Upturn reports that the production is expected to use physical film rather than relying only on digital cinematography. The outlet says that choice could add depth to the supernatural material, though that is its own assessment rather than a stated aim from the studio.

Filming is anticipated to begin in New York in 2027. The screenplay is being written by [Jonathan Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Tropper), and Levy is in the director's chair. The project was unveiled at San Diego Comic-Con in July 2026, and Marvel has since scheduled the release for 28 July 2028, roughly two years away.

## Gosling's Star Wars: Starfighter arrives first

Ghost Rider is not Gosling's next release. According to [NOW Toronto](https://nowtoronto.com/culture/star-wars-a-new-hope-starfighter-ryan-gosling-disney/), [Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars): Starfighter reaches cinemas on 28 May 2027, with Gosling as a cynical rogue confronted by his mysterious past. The studio's description has him hurled into a dangerous adventure across the galaxy.

Levy also directs that film, which the outlet says is set about five years after The Rise of Skywalker. The cast includes [Matt Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Smith), [Mia Goth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Goth) and [Amy Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Adams). NOW Toronto notes Levy's earlier work with fellow Canadian [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) on The Fall Guy and Deadpool & Wolverine, and credits Gosling for his recent turns in Project Hail Mary and Barbie. For earlier coverage, see our report on the [Starfighter set photo](https://moduscarry.com/ryan-gosling-star-wars-starfighter/).

## A classic returns to cinemas

The same report says Disney will re-release a restored version of the original 1977 film, now titled A New Hope, on 19 February, including a special 70mm IMAX edition. A new poster by British artist Matt Ferguson accompanies it. NOW Toronto frames the re-release as the start of a new run of Star Wars projects, with Starfighter the first new film in that slate.
