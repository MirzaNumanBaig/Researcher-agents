**Quick summary**

- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) has been sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison, according to The Irish Times.
- He was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, and acquitted of two rape charges.
- The judge, Joel Bennathan, said women keep the right to say no whatever they wear, drink or where they go.
- Commentators are now asking whether the case is a tipping point for consent prosecutions.

The Russell Brand verdict is now being read as more than the end of one trial. In an opinion column published on 15 November, [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/opinion/2026/11/15/rachel-odwyer-the-russell-brand-verdict-brought-up-memories-of-an-incident-ive-never-shared/) says the former comedian has been sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison for the rape of one woman and the sexual assault of three others.

## What Russell Brand was convicted of

According to the column, the jury found Brand guilty of three counts of sexual assault, relating to 2004, 2005 and 2009, and one count of rape in 2009. The rape complainant was 21 when she met him after a stand-up show at London's Hammersmith Apollo and later went to his home.

The paper reports that she described feeling she had less right to be upset than she did. She had admired him, and she had initially consented to what happened. Brand was acquitted of two other rape charges.

## The judge's words

Judge Joel Bennathan was described as unequivocal. As quoted by The Irish Times, he said the women "took perfectly reasonable decisions to go to a party and have a few drinks, or to go to your house for one type of consensual sex". He added that whatever women wear, wherever they go and whatever they drink, "they retain the right to say no".

The column notes that cases where consent is given and then withdrawn are notoriously hard to prosecute. For that reason, it says, the 10-year sentence is being hailed as a milestone in how sexual violence is prosecuted.

## Questions about the trial itself

The piece is also critical of how the case was run. It says the cross-examination of the complainants has been called "deeply uncomfortable" and calls it "actively regressive". It quotes the defence barrister telling the jury that none of the women resisted, screamed or tried to leave.

The author also points to Brand's public image. In his 2007 memoir, now withdrawn from print, he described himself as a man handed a "Wonka ticket" to a "sex factory" because of fame. The column argues he presented himself as a lovable sex pest, not a coercive or violent one.

This is an opinion piece, so its framing is the writer's own. The facts above, the convictions, the sentence and the judge's remarks, are the parts it reports about the case.

## What happens next

The column asks whether the conviction is a tipping point or a win for consent that leaves deeper habits unchanged. Related developments are already moving.
