**Quick summary**

- The 2026 live-action *Street Fighter*, directed by Kitao Sakurai, has reportedly taken US$100.8 million worldwide against an estimated US$82 million budget.
- No sequel has been confirmed, and ScreenRant says it is too early to say whether a second film will happen.
- Critics were mixed: Rotten Tomatoes lists 42% and Metacritic 43/100 from 28 critics.
- Sequel speculation has focused on characters such as Sagat, who is set up in a post-credits scene.

Two months after it opened, the new *Street Fighter* film is still driving conversation, and the loudest question is whether a second film will follow. Nothing has been announced. What exists is a film that has cleared its budget on paper, a split critical reception and an ending that leaves the door open.

## Where the box office stands

According to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/street-fighter-movie-sequel-game-characters-ranked/), the film has reportedly earned US$100.8 million worldwide. The estimated production budget is US$82 million. That is a gross of about 1.2 times the budget, which sits at the thin end of what a big studio release needs once marketing and cinema revenue splits are counted. Our data tags the run as weak for that reason.

The run is also winding down. Our earlier coverage reported that the film was [leaving 3,000 theatres before its digital debut](https://moduscarry.com/street-fighter-box-office-digital-release/) and [losing 60% of its screens as Godzilla closed in](https://moduscarry.com/street-fighter-box-office-screens-godzilla/). A sequel decision would likely rest on how the film performs once it reaches home viewing.

## What critics made of it

Reviews were divided. The film holds 42% on Rotten Tomatoes and 43/100 on Metacritic, based on 28 critics, according to [Wikipedia's film entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(2026_film)). The critics' summary there credits the film with affection for the games and plenty of visual flair, but faults its muddled storytelling and juvenile humour.

ScreenRant's view is friendlier. It describes the film as a light, polished game adaptation that leans into the series' absurdity.

## The cast and the setup for more

Sakurai's film stars Andrew Koji as Ryu, Noah Centineo as Ken Masters and Callina Liang as Chun-Li, with David Dastmalchian as M. Bison and 50 Cent as Balrog, according to ScreenRant. Mel Jarnson plays Cammy, and the fate of Juli after her fight with Chun-Li is left unclear.

The ending is open. ScreenRant's ranking of characters it would like to see in a follow-up puts Sagat first, because a post-credits scene sets him up and Bison is short on allies. T. Hawk, Dudley, Juni, Juri, Adon, C. Viper and Sakura follow. That list is the outlet's opinion, not a studio plan.

## For fans waiting on a sequel

[Comic Book Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources) has also published a guide to five martial arts films for fans of the movie. It points to *Enter the Dragon* for its island tournament, which echoes the World Warrior Tournament, and to *The Raid 2*, *Ong-Bak* and *Kung Fu Hustle* for stunt work and fight choreography. It also lists the 2021 *Mortal Kombat*. For more, see all our [Street Fighter](https://moduscarry.com/tag/street-fighter/) coverage or the [Movies](https://moduscarry.com/category/movies/) section.

## What happens next

No studio has announced a second film. The next signals to watch are the home-viewing numbers and any statement from the filmmakers or cast. Until then, a sequel remains fan speculation rather than a plan.
