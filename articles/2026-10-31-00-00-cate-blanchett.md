**Quick summary**

- The first five short films from [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett)'s Displacement Film Fund had their Japan premiere at the Tokyo International Film Festival, Variety reported on 30 October 2026.
- Directors [Mohammad Rasoulof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Rasoulof) and [Shahrbanoo Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrbanoo_Sadat) said they did not want a separate Oscar category for exiled filmmakers.
- Blanchett welcomed the Academy's recognition of displaced artists, calling it "very profound".
- A New York qualifying screening is planned for mid-November, and a second group of filmmakers is now making its films.

Two of the world's most prominent exiled directors have told a Tokyo audience they do not want an Oscar category of their own. Rasoulof and Sadat spoke at the Tokyo International Film Festival, where Blanchett's Displacement Film Fund gave its first five short films their Japan premiere, according to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/festivals/rasoulof-sadat-push-back-oscar-category-exiled-filmmakers-1236934877/).

## What the filmmakers said about labels

Sadat, an Iranian-born Afghan director whose fund-backed short is "Super Afghan Gym", rejected the idea outright. She told the panel she does not like being labelled, and that she does not see herself as "the displaced filmmaker". She called the idea a joke and said she was unsure she would want to sit in that category "even if they give me the Oscar".

Rasoulof, who directed the fund's "The Sense of Water", framed it as a question of energy. He said he wants to spend his on making films, and that where they are shown may matter less than it seems. Specific categories, he suggested, can sometimes create problems. He would rather focus on closing the distance between filmmakers and audiences.

[Clare Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Stewart), managing director of International Film Festival Rotterdam (IFFR), made a practical point. She noted that Rasoulof's "The Seed of the Sacred Fig", nominated in the last Oscar cycle, was among the first films to benefit from the Academy's rule change, and that it represented Germany. Stewart said she personally saw no need for a separate category.

## Blanchett on the Academy's ruling

Blanchett, the fund's co-founder, said everyone on the panel had welcomed the Academy's ruling and the way it acknowledges the reality facing artists worldwide. Wars and conflicts displace people through no fault of their own, she said, and artistic careers are often interrupted. The recognition "sounds like a small thing", she added, "but it is very profound".

She also tied the project to the film industry's habits. Because the business is used to international co-productions, she said, the cultural diplomacy inside it is something she wishes more governments would copy.

## How the fund began

The fund backs filmmakers who are displaced themselves, or who have a track record of telling authentic stories about displacement. Variety reports it took shape at the UNHCR's Global Refugee Forum in December 2023. There Blanchett, a UNHCR goodwill ambassador for more than a decade, met Yanai Koji, a director and senior executive officer at Uniqlo parent Fast Retailing.

Blanchett put the motivation in numbers: one in 70 people around the world is displaced, she said, so why are their stories not more in the mainstream? She described the fund as a chance to counter the "toxic narrative" that often surrounds displaced people.

In October 2024, Stewart told Blanchett about IFFR's Hubert Bals Fund, which the festival has run for 30 years to support filmmakers where infrastructure is thin or financing is politically difficult. IFFR agreed to manage the new fund, which launched at the festival in January 2025. Its founding partners are Yanai's production company Mastermind, Uniqlo, the Tamer Family Foundation, the Amahoro Coalition and the Dutch foundation Droom en Daad. The SP Lohia Foundation joined as a main partner at IFFR this year.

## The first cohort

The first group was announced at Cannes in 2025 and includes Rasoulof, Sadat, Mo Harawe, Hasan Kattan and Maryna Er Gorbach. Their films premiered at IFFR in January and have since screened in London and El Gouna, with São Paulo still to come. Blanchett said the directors had full freedom over subject and point of view, and their stories did not have to address displacement directly.

## What happens next

The first cohort's films have already had an Academy qualifying screening in Los Angeles, with another set for New York in mid-November, Blanchett said. She described the aim as serving Academy considerations, but first of all letting the American audience see the films. A second cohort is currently making its films.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Displacement Film Fund?
It is a fund co-founded by Cate Blanchett that supports filmmakers who are displaced or who have a record of telling authentic stories about displacement. IFFR's Hubert Bals Fund manages it.

### Who made the first films?
The first cohort is Mohammad Rasoulof, Shahrbanoo Sadat, Mo Harawe, Hasan Kattan and Maryna Er Gorbach, according to Variety.

### Do the directors want an Oscar category for exiled filmmakers?
Rasoulof and Sadat both said no, and IFFR's Clare Stewart said she saw no need either. Blanchett welcomed the Academy's existing recognition of displaced artists.
