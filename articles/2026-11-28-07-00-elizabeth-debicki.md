**Quick summary**

- [Elizabeth Debicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Debicki) plays Hollywood agent Karen Crosby in *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*.
- GQ calls the role one of the film's most beguiling and says she is the closest thing the film has to a co-lead.
- Debicki told That Shelf that costume designer Trish Somerville and director David Fincher dressed Karen in shades of white.
- GQ also calls the film one of the best of the year.

Elizabeth Debicki has emerged as the surprise centre of *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*, with GQ and That Shelf both singling out her turn as agent Karen Crosby in pieces published on 27 November 2026. The film is written by [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino) and directed by [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher), and it stars [Brad Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt).

## Why critics keep returning to Karen Crosby

GQ's writer argues that the film's early doubters had reasons to worry. A Fincher film built from a Tarantino script, the piece notes, risked a clash of two strong styles. The review says the result works, and it ranks the film among the best of the year.

Within that, Debicki gets the most attention. [According to GQ](https://www.gq.com/story/elizabeth-debicki-cliff-booth), she plays Karen with an icy, over-it resolve, and her fascination with Cliff produces what one critic called Tarantino's best-written romance since [*Jackie Brown*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Brown). The reviewer says Karen is the closest thing to a co-lead, yet viewers leave wanting more of her.

GQ also highlights a long monologue in which Karen explains why she thinks film entertains but rarely rises to true art. It is delivered in front of her client Charlie Peters, played by [Scott Caan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Caan). Karen also name-drops critic [Pauline Kael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauline_Kael) when she describes her own past as a film critic.

## A film that moves forward from 1969

The story picks up seven years after the 2019 hit [*Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Once_Upon_a_Time_in_Hollywood), which won Pitt an Oscar for playing stuntman Cliff Booth, [as That Shelf notes](https://thatshelf.com/how-elizabeth-debicki-gets-her-freak-on-with-cliff-booth/). This time the setting is 1977, with Cliff juggling odd jobs until Karen calls. She hopes he can advise a client who insists on doing his own stunts. Her interest in Cliff soon goes beyond business, according to That Shelf.

For Debicki, who won a Golden Globe and an Emmy for playing Princess Diana in *The Crown*, the appeal was the pace. She told That Shelf that her scenes often run 10 to 12 pages, which is rare now. She said the old-school rhythm made her think about how people focused on each other in a "less noisy" analogue world, with Karen not taking "a thousand calls" once Cliff arrives.

## The meaning of all that white

The costume work is part of the character, too. Debicki credited costume designer Trish Somerville, saying she and Fincher decided Karen would always wear varying shades of white. She told That Shelf that this made Karen seem removed and untouchable at first, and that she then becomes "a little less white, a little less clean" as the story goes on.

That Shelf says the pressed, poised look contrasts with Cliff's dishevelled surfer style, and it describes Debicki's performance as fearless. The outlet adds that she often steals scenes in what is otherwise Pitt's star vehicle. Debicki herself describes Karen as savvy and fun, and says Cliff's irreverence as an outsider unlocks something in her.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who does Elizabeth Debicki play in The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth?

She plays Karen Crosby, a high-powered Hollywood agent who ends up drawn to Cliff. GQ describes her as the closest thing to a co-lead.

### Who made The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth?

Tarantino wrote the script and Fincher directed. Pitt returns as Cliff, seven years after *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*, according to That Shelf.

### Why does Karen Crosby wear white?

Debicki says Somerville and Fincher chose shades of white to make Karen seem untouchable at the start, then let her look become less clean as she gets closer to Cliff.
