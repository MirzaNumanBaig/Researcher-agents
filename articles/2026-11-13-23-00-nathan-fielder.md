**Quick summary**

- A24 announced on Wednesday, 4 November, that NASA asked to show *You Can See Everything* on the International Space Station.
- The studio says the request was granted. No screening date has been given.
- The documentary, directed by [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) and [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim), opened in US cinemas on 16 October.
- According to Mashable, it had earned more than US$64 million in North America by 4 November.
- Audiences in several countries, Australia among them, are still waiting for a local release.

A24 says astronauts about 250 miles above Earth will soon get a private screening of Nathan Fielder's Elizabeth Holmes documentary. The studio announced on 4 November that NASA had asked to show *You Can See Everything* on the International Space Station, and that the request was granted. [Mashable reported](https://mashable.com/entertainment/you-can-see-everything-a24-nasa-iss-screening) the news this week.

## How A24 announced the NASA request

According to Mashable, A24 posted on social media with an image from the film under a cropped email. The subject line read "FWD: [EXTERNAL] NASA Request for Private Viewing". The caption said the request was granted and that the film was "coming soon" to the station.

There is no screening date yet. Mashable notes that the move adds to the astronauts' movie-night options, though it does not say what else is in the station's library. That is all the public record shows so far, and neither NASA nor A24 has given further detail in the sources we reviewed.

## What the film is about

The documentary starts with Elizabeth Holmes inviting a film crew to follow her through the 34 days before she reported to prison in 2023. It then widens into a project that ran for three years, according to Mashable. Holmes, the Theranos founder, was convicted in 2022 of defrauding investors after the promises behind her blood-testing startup unravelled.

Viewers who know the story from fiction may remember [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) playing Holmes in Hulu's *The Dropout*. This film takes a different route, built around Holmes herself and the people who filmed her.

## A hit on Earth, a wait elsewhere

Mashable describes the film as a critical and commercial hit. Its North American total of more than US$64 million as of 4 November is a notable figure for a documentary, which usually plays to far smaller audiences. For wider context on the film's reception, see our earlier coverage: [Nathan Fielder's You Can See Everything: Word Games and Sneakers](https://moduscarry.com/nathan-fielder-you-can-see-everything-2/).

The international rollout is the sore point. Mashable quotes fans who joked that the film would reach orbit before Europe, the UK or Mexico. One user wrote that Australia "still has to wait". Others suggested a follow-up idea, pointing to Fielder's series *The Rehearsal*, where one commenter said he "recreates the moon landing by actually landing on the moon". That is a fan joke, not an announcement.
