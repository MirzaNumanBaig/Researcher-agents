**Quick summary**

- Sylvester Stallone I Play Rocky: director [Peter Farrelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrelly) says Stallone approved the film but had no direct involvement.
- Farrelly advised Stallone not to take a producer credit, so the film would not look shaped by its subject.
- Stallone then told an interviewer he knew nothing about the film, which set off a wave of online anger.
- Farrelly shared the story at a Deadline Contenders event, according to CinemaBlend.
- Stallone has since said he enjoyed the film and Anthony Ippolito's performance as him.

## The advice that backfired

The story of [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) and *I Play Rocky* includes an awkward moment: Stallone said he knew nothing about the film, and it caused an uproar before the film opened. Farrelly, who directed the biopic, told the story himself during a Deadline Contenders conversation, as [CinemaBlend reported](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/sylvester-stallone-caused-drama-after-i-play-rocky-announced) on Sunday.

*I Play Rocky* follows the making of the original 1976 *Rocky*, a film that turns 50 this year. Stallone did not work on the new movie. According to CinemaBlend, he did give the creative team his official blessing, and Farrelly says he offered early input on how his family, including his father, Frank Stallone Sr., should be portrayed.

The trouble began with a credit. Farrelly asked Stallone whether he should join as a producer, and said he advised against it. Stallone comes across well in the film, and an "executive produced by Sylvester Stallone" card at the end might lead audiences to assume he had steered the story. Farrelly said that was not the case. Stallone agreed, and Farrelly asked him to keep a little distance.

## How "a little distance" became a headline

About a week later, Farrelly said, an interviewer asked Stallone for his thoughts on a movie about the making of *Rocky*. Stallone's answer, as Farrelly tells it, was that he knew nothing about it.

The reaction was fast. Farrelly described social media filling with posts accusing the filmmakers of making the film without even speaking to Stallone. He said [Frank Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Stallone), the actor's brother, was among those speaking out against the project.

Farrelly phoned Stallone to ask what had happened. In his telling, Stallone replied that he had simply done what he was asked and separated himself from the film. Farrelly's answer was that he had not meant it to go that far. Stallone, he said, promised to keep that in mind from then on.

CinemaBlend calls it a simple misunderstanding. The account comes from Farrelly alone.

## A warm reception since

Whatever the early friction, the outlet says no lasting harm appears to have been done. It reports that the film has been well received and is attracting awards-season buzz, and that [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) has given it a public thumbs-up.

Stallone himself, CinemaBlend reports, said he had expected to be "hypercritical" when he watched it. He ended up enjoying the film overall, including Ippolito's portrayal of him. The film is now in cinemas, according to the outlet.

## Stallone's other screen role: Tulsa King

Stallone's current television work is also in the news. In a piece for [Comic Book Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources), writer Phineas Larson looked at Season 4, Episode 5 of *Tulsa King*, titled "Dinosaurs". In it, Stallone's character Dwight Manfredi has crew members dig up a fossil at Fennario Ranch, and a palaeontologist, Dr. Henstock, values the Acrocanthosaurus skull at more than a million dollars.

[CBR's analysis](https://www.cbr.com/tulsa-king-sets-up-sylvester-stallone-ending/) reads the territorial injuries on the fossil as a metaphor for Dwight's feud with the gangster Quiet Ray. The writer argues the show is hinting at a violent end for Dwight. That is the critic's interpretation, not anything confirmed by the show's producers, and no ending has been announced.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Sylvester Stallone produce I Play Rocky?

No. Farrelly says he advised against a producer credit so the film would not look shaped by Stallone, and Stallone agreed. He gave his approval but had no direct involvement, according to CinemaBlend.

### Who directed I Play Rocky?

[Peter Farrelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrelly) directed it. CinemaBlend notes he is also known for *Dumb and Dumber*.

### What did Stallone think of the film?

CinemaBlend reports he expected to be "hypercritical" but enjoyed the film, including Anthony Ippolito's performance as him.

### Who plays Dwight Manfredi in Tulsa King?

Stallone plays Dwight Manfredi in Taylor Sheridan's *Tulsa King*, which streams on Paramount+, according to CBR.
