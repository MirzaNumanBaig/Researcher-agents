**Quick summary**

- [Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe) has said more than once that Master and Commander: The Far Side of the World should have led to more films.
- He told ABC in December 2025: "We should have made half a dozen Master and Commanders", according to ScreenRant.
- The 2003 film earned about 10 Oscar nominations but, per ScreenRant, made roughly $212 million worldwide against a reported $150 million budget.
- The source novels run to 20 finished books, so material was never the problem.

Russell Crowe is still talking about the sequels he never got to make. A ScreenRant piece published on 14 November 2026 revisits his comments about Master and Commander: The Far Side of the World, the 2003 naval epic in which he played Royal Navy captain Jack Aubrey, and argues the film is one of Hollywood's most obvious abandoned franchises.

## What Crowe has said about more films

Crowe has been consistent. Speaking to GQ in 2024, he said, "We should've definitely done more than one movie," according to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/russell-crowe-master-and-commander-masterpiece-sequels/). He explained that the film collected around 10 Academy Award nominations and did well overseas, but missed the box-office level it needed in the United States.

By December 2025 he had gone further. In an interview with ABC, Crowe said, "We should have made half a dozen Master and Commanders." That is a long way from one sequel, and it suggests he still sees the role as unfinished business more than two decades on.

## The film and its story

Master and Commander follows Aubrey as he commands HMS Surprise during the Napoleonic Wars. After the ship is ambushed by the larger French vessel Acheron, he chases it across the South Atlantic. His determination keeps pulling him into conflict with his closest friend, ship's surgeon Dr Stephen Maturin, played by [Paul Bettany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bettany).

ScreenRant singles out the first clash with the Acheron, with its cannon fire, splintering timber and hand-to-hand fighting, and praises the cramped, strictly ordered life below deck. The friendship between Aubrey and Maturin, it says, gives the film its emotional centre.

## Why the books made sequels look easy

The film drew on Patrick O'Brian's Aubrey-Maturin series, which has 20 completed novels and an unfinished 21st. ScreenRant says the later books follow Aubrey's rising career and trickier naval assignments, along with Maturin's intelligence work and personal life. That, it argues, could have supported at least five more films.

Few franchises start with that much written down. Most have to invent their next chapter. This one only had to pick a book.

## Why it never happened

The numbers explain it. ScreenRant reports a budget of about $150 million for a historical adventure with no established franchise behind it. Worldwide takings reached about $212 million. That is a healthy return on paper, but studios usually want more than a film merely covering its costs before they commit to a series, and Crowe's own comment about the US result points the same way.

Critical praise was not enough to change that. Ten Oscar nominations did not turn into a second voyage.
