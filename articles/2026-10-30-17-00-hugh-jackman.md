**Quick summary**

- [Liev Schreiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liev_Schreiber) says [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) is the reason he started strength training in his early 40s.
- The two worked together on the 2009 film X-Men Origins: Wolverine, where Schreiber played Sabretooth.
- Schreiber, now 59, trains on a four-day split with celebrity trainer Don Saladino, according to Men's Health UK.
- He also boxes once or twice a week and plays hockey to protect his mobility.

Liev Schreiber has credited [Hugh Jackman](https://moduscarry.com/tag/hugh-jackman/) with changing the way he trains. Speaking on the Stronger Podcast, as reported by [Men's Health UK](https://www.menshealth.com/uk/building-muscle/a74203319/liev-schreiber-muscle-routine/), the 59-year-old said Jackman got him into the gym while the pair prepared for 2009's X-Men Origins: Wolverine.

## "This isn't a suit, mate"

Schreiber had boxed and played football, but he told the podcast he never lifted for his body. He said he had long held the idea that working out was for actors who could not act, and he now calls that view wrong.

The turning point came a few weeks before filming. Schreiber says he asked Jackman when he would be handed the suit that gave the character his muscles. Jackman's reply, as Schreiber tells it, was that it was not a suit and he would have to work out.

Schreiber describes that preparation as the hardest physical training of his career. It also changed how he approached his roles. Men's Health notes that building his body helped him understand how a character moves and feels, not only how he looks.

## A trainer introduced by Jackman

Jackman did more than shame a sceptical co-star into the weights room. According to the magazine, he introduced Schreiber to celebrity trainer Don Saladino, and the two have worked together ever since.

The results show in Schreiber's later work. Men's Health says he has trained consistently for about 15 years, for the fixer in [Ray Donovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Donovan) and most recently for the former soldier Jonah Lynn in the Apple TV show Nocturne.

## The four-day plan he follows now

The goal has shifted since the Sabretooth days. Back then it was to add muscle as fast as possible. Now, Men's Health reports, Schreiber uses a four-day split to stay lean, athletic and strong:

- Day one: chest and biceps
- Day two: legs
- Day three: shoulders and triceps
- Day four: back

Saladino adds core work a couple of times a week, according to the report.

## Boxing for mobility, not for fights

Schreiber also plays hockey and boxes once or twice a week. He told the podcast he does not box to fight. His aim is to keep his mobility, proprioception and fast-twitch reactions as he ages, with the focus on movement and footwork.
