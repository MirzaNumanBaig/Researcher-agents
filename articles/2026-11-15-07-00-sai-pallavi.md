**Quick summary**

- A scene in Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana: Part 1 shows Sai Pallavi's Sita addressing Rama, played by Ranbir Kapoor, simply as "Ram".
- Eastern Eye reports that some viewers feel the line lacks the reverence traditionally tied to the couple.
- Others point to passages in Valmiki's Ramayana, including the Ayodhya Kanda, to argue the usage is not inaccurate.
- Eastern Eye says the two-part film is reportedly budgeted at around ₹4,000 crore and is slated for Diwali 2026.

A single line of dialogue has become the latest talking point around [Sai Pallavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Pallavi)'s Sita. In a scene from Ramayana: Part 1, her character calls Rama "Ram" to his face, and according to [Eastern Eye](https://www.easterneye.biz/sai-pallavi-sita-ram-calling-ram-debate/), audiences are divided over whether that choice suits the epic.

## What the Sai Pallavi Sita dialogue debate is about

The scene pairs Sai Pallavi with [Ranbir Kapoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranbir_Kapoor), who plays Rama in the adaptation directed by [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari). Eastern Eye reports that some viewers do not think the dialogue carries the reverence they associate with Sita's bond with her husband.

The report says that expectation comes from devotional traditions and popular retellings, where the relationship is usually shown through respect, affection and devotion. Audiences raised on those versions, it suggests, have a fixed idea of how the two should speak on screen.

For some, then, the objection is less about the name itself and more about the cultural and emotional weight of how she says it.

## The case made for the line

Supporters of the film see it differently. Eastern Eye says they have pointed to Valmiki's Ramayana, including passages in the Ayodhya Kanda, to argue that Sita addressing Rama by name does not contradict the original text.

Their argument is that the Sanskrit epic should be read in its own textual context, not judged only against the conventions of later devotional versions. Eastern Eye notes that the gap between Valmiki's narrative and the many regional and devotional Ramayanas matters when assessing this kind of criticism.

The outlet does not say who is right, and it does not report a response from the filmmakers or the cast.

## Other questions around the adaptation

The discussion has widened beyond Sita's dialogue. According to the report, viewers are also asking how the film will handle other figures. Yash plays Ravana, and audiences are watching how the film balances the character's power, intelligence and darker side. There are also questions about whether Shabari and Jatayu, two characters from the epic, will appear at all or have been cut.

Eastern Eye frames this as the usual problem of adapting a sprawling story: filmmakers have to decide which episodes stay, how much detail to keep and how to present well-loved characters without losing the larger arc.

## The scale of the project

The two-part adaptation is based on Valmiki's Sanskrit Ramayana and stars Kapoor as Rama, Sai Pallavi as Sita and Yash as Ravana. Eastern Eye says it is reportedly being made on a combined budget of around ₹4,000 crore, which would place it among the most ambitious Indian productions, and that it has international ambitions alongside its Indian release.

The same report places the release of Ramayana: Part 1 in Indian theatres during Diwali 2026. Note that Eastern Eye describes the debate as arising "even before" that theatrical release.
