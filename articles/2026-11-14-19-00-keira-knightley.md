**Quick summary**

- [Keira Knightley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keira_Knightley), 41, says she enjoys the "morally ambiguous" people she plays in the spy series Black Doves.
- She plays Helen Webb opposite Ben Whishaw and Sarah Lancashire, according to Female First.
- Her comments to People were published alongside older remarks to The Independent and The Guardian.
- Those earlier interviews cover her 25-year career and the anger she sees among actors.

[Keira Knightley](https://moduscarry.com/tag/keira-knightley/) says the appeal of her latest role is that nobody in it is simple. The 41-year-old told People that the people in [Black Doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Doves) are "morally ambiguous" and "incredibly complicated creatures" in strange situations, according to [Female First](https://www.femalefirst.co.uk/celebrity/keira-knightley-loves-playing-complicated-characters-1453685.html).

## Why Keira Knightley enjoys the Black Doves role

In the spy action thriller, Knightley plays Helen Webb. She stars with [Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw), who plays Sam Young, and [Sarah Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Lancashire).

Her description of the job is modest. For her, she told People, the interest lies in watching these characters and seeing what happens to them. She did not claim to defend them.

Whishaw was just as frank. The 46-year-old said Sam and the people around him are "really bad", yet he finds that quality "strangely sympathetic". Both actors, in other words, are drawn to characters who would struggle to pass a moral test. ## A career that could have gone another way

Female First also pulled in older comments. Knightley's breakout came with Bend It Like Beckham in 2002, but she has said her life could easily have taken a different path.

Speaking to The Independent, she said the adult version of her knows people can go through very hard periods and not come out with a successful career or a healthy bank balance. She called herself "incredibly fortunate".

She also drew a line between how the public saw her as a teenager and how it sees her now. At 18, she said, you have little work and are "all image". After a career of about 25 years, people can look at a body of work, where some projects worked and some did not, and call it a career. Being a woman of 40, she added, simply changes how people respond to you.

## The anger behind performances

The most pointed remark came from an earlier chat with The Guardian. Knightley said she sees "an inherent rage" in actors that is "masked brilliantly but easy to access". She was clear that she did not mean people behave badly, because generally they do not.

Her explanation was the industry itself. Acting is subjective, and when things go wrong it happens in public. It is also a search for a truth that cannot be found, she said, because the work is fiction. That tension, she suggested, may be the "coiled spring" that some performances grow from. It is a neat fit for a series about people living double lives.
