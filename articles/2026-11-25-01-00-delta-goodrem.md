**Quick summary**

- [Delta Goodrem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Goodrem) said on Good Morning Britain on Tuesday 24 November that her cancer and loss of speech inspired this week's Couple's Choice routine.
- She says the dance is about empowerment and resilience, with "a lot of drama" in it, according to The Mirror.
- Her partner [Kai Widdrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Widdrington) teased a "show first" but did not say what it is.
- Goodrem landed in her first dance-off last Sunday and was saved by all four judges, sending Jaime Winstone home.
- Strictly returns on Saturday at 6:30pm on BBC One.

The Australian singer used a breakfast television appearance to explain why this weekend's dance matters so much to her. Goodrem, 42, appeared with Widdrington on [Good Morning Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Morning_Britain), where she spoke about the illness she faced as a teenager and how it will shape their [Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing) Couple's Choice.

## Why the Couple's Choice is personal

According to [The Mirror](https://www.mirror.co.uk/tv/tv-news/strictly-delta-goodrem-cancer-paralysis-37909130), Goodrem was diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma in 2003 after noticing a lump in her neck, and went into remission in 2004. She was 18 and had just seen her debut album, Innocent Eyes, break records in Australia.

She told the programme her plans and dreams had been coming true when everything stopped, and that her "heart broke so much" while she went through chemotherapy.

She also described a nerve paralysis in her tongue that left her unable to speak. The Mirror reports that months of daily speech therapy followed before she could talk again.

"It's about empowerment," she said of the routine. She added that her music is meant to lift listeners up, and that the dance will carry plenty of drama while being about "being warrior being resilient".

## A rough few weeks for the pair

The couple have lost time this series. The Mirror says Goodrem has been dealing with a broken rib for five weeks and called it a "shock to the system", adding that she wakes each day checking whether it is improving.

Widdrington was taken to hospital about two weeks ago. He told The Mirror he had a "really bad bug" and became depleted, and that doctors told him to rest and take on fluids. On Good Morning Britain he said he was "fully recovered", according to the [Manchester Evening News](https://www.manchestereveningnews.co.uk/news/tv/strictly-come-dancings-delta-goodrem-34957108). Goodrem recalled him insisting from the ambulance that he would be back the next day.

## The Blackpool week result

Last Sunday, 22 November, the pair were in the bottom two for the first time. The [Daily Star](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/tv/bbc-strictly-pro-calls-rule-37909262) reports they scored 39 in Blackpool week and sat second on the leaderboard behind three couples who tied for first. The judges then unanimously saved Goodrem, and [Jaime Winstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Winstone) left as the eighth celebrity to go.

Former professional [James Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Jordan_(actor)) told the Daily Star he was not surprised. He blamed the number of tied scores, which he argued make it easy for the order to flip, and suggested the head judge should rank tied couples. Those are his views, not a rule the BBC has announced.

Another former professional, Vincent Simone, told Express.co.uk, in comments carried by the [Daily Star](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/showbiz/delta-goodrem-faces-fresh-blow-37908617), that strong dancers rarely get public backing because viewers assume they are safe. He tipped John Nellis and Lacey Turner as the likelier winners, which is opinion rather than fact.

After the result Goodrem thanked supporters on Instagram. She said they were relieved to stay and sad to see Winstone and [Carlos Gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gu) leave, and that the pair would work even harder this week.

## The Blackpool noise incident

Goodrem was also asked about the unusual noises heard during live feedback from judge [Motsi Mabuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motsi_Mabuse) after the opening dance on Saturday. Host [Emma Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Willis) later apologised, and the BBC said it was investigating.

The Manchester Evening News quotes a Lancashire Police spokesperson saying a formal complaint had been made and a mobile phone seized. Asked by [Ed Balls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Balls) and [Susanna Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susanna_Reid) whether she heard it, Goodrem said yes, but that she did not know what was being played and found the judges professional.
