**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) received the Praemium Imperiale in Tokyo, according to Town & Country.
- Princess Hanako presented the award at Meiji Kinenkan, with a medal and a 15 million yen honorarium.
- On 30 October, Blanchett introduced five shorts funded by her Displacement Film Fund at Tokyo International Film Festival, Screen Daily reports.
- The festival's 39th edition runs from 26 October to 4 November.

Cate Blanchett has had a busy stretch in Japan. The Australian actor accepted the Praemium Imperiale, a lifetime achievement honour, and then took the stage at Tokyo International Film Festival on 30 October to talk about the filmmakers her Displacement Film Fund supports.

## A lifetime honour from the Japan Art Association

[Town & Country](https://www.townandcountrymag.com/leisure/arts-and-culture/a74295665/princess-hanako-cate-blanchett-praemium-imperiale/) reports that Blanchett received the Praemium Imperiale this week. The prize was established in 1988 and is presented by the Japan Art Association. It covers theatre and film, music, painting, sculpture and architecture.

Princess Hanako, also known as Hanako, Princess Hitachi, and the aunt of Emperor Naruhito, handed over the award at Meiji Kinenkan in Tokyo. Her husband, Prince Hitachi, 90, is the association's honorary patron. Blanchett also received a commemorative medal and an honorarium of 15 million yen, which the magazine put at about US$96,000.

She was in distinguished company. The magazine lists artist Jenny Holzer, sculptor Andy Goldsworthy, architect Daniel Libeskind and conductor Herbert Blomstedt among this year's honorees. Earlier theatre and film recipients include Martin Scorsese, Ang Lee, Judi Dench and Catherine Deneuve.

## Why Blanchett says doubt matters

At a press conference tied to the award, Blanchett described acting as a profession that can sink into "a quagmire of literalness" at its worst, but at its best can leave people speechless. The quotes come from Town & Country, which cites the Hollywood Reporter.

She went on to say that the doubt she carries about ever fully communicating is her artistic starting point. As a woman, she said, that doubt is often read as a lack of confidence, and she rejected that reading. In "an uncertain world riddled with unthinking certainty", she called doubt vital to the creative process because it opens up questioning, conversation and change.

## Five shorts from the Displacement Film Fund

The festival appearance was a different kind of event. According to [Screen Daily](https://www.screendaily.com/news/cate-blanchett-talks-dispelling-the-toxic-narrative-around-displaced-people-through-film/5221943.article), the Displacement Film Fund (DFF) screened five shorts by acclaimed directors in Tokyo on 30 October. Blanchett, a co-founder of the fund, appeared alongside exiled Iranian director [Mohammad Rasoulof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Rasoulof) and Afghan filmmaker [Shahrbanoo Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrbanoo_Sadat).

The fund was set up last year to back displaced filmmakers, or those with a proven record of authentic storytelling about displacement. Screen Daily says it is supported by the Hubert Bals Fund at International Film Festival Rotterdam. Blanchett said the aim was to let these voices be seen "as a cohort, as a collective" rather than only as individuals.

She also said the filmmakers were given "absolute freedom". Their films did not have to address displacement directly, though she wanted each director to have lived experience of it or real sympathy for it. Cinema, she said, can dispel "the toxic narrative" around displaced people, because being displaced is not the defining characteristic of a person.

## The films and the directors

Rasoulof presented *Sense Of Water*, a 40-minute film and his first shoot outside Iran. It follows an Iranian writer in Germany who begins an affair while struggling with exile, and Screen Daily describes it as partly autobiographical. Rasoulof spoke about the fear of working in German as well as Farsi. He fled to Germany in May 2024 after Iranian authorities sentenced him to jail and flogging over *The Seed Of The Sacred Fig*. The film won the Cannes special jury prize that month. He will receive the festival's honorary Kurosawa Akira Award on 2 November.

The other DFF shorts are *Allies In Exile* by Syria's Hasan Kattan, *Rotation* by Ukraine's Maryna Er Gorbach, and *Whispers Of A Burning Scent* by Somalia's Mo Harawe. Sadat's *Super Afghan Gym* completes the set. Her feature [*No Good Men*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Good_Men) opened this year's Berlinale. She said displacement "is a universal thing", whether it is felt in a new country or in one's own body.
