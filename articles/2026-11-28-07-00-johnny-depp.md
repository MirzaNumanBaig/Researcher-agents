**Quick summary**

- Ebenezer, a new take on Charles Dickens' Christmas story starring Johnny Depp as Scrooge, opened on 12 November 2026.
- It has earned about US$79.6 million worldwide against a US$38 million budget, roughly 2.1 times its cost, according to Wikipedia's film entry.
- Reviews are mixed: Rotten Tomatoes lists 55%, and Metacritic gives 46 out of 100 from 38 critics.
- Ti West, known for horror films X and Pearl, directed the film.

[Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) has put his Scrooge on the big screen, and the numbers say the film has at least paid its way. Ebenezer, which opened on 12 November 2026, has taken about US$79.6 million at the box office on a reported budget of US$38 million, according to figures collected on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer_(2026_film)). Critics are far less settled on whether it counts as the comeback many hoped for.

## What Johnny Depp's Ebenezer is

The film is a fairly faithful adaptation of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' 1843 novella A Christmas Carol, according to a review by [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/culture/article/h1kycc11yxx). Depp plays the miserly Ebenezer Scrooge, who despises Christmas until a night of ghostly visitors changes him.

[Ti West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti_West) directed. He is best known for horror, and the cast around Depp includes Andrea Riseborough, Tramell Tillman, Ian McKellen and Rupert Grint, per the film's Wikipedia entry. Ynetnews describes the setting as gothic, with swirling fog and croaking ravens, and sees it as a return to the style Depp built his name on with [Tim Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Burton).

## How the box office looks

Against its US$38 million budget, the US$79.6 million gross puts Ebenezer at about 2.09 times its cost. That places it in the mid-budget range. It is early to call a final verdict, because a film's earnings and the costs of marketing and distribution are not the same thing, and the run is still going. Still, a double-the-budget gross is not what a flop looks like.

## What critics make of his Scrooge

The reception is lukewarm rather than hostile. Rotten Tomatoes sits at 55%, and Metacritic at 46 out of 100. The Rotten Tomatoes consensus, as quoted on Wikipedia, says the film has evocative production design but plays things too safe to stand out among the many Dickens remakes.

Individual reviews vary. Peter Bradshaw of The Guardian gave it three stars out of five, calling it a serviceable, faithful adaptation in which Depp is "pretty good", according to Wikipedia's summary. Brandon Wu of The New York Times was harsher, calling the film bland and saying that at his worst Depp looks like a man in a Scrooge costume in a Christmas advertising campaign.

The Ynetnews reviewer lands somewhere in between. The review calls the film a step in the right direction for Depp, but not nearly enough to count as a full comeback. It also notes that Depp, at 63, is finally playing a character who looks his age, though the reviewer felt his makeup and walk recalled Danny DeVito's Penguin in [Batman Returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Returns). The same review points out how much the story's shape has influenced later Christmas films, from [Home Alone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Alone) to [Die Hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Hard).

## What happens next

Several outlets are already asking what the run means for Depp's career, and whether the film will reach streaming before Christmas. Those reports could not be read in full for this article, so we are not repeating their claims. Nothing in the sources we could read confirms a streaming date.

What the figures do show is a film that has recouped its budget on the big screen while dividing critics. Whether that is enough to change how Hollywood sees Depp is a question the next few weeks of box office, and any further announcements, will help answer.
