**Quick summary**

- [Kristofer Hivju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristofer_Hivju) joins season two of the Swedish dramedy *Där solen alltid skiner* (*Where the Sun Always Shines*) on SkyShowtime.
- He plays Anker, an eccentric Norwegian intellectual and artist living outside Palma, according to SkyShowtime.
- The show was created and directed by [Felix Herngren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Herngren), and season two has six episodes.
- MovieZine's early write-up on 17 November called the new season better than the first and singled out Hivju.

The *Game of Thrones* actor Kristofer Hivju is the main new addition to season two of *Where the Sun Always Shines*, and his role as Anker is already drawing attention. The Swedish series, a SkyShowtime Original, returned in November with its first episodes streaming, according to [MovieZine](https://www.moviezine.se/nyheter/dar-solen-alltid-skiner-ar-tillbaka-och-annu-battre-an-sist). The Kristofer Hivju Anker casting was announced in May, when filming began in Mallorca.

For an actor best known to Australian viewers as a bearded giant of Westeros, it is a comic turn in a very different setting: a sun-drenched island and a family that cannot quite settle.

## Who is Anker?

SkyShowtime's [casting announcement](https://corporate.skyshowtime.com/265475-kristofer-hivju-johan-glans-and-celie-sparre-join-the-cast-of-skyshowtime-original-where-the-sun-always-shines-dar-solen-alltid-skiner/) describes Anker as an eccentric Norwegian intellectual and artist. He lives in an architect-designed home outside Palma, rejects modernity and is chasing an obsession with Roman ideals and a search for his "true" humanity.

Hivju said in the announcement that he loved season one and that joining the cast as Anker was "a real joy". The Spanish site Foro3D describes the character as an art dealer who restores the lead's drive. That label does not match SkyShowtime's own description, so this article uses the studio's version.

MovieZine says Anker is a Roman-era romantic with firm ideas about manhood, who sets out to help the character Tom recover what he has lost. According to the review, the plan does not go as intended.

## What changes in season two

Foro3D reports that the new run opens after a one-year time jump, with the Kraft family still in Mallorca. The site says the move lets the writers rearrange family tensions without repeating the first season's friction.

The announcement says the series follows two brothers and their families as they try to build new lives in Spain, with unresolved conflicts still in play. MovieZine adds that the adults face marriage troubles and self-image problems, while the younger characters rebel and question their parents. One thread has Linn, the daughter of Maja and Tim, pulling away from her mother.

Erik Ahrnbom is head scriptwriter this season, with Maria Nygren and Martin Larsson also writing, according to SkyShowtime. Foro3D credits his scripts with giving characters more to say beyond looking at the sea.

## Who is behind the series

Herngren created and directed the show, which is made by the Scandinavian production company FLX and co-produced by SkyShowtime and Sweden's public broadcaster SVT. Sussan Treschow is the producer. Returning cast members include Per Lasson, Lisa Linnertorp, Erik "Jerka" Johansson and Rakel Wärmländer, while Johan Glans and Celie Sparre joined Hivju as new faces.

## How it has been received

MovieZine's Jonna Vanhatalo wrote on 17 November that season two is better than the first. The reviewer called it darker, with more sorrow and strained relationships, yet funnier. Iris Herngren was praised as Linn, and Hivju was named the standout. The outlet noted that its piece was not a full review.

Foro3D reached a similar view on the writing, saying the structure is more precise than in the first run. These are two outlets, so they are early reactions rather than a settled verdict.

## What happens next

MovieZine reports that the first two episodes are streaming on SkyShowtime and that the sixth episode is the finale. Neither SkyShowtime's announcement nor the sources reviewed give an Australian release date, so viewers here should check local availability. Hivju has not announced anything further about the role.
