**Quick summary**

- HuffPost UK has listed 11 early Ben Whishaw screen roles that audiences may have forgotten.
- The list is pegged to his Netflix spy thriller Black Doves.
- It includes his first film, The Trench, shot when he was 16, and a Layer Cake part that was rewritten around him.
- He has worked with Daniel Craig six times, according to the piece.

[Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw) is best known now as the voice of Paddington, Q in the Bond films and the star of [Black Doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Doves). A new retrospective from HuffPost UK digs further back, and the early credits show an actor who was getting noticed long before the big franchises.

## A first shoot that drew blood

According to HuffPost UK, Whishaw's first film was the war drama The Trench, which followed soldiers in the days before the Battle of the Somme. The cast included a pre-fame [Cillian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_Murphy), [Danny Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Dyer) and [Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy), alongside [Daniel Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Craig).

Whishaw told Empire earlier this year that the shoot was overwhelming. He was 16 and turned 17 during filming. In one scene he had to sprint down the trench and round a corner. He lost his footing and crashed into Craig, and his sharp helmet cut the older actor's nose open.

## Small parts, familiar faces

In 2003 he appeared as a production assistant in Ready When You Are, Mr. McGill, a remake of an ITV anthology episode. The piece says he can be spotted in the background of many shots holding a walkie-talkie and looking harried, with [Tom Courtenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Courtenay) leading a cast that included [Bill Nighy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Nighy), [Tamsin Greig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamsin_Greig) and [Sally Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Phillips).

The same year he starred in the TV film The Booze Cruise as Daniel, one of five men on a trip to France for cheap alcohol. His co-stars were [Martin Clunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Clunes), [Neil Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Pearson), [Mark Benton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Benton) and [Brian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Murphy_(actor)). The character returned in two sequels, but [Tom Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Bennett_(actor)) took over the part.

## The Layer Cake rewrite

Whishaw's second collaboration with Craig was Layer Cake, directed by [Matthew Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Vaughn). He played Sidney, the spoiled young nephew of a low-level drug dealer. Though a minor character, his actions drive the film's violent and ambiguous ending, HuffPost UK notes.

Citing a GQ profile marking the film's 20th anniversary, the article says Sidney was originally written as a large man in his 40s. Vaughn recalled that Whishaw walked in as a skinny kid and he doubted the casting, until the actor read the lines. Vaughn said he then rewrote the role because he could see the actor was magical.

## From Stoned to The Zero Theorem

Whishaw played Rolling Stones guitarist Keith Richards in Stoned, a fictionalised account of Brian Jones's death. HuffPost UK says the film was poorly received, though the following year he starred in Perfume, which it calls his major film breakout.

A year after debuting as Q in Skyfall, he appeared in Terry Gilliam's The Zero Theorem as one of three unnamed doctors, opposite Christoph Waltz, Matt Damon and Tilda Swinton. Around then he also featured in Days and Nights, a modern retelling of Chekhov's The Seagull.
