**Quick summary**

- Explicit noises played over the judges' feedback to [Delta Goodrem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Goodrem) during the live Blackpool episode of Strictly Come Dancing.
- Lancashire Police said a formal complaint was made, a mobile phone was seized and officers are checking whether any offences were committed.
- The BBC apologised, said it is investigating how it happened and will edit the episode on iPlayer.
- Goodrem and partner Kai Widdrington scored 36 out of 40 for their quickstep, then landed in the dance-off.

Police have been called in after a live broadcast of [Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing) from Blackpool was interrupted by what sounded like a recording of sexual noises, just as the judges were critiquing Australian singer Delta Goodrem and her partner [Kai Widdrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Widdrington). The BBC One show carried on, but the incident has become a story in its own right.

## What happened during the live show

According to [Nine.com.au](https://www.nine.com.au/entertainment/celebrity/police-called-after-explicit-audio-interrupts-delta-goodrem-on-strictly-come-dancing-20261123-p61gin.html), judge Motsi Mabuse was mid-critique when the sounds began, apparently playing from a mobile phone. Mabuse turned to look for the source and said: "It's not me!"

Her fellow judges, [Craig Revel Horwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Revel_Horwood), [Shirley Ballas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirley_Ballas) and Naton Du Beke, kept going, as did Goodrem and Widdrington. The show was live, so there was no chance to stop and reset.

At the end of the episode host [Emma Willis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Willis) apologised on air for any audio disruption and for offence caused, before the judges' leaderboard was shown.

## The police and BBC response

A BBC spokesperson said the broadcaster apologised to viewers who were offended and is "investigating how this happened". Nine.com.au reports that the episode will be edited before it appears on BBC iPlayer.

Lancashire Police confirmed it was aware of an incident during filming in Blackpool. A spokesperson said officers had spoken to the event organisers and received a formal complaint, and that a mobile phone had been seized. Police are now looking into whether any criminal offences took place. No charges have been reported.

Nine.com.au also reports that a content creator, Daniel Jarvis, who has previously claimed responsibility for a similar audio interruption on the BBC's Match of the Day, streamed the Strictly disruption on social media. That is the outlet's account of his past claim, and police have not named anyone in connection with the phone they seized.

## Goodrem's night on the dance floor

The interruption landed on an otherwise strong return. Goodrem and Widdrington had missed the previous week's live show because of illness, according to Nine.com.au. Back in Blackpool, their quickstep earned 36 out of 40, the second-highest score of the night.

Public votes were less kind. [Who](https://www.who.com.au/entertainment/tv/delta-goodrem-strictly-come-dancing-fan-backlash/) reports the pair still ended up in the dance-off against [Jaime Winstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Winstone) and [Carlos Gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gu). All four judges chose to save Goodrem, and Winstone went home.

Some viewers argued online that Goodrem should never have been in the bottom two, and a few called for the public vote to be dropped.
