**Quick summary**

- [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) auditioned for the lead in the Swedish epic Arn: The Knight Templar but was not cast.
- His father and two of his brothers all landed roles in the 2007 film.
- He told Esquire in 2025 that the experience "stung a little bit", according to Far Out Magazine.
- By his own account, he found little other work for five years after Zoolander in 2000.

Alexander Skarsgård has spoken candidly about the 2007 Swedish film that his whole family seemed to join without him, Arn Knight Templar. According to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/2007-alexander-skarsgard-not-cast-in-movie-father-two-brothers-were/), the actor auditioned for the title role in Arn: The Knight Templar, missed out, and then watched his father and two brothers take parts in what was the most expensive feature film in Swedish cinema history.

## A family business in Stockholm

Skarsgård grew up in Stockholm, where his father, Stellan, worked mainly as a theatre actor. Far Out reports that seven of Stellan's eight children eventually worked in film in some form. Fame arrived for the family in 1989, when Stellan was cast as Carl Hamilton in Täcknamn Coq Rouge, a role the outlet likens to the Swedish [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond). The character comes from the [Coq Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Hamilton_novels) novels.

Skarsgård told Esquire in 2025 that this was the first time his friends had seen his father's work. He described a dad who went from a cerebral artist to someone in tight jeans and cowboy boots, driving a Corvette.

His younger brother Gustaf, later Floki in the History Channel series Vikings, played the spy's son in that first film. It did not bother Skarsgård then. Per Far Out, he had made his own screen debut at seven in Åke and His World, and landed a breakthrough lead in the TV film The Dog That Smiled the same year.

## The Arn Knight Templar role that got away

Nearly two decades on, the situation was different. Arn: The Knight Templar was a historical epic about a fictional Swedish knight, based on a novel trilogy by the same author as the Hamilton books. Skarsgård went for the lead and did not get it.

Then the family joined the production. He recalled that his father played one of the chieftains, Gustaf played a friend of the lead, and his much younger brother Bill, then a teenager, played a young prince. Valter, another younger brother, turned up in the sequel, Arn: The Kingdom at Road's End, according to the report.

"It was a year of sitting around the dinner table hearing stories from the set," he said, as quoted by Far Out. He added that when you come close to a job and lose it, you never want to hear about the project again. He also admitted he quietly hoped the film would flop.

## A slow stretch before the breakthrough

The disappointment landed during a lean period. Skarsgård had appeared as one of the gasoline fight models in Ben Stiller's Zoolander in 2000 and assumed steady work would follow. It did not. "Except for small supporting roles in Sweden, I didn't get another job for five years after that," he said, per the same report.

The film's cast also featured Michael Nyqvist and [Joel Kinnaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Kinnaman). Skarsgård's verdict on the two Arn films, according to Far Out, is that they did not turn out that well.
