**Quick summary**

- *You Can See Everything*, the documentary by comedian and film-maker [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder), opens in the UK on 20 November.
- The Observer reports it was made for $7 million and has taken more than $81 million at the US box office.
- It premiered at the Telluride Film Festival in September and runs 174 minutes.
- The Observer says it may be hard to categorise at the Oscars, but a best picture nomination is possible.

The film that turned a cult comedian into a mainstream name is about to reach British cinemas. According to [The Observer](https://observer.co.uk/culture/film/article/nathan-fielder-is-telling-it-like-it-is-or-isnt-or-might-be), *You Can See Everything* opens in the UK on 20 November, two months after its premiere at the Telluride Film Festival, and the paper describes it as a global pop culture phenomenon.

## What the film is

Fielder, 43 and Canadian, moved into the home of Elizabeth Holmes, the disgraced founder of the blood-testing company Theranos, in 2023. He stayed with her, her partner Billy Evans and their children for the final 34 days before she began an 11-year prison sentence in Bryan, Texas, The Observer reports.

The 174-minute result was co-directed and produced with [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim). The Observer says it plays like a thriller and builds Hitchcock-style suspense, with Fielder lingering by doors and watching from the shadows. It also uses re-enactments, including a part for [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried), who played Holmes in the biopic *The Dropout*.

## The numbers behind the buzz

The Observer puts the budget at $7 million (about £5.3 million) and the US takings above $81 million. Wikipedia's entry for the film lists a higher total of roughly $86.5 million, which is about twelve times the budget. Figures are still moving, so treat both as snapshots.

Critics have been kind. Wikipedia's reception section records a 96% score from 226 reviews on Rotten Tomatoes and 97 out of 100 from 42 critics on Metacritic. It also notes that audience-polling firm PostTrak found a 98% positive response.

## A London welcome

The Observer's writer attended a gala screening in London where Fielder and Oppenheim took part in a Q&A moderated by [Jesse Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Armstrong), creator of *Succession*. [James Norton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Norton_(actor)), [Richard Gadd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gadd) and [Richard Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Curtis) were in the audience.

A second Q&A, led by [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde), drew a crowd that included [Kate Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Moss), [Margaret Qualley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Qualley) and [Armando Iannucci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Iannucci). The paper adds that a photographed coffee with [Louis Theroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Theroux), a longtime admirer, felt like a passing of the baton in documentary.

## From Nathan For You to the cinema

The Observer traces a clear line through his career. *Nathan For You* ran for four series from 2013 to 2017 and had him giving absurd business advice to small companies. He later co-starred with [Emma Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Stone) in the 2023 comedy *The Curse* and has worked with [Sacha Baron Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacha_Baron_Cohen).

*The Rehearsal* took the method further. Its second season, on aviation safety, saw him build an airport terminal and train until he could fly 150 passengers in a Boeing 737 between California and Las Vegas. He also founded [Summit Ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_Ice_Apparel), an outdoor clothing line whose proceeds go to Holocaust awareness, the paper notes.

## What happens next

The UK release on 20 November is the immediate test. After that, awards season. The Observer says the film may prove difficult to categorise for the Academy Awards, partly because of the re-enactments, yet it could still receive a best picture nomination. No documentary has ever won that award.

Readers in Australia should check local cinema listings and streaming announcements. The sources reviewed here do not give an Australian date.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does You Can See Everything open in the UK?

It opens on 20 November, according to The Observer. It premiered at the Telluride Film Festival in September.

### How much did the film cost and earn?

The Observer says it cost $7 million and has taken more than $81 million at the US box office. Wikipedia lists about $86.5 million.

### Who directed it?

Nathan Fielder co-directed it with Lance Oppenheim, and the pair also produced it.

### Could it be nominated for an Oscar?

The Observer says it may be hard to categorise but could earn a best picture nomination. No documentary has won that category.
