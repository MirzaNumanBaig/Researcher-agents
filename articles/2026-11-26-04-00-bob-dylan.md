**Quick summary**

- The Band played its farewell concert at the Winterland Ballroom in San Francisco on Thanksgiving Day, 25 November 1976, 50 years ago this week.
- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) was among more than a dozen guests, but he decided mid-show he did not want his set filmed.
- Only two of his songs, "Forever Young" and "Baby, Let Me Follow You Down", made it into the finished film.
- "The Last Waltz" opened on 26 April 1978 and was added to the US National Film Registry in 2019.

[Bob Dylan](https://moduscarry.com/tag/bob-dylan/) almost kept himself out of one of the most celebrated concert films ever made. According to a Men's Journal story republished by [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/festivals/articles/bob-dylan-almost-pulled-scorsese-130000386.html), Dylan decided during the intermission of The Band's farewell show, 50 years ago this week, that cameras should not film his performance. The result was a scramble that shaped what audiences still see in Bob Dylan's The Last Waltz appearance.

## A farewell night in San Francisco

The Band played its final concert as its original lineup at the Winterland Ballroom on Thanksgiving Day 1976. The lineup was Robbie Robertson, Levon Helm, Rick Danko, Richard Manuel and Garth Hudson. [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) filmed it.

Promoter Bill Graham produced the evening. Doors opened to an audience of 5,000, who were served turkey dinners from 5 p.m. The night also included ballroom dancing to an orchestra and readings by poets, among them Lawrence Ferlinghetti. According to Rolling Stone, as cited in the report, Robertson pitched the idea of one last show to Graham.

The guest list was remarkable. Dylan, Neil Young, Joni Mitchell, Eric Clapton, Van Morrison, Muddy Waters, Ringo Starr, Ronnie Wood, Neil Diamond, Dr. John, Paul Butterfield and Ronnie Hawkins all joined The Band on stage.

## Why Dylan wanted the cameras off

Dylan had agreed to perform. During the intermission, though, he changed his mind about being filmed. The report says he worried a concert film would draw attention away from his own movie, "Renaldo and Clara".

That created a real problem. Warner Bros. had agreed to finance the project only if Dylan appeared in it. Helm later recalled that Scorsese "went nuts" and that Robertson "became totally pale." Robertson, producer Jonathan Taplin and Scorsese asked Graham to speak to Dylan.

## The compromise

In his memoir, "This Wheel's on Fire", Helm wrote that word eventually came back that "the last two songs in Bob's part of the show could be filmed, and only the last two." Those songs, "Forever Young" and "Baby, Let Me Follow You Down", are Dylan's performances in the finished film.

Robertson also assured Dylan that "The Last Waltz" would not be released until after "Renaldo and Clara". The film arrived in 1978, so that promise held.

Scorsese, who was finishing "New York, New York" at the time, shot the concert on 35 mm cameras and worked from a script that set out the set list and lyrics, Rolling Stone reported. Two numbers, "The Weight" with the Staple Singers and "Evangeline", were filmed afterwards on a soundstage.

## A long relationship with film

Dylan's link to Scorsese did not end there. Far Out Magazine [notes](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/10-best-bob-dylan-needle-drops-in-movies/) that Scorsese later documented Dylan's life in [No Direction Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Direction_Home) and made the mockumentary "Rolling Thunder Revue: A Bob Dylan Story by Martin Scorsese", about the [Rolling Thunder Revue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Thunder_Revue) tour. The same piece points out that Dylan won an Academy Award for Best Original Song for ["Things Have Changed"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Things_Have_Changed) from "Wonder Boys".

## What happens next

Nothing new has been announced in the sources about a Dylan screening or release tied to the anniversary. What the record shows is that "The Last Waltz" remains a fixture: Rolling Stone has called it the single greatest concert movie of all time, and the Library of Congress selected it for preservation in 2019. All five members of The Band have since died. Hudson, the last surviving member, died in January 2025 aged 87.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Bob Dylan perform in The Last Waltz?

Yes, but only briefly on screen. His two filmed songs in the finished movie are "Forever Young" and "Baby, Let Me Follow You Down".

### Why did Dylan not want his set filmed?

According to the report, he feared the concert film would take attention from his own movie, "Renaldo and Clara".

### When was The Last Waltz released?

It opened in theatres on 26 April 1978, after the concert on 25 November 1976.

### Where did the concert take place?

At the Winterland Ballroom in San Francisco, with an audience of 5,000.
