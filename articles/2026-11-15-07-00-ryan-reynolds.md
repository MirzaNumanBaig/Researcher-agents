**Quick summary**

- The English Football League has sent all 72 member clubs an official warning about social media posts that mock rivals' disciplinary cases, according to Sports Illustrated.
- The trigger was a skit by Wrexham co-owners Ryan Reynolds and Rob Mac that poked fun at Southampton's Spygate scandal.
- EFL chief executive Trevor Birch wrote that club channels risk putting short-term engagement ahead of judgment and responsibility.
- Southampton and Birmingham City have also posted digs at rivals, so the warning is aimed at the whole league.

The EFL has told every one of its clubs to rein in social media jokes about rivals' disciplinary troubles, after a video from [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney) made light of Southampton's Spygate affair. [Sports Illustrated reported](https://www.si.com/soccer/ryan-reynolds-rob-mac-official-efl-warning-wrexham-social-media-post) the letter on 14 November, saying it was first covered by the Daily Mail.

## What the skit showed

Before Wrexham played Southampton in the league this September, the two co-owners posted a short video styled as an advert for Firefox, the club's front-of-shirt sponsor. According to Sports Illustrated, Reynolds jumps out from behind a tree and says that, "for no particular reason", he wants to talk about privacy. Mac then adds that the topic is how to stop spies from seeing what you are doing.

The tree is the joke. It refers to the Spygate scandal, in which a Southampton analyst intern was accused of filming rivals' training from behind a tree. The club's own post thanked Firefox for "keeping moles out of our Racecourse and data".

Southampton manager Tonda Eckert was not amused. He told BBC Radio Solent that he had seen the video and that it changed nothing. He added that the situation was not particularly funny for him, and said that was all he had to say.

## Why Spygate still matters

Sports Illustrated sets out the background. Southampton were preparing for the first leg of their Championship playoff semifinal against Middlesbrough when the accusation emerged. The EFL charged the club but let the tie go ahead, and Southampton won 2-1 on aggregate. Four days before the playoff final against Hull City, they were thrown out and replaced by Middlesbrough. Hull won the final regardless. Southampton have since admitted to spying on Ipswich Town and Oxford United.

Wrexham were affected too. They finished seventh in the Championship last season, one place outside the playoff spots. According to Sports Illustrated, had Southampton been removed before the post-season began, Wrexham could have been entitled to a shot at promotion. Hull owner [Acun Ilicali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acun_Il%C4%B1cal%C4%B1) argued as much. That gives the Wrexham owners a stake in the story beyond a good gag, though the report does not say it shaped the video.

## What the EFL letter says

[Trevor Birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Birch) wrote to the chairs and executives of all 72 clubs. In his words, one post may be an isolated lapse, but together they risk building a culture where short-term engagement is put ahead of judgment, responsibility and long-term reputation.

Birch was clear that the league is not trying to remove humour or rivalry from club channels. The concern is content that becomes disparaging, targets an individual, or questions the integrity of a club or the competition. He also warned that repeated public exchanges can inflame relationships and weaken confidence in the fairness of the league's competitions.

Wrexham are not the only club in the frame. Sports Illustrated notes that Southampton hit back on social media after Wrexham were fined for fielding two ineligible players last month, and that Birmingham City have also aimed a dig at the Saints.

## What happens next

The letter is a warning, not a sanction. Sports Illustrated does not report any charge against Wrexham, Reynolds or Mac, and none has been announced. Whether club accounts, including the one run in the style of the [documentary series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Wrexham) that made the owners famous, change their tone is something fans will be watching for. ## Frequently asked questions

### Did the EFL punish Ryan Reynolds or Wrexham?

No punishment has been reported. The EFL sent a general warning to all 72 clubs, according to Sports Illustrated.

### What was the Wrexham skit about?

It was a mock Firefox advert in which Reynolds and Rob Mac talked about stopping spies, a reference to the Southampton Spygate scandal.

### What is Spygate?

It is the row over a Southampton analyst intern accused of recording Middlesbrough's training session before a playoff semifinal. Southampton were later removed from the playoffs and have admitted to spying on Ipswich Town and Oxford United.
