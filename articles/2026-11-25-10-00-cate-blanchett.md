**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) is promoting the five short films from the first Displacement Film Fund cohort, which she helped launch last year.
- The shorts have streamed weekly on The New Yorker's website and YouTube page since 28 October.
- The aim is to qualify for the Academy's short film shortlists, which are due on 15 December.
- Blanchett told Gold Derby that awards are not the motivation, but that a place in the conversation brings bigger audiences.

[Cate Blanchett](https://moduscarry.com/tag/cate-blanchett/) is using awards season to put a spotlight on the Displacement Film Fund, telling Gold Derby that more people see a film when it is part of the Oscar conversation. The two-time Oscar winner is backing five shorts that are now reaching American viewers through The New Yorker, with the Academy's shortlist announcement set for 15 December.

## What the Displacement Film Fund does

According to [Gold Derby](https://www.goldderby.com/film/2026/cate-blanchett-displacement-film-fund-short-films-oscars/), the fund was launched last year to give grants to filmmakers telling urgent stories about people forced from their homes and communities by political or societal causes. Blanchett and her partners picked five shorts for the first cohort, and they premiered at the International Film Festival Rotterdam in February.

Blanchett came to the cause through her work as a goodwill ambassador for the UN refugee agency, UNHCR. She told the outlet that she has heard remarkable, funny and inspiring stories that rarely reach the screen. She also pointed to the scale of the issue: one in every 70 people is displaced. The fund, she said, exists to amplify filmmakers whose voices the mainstream often overlooks.

## The five films

The first cohort covers a wide stretch of the globe and mixes narrative and documentary work, Gold Derby reports:

- "Rotation", by Maryna Er Gorbach, follows a Ukrainian woman who joins her country's war effort against Russia.
- "Sense of Water", by Mohammad Rasoulof, is a portrait of an exiled Iranian author.
- "Super Afghan Gym", by Shahrbanoo Sadat, goes inside a Kabul fitness centre during the one hour a day when women may exercise.
- "Whispers of a Burning Scent", by Mo Harawe, tracks a Somali musician balancing an upcoming gig with a court case.
- "Allies in Exile", by Hasan Kattan and Fadi Al Halabi, follows two Syrian journalists who try to keep up with news from home while starting over in the United Kingdom.

## Why the Oscar race matters

The shorts have been premiering weekly through The New Yorker's annual screening series of non-fiction shorts, with an eye on the Academy's shortlists. Blanchett said awards are not why the fund exists. "More people go and see your films if you're in that conversation," she said, drawing on her own career.

She spoke during a trip to New York, where she showed all five films to an audience at Manhattan's Film Forum. Kattan joined the press push by video because, according to Gold Derby, the U.S. Department of State had not approved a visa for him to travel in person. He said the fund offered hope, and that the support and belief mattered even more than the money.

Blanchett singled out Kattan's courage as one reason "Allies in Exile" was chosen. She said her son saw the film in New York and had to step outside afterwards to call his brothers, he was so moved.
