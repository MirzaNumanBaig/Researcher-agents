**Quick summary**

- [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) says his planned *Cleopatra* film fell apart because of the fallout from [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen)'s comedy *The Interview*.
- He made the comments in a video with his daughter Francesca for Tribeca's *Crafty* series, reported on 30 October 2026.
- Scorsese said [Angelina Jolie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelina_Jolie) was set to play Cleopatra VII and [Eric Roth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Roth) was on the script.
- Entertainment Weekly says it has asked Rogen's representatives for comment.

Martin Scorsese has jokingly pinned the death of his Cleopatra epic on Seth Rogen. In a new Tribeca video released this week, the director told his daughter that the Sony hack that followed *The Interview* "ruined everything" for a film he was ready to make with Angelina Jolie in the lead.

## What Scorsese said about Cleopatra

The exchange comes from *Crafty*, a video series on Tribeca's YouTube channel in which Francesca Scorsese talks with her father. According to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/10/martin-scorsese-seth-rogen-ruined-cleopatra-film-1237210059/), she asked him about a movie he wished he had made, and the conversation drifted to the big Roman epic she felt he had always wanted to direct.

"Remember we were gonna do Cleopatra at one point, and then they made fun of [Kim Jong Un], and he got mad and he blew up everybody at Columbia," Scorsese said, as quoted by Deadline. "Seth Rogen, right? And then he got mad, and [it] ruined Cleopatra."

He added that the project was close. "We were ready on the script," he said, and he described Jolie as Cleopatra VII with the line "It was gonna be really wild." When Francesca said she was "so mad about" it, he replied: "Well, tell Seth, because they ruined everything. Just because Kim Jong Un doesn't have a sense of humor."

[The A.V. Club](https://www.avclub.com/martin-scorsese-blames-sony-hack-seth-rogen-for-cleopatra) notes that Scorsese named the wrong North Korean leader in the clip. He said Kim Jong Il, but the film's target was [Kim Jong Un](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Jong_Un). [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/martin-scorsese-seth-rogen-ruined-cleopatra-movie-12203024) adds that Roth, who later wrote *Killers of the Flower Moon* with Scorsese, was attached to the screenplay.

## How The Interview led to the Sony hack

*The Interview*, co-written and directed by Rogen and Evan Goldberg, follows a tabloid TV host, played by James Franco, and his producer, played by Rogen. They land an interview with Kim Jong Un, played by Randall Park, and the CIA then recruits them to assassinate him.

According to Entertainment Weekly, the film drew a threat from the North Korean government and was followed by a hack of Sony Pictures that leaked private emails and internal studio information. The outlet reports that the FBI concluded the North Korean government ordered the hack. The A.V. Club says the leaks exposed sensitive studio business and that Sony eventually released the comedy on Netflix.

It is worth keeping the claim in proportion. The account of why *Cleopatra* stalled is Scorsese's own recollection, delivered as a joke. None of the three outlets reports a statement from Sony or Columbia confirming that the hack ended the project.

## A long list of Cleopatra attempts

The A.V. Club puts Scorsese's near miss in a longer history. Since the 1963 Elizabeth Taylor film became one of Hollywood's most notorious fiascos, many directors have tried to revive the story. The outlet says Scorsese won an early 2010s race to direct a Scott Rudin-produced version based on the biography *Cleopatra: A Life*, with Jolie attached.

Other names reportedly circling the project included David Fincher, Paul Greengrass, Ang Lee and James Cameron, who wanted to shoot it in 3-D. More recently, Patty Jenkins was set to direct Gal Gadot in a version of the story, according to the same report.

## The joke that The Studio already made

Scorsese and Rogen are on good terms. As Deadline and Entertainment Weekly both note, Scorsese played a fictional version of himself in the first episode of Rogen's Apple TV series *The Studio*. In that episode, Rogen's studio head, Matt Remick, hires him for a Kool-Aid film and then faces pressure to drop it for a rival project.

Rogen co-directed every episode of the series. He told Entertainment Weekly that working with Scorsese was "very scary" until it happened, then "very fun". He said he had even kept a second camera crew in reserve in case Scorsese disliked the show's one-take style. Scorsese loved it, Rogen said, comparing it to the French New Wave. We covered a related moment in [Seth Rogen on The Studio Season 2 and a Scorsese jab](https://moduscarry.com/seth-rogen-on-the-studio-season-2-and-a-scorsese-jab/).

## What happens next

No reply from Rogen had been reported at the time of writing. Entertainment Weekly says it has contacted his representatives. Given the tone of the exchange and their working relationship, a good-humoured response is plausible, but that is speculation until he speaks.

## Frequently asked questions

### What did Martin Scorsese say about Seth Rogen?

He said Rogen's film *The Interview* led to events that "ruined Cleopatra", his planned film with Angelina Jolie. He delivered the line as a joke while talking with his daughter Francesca for Tribeca's *Crafty* series.

### Was Cleopatra officially cancelled because of the Sony hack?

That is Scorsese's account. The outlets covering the story report his comments but do not cite any confirmation from Sony or Columbia Pictures.

It does not appear so. Scorsese appeared as himself in the first episode of Rogen's Apple TV series *The Studio*, and Rogen has described the experience of working with him as fun.
