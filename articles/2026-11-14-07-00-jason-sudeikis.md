**Quick summary**

- [Jason Sudeikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Sudeikis) hosts the 10th annual Thundergong! benefit on Saturday, 14 November 2026, at the Uptown Theater in Kansas City, Missouri.
- The show raises money through the Steps of Faith Foundation for uninsured and underinsured amputees who need prosthetic limbs.
- The 2025 edition raised a record $1.13 million, according to KMBC.
- The lineup includes Brendan Hunt, Weird Al Yankovic, Will Forte and Sam Richardson, according to organisers.

Jason Sudeikis is in Kansas City this weekend to host Thundergong!, a musical variety night that marks its 10th year on Saturday, 14 November. The benefit takes place at the Uptown Theater at 3700 Broadway Blvd., Kansas City, and mixes comedy, live music and storytelling to pay for prosthetic limbs for people who cannot afford them, as reported by [KMBC](https://www.kmbc.com/article/jason-sudeikis-thundergong-benefit-2026/74424062) and [KCTV](https://www.kctv5.com/2026/11/13/jason-sudeikis-steps-faith-presents-thundergong-kansas-city/).

## Who is on the Thundergong! 2026 lineup?

Sudeikis is host. KMBC lists his *[Ted Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lasso)* co-star [Brendan Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Hunt), comedy musician [Weird Al Yankovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22Weird_Al%22_Yankovic) and actor [Will Forte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Forte), plus "many more". KCTV adds [Sam Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Richardson_(actor)) to the list of performers named by organisers.

Neither outlet has published a running order or set times. The reports describe a mix of music and comedy, not a fixed programme.

## Where the money goes

Proceeds go to the Steps of Faith Foundation. KCTV says the charity helps relieve the financial burden of prosthetic care, supporting amputees as they return to their communities, families and workplaces. Its chief executive, Billy Brimblecom, joined Sudeikis in local interviews to talk about the event.

Steps of Faith board member Will Gregory told KMBC the need is large. "A lot of people don't know this, but 500 people every day in the United States lose a limb," he said, adding that insurance for the most part does not cover the cost. He said the foundation helps people in Kansas City and across the country.

## A record to beat

The benefit has grown into a major fundraiser. Last year's show brought in $1.13 million, which KMBC describes as record-breaking. That makes the 10th edition the one to watch: if ticket sales and giving hold up, the total could pass that mark, though no target has been announced.

## What happens next

The show runs on Saturday night at the Uptown Theater. The sources do not say when a final total will be released.
