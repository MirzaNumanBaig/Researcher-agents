**Quick summary**

- Christopher Nolan's longtime casting director, John Papsidera, says [Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger) was the third actor the team considered for the Joker in *The Dark Knight*.
- He spoke at the Nashville Film Festival, according to a Yahoo report published on 5 November 2026.
- Per that report, Leonardo DiCaprio was sent the script first, followed by [Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix).
- Papsidera told Collider, without naming names, that one actor never replied and the other declined big films.
- Ledger described the Joker as an anarchist, which matched Nolan's own view of the character.

The man who cast *The Dark Knight* says the film's most celebrated performance was not the first one he and Nolan had in mind. Speaking at the Nashville Film Festival, John Papsidera said the team discussed only two actors in detail before they landed on [Heath Ledger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath_Ledger), according to a [Yahoo Entertainment report](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/leonardo-dicaprio-offered-joker-role-105127193.html).

## Who was offered the Joker first

Yahoo, citing TV Intelligentsia, reports that Nolan first sent the script to Leonardo DiCaprio and then to Joaquin Phoenix. It was only after both passed that the filmmakers turned to Ledger. The outlet notes that DiCaprio went on to work with Nolan on *Inception*, while Phoenix later played the character in the *Joker* films directed by [Todd Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Phillips).

Papsidera did not name the actors himself when he spoke to Collider at the festival. Yahoo says the account does suggest DiCaprio never responded to the script at all. Treat the identification of the two actors as the report's reading of the festival remarks, not as a statement from either star.

## What the casting director said

In his Collider interview, Papsidera said the team talked about more actors in general, but only discussed two in specific terms. In Yahoo's quotation, he described the first as someone "we never heard back from". The second came in, he said, and explained that he did not want to make big movies. "And the third one was Heath," he added.

That sequence matters because of how the role is remembered today. Yahoo describes Ledger's Joker as one of the most influential performances in cinema history, and the casting story shows how little certainty there was at the start.

## Why Ledger fitted the part

Papsidera said the team knew quickly that Ledger was right. According to his account, the actor saw the Joker as an anarchist whose only aim was to tear everything down. That, Papsidera said, was exactly how Nolan saw the character and wanted him portrayed.
