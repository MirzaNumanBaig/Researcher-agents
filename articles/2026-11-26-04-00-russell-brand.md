**Quick summary**

- The UK's harassment and bullying watchdog CIISA says it has seen a spike in whistleblowing reports since [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) was convicted, according to Variety.
- Brand was sentenced to 10 and a half years in prison after a jury found him guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.
- The Government said on Tuesday that its plans to curb the use of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) will be brought forward to January, Metro reports.
- Brand says he is not guilty and has sent a message to followers of his podcast from prison.

The conviction of Russell Brand is already changing how Britain's creative industries handle complaints. The Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA) says it has received a spike in whistleblowing reports since the comedian was jailed earlier this month, [Variety reported on Wednesday](https://variety.com/2026/tv/global/russell-brand-ciisa-reports-spike-1236957913/).

## Watchdog reports a rise in whistleblowing

According to Variety, the reports cover every sector CIISA represents: film, television, music, theatre and live performance. CIISA is an officially designated whistleblowing body, and its confidential service lets people raise concerns while the body builds a picture of patterns and problems. Those findings can then go back to policymakers and the industry. Where necessary, CIISA can also make "lawful referrals".

The figures came as CIISA held an event in Parliament on Wednesday. Variety says it was hosted by chair [Baroness Helena Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helena_Kennedy%2C_Baroness_Kennedy_of_The_Shaws) and attended by MPs, including culture committee chair [Caroline Dinenage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Dinenage), as well as BBC chair [Samir Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Shah) and former BBC director general [Tim Davie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Davie).

The event also launched CIISA's register of organisations that contribute financially. Supporters can carry a registration mark, and no company is expected to pay more than 0.1 per cent of its UK turnover. CEO Jen Smith said CIISA offers a chance to spot harmful behaviour across different projects.

## Government brings forward NDA curbs

Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham) has called Brand's conviction a "watershed moment", Metro reports. Writing in The Guardian, he argued that powerful men should no longer be able to use fame and wealth to escape accountability. On Tuesday the Government said its plans to make it harder to silence victims with NDAs would arrive in January.

Zelda Perkins CBE, who was Harvey Weinstein's assistant and signed an NDA at 24, told ITV the cases of Brand and [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) were "the tip of the iceberg". She called it "the beginning of the reckoning" for the UK's creative industries. Walliams, 55, denies any wrongdoing, and Metro notes he lost a High Court bid earlier this month to stop media organisations publishing allegations against him.

For more on the debate, see our earlier report on [how the case puts UK media NDAs under scrutiny](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-ndas-channel-4/).

## Brand's message from prison

Brand, 51, is held at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire. The Independent reports that he sent a message, read out by his wife Laura Gallacher on their Sunday Service podcast, saying he is "not guilty" of the offences. The message drew widespread criticism. The Prison Service of England and Wales asked YouTube to remove the recording as offensive to victims, but Google declined, and prison authorities are examining whether he breached regulations by communicating publicly from custody.

Comedian Rosie O'Donnell also responded, The Independent says. She described Brand's religious conversion, which began with a baptism in April 2024, as "performative", and urged him to admit guilt and apologise to those he harmed.

## What happens next

CIISA says it will use the reports to identify patterns and pass them on to policymakers and industry, with referrals where needed. The Government's NDA measures are due in January. Prison authorities have not yet said what they will decide over Brand's podcast message.
