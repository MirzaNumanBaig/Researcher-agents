**Quick summary**

- Editor Joe Walker says he finished work on *Dune: Part Three* two or three weeks before a recent interview, according to The Hollywood Reporter.
- Walker says the film runs faster than the first two and is set almost two decades after the end of Part Two.
- His next job is editing [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s upcoming [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond) film.
- Separately, Villeneuve's *Arrival* turns 10 this month, and Space.com has revisited how it came together.

The Denis Villeneuve Dune Part Three project has reached the finish line, at least on the editing side. Joe Walker, the Oscar-winning editor who has cut Villeneuve's films for years, told [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joe-walker-on-dune-part-three-villeneuve-mcqueen-james-bond-1236754607/) at the ToruńCamerimage festival that he wrapped the film about two or three weeks earlier. He also confirmed he is moving on to Villeneuve's Bond picture.

## Where Dune: Part Three stands

Walker, who received this year's Editor Award at Camerimage, said his assistant was still checking the 70mm prints and matching the grade, down to making sure every blue eye looks blue. He has signed off on the main versions: 2.20 (70mm), 1.90 (digital IMAX), 1.43 (full-frame IMAX 70mm), Dolby Vision, plus the IMAX and Atmos mixes. "The number of versions you have to do gets insane," he said.

Cutting finished in the summer. After that came a further two or three months of dubbing, music and visual effects. Walker thinks only 41 screens will be able to show the full IMAX version, though he said the effort behind it matches that of the 2.20 version most viewers will see.

## A faster, bigger film

Walker says this instalment is different and "going to surprise people". The story jumps almost two decades beyond the end of Part Two, and he describes a new look and a new pace. It was shot for faster cutting, he said, because the contemplative style of Part One did not suit this story.

Scale has grown too. Walker recalled roughly a 44-day shoot involving the sandworms on Part Two, against almost 100 days on Part Three. He could not give an exact figure but said there is "a huge amount of worm".

Much of it was still built for real. Walker said the castle sets, designed by production designer [Patrice Vermette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Vermette), were constructed to scale, and the worm scenes were filmed with a gimbal rig and finished later with wind and sound effects. His summary was that the actors had a lot of things thrown at them.

## How Villeneuve works with his editor

Walker said Villeneuve plans heavily on large productions, starting from the script and moving to storyboards. The director works with Toronto-based storyboard artist Sam Hudecki, refining scenes and adjusting the script as they go. Walker names the storyboards and Vermette's art book as two of his most important guides.

He has edited *Sicario*, *Arrival*, *Blade Runner 2049* and the Dune films for Villeneuve, and was nominated for an Oscar for *Arrival*. He won the Academy Award for editing in 2022 for *Dune*. Walker also linked the series to the festival's focus on artificial intelligence, noting that Frank Herbert's world is built on the aftermath of the Butlerian Jihad, a war against thinking machines.

## Arrival turns 10

Villeneuve's earlier science fiction film is also in the news. Space.com marked 10 years since the November 2016 release of *Arrival*, tracing it back to screenwriter Eric Heisserer's reading of Ted Chiang's story "Story of Your Life". Producers at 21 Laps pursued the rights, and Villeneuve, then fresh from the Oscar-nominated *Incendies*, told them he had always wanted to make a science fiction film.

Producer Aaron Ryder told SFX magazine that Villeneuve saw it as an "alien arrival movie" rather than an invasion story, according to Space.com. Villeneuve made *Prisoners*, *Enemy* and *Sicario* before starting it.

## What happens next

Walker's comments suggest Dune: Part Three is essentially ready, with final print checks continuing. The sources do not give a release date, so audiences will need to wait for the studio to confirm one. Walker's next job is the Bond film, which he called a "bucket list" project for a British editor.
