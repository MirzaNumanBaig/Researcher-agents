**Quick summary**

- UK Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham) has promised to tackle non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) after the sentencing of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), according to Deadline.
- Burnham called the case a "watershed moment" for the entertainment industry and for the country.
- New laws stopping NDAs from gagging employees who suffered harassment or discrimination have been brought forward to January.
- The government is also considering a staggered age of consent for 16 and 17-year-olds, but has not yet set out any details.

The UK government says the Russell Brand case will change how powerful people are allowed to silence others. Prime Minister Andy Burnham used his first public comments on Brand to promise tougher rules on NDAs, and the government is also looking at the age of consent, according to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/russell-brand-andy-burnham-ndas-david-walliams-1237233158/) and the [Daily Star](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/news/uk-news/andy-burnham-change-age-consent-37911775).

## What Burnham said about Russell Brand NDAs

Burnham described the case as a "watershed moment, not just for the culture of the entertainment industry but for us as a nation", Deadline reports. Writing in The Guardian, he said he would keep working so that NDAs cannot be used to silence others, while still protecting the identity of victims who want that.

"This is about rebalancing the scales of power away from perpetrators and towards victims," he wrote. He also quoted the judge in the Brand case, who said women keep the right to say no and to demand justice from the criminal courts, whatever they wear, wherever they go and whatever they drink.

Deadline reports that Brand received a 10-year sentence for rape. The Daily Star describes it as ten and a half years for rape and sexual assault. The two outlets differ on the figure, so check the court record for the exact term.

## The law is moving faster

According to Deadline, new laws saying NDAs can no longer gag employees who have suffered harassment or discrimination have been brought forward to January. Burnham wants to go further and cover "freelance employees and fans who experience abuse". Both groups are relevant to the Brand case.

The head of the broadcasting union Bectu, Phillipa Childs, said the government must urgently reassure creative workers that freelancers and other self-employed people will have the same protections as employees. Otherwise, she warned, the ban risks creating "a giant loophole".

The Daily Star adds that Burnham wants to "draw a line in the sand" on the idea that powerful men can escape justice by silencing their victims. Victims Minister Alex Davies-Jones welcomed the move. Zelda Perkins, who founded Can't Buy My Silence UK after working as an assistant to Harvey Weinstein, called the promises "significant".

## Age of consent under review

The government is also looking at "staggering the age of consent", Burnham confirmed in The Guardian. The aim is to stop men in their 20s and older from having sex with 16 and 17-year-olds, Deadline reports.

The Daily Star says the government will ask survivors, parents, young people, victim support services and the wider public to share their experiences. No specific age limits or age gaps have been announced. The paper also notes that on 17 November Burnham said he would not rush into sweeping reforms.

## Walliams and the wider industry debate

The debate has also been driven by allegations against comedian [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), which he strongly denies. Deadline says the Brand sentence and the Walliams allegations have renewed scrutiny of NDAs in entertainment.

Over the weekend, Channel 4 News boss Esme Wren emailed staff to say that ITN, which operates the programme, no longer uses NDAs and has apologised for past cases. Actors including [Natalie Dormer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Dormer) and [Richard Armitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Armitage_(actor)) have signed an open letter calling on the industry to acknowledge its role in enabling bad behaviour. For more on that, see our report on the [open letter from 500 creatives](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-open-letter-2/) and on [UK media NDAs under fresh scrutiny](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-ndas-channel-4/).

## What happens next

Parliament's Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee is investigating how the industry uses NDAs. Deadline says its evidence sessions begin in about two weeks. The new NDA rules are due in January, and the age of consent proposals are still at the consultation stage. ## Frequently asked questions

### What did Andy Burnham promise after the Russell Brand sentencing?

He promised to stop NDAs being used to silence victims, to extend protections to freelancers and fans, and to consider a staggered age of consent. The government has brought forward its NDA law changes to January.

### Does the new NDA law cover freelancers?

Not yet, as far as reported. Burnham says he wants to extend protection to freelancers, and Bectu has warned that leaving them out would create a loophole.

### Has the age of consent been changed?

No. The government is only considering a staggered approach and will consult survivors, parents, young people and support services. No age limits have been announced.
