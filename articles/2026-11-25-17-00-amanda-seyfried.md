- [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) appeared on Jimmy Fallon's "Tonight Show" on Tuesday to talk about *You Can See Everything*, according to Variety.
- She said she often did not know what the script said until she arrived on set.
- Her description of the access: something like a "five-factor authentication app" that might let her read it the night before.
- Variety reports the film has become the first documentary in decades to pass US$100 million at the global box office.

Amanda Seyfried You Can See Everything stories keep coming, and her latest reveals how tightly the script was guarded. Speaking to Jimmy Fallon on "The Tonight Show" on Tuesday evening, the actress said she often had no idea what she would be performing until she reached the set, [Variety reported](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/amanda-seyfried-you-can-see-everything-script-elizabeth-holmes-nathan-fielder-1236957716/).

## What Seyfried told Fallon about the You Can See Everything script

Variety says Seyfried alluded to the script being kept under lock and key. In her words, she did not know what the script would be until she got to set, or it would be "like, a five-factor authentication app" where she could perhaps read it the night before, per Variety.

It is a light remark, but it fits the way the project came together. She described a film that began as one thing and ended as another. "At first, they had just done their whole documentary, and then I just jumped in kind of at the last-minute, and it turned into something else," she told the host.

## How she came to play Elizabeth Holmes

According to Variety, the film is a documentary from [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) and Lance Oppenheim about Elizabeth Holmes. Once Holmes was sent to prison, Fielder asked Seyfried to take her place, and she reenacted scenes as Holmes.

Seyfried said she had plenty of material to study because Holmes was a "very public-facing person". The catch was footage of Holmes in the present day, or from the period when the filmmakers were living with her. The only footage she had was the documentary itself, and she told Fallon she could not watch it until she was on set.

In other words, she drew on public research about Holmes, while the filmmakers' own footage stayed out of reach until she was on set.

## A documentary that has become a phenomenon

Variety describes the film as a "bona fide cultural phenomenon". It says the documentary has become the first in decades to cross US$100 million at the global box office, has inspired countless memes and has pushed the boundaries of the genre.

The trade outlet also says the film has cemented Seyfried's status as an awards contender, which explains the press rounds. Her Fallon visit was her latest stop. Those awards claims are Variety's assessment; no nominations have been mentioned in the sources.
