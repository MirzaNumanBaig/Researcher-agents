**Quick summary**

- [Bug Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_Hall), 41, told People he had $10 in the bank before he started his GoFundMe.
- The fundraiser had a $2,000 goal and has raised more than $29,000, according to People.
- Hall says the money has let him start building the family's house in rural Arkansas with a sawmill.
- He and his wife, Jill, are expecting their sixth child.

## The Bug Hall GoFundMe: $10 in the bank

The Bug Hall GoFundMe has already changed what his family can do. Bug Hall says the money he raised online has already changed what his family can do. The former child actor, best known as Alfalfa in the 1994 film version of [The Little Rascals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Gang), told [People](https://people.com/bug-hall-says-gofundme-support-helped-his-family-start-building-dream-home-exclusive-12259292) in an exclusive interview that he had only $10 in the bank when he asked for help.

According to People, a rushed move to the family's new property had used up nearly all their savings. Temperatures were dropping, there were only four days of propane left to heat the camper, and his wife was close to giving birth. "By the time I was done, we had $10 in the bank, and I was like, 'What do I do?'" he said.

## From $2,000 to more than $29,000

Hall launched the fundraiser with a $2,000 goal. It has since raised more than $29,000, People reported. He says he meant to close it once it hit that figure, but a wave of mass reports stopped him doing so.

After working with GoFundMe to sort out the problem, Hall says he deleted the page once the money was ready to transfer. The platform later contacted him and offered to restore it so he could update donors and clear up confusion about the campaign, according to the interview. We covered the earlier backlash and the paused payout in our report on the [Bug Hall GoFundMe](https://moduscarry.com/bug-hall-gofundme-2/).

Hall described the response as overwhelming. He said he had expected to be saving for building equipment for two or three years. Instead, he said, "we've got the sawmill here, and we're actually building our house." Friends from his Hollywood years, some he had not heard from in a long time, also got in touch to wish him well.

## Why he left Hollywood

People's piece goes back to what came before the move. In his last years in the industry, Hall said, he was focused less on acting and more on developing television and film projects through his own company. One of them, the Netflix animated series *A Tale Dark & Grimm*, was nominated for an Emmy.

He told People he was in the middle of producing the show when he had what he called a "very public, very embarrassing relapse". Hall has said he struggled with addiction and alcoholism earlier in life. His second daughter had recently been born, and he said the relapse made him unwilling to have his children grow up watching him swing back and forth.

He took six months away from work. He also said he had grown uncomfortable feeling unable to speak openly about his beliefs around colleagues, business partners and investors, calling it a "double life". His Catholic faith, which he embraced in 2013, also shaped the decision to leave, he said. ## What happens next

The most immediate event is the birth of the couple's sixth child. People reported that the family is working toward a self-sufficient farm lifestyle on the Arkansas land, and that the house build is now under way. Hall has not given a completion date.
