**Quick summary**

- Gladiator has arrived on Netflix in the United States, alongside Gladiator II, according to Collider on 25 October.
- L.A. Confidential, Russell Crowe's 1997 neo-noir, is leaving Prime Video in the US, Collider reports.
- Crowe's Western The Weight, with Ethan Hawke, is now on premium video on demand (PVOD).
- Collider says the psychological drama Unabomber has passed 50 million views on Netflix.

[Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe) has a busy week on screens. Gladiator, the film that made Maximus a household name, is now streaming on Netflix in the United States, while one of his best-reviewed films is about to disappear from Prime Video. Both moves were reported by Collider over the weekend of 25 October.

The timing matters because Crowe has several titles in circulation at once. Our earlier coverage of [Gladiator landing on Netflix](https://moduscarry.com/russell-crowes-gladiator-lands-on-netflix-this-weekend/) flagged the arrival, and this week's reports confirm it is live.

## Is Gladiator on Netflix now?

Yes, in the United States. Collider says Gladiator joins Gladiator II on the service, 26 years after the original opened in cinemas in May 2000. The site's report does not cover other regions, so Australian viewers should check their local Netflix listings.

The film is set in 180 CE. Crowe plays the Roman general Maximus, who is betrayed after Emperor Marcus Aurelius names him as successor. Joaquin Phoenix plays Commodus, the emperor's son, and Ridley Scott directed. Collider lists a cast that also includes Connie Nielsen, Oliver Reed, Richard Harris and Djimon Hounsou.

That is the film coming in. The next title is going out.

## Why is L.A. Confidential leaving Prime Video?

Collider reports that L.A. Confidential is due to leave Prime Video in the US in November 2026, though the excerpt we could read does not give the exact day. Released in 1997, the neo-noir is based on a novel by James Ellroy and was both a critical and commercial hit, according to Collider.

Crowe plays Wendell "Bud" White, a hot-headed LAPD officer in 1950s Los Angeles. Collider says the film holds a 99% score on a review aggregator, and calls it the best-reviewed film of his entire career. It came a few years after his breakout in the Australian film Romper Stomper.

If you want to see it on Prime Video, the window is closing. But Crowe's newest work is not on a subscription service at all.

## What do critics say about The Weight?

The Weight, a 1933-set Pacific Northwest Western, is now available on PVOD, according to Collider. The same outlet says it is the best-reviewed film Crowe has made since The Insider in 1999.

The Movie Buff's review is warm. It calls the film a "rugged post-Western" and a remarkable debut for director Padraic McKinley. [Ethan Hawke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Hawke) plays Samuel Murphy, a man sent to a penal camp who is offered a deal by Crowe's Warden J.J. Clancy: pick three men and move a load of government gold across rough country in return for freedom.

The reviewer describes Crowe as "classically powerhouse" and praises the supporting cast, including [Austin Amelio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Amelio), [Sam Hazeldine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Hazeldine) and [Avi Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avi_Nash). The review compares the film's debut-director confidence to [Slow West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_West) and sees echoes of [Justin Kurzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Kurzel). It also says the film falls short of The Treasure of the Sierra Madre, the classic it invites comparison with. That is one reviewer's view, not a consensus.

More on how the film is performing is in our post on [The Weight climbing the streaming charts](https://moduscarry.com/russell-crowes-the-weight-climbs-the-streaming-charts/).

## What about Unabomber?

Collider also notes that Netflix's psychological drama Unabomber, which features Crowe, has passed 50 million views on the service. The report gives no breakdown of that figure or the period it covers, so treat it as a headline number only.

## What happens next

L.A. Confidential is due to leave Prime Video in the US in November 2026, per Collider. Gladiator and Gladiator II are available on Netflix in the US now, and The Weight can be rented or bought on PVOD.
