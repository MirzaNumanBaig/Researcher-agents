**Quick summary**

- More than 500 actors, directors, writers and producers have signed an open letter titled "Let's make this a turning point", according to Deadline.
- It calls for an end to a "culture of open secrets" in the British entertainment industry after Russell Brand's prison sentence.
- Brand is serving 10-and-a-half years for rape and sexual assault, as reported by Deadline and Metro.
- Separately, unnamed sources have made new claims about Katy Perry's marriage to Brand. None of them is confirmed.

The Russell Brand open letter has grown well beyond its first signatories. More than 500 people from film, television and theatre have now put their names to a call for the industry to change, [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/11/uk-stars-open-letter-brand-walliams-dormer-armitage-1237224955/) on Monday. It follows the sentencing of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) to 10-and-a-half years in prison.

## What the open letter says

The letter is titled "Let's make this a turning point" and describes itself as an open letter to the creative industry from the creative industry. Early signatories included [Natalie Dormer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Dormer) and [Richard Armitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Armitage_(actor)), along with Susan Wokoma, Les Dennis and Jo Joyner. Deadline's update says [Susan Sarandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sarandon) became the first major American star to sign.

Its central argument is that a sentence alone is not a turning point. The authors say the sector must first admit its part in enabling the behaviour at the centre of the convictions. They ask for more than "PR-drafted statements, vague promises or weak admissions that more could be done".

The letter also warns against treating Brand and [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) as "one-offs". It points out that it took some seventeen years for justice, according to the text published by Deadline. Walliams has been named as the subject of allegations of sexually exploitative behaviour towards young women and teenagers. Police are investigating, and he denies the claims.

## What signatories want from broadcasters and government

The letter urges broadcasters, studios, independent producers, organisations and unions to review their processes and protect people who speak out. It asks the government to "act, not debate" by increasing support for victims and making sure police forces are fully resourced.

It was organised by writer and producer Ben Fletcher and actor and campaigner Alexa Morden. Morden hosts the industry podcast The 98%. She told Deadline she hopes the letter makes these systemic issues "impossible to ignore", pointing to hierarchies of power and a culture of silence.

The timing matters. Deadline says Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy was convening industry leaders, including BBC boss Matt Brittin, on the same afternoon.

## Where the case stands

Brand pleaded not guilty. AOL reports that a jury at Southwark Crown Court convicted him of sexually assaulting four women between 2004 and 2009. Metro says he is serving his sentence at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire. AOL adds that the judge, Joel Bennathan, said Brand showed "casual and arrogant disregard" for women who refused him.

His wife, Laura Brand, said on her "Sunday Service" programme that she will "absolutely honor" the prison's rules after a message from him drew attention. Variety reports the UK government asked YouTube to remove the video, which Google refused. We covered that earlier in [Laura Brand's pledge to honour prison rules](https://moduscarry.com/laura-brand-prison-rules/).

## New claims about Katy Perry's marriage

Tabloid-sourced claims about [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) have also resurfaced. Metro, citing a source quoted by The Sun, says Perry "kept a lot of the really dark stuff private" during the 2010 to 2012 marriage. The same source alleges Brand tried to control her. These are anonymous claims. Metro says it has asked Perry's representative for comment.

Perry has spoken publicly only in general terms. In a 2013 Vogue interview she described Brand as "very controlling" and said she later learned a "real truth" she declined to share. The Mail on Sunday reported, again through an unnamed source, that she is considering a series or book. Read more in our report on [Perry weighing a book and series](https://moduscarry.com/katy-perry-russell-brand-book/). AOL notes Perry was not asked to testify, and the Crown Prosecution Service confirmed police never approached her.

## What happens next

The letter's organisers are asking for responses from broadcasters and unions. The Nandy meeting could show whether the government will act on its calls. Walliams remains under police investigation. Perry has not publicly confirmed any of the reports about a book or series. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Russell Brand open letter?

It is a letter titled "Let's make this a turning point", signed by more than 500 creatives. It asks the entertainment industry to end a "culture of open secrets" and to protect people who report abuse.

### Who has signed the letter?

Signatories include Natalie Dormer, Richard Armitage, Susan Wokoma, Les Dennis, Jo Joyner and Susan Sarandon, according to Deadline.

### How long is Russell Brand's sentence?

Deadline and Metro report a sentence of 10-and-a-half years for rape and sexual assault.

### Are the Katy Perry claims confirmed?

No. They come from unnamed sources quoted by The Sun and The Mail on Sunday. Metro says it has contacted Perry's representative.
