**Quick summary**

- [Martha Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Stewart) says [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) will play her in a film about her life, the Cate Blanchett Martha Stewart biopic.
- Stewart made the remark on Wednesday, 18 November (US time), in a conversation with Jay Leno at a California resort.
- She says Blanchett phoned her about two weeks earlier, and that the film covers part of her life, possibly up to and including her trial.
- TMZ reports the project is titled "Good Thing" and that Janicza Bravo is set to direct. Neither the studio nor Blanchett has commented in the sources we read.

The Cate Blanchett Martha Stewart biopic has its first public confirmation, from Stewart herself. Speaking this week, the 85-year-old homeware figure said the two-time Oscar winner is playing her in a film about part of her life.

## What Martha Stewart said

According to [the Daily Mail](https://www.dailymail.com/tvshowbiz/article-16306395/martha-stewart-cate-blanchett-biopic-trial.html), Stewart was appearing with comedian [Jay Leno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Leno) at Yaamava Resort & Casino when he asked which actress she would pick to play her. Instead of naming a wish, she described what had already happened. She said Blanchett had called her two weeks earlier and that Blanchett and a collaborator were writing a film about part of her life.

Stewart said she had not been told which part. "They won't tell me exactly what part," she said, adding that she thinks it runs up to and through the trial. [Female First](https://www.femalefirst.co.uk/celebrity/martha-stewart-delighted-cate-blanchett-cast-biopic-1453949.html) quotes her calling Blanchett "so perfect for the part."

The event was the launch of "Martha's Forest of Wonder," a resort-wide holiday experience in Southern California, according to [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/11/19/martha-stewart-talks-casting-cate-blanchett-in-her-biopic/).

## The Cate Blanchett Martha Stewart biopic: what is known

TMZ reports that the film is called "Good Thing", a nod to Stewart's signature phrase. It says Variety first reported the project in April, and that [Janicza Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janicza_Bravo), who directed the 2020 film "Zola", is attached as director. We have not seen the Variety piece, so that detail rests on TMZ's account.

TMZ also says the story takes in Stewart's rise and her criminal trial. The Daily Mail frames it as a film that follows her life before the stock trading scandal. Those descriptions overlap but are not identical, and Stewart herself said she does not know the exact scope. Until the filmmakers say more, the timeline of the story should be treated as unconfirmed.

## Why the trial matters to the story

The case is the best-known turning point in Stewart's public life. According to TMZ, she was convicted in 2004 of conspiracy, obstruction of an agency proceeding and two counts of making false statements to federal investigators. She served five months in prison followed by five months of home confinement. Female First describes the case as arising from an insider trading scandal, though the charges as TMZ lists them were about obstruction and false statements.

A film that stops at or near that moment would cover her climb from caterer and author to a household brand, then the fall. For Blanchett, it is another real-life role in a career that has included several portraits of public figures. You can follow her recent work in our coverage of her role in [Alice Birch's Sweetsick](https://moduscarry.com/cate-blanchett-sweetsick/).

## What Stewart did not say

The sources leave several questions open. Stewart gave no start date for filming, no release window, no studio and no cast beyond Blanchett. She also did not say whether she is involved in the script or has seen it. Female First relied on a video of the exchange shared online.

## What happens next

An official confirmation from the producers or from Blanchett would settle the casting. Until then the news rests on Stewart's own account, repeated consistently across the three outlets we read.
