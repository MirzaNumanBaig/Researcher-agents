**Quick summary**

- *I Play Rocky*, a biopic about how [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) got *Rocky* made, expanded to four times as many cinemas last weekend, according to MovieWeb.
- It opened in seven theatres, grew to 29, and has earned US$1.8 million so far.
- The film holds 87% on Rotten Tomatoes from 144 reviews and a 98% verified audience score.
- The nationwide US release is set for Nov. 20, one day before the 50th anniversary of the original film's premiere.

*I Play Rocky*, the film about Sylvester Stallone's long fight to get *Rocky* onto the screen, is moving from a limited run to a wide release. After quadrupling its cinema count over the weekend, it opens nationwide in the United States on Nov. 20, according to [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/i-play-rocky-box-office-november-2026/).

## A small start with a big jump

The numbers so far are modest, and MovieWeb is clear about why. The film debuted in just seven theatres, then expanded to 29 last weekend. Earnings rose 46% on that expansion, and the cumulative gross sits at US$1.8 million.

MovieWeb argues the total reflects the tiny footprint rather than a lack of interest. The real test arrives on Nov. 20, when the film plays everywhere and we find out whether audiences take to Stallone's origin story the way they took to the 1976 original. Those wide-release figures are not in yet, so any forecast is guesswork.

## What critics and audiences say

[Peter Farrelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrelly) directs, and [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) plays the young Stallone. On [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) the film has an 87% Certified Fresh rating from 144 reviews. MovieWeb says critics have praised its crowd-pleasing energy and Ippolito's committed performance.

Audiences are warmer still. The verified Popcornmeter score stands at 98%. For a film that has played in so few cinemas, that is an early sign of word of mouth, though a small sample of early viewers is not the same as a mass audience.

MovieWeb's own review calls the film "a refreshing study in optimism" and describes the lead as "born for this role". It also says the film works as a behind-the-scenes look at creative problem-solving.

## The story behind the story

The film retells one of Hollywood's best-documented underdog stories. According to MovieWeb, Stallone was a cash-strapped actor who wrote the *Rocky* script in just over three days. He turned down lucrative studio offers for his handwritten screenplay and insisted on playing the title role himself.

That gamble paid off. *Rocky* went on to win the Oscar for Best Picture and launched a global franchise. If you follow the actor's career, our [Sylvester Stallone archive](https://moduscarry.com/tag/sylvester-stallone/) covers more, including the [auction of his first manager's Rocky papers](https://moduscarry.com/sylvester-stallone-rocky-auction/).

## A 50-year timing

The wide release lands at a neat moment. The original *Rocky* premiered in New York City on Nov. 21, 1976, so the biopic arrives almost exactly half a century later. MovieWeb frames it as more than nostalgia, a way to introduce the story to a new generation of cinema-goers.

## What happens next

The film opens nationwide in the United States on Nov. 20. The first wide-release weekend will show whether the early enthusiasm carries over to bigger crowds.
