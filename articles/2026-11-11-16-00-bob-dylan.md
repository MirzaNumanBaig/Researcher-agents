**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) played "Sugar Baby" live for the first time since 2012 at a Saturday show in Italy, according to Rolling Stone and JamBase.
- His official setlist archive shows the last performance was on 14 November 2012 in Toronto, JamBase reports.
- Columbia and Legacy Recordings have released a 39-CD set from the 1976 Rolling Thunder Revue, with 371 previously unreleased tracks.
- The Lights of Autumn tour continues in Ljubljana, Slovenia, on Monday.

Bob Dylan pulled "Sugar Baby" out of storage on Saturday night in Italy, playing the closing track from his 2001 album "Love and Theft" for the first time in almost 14 years. The surprise came in the same week a huge archive of his 1976 concerts reached shelves, a rare moment where the singer's past and present are both in the spotlight.

## A 14-year gap ends in Italy

[Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/bob-dylan-sugar-baby-first-time-since-2012-1235654727/) places the performance in Bologna, while [JamBase](https://www.jambase.com/article/bob-dylan-sugar-baby-italy-audio-setlist) names the venue as Unipol Arena in Casalecchio di Reno, on the city's edge. The song arrived as the penultimate number in a 17-song set.

JamBase says Dylan's official setlist archive lists the previous outing for "Sugar Baby" as 14 November 2012 in Toronto. Rolling Stone notes there is no video, because fans must lock their phones in pouches at his shows, but unusually clean audio has since circulated.

Dylan played electric keyboard all night and added harmonica on "Under the Red Sky" and "I Shall Be Released", per JamBase. The band was bassist Tony Garnier, drummer Anton Fig and guitarists Joel Paterson and Julian Lage.

## A tour full of long-lost songs

"Sugar Baby" is the latest in a run of changes since the Lights of Autumn tour began in Oslo on 17 October. Rolling Stone lists covers of Randy Newman's "Louisiana 1927" and Guy Clark's "Dublin Blues", plus new arrangements of "Tangled Up in Blue", which includes a new verse, and "Tryin' to Get to Heaven".

Several songs have also returned after long absences: "Crash on the Levee (Down in the Flood)", "Girl from the North Country", "Million Miles" and "High Water". In Italy he added covers of Bo Diddley's "I Can Tell" and "I Can't Seem to Say Goodbye", a song written by Don Robertson and recorded by Jerry Lee Lewis, JamBase says. He again picked four tracks from 1997's "Time Out of Mind".

The guitar chair has been busy. Rolling Stone reports that Mike Seal joined at the start of the tour but left after eight shows without explanation. Lage replaced him, and it is his fifth stint with the group since June. Rolling Stone expects any further surprises to come from "The Basement Tapes", "Time Out of Mind" or "Love and Theft", the three albums he has leaned on lately.

## The 1976 box set

The archive release is Bob Dylan, The Rolling Thunder Revue: The 1976 Live Recordings. [World Music Central](https://worldmusiccentral.org/bob-dylans-1976-rolling-thunder-revue-revisited-in-39-cd-collection-featuring-371-previously-unreleased-tracks/) reports that Columbia Records and Legacy Recordings issued a 39-CD set of 381 tracks, 371 of them previously unreleased. It covers the second leg of the [Rolling Thunder Revue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Thunder_Revue), which ran from 18 April in Lakeland, Florida, to 25 May 1976 in Salt Lake City, Utah.

A separate three-LP edition with 22 tracks presents the full 16 May concert in Fort Worth, Texas, remixed from the original multitrack tapes. The box also includes the complete 23 May show in Fort Collins, Colorado. That night supplied the material for "Hard Rain", an NBC television special that aired only once. A booklet carries an essay by [Mikal Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikal_Gilmore) and rare photographs. Gilmore calls Dylan's "Idiot Wind" in that special "among the most rageful and brilliant performances Dylan ever committed before camera".

## Who played on the tour

[Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez) returned for duets, including "Blowin' in the Wind" and "Railroad Boy". The band featured bassist [Rob Stoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Stoner), violinist Scarlet Rivera and drummer [Howie Wyeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Wyeth), all of whom played on 1976's "Desire". Guitarists Mick Ronson, [Steven Soles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soles), [David Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mansfield) and T Bone Burnett completed the lineup.

[FLOOD](https://floodmagazine.com/236263/bob-dylan-the-rolling-thunder-revue-the-1976-live-recordings/) reviewed the set warmly. It says the collection captures 22 shows, each sounding more driven than the last, and calls it the best commercial Dylan concert collection apart from "Before the Flood". The reviewer also highlights duets with Willie Nelson from the Houston stop and says Burnett has described the tour as "the first national punk rock tour". Counts differ slightly between outlets, with World Music Central citing a 25-date itinerary for the full tour.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Bob Dylan last play "Sugar Baby"?
According to JamBase, citing his official setlist archive, the previous performance was on 14 November 2012 in Toronto. He played it again on Saturday in Italy.

### What is on the new Rolling Thunder Revue box set?
It is a 39-CD collection with 381 tracks from the 1976 tour's second leg, including 371 previously unreleased recordings, according to World Music Central. A three-LP Fort Worth concert is sold separately.

### Is Bob Dylan still touring?
Yes. The Lights of Autumn tour began in Oslo on 17 October and, per Rolling Stone, continues in Ljubljana on Monday.
