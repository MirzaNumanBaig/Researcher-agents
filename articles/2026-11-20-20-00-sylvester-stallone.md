**Quick summary**

- *Rocky* premiered in New York on 20 November 1976, which makes today its 50th anniversary.
- The Guardian has republished critic Derek Malcolm's original review from 1977, when the film reached the UK two months after its US debut.
- Malcolm called [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) "unquestionably a discovery" while judging the film itself to be old-fashioned crowd-pleasing.
- Stallone wrote the script and insisted on playing the lead, according to the review.

Half a century after *Rocky* first hit cinema screens, [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/nov/20/rocky-sylvester-stallone-review-1977) has pulled Derek Malcolm's original review out of its archive. It shows a critic who enjoyed being won over and was a little suspicious of how easily it happened. For anyone who knows the film only as a boxing legend, it is a useful look at how the story landed when nobody knew what Stallone would become.

## A film that nearly did not get made

Malcolm opens with the film's backstory. According to the review, Stallone's script was shopped around from studio to studio, and he held out until he got to star in his own picture. Malcolm wrote that this was "the story of half the most successful movies ever made", and added that the real-life version was about as unlikely as the fiction on screen.

That context matters for today's anniversary. The review treats Stallone as both writer and lead, and it gives him credit for each role. Malcolm said the performance was "impossible not to watch", whether or not the reader liked the film.

## What the 1977 critic liked, and what he doubted

The review is not a simple rave. Malcolm accepted that *Rocky* could be read as "skilfully manufactured romantic flim-flam", a grimy city love story built to be a hit. He admitted it manipulated its audience from start to finish, and that viewers could feel played even while enjoying it.

Still, he argued the film had solid values. He rated it above the television series *Roots* for saying more in less time, and praised director John Avildsen, whose earlier credits he listed as *Joe* and *Save the Tiger*. In his view the whole film hung together and stayed comfortably inside its own limits.

His closing verdict leaned toward generosity. He called the film "basically pap" and a throwback to the old dream factory of Hollywood, then said he preferred to think it succeeded because of what it was, not what it was not.

## The cast the critic singled out

Malcolm described Rocky as a small-time Philadelphia boxer who earns extra money working for a neighbourhood loan shark. He is a gentle giant who talks to his pet fish and who, in the critic's words, is the sort of man you want something good to happen to.

Around him, the review points to several supporting players:

- [Talia Shire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talia_Shire) as the quiet woman with "a brain rather than a body" who becomes his partner.
- [Carl Weathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Weathers) as the heavyweight champion, whom Malcolm described as "doing a passable Ali".
- [Burt Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burt_Young) as her brother, who is trying to get her settled.
- [Burgess Meredith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgess_Meredith) as the old trainer who throws Rocky out of his gym and then returns to manage him.

Malcolm said these small parts worked because Stallone and Avildsen paid attention to observed detail. He also summed up the premise in a line of dialogue: the champion against the "Italian Stallion", which a promoter calls "very American". The fight, as he tells it, ends with Rocky still standing after 15 rounds.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Rocky premiere?

According to The Guardian, *Rocky* premiered in New York on 20 November 1976. It reached UK cinemas two months later.

### Who reviewed Rocky for The Guardian in 1977?

Derek Malcolm wrote the review. It has now been republished as part of the paper's archive series.

### Who wrote Rocky?

Sylvester Stallone wrote the script and starred as Rocky, according to the review. John Avildsen directed.

### Did the 1977 critic like Rocky?

Largely yes, with caveats. Malcolm praised Stallone and the direction but called the film manipulative and old-fashioned.
