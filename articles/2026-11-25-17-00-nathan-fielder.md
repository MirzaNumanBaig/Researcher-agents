Two stories are keeping [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) in the headlines this week: an actress describing how secretive his latest film was to make, and a magazine looking at the clothing label he runs on the side.

## Seyfried on a script she could barely read

Amanda Seyfried appeared on Jimmy Fallon's "Tonight Show" on Tuesday evening to talk about her role in *You Can See Everything*, the film Fielder made with director Lance Oppenheim, [according to Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/amanda-seyfried-you-can-see-everything-script-elizabeth-holmes-nathan-fielder-1236957716/).

She said she joined late. By her account, the team had already finished their documentary, and her arrival at the last minute turned the project into something different. Once Elizabeth Holmes is sent to prison, Fielder asked Seyfried to take her place and reenact scenes as Holmes.

Research was not the problem, Seyfried said, because Holmes was a very public-facing person. The trouble was the present day. The only footage she had of Holmes from the period when the filmmakers were living with her was the documentary itself, and she could not watch it until she was on set.

The script was just as tightly held. Seyfried joked that she would not know what it said until she arrived, or that it would be something like a "five-factor authentication app" that might let her read it the night before.

## A documentary that behaves like a hit

Variety describes the film as a cultural phenomenon. It says the film has become the first documentary in decades to cross US$100 million worldwide, has inspired countless memes and has strengthened Seyfried's standing as an awards contender.

Other trackers put the figure a little lower. The film's [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything) lists a gross of about US$93 million against a budget of roughly US$7 million, and the totals differ by source. The film was released on 6 September 2026, and Wikipedia records a 97% score on Rotten Tomatoes from 229 reviews.

For readers who want the earlier coverage, we have also reported on the film's [UK documentary box office record](https://moduscarry.com/nathan-fielder-you-can-see-everything-box-office/) and on [the US$100 million milestone](https://moduscarry.com/nathan-fielder-you-can-see-everything-100-million/).

## The clothing label behind the comedian

British GQ turned to a different corner of Fielder's life: [Summit Ice](https://www.gq-magazine.co.uk/article/nathan-fielder-summit-ice-24-11-26), a not-for-profit outdoor apparel company. Unlike the plain sweaters and New Balance trainers Fielder wears himself, the brand is built around function. GQ says most of its profits go to charities that raise awareness of the Holocaust and fight intolerance.

The range includes four shell jackets, made up of two versions each of the "Cypress" and the "New Original". Both launched in 2023 as part of what the brand calls a "new era". GQ says a slump in sales pushed the company to improve the technical quality of its gear. There are also logo-free hoodies, caps and tees, plus socks and a baby onesie printed with "Deny Nothing", a reference to Holocaust denial.

## How a prank became a business

GQ traces the label to 2015. Fielder says he wanted to take business from Vancouver jacket maker Taiga after learning that it had once included a tribute to the late journalist Doug Collins, a Holocaust denier, in an advertising flyer. GQ notes the flyer circulated in 2001, that Taiga has never made a public statement about it, and that the company did not respond to GQ's request for comment.

Summit Ice became the subject of a *Nathan For You* episode, "Horseback Riding/Man Zone". That episode later featured in *The Rehearsal*, where Fielder discusses why Paramount+ removed it from streaming. Paramount told GQ the decision followed a "standards review".

What began as a bit has raised real money. GQ reports that Summit Ice made US$300,000 (about £230,000) in sales in its first eight weeks and has raised hundreds of thousands of dollars for charity. A decade on, it says the label is still going.

## What happens next

No new Fielder project was announced in either story. Seyfried's press round continues as awards season builds, and GQ expects more attention on Summit Ice while the film keeps selling out screenings.
