**Quick summary**

- [Druski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druski) has described a childhood that was financially comfortable but difficult at home, in an interview that has resurfaced online.
- He says he used jokes to lift the mood in his family, and that this became the root of his comedy.
- He told the Diary of a CEO podcast that he avoids "punching down" in his material.
- He also credits Drake's 2020 "Laugh Now Cry Later" video with changing how brands treated him.

The comedian Druski says his "broken home" childhood is where his comedy began. In an interview with Steven Bartlett's Diary of a CEO podcast, which has resurfaced this week, he said humour was how he tried to keep the peace, according to a report by [TNX Africa](https://www.tnx.africa/entertainment/article/2001562851/druski-how-growing-up-in-a-broken-home-shaped-my-comedy).

## A comfortable home that was not an easy one

Druski said money was never the problem. Per TNX Africa's account of the interview, his father worked as a United Airlines pilot and his mother held a job with the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Both were well paid.

Life behind closed doors was a different matter. He described strict discipline and physical abuse, and summed up the contrast in one line: his family was "a broken home" but "not a broke home".

Druski did not frame the story as a grievance. He said he was the child who always tried to make the family smile, whatever else was happening. Those are his own recollections of events, and the report relies on his account alone.

## How a coping habit became a comedy style

The comedian said the habit of telling jokes to ease tension stayed with him and eventually defined his work. He told the podcast his comedy comes from "that comforting type of space".

He also said it made him alert to other people's moods. In his words, he can read a room and sense danger, happiness or sadness in almost any situation.

He added that he does not like to "punch down". Instead he looks for the funny side of a bad situation in a way that works for both him and the other person.

## The Drake video that changed his standing with brands

The interview was not only about childhood. Druski said the turning point in his career was being asked to appear in Drake's 2020 music video for "Laugh Now Cry Later". Before it, he said, brands mostly sent him free merchandise. Afterwards they took him seriously.

He named Lil Durk, Marshawn Lynch and Kevin Durant among the established names he appeared alongside. He said that appearing beside [Lil Durk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lil_Durk) and [Marshawn Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshawn_Lynch) gave him wider visibility, and that brands began to see him "as an actual star".

That shift is visible in his current projects. He now has a Netflix deal, and readers can follow the latest on the series in our post on [Druski's Netflix series Collect & Praise](https://moduscarry.com/druski-collect-and-praise/).
