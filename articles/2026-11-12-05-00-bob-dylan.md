**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) met Pope Leo in recent days, according to Premier Christianity.
- The Vatican culture ministry official who arranged the meeting said artists such as Dylan "have antennae" for the world.
- On 9 November in Ljubljana, Dylan played an 18-song set that kept Love and Theft rarities in rotation, reports Cult Following.
- Sugar Baby returned on 7 November in Italy for the first time since 2012.
- The Lights of Autumn tour finishes on 8 December, after five nights at London's Royal Festival Hall and a Sheffield date.

Bob Dylan has had a busy week away from the usual script. The songwriter met Pope Leo, and he has since been back on stage in Slovenia, digging up songs most fans had not heard live in years. Both stories say something about where Dylan is in his career: still curious, still hard to predict.

## A meeting with Pope Leo

Premier Christianity reported that Dylan's recent meeting with the pontiff drew attention, partly because of what he chose to wear. The magazine noted that Dylan has met and even performed for earlier popes. It also pointed out that he wrote a song called "I Dreamed I Saw Augustine", a nod to the saint behind the order Pope Leo belongs to.

The warmth appears to run both ways. [Antonio Spadaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Spadaro), the Vatican culture ministry official who arranged the encounter, said artists like Dylan can be prophetic and sense changes in the world faster than politicians. "The Pope listens and asks questions," he said.

## Dylan's long road through faith

The same Premier Christianity piece, written by Derek Walker, traces how Dylan's beliefs have shifted. Born Robert Zimmerman to Jewish parents in Minnesota, he was shaped early by folk heroes [Woody Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Guthrie) and [Pete Seeger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Seeger). His early protest songs included "With God on Our Side", which questioned religious nationalism.

The turning point came around 1978 and 1979. After a gruelling world tour and the end of his first marriage, Dylan said a small silver cross thrown on stage in San Diego stayed in his pocket. He later described feeling a presence in the room. He studied the Bible, and the result was [Slow Train Coming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_Train_Coming), with [Mark Knopfler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Knopfler) on guitar and the [Muscle Shoals Horns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_Shoals_Horns). Many fans and critics reacted harshly, and Walker compares the shock to Dylan going electric at Newport in 1965.

## Surprises on the Lights of Autumn tour

The tour has been just as unpredictable. Cult Following reported that Dylan's 9 November show at Arena Stozice in Ljubljana ran to 18 songs and again shuffled the list. Early Roman Kings appears to have taken the place of False Prophet, which Dylan played at every show from 2021 to 2025.

The deep cuts keep coming:

- Sugar Baby was played on 7 November in Casalecchio di Reno, Italy, its first outing since 2012, and stayed in the Ljubljana set.
- High Water (For Charley Patton) and Million Miles have also returned.
- Million Miles had been played only about 70 times before this tour but is now a regular towards the end of the night.
- Love Sick, Tryin' to Get to Heaven and Not Dark Yet from Time Out of Mind still anchor the evening.

The closer alternates between I Shall Be Released and Every Grain of Sand, with the first chosen for the last two shows. For more on the song's return, see our earlier piece, [Bob Dylan Revives 'Sugar Baby' and Opens the 1976 Vaults](https://moduscarry.com/bob-dylan-sugar-baby-rolling-thunder/).

## What happens next

Dylan plays two nights in Vienna, then Salzburg on 14 November, followed by four dates in Germany. He then moves to England for the final 10 shows, including five at London's Royal Festival Hall and one at Sheffield's Utilita Arena. The tour ends on 8 December.
