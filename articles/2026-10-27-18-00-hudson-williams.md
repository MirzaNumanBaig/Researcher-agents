[Hudson Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Williams), best known for [Heated Rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heated_Rivalry), has a new genre role. He appears in Yaga, an eight-episode folk horror mystery from AMC+ that, according to a recent Comic Book Resources report, was scheduled to air on 23 October.

## What Yaga is about

The series was created by [Kat Sandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat_Sandler), who adapted it from her play of the same name. Per the official synopsis quoted by Comic Book Resources, a private detective named Rapp, played by [Noah Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Reid), arrives in a small coastal town to look into the disappearance of Henry Park. Park is the young heir to a local fishery and, the outlet says, is believed to have ties to ancient magic. Williams plays Park.

Rapp does not get a warm welcome. He clashes with a wary local detective, Carson, played by [Clark Backo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Backo), and runs into a university professor called Katherine, played by [Carrie-Anne Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie-Anne_Moss). The synopsis describes her as a woman with a taste for younger men. The town is named Whittock.

Comic Book Resources compares the setup to [Twin Peaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Peaks): a small town, sudden disappearances and an outsider investigator working through a maze of odd suspects. It adds that Yaga turns that template into something far darker.

## A Slavic myth, reworked

The title points to Baba Yaga, a figure from Slavic folklore. Comic Book Resources describes her as a man-eating demon who guards the border between life and death, and notes the character dates back as far as 1755. In the legend she tests those who enter her forest and spares the pure of heart.

Fans should not expect a literal witch in a hut on chicken legs. The outlet says early reviews suggest the character is metaphorical and carried by Katherine instead. It also reports that the trailer promises blood, gore and folkloric scares across the eight episodes, and compares the visual style to Midsommar and The Ritual.

## The themes behind the scares

Sandler keeps the myth's core ideas, according to the same report. Those include the power of nature, with the witch standing in for Mother Earth, and the ethics of how she treats people she judges cruel, rude or greedy. The tagline, "Women - we're angry about everything", signals where the series is heading.

Comic Book Resources says Yaga uses female rage to explore wealth, male entitlement and the way society treats older women. Katherine is not a one-note hero, the outlet adds, and some of her actions are questionable. It says Sandler has described that as deliberate.
