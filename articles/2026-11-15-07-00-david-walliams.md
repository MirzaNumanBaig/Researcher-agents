**Quick summary**

- Richard Osman said on his podcast that men who worked in 1990s and 2000s TV should feel "some sense of shame" over the David Walliams allegations, according to The Independent.
- Comedian Lucy Beaumont says she was one of the whistleblowers who contacted The Guardian, and now backs a campaign to change UK consent laws.
- The allegations come from a Guardian and ITV News investigation. Walliams denies any wrongdoing and says he is "not a sexual predator".
- The Spectator reports HarperCollins stopped selling his back catalogue in September 2026, and The Bookseller has called the moment one to "consider everything".

Two well-known comedy figures have publicly responded to the [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) allegations this week, and both are asking the same question: how did the television industry miss it, and what should change? The comments from [Richard Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Osman) and comedian Lucy Beaumont add to a story that has already reached the police, publishers and government. Walliams denies wrongdoing.

For earlier coverage, see our [David Walliams tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/david-walliams/) and the report on the [Met Police investigation](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-police-investigation-met/).

## What the investigation alleges

The Independent reports that Walliams was accused of sexual misconduct involving girls as young as 15, following reporting by The Guardian and ITV News. Multiple women accused him of "grooming" them as teenagers. One woman described an alleged encounter at 15 as a "sexual assault".

Walliams, 55, tried to obtain an injunction to stop the reporting, The Independent says. He has denied the allegations. In a letter understood to have been sent to the journalists months earlier, he wrote that he sometimes "behaved thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual sexual relationships with, and that he regrets it. He also wrote: "I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law."

## Richard Osman: a "sin of omission"

Osman, who first worked with Walliams in 1999 when the latter still went by David Williams, discussed the case on The Rest is Entertainment with co-host [Marina Hyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Hyde). He told listeners that the pair worked on a computer game show and then on a pilot with [Simon Pegg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Pegg), and that they fell out soon afterwards.

He said it was hard for any man who worked in TV in those decades "to not feel blind" or to avoid "some sense of shame". He called it "a sin of omission" rather than commission, and described 1990s "laddy culture" as a "weird aberration".

Osman and Walliams later co-created the Channel 4 sitcom Boyz Unlimited with [Matt Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lucas). It was cancelled after one series.

Osman also offered a practical fix. He proposed a mandatory, anonymisable exit interview with HR on every production, with patterns of complaints shared across companies and channels. "You can't force anyone to go to the police," he said, but producers can decide not to employ someone.

## Lucy Beaumont: "I was one of the whistleblowers"

Beaumont, 43, told Sky News on The Cathy Newman Show that she had never worked with Walliams or seen inappropriate behaviour from him. She said a young colleague, on their first TV job, told her about being asked to take girls over 16 to his dressing room. She said she told The Guardian, and that she did not know whether the allegations were true. These are her claims and they are not independently established.

She has since backed the Close The Consent Gap campaign. Under UK law, adults can legally have sex with 16- and 17-year-olds, and she called that "insane". Alan Carr and Vicky Pattison both responded supportively to her Instagram post.

## Publishers under scrutiny

The Spectator reports that HarperCollins opened an internal inquiry in 2023, which ended in 2024 without consequences for Walliams, according to The Guardian. Walliams's spokesperson says he was never told of it or given a chance to respond. The publisher said in December 2025 it would release no new titles, and stopped selling his back catalogue in September 2026.

The Bookseller's editor's letter says the episode is a moment to reconsider the whole trade. The trade title has also covered the Met Police investigation and retailer Waterstones returning its remaining stock. Our own report covers the [NDA row and calls for legal change](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-nda-rules/).

## What happens next

The Met Police investigation is continuing, and no charges have been reported. Culture Secretary [Lisa Nandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Nandy) is due to meet industry leaders on safeguarding, according to The Bookseller's coverage. Osman's call for HR exit interviews has not yet been taken up by any broadcaster.
