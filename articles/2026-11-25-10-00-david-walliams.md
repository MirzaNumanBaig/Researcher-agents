**Quick summary**

- A joint Guardian and ITV News investigation reports that HarperCollins staff raised concerns about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) for years.
- The publisher commissioned a law firm inquiry in 2023, which ended in 2024 with no action against him, according to the reporting.
- At least five women spoke to the inquiry. Walliams was not interviewed, according to the investigation.
- One woman reportedly signed a non-disclosure agreement and received a five-figure payment.
- The allegations are unproven, and the report summarised here does not include a response from Walliams.

The way HarperCollins dealt with complaints about David Walliams is now under close scrutiny. A joint investigation by The Guardian and ITV News, summarised on 24 November 2026 by the entertainment site Pajiba, says staff raised concerns about the author's conduct for years before the publisher acted. These are allegations, and none has been tested in a court.

## What the investigation reports

According to Pajiba's account of the investigation, women who worked at HarperCollins complained to management over a long period about alleged inappropriate behaviour. The claims include relationships with interns and an approach to a teenage girl at a book signing. Several sources said the concerns were not acted on for nearly a decade.

The women who spoke to the journalists did so anonymously, fearing professional consequences. They described feeling "isolated", "overwhelmed" and "shut down". Some said they were told they could stay away from the office when Walliams was there.

One woman who raised concerns with managers in 2015 said nothing happened because he was central to the company hitting its yearly targets. Another said he would have been removed had he not been making the company money.

## The 2023 inquiry and the NDA

The report says HarperCollins only took formal action in 2023, when it brought in the City law firm Allen & Overy. A headline in the legal trade title The Lawyer also describes the probe as led by legacy A&O. At least five women spoke to the inquiry, some with first-hand experience of the alleged conduct. Walliams was never interviewed.

The inquiry ended in 2024 and no action was taken against him. One former employee quoted in the report called it "a cover-up", and several said they believed it was a way to close down complaints. One woman who signed an NDA received a five-figure payment, the investigation says.

HarperCollins then kept publishing his books for close to two years after the inquiry. Pajiba reports that the publisher later dropped him after a new chief executive arrived, and that it stopped publishing his back catalogue. That was notable because he had long been one of its biggest sellers.

## Wider industry reaction

[Philip Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Jones_(producer)), editor of The Bookseller, was quoted calling it a "#MeToo moment" for the industry and urging the whole sector to stop ignoring the problem.

The story has also reached broadcasting. A social media post carrying the headline of a Channel 4 News item says its boss, [Esme Wren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esme_Wren), emailed staff about the use of NDAs after the [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) and Walliams controversies. ## What happens next

The investigation raises questions about how much HarperCollins knew, and when. Whether the publisher releases a fuller account of the 2023 inquiry is not stated in the reporting we could read. Calls for industry-wide change are likely to continue, and further responses from the publisher or from Walliams would be the next developments to watch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who led the HarperCollins inquiry into David Walliams?

The City law firm Allen & Overy ran the inquiry, which began in 2023 and ended in 2024, according to the investigation as summarised by Pajiba.

### Was David Walliams interviewed?

No. The report says at least five women spoke to the inquiry, but Walliams was never interviewed.

### Has HarperCollins dropped David Walliams?

Yes, according to the report. It says the publisher dropped him after a new chief executive arrived and no longer publishes his back catalogue.

### Are the allegations proven?

No. They are claims reported by anonymous sources, and no finding has been made against him in the reporting we read.
