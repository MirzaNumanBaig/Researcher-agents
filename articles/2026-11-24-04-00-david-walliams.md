**Quick summary**

- More than 500 actors, directors, writers and producers have signed an open letter calling for an end to a "culture of open secrets" in the UK entertainment industry, according to Deadline.
- The letter follows the sentencing of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) and the naming of [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) in allegations reported by The Guardian and ITV News.
- Walliams denies the allegations and says he has "not broken the law".
- London's Metropolitan Police are investigating separate allegations about a man in his 50s, and are also reviewing the Guardian and ITV material.

A group of more than 500 British and international creatives has signed an open letter urging the entertainment industry to confront what it calls a "culture of open secrets", days after allegations about comedian and author David Walliams became public. Deadline reported the letter's progress on 23 November. Walliams denies the allegations.

## What the open letter says

Titled "Let's make this a turning point", the letter describes itself as an open letter to the creative industry, from the creative industry. [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/uk-stars-open-letter-brand-walliams-dormer-armitage-1237224955/) reported that it first had nearly 400 signatories, and that the number passed 500 after [Susan Sarandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sarandon) became the first major American star to sign.

Early British signatories include [Natalie Dormer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Dormer), [Richard Armitage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Armitage_(actor)) and [Susan Wokoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Wokoma), along with Les Dennis and Jo Joyner.

The letter asks broadcasters, studios, independent producers, organisations and unions to review their processes and protect people who speak out. It argues that Brand's sentencing alone should not count as a turning point, and it warns the industry against treating Brand and Walliams as "one-offs". It also asks the government to "act, not debate".

Deadline said the letter was organised by writer and producer Ben Fletcher and actor and campaigner Alexa Morden, who hosts The 98% podcast. Morden said she hoped it would make the systemic issues "impossible to ignore".

## Where the allegations stand

According to Deadline, Brand was sentenced to 10 and a half years in prison the Monday before. A day later, Walliams was named as the man at the centre of allegations of sexually exploitative behaviour towards young women and teenagers. Some of the alleged behaviour is claimed to have happened around ITV's Britain's Got Talent.

Reuters, via [Interaksyon](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/trends-spotlights/2026/11/23/321995/uk-police-investigate-allegations-against-best-selling-childrens-author-walliams/), reported that The Guardian and ITV News said multiple women had accused Walliams, 55, of abusing his position. An injunction that had blocked publication was lifted on the Tuesday. Walliams has denied the allegations.

In a letter to the journalists, quoted by Reuters, he wrote that at times he had "behaved thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual sexual relationships with. He added that a power imbalance is inevitable when one person is famous, but that abusing that power is something he "never did".

## The police investigation

The Metropolitan Police said it had received a number of allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s. Police said these were separate from those reported by ITN and The Guardian. They did not name Walliams, as is customary, but said detectives were also looking at the Guardian and ITV material for evidence of offences.

Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, who leads the investigation, encouraged anyone who had been a victim of a sexual offence to come forward, no matter when it happened. [Metro](https://metro.co.uk/2026/11/23/david-walliams-unearthed-diary-reveals-shame-behaviour-29811913/) reported that the Met confirmed on 13 November that the complaints were non-recent.

Metro also reported that The Mirror had shared passages from Walliams' 2012 book Camp David, which included diary entries from the late 1990s and early 2000s. In them he wrote about shame over past encounters. The entries predate the allegations and are not evidence of them.

## Business fallout

Closer reported that Alton Towers closed its World of David Walliams attraction, that his literary agency dropped him, and that his books were pulled from some shops, libraries and schools. Reuters said HarperCollins UK ended its relationship with him in December 2025 after the Daily Telegraph reported on an internal inquiry into harassment allegations. A spokesperson for Walliams told the BBC then that he strongly denied behaving inappropriately. ## What happens next

The police investigation continues, and no charges have been reported. Deadline said Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy was convening industry leaders, including BBC boss Matt Brittin, to discuss the issue. The open letter's signature list is still growing. ## Frequently asked questions

### Who signed the open letter?
More than 500 actors, directors, writers and producers, according to Deadline. Named signatories include Natalie Dormer, Richard Armitage, Susan Wokoma and Susan Sarandon.

### Has David Walliams responded?
Yes. He denies the allegations. He has said he is "not a sexual predator" and has "not broken the law", as quoted by Metro and Reuters.

### Is David Walliams being investigated by police?
London police are investigating separate non-recent allegations about a man in his 50s and are reviewing the Guardian and ITV material. They did not name Walliams. No charges have been reported.
