Joseph Zada Woody Harrelson homework is the talk of the *Hunger Games* press run: the Sydney-born actor says he spent a month doing little else but watching Harrelson's films before taking on the role Harrelson made famous. He shared the detail with The Hollywood Reporter at Monday's New York premiere of *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, where he plays the younger Haymitch Abernathy.

## Joseph Zada, Woody Harrelson and a month of homework

Harrelson played Haymitch in the original films. Zada plays him at 16, during the 50th Hunger Games. According to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/joseph-zada-woody-harrelson-hunger-games-premiere-1236757437/), he called the Harrelson study a "base layer" and said that "all that I did" for a month was look at everything Harrelson has ever done.

He was clear that imitation was not the aim. "Past that, it was just up to me to bring myself to it," he told the outlet, adding that what the screen wants is "the true light of yourself". Variety Australia reports a similar line from his Rolling Stone AU/NZ interview, where he said the goal was not to copy Harrelson but to keep some of the older actor's energy while finding his own take.

## A heavy film to leave on set

The Hollywood Reporter describes the story, the fifth novel in Suzanne Collins' series, as the darkest and most brutal of the books. Co-star Maya Hawke said at the premiere that the story is "gut-wrenching" rather than hopeful.

Asked how the cast shook the material off after filming, Zada said he did not try. He wanted to give the film "every single inch" of himself, and said it paid off. [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)), whose character has a romance with Haymitch before the Games, said the two tried to keep things lighter between takes to ease the strain.

The premiere itself drew [Jennifer Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lawrence), [Josh Hutcherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hutcherson), Harrelson and [Elizabeth Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Banks) from the original run. [Rachel Zegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Zegler) and [Tom Blyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Blyth) of *The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes* attended too, along with [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace), [Elle Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Fanning), [Glenn Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Close), [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes), [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons), Kieran Culkin and Hawke.

## The Australian angle

Variety Australia notes that Zada, 21, was born Joseph Cumpston and took the surname Zada from his grandmother, who had Afghan and Aboriginal heritage. The Aboriginal line traces to his paternal great-grandmother and connects him to the Barkandji people of western New South Wales. He told the outlet that staying connected to that background is "a really important part of my identity" and "grounding".

Before this film, his credits included *Invisible Boys* and Prime Video's *We Were Liars*, per Variety Australia. The film is directed by Francis Lawrence, who has made the series' films since *Catching Fire*, according to 25YearsLaterSite, which cites CBR for that detail.

## What the early reactions say

Reactions posted after the London premiere were collected by Parade, as relayed by 25YearsLaterSite. Nikita Francois of The Nerds of Color called Zada "electrifying", and Griffin Schiller called him "a revelation". These were social media posts, not full reviews, and premiere-night praise tends to run warm. The Hollywood Reporter has published its own review, which it headlines as crediting Zada with a "star-making turn". We have not read its full text, so we are not quoting it further.

## What happens next

The film arrives in Australian cinemas on 19 November and in US theatres on Friday 20 November, with the same sources noting select early screenings from 18 November.
