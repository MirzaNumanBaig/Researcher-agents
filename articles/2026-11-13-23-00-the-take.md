**Quick summary**

- Netflix's heist thriller The Take is scheduled for release on 15 January 2027.
- [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson) plays Noah, a former Army captain, opposite [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington) as master thief Christian Carter.
- [Fernando Meirelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Meirelles), the director of City of God, is behind the camera.
- Australian outlet Boss Hunting says the first look has fed online talk about Pattinson and James Bond. That is speculation, not an announcement.

The Take, Netflix's upcoming heist thriller, arrives on 15 January 2027, and its first look has revived a familiar question: could its star be the next James Bond? [Boss Hunting](https://www.bosshunting.com.au/entertainment/movies/robert-pattinson-the-take-netflix) argues the film reads like an audition tape for Pattinson, though nobody involved has said so.

## What is The Take about?

The Take follows Noah, played by Pattinson, a former Army captain. Washington's character, a master thief named Christian Carter, recruits him for a job that sounds simple on paper: robbing the richest bank in Manhattan.

There is a catch. Noah's girlfriend, Rose, works at that bank as a teller. She is played by [Daisy Edgar-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Edgar-Jones), so one side of the plan puts the person he loves directly in the path of the robbery.

According to Boss Hunting, the first look points to a nonlinear, puzzle-like structure. Much of the story is told through Noah's point of view, which means viewers know only what he knows. That sets up a film built on double-crosses and shifting loyalties rather than a straight run through the heist.

## Who is in the cast and who directs?

Meirelles is best known for City of God, and he directs here. Beyond the three leads, the film's Wikipedia entry lists Danai Gurira, Sean Harris and Moisés Arias in the cast. Wikipedia gives the release date as 15 January 2027, which matches the Boss Hunting report.

Meirelles told Netflix's Tudum site that Pattinson is dependable on set, according to Boss Hunting. His words, as quoted there: "He's never late; he's always available. If I need to do 20 takes, he's there."

For more on the film's earlier coverage, see our posts on the [first look at Pattinson and Washington](https://moduscarry.com/the-take-first-look-pattinson-washington/) and the [heist film overview](https://moduscarry.com/the-take-netflix-heist-film/).

## Why the Bond talk keeps coming back

The James Bond role has been open since Daniel Craig's last outing in No Time to Die, which Boss Hunting says came more than five years ago. The outlet notes that names such as Josh O'Connor, [Jack Lowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lowden) and [Callum Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callum_Turner) have been tossed around in the latest round of speculation.

Boss Hunting's writer adds Pattinson to that conversation. The piece points to a moment in the trailer where the actor walks out of a vault, which it reads as a nod to Bond's opening title sequences. The writer also admits it may not be planned.

There is a caveat. Pattinson plays an American in The Take and uses an American accent, a detail Boss Hunting praises. The outlet's argument is that the film shows he can carry a major thriller opposite a star of Washington's standing. That is an opinion piece's reading of a first look, not a casting report. Treat it as talk until the studio behind the franchise says otherwise.

## What happens next

The Take is due on Netflix on 15 January 2027. More footage and interviews are likely before then, and Meirelles has already spoken to Netflix's Tudum about the shoot. Any news on a new Bond will come from the franchise's owners, not from a streaming thriller.
