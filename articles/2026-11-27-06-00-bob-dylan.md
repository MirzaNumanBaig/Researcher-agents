**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) opened the UK leg of his Lights of Autumn tour at the Bournemouth International Centre on 25 November.
- The 85-year-old played in a black hoodie, said nothing to the crowd and mostly skipped his famous 1960s albums, according to The Telegraph.
- The Guardian said his voice is stronger than it was four decades ago; UNCUT called the show "spellbinding and immortal".
- The Telegraph reports the tour runs until 8 December, with Birmingham next.

The Bob Dylan UK tour began at the Bournemouth International Centre this week, and the first reviews agree on two things: the show was quiet, and his voice was better than expected. The 85-year-old kicked off the Lights of Autumn tour with no chat and no greatest-hits sweep, reports from [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/nov/26/bob-dylan-review-bournemouth-international-centre-uk-tour), UNCUT and The Telegraph show.

## A voice in better shape than expected

The Guardian's reviewer called the strength of Dylan's voice "the unexpected surprise of the evening". The paper said he sounded clearer than when the writer first heard him four decades ago, his vocals a raw contrast to a smooth band.

The Guardian said he led the group from behind his piano, hoodie up, through a show with no gaps, using small cues rather than words. The Telegraph, whose writer sat in the front row, put the running time at about 90 minutes. Reviews differ on that point.

UNCUT said Dylan has worn the hood so consistently, including on a recent visit to the Vatican, that it now reads as part of who he is. Its critic found the anonymity natural rather than awkward.

## A set that avoids the obvious

Anyone hoping for the hits was likely to leave disappointed. The Telegraph wrote that the tour centres on Dylan's own later catalogue and the songs around it, and that fans who come for the familiar material will not get it.

According to UNCUT, the evening opened with "Watching the River Flow" and moved through "Love Sick", "Tryin' to Get to Heaven", "High Water (For Charley Patton)" and "Black Rider". "It Ain't Me, Babe" was the only 1960s hit, and the review said it was reshaped with the looseness of an early 1970s rock session.

The Guardian said a subdued jazz version of "Tangled Up in Blue" typified his habit of wrapping old songs in new arrangements. UNCUT reported that the song has been given new verses on this tour that acknowledge time passing.

## The band behind him

UNCUT singled out drummer Anton Fig and guitarist Julian Lage, a jazz player Dylan has used whenever possible since the summer. It added that the band lost both of its long-serving guitarists during a turbulent year but now sounds tight, with bassist Tony Garnier a constant. The Telegraph described the group as excellent, drifting between jazz, country and blues.

The Telegraph said the evening closed with "I Shall Be Released", and that Dylan stepped forward at the end and stared at the crowd for a few seconds before walking off. The Guardian called the closer a gift to listeners who wanted something familiar to hum on the way home.

## Dylan on his own back catalogue

The tour arrives as Dylan's old work is in the news for other reasons. Far Out Magazine highlighted a past remark in which he said he never really liked The Basement Tapes, recalling that the songs were made for his publishing company and that Columbia wanted to release them. American Songwriter also ran a list of songs he wrote for others, including "Wanted Man" for [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash), "Farewell, Angelina" for [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez) and "If Not for You" for [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison).

See also our coverage of [The Last Waltz at 50](https://moduscarry.com/bob-dylan-the-last-waltz/) and the [Bob Dylan tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/bob-dylan/).

## What happens next

According to The Telegraph, Dylan played Birmingham the night after Bournemouth and will tour the UK until 8 December. Both The Guardian and UNCUT frame the shows as another phase of his long-running Never Ending Tour.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Bob Dylan's UK tour start?
The UK leg opened at the Bournemouth International Centre on 25 November, according to reviews published on 26 November.

### Does Bob Dylan play his greatest hits on this tour?
Mostly not. The Telegraph says the set focuses on later and lesser-played material, with "It Ain't Me, Babe" the one 1960s hit, according to UNCUT.

### How did critics rate Dylan's voice?
The Guardian said it was stronger and clearer than 40 years ago. UNCUT said he sang with strength and truth.
