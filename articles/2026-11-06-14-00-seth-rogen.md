**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) says he auditioned for HBO's *Band of Brothers* in 2000 and knew at once he would not get the part.
- He told the story on The A24 Podcast with [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde), recorded to promote their film *The Invite*.
- By his own account he arrived in a Stussy T-shirt, cut cargo shorts and Airwalks, while about 35 other actors wore full military uniforms.
- He also failed to win a small part in *8 Mile* in 2002, according to Far Out Magazine.

Seth Rogen has told a story against himself that most actors would keep quiet. During a 2026 appearance on The A24 Podcast, the Emmy-winning star of *The Studio* described how he turned up to audition for *Band of Brothers* in 2000 dressed for a day at the skate park, and realised the moment he opened the door that the role was gone, [Far Out Magazine reports](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/2000-seth-rogen-auditioned-for-band-of-brothers/).

## The room full of uniforms

Rogen told host Olivia Wilde that he went to every audition in the same outfit, whatever the role, because he did not own many clothes. For *Band of Brothers*, the war miniseries from [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg) and [Tom Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hanks), that outfit was a Stussy T-shirt, cut cargo shorts and Airwalks.

His description of the moment is the best part. He said he opened the door to find roughly 35 men in head-to-toe military uniforms. His own verdict, as quoted by Far Out: "I don't think I'm going to get this one." He did not.

It had never occurred to him, he said, to dress for the part. He also recalled that at auditions the front desk would often ask whether he was dropping off or picking up, because staff took him for a courier.

## A pattern, not a one-off

Rogen was 17 when he landed the role of Ken Miller on NBC's *Freaks and Geeks* in 1999. The series ran one season, but it became a critically admired cult hit, and Far Out notes that its cast was celebrated afterwards. That early success, the magazine suggests, gave him the confidence to try for almost anything.

On the podcast he admitted he was poor at the audition process in general. He also recalled trying out for Cheddar Bob in the 2002 film [8 Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_Mile_(film)), a role that went to [Evan Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Jones_(actor)). So the *Band of Brothers* miss came two years before another near-miss in a very different type of film.

## What the show did for others

*Band of Brothers* followed a parachute infantry regiment through the Second World War, and Far Out describes it as a huge hit for HBO. It was also a launchpad for several careers, including those of [Damian Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damian_Lewis), [Ron Livingston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Livingston) and [Scott Grimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Grimes).

Rogen went another way. He became one of the leading faces of 2000s comedy through films such as [Knocked Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up) and *Pineapple Express*. In 2022 he finally worked with Spielberg, appearing in *The Fabelmans*. By then, as Far Out puts it, he was famous enough to wear whatever he liked to work.
