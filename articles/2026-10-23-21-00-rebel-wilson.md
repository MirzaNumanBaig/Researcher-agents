**Quick summary**

- The NSW Supreme Court on Friday 23 October 2026 dismissed a claim against [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) by the producers of her film The Deb, awarding them $10 in damages.
- Justice Elisabeth Peden awarded Wilson $359,000 plus interest, according to The Sydney Morning Herald and the ABC.
- It is Wilson's second win this year, after she was cleared of defaming the film's star, Charlotte MacInnes.
- MacInnes has appealed, and a separate defamation case brought by the producers is still before a court in California.

Rebel Wilson has won again in court over The Deb. On Friday the NSW Supreme Court found against the UK production company that sued her for $15 million, awarding the group a token $10 and ordering it to pay Wilson $359,000 in unpaid producer fees plus her legal costs, the ABC reports.

## What the court decided

Justice Elisabeth Peden gave a short decision, with a written judgement to follow. According to the ABC, the company AI Film had argued that false statements Wilson made on social media, and a fake takedown website, left The Deb unprofitable. The judge dismissed the case, finding that although Wilson did ask for a "malicious" website to be created, there was no proof it caused AI Film any financial loss.

The $10 was nominal damages to a subsidiary, AI Films, for technical breaches of its social media contract. The $359,000 went to Wilson's company, Camp Sugar. The ABC quotes the judgement: "I am not satisfied AI Film … have demonstrated a causal connection between the misleading or deceptive representations and any loss or damage."

The judge also found that none of the people named on the website were AI Film producers. That detail matters, because the website made claims about another producer, Amanda Ghost.

## The money behind The Deb

The numbers explain why the fight got so heated. The Sydney Morning Herald reports The Deb was made on a $22 million budget and took just $335,000 at the local box office. It has not yet screened outside Oceania.

The producers, [Amanda Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Ghost), [Gregor Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Cameron) and Vince Holden, alleged that the shortfall could be traced to Wilson's online statements about them. The Herald says they also alleged she tried to devalue the film so she could buy it from them. Wilson denied this and countersued.

On the other side, the ABC reports that the judge found the UK producers had added $900,000 in extra fees for themselves to the film's budget without telling Wilson. The court also heard emails from October 2025 in which international distributors raised concerns that the film felt "small and distinctly Australian".

## Wilson's response

Wilson marked the result on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/rebelwilson/) with a photo of herself in a University of NSW graduation gown holding her law degree. "This girl never knew how much she was going to need that law degree!!" she wrote.

She added that she spoke up because she believed there were serious concerns on the film, and that defending herself had come at "an enormous personal, emotional, and financial cost". She said she was proud she had stood her ground "for myself and for others I was trying to protect", according to the Herald.

## How this follows the MacInnes case

Earlier this year the Federal Court cleared Wilson of defaming Charlotte MacInnes, The Deb's lead actor. MacInnes had claimed four social media posts implied she was a liar who walked back a sexual harassment complaint to help her career. The Herald says MacInnes denied making or retracting a complaint.

The ABC reports that Justice Peden reached the same conclusion on that point as the defamation judgement did: that MacInnes had made a complaint and later recanted it. MacInnes has lodged an appeal, so that question isn't settled for good.

## What happens next

Three things are still open. The written judgement from Justice Peden is yet to come. MacInnes's appeal against her defamation loss is pending. And the producers are suing Wilson for defamation in California, the ABC says.

In that US case, a filing reported by Deadline and republished by The News International asked to move a 19 January trial to 22 March 2027, because Wilson's lead lawyer, Allyson K Thompson, has been diagnosed with breast cancer and will be on medical leave from 30 November. The parties agreed in principle to the new date, which the court still has to approve.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much was Rebel Wilson awarded?
The NSW Supreme Court awarded Wilson $359,000 plus interest in unpaid producer fees, and ordered AI Film to pay her legal costs, according to the ABC and The Sydney Morning Herald.

### Why did the producers only get $10?
The ABC reports the $10 was nominal damages for technical breaches of a social media contract. Justice Peden found AI Film could not prove its claimed losses were caused by Wilson's statements.

### Is the legal fight over The Deb finished?
No. Charlotte MacInnes has appealed her defamation loss, and the producers' defamation case against Wilson in California is still going, with a trial date proposed for 22 March 2027.
