- [Hudson Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Williams) is one of the celebrity guests on season two of the CBC series The Assembly.
- The six-part season premieres on 1 November at 9 p.m. on CBC TV and CBC Gem, according to Yahoo News Canada.

[Heated Rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heated_Rivalry) star Hudson Williams will sit in the interview chair when The Assembly returns for a second season on 1 November, according to [Yahoo News Canada](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/assembly-returns-season-hudson-mack-180027484.html). The Vancouver-filmed CBC series will air at 9 p.m. on CBC TV and CBC Gem, with extended producer's cuts on Gem the same day.

## What The Assembly is

The show removes the usual protections of a celebrity interview. There are no advance questions, no publicists hovering nearby and no promotional talking point to steer toward. Instead, a group of autistic and neurodivergent interviewers ask what they genuinely want to know.

Up to 40 interviewers take part, chosen from a pool of more than 70. Each submits three to five questions. Producers Sean De Vries and Stephen Sawchuk review them mainly to avoid repetition, not to soften them, and every participant is guaranteed at least one question, Yahoo News Canada reports.

Guests are unpaid, commit to a three-hour shoot and arrive without having seen the questions. De Vries told the outlet that casting the celebrity remains the biggest challenge.

## The season two guest list

Alongside Williams, the six-part season features Galifianakis, Olympic hockey champion Poulin, Miss Universe Canada and North of North actor Callingbull, and singer-songwriter Lights.

The article does not say what Williams discussed on camera, so viewers will have to wait for the broadcast. What other guests have said gives a hint of the tone. Baruchel, whose credits include This Is the End, BlackBerry and How to Train Your Dragon, admitted he was "scared" before the shoot but came away valuing it. He spoke on the show about his father's addiction, and called the experience a "good natured, friendly, loving deposition."

Lights said the appeal was the unscripted exchange, describing a mix of silly, simple and deeply emotional questions with no build to a climax. She was asked about cutting off her long hair and about her bisexuality, according to the report.

## Why the format matters

De Vries said the openness is not a gimmick. In his view, when people feel someone genuinely cares about them, they tend to become more authentic and vulnerable. His test for any question is whether he would want to see anyone, anywhere, answer it.

The approach took time to build. Producer Small Army Entertainment spent six to eight months developing the Canadian version and building relationships with community organisations. It previously made two seasons of Push, a series about people with physical disabilities.

After season one, CBC told the producers it had received an unusually large volume of positive viewer messages, De Vries said. Some of the most meaningful responses, he noted, came from participants and caregivers who felt the community had been portrayed truthfully.
