**Quick summary**

- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) has reportedly applied for two jobs at HMP Littlehey, a cleaning role and a food preparation role, according to The Sun on Monday.
- The pay is said to be about £20 a week, roughly A$40. The figure comes from The Sun's source and has not been confirmed by the prison service.
- The same report says he spends his allowance on phone credit to call his family, plus eggs, dates and bananas.
- Brand is serving a sentence of 10 years and six months for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He denies wrongdoing.
- A separate investigation into a message he sent out from prison is still under way, according to the Prime Minister's office.

Russell Brand has reportedly asked for work inside the prison where he is serving a 10-and-a-half-year sentence, according to a report by The Sun that was picked up on Tuesday by Page Six, Yahoo and the NZ Herald. The comedian, 51, is said to have applied for a cleaning job and a food prep job at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire.

All of the detail below traces back to one British tabloid and an unnamed source. Treat it as reported, not confirmed.

## What The Sun says about the prison job

According to [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/24/celebrity-news/russell-brand-applies-for-menial-26-a-week-prison-job-as-his-spending-habits-behind-bars-are-revealed/), a job at the low-security prison would pay about £20 a week. That converts to roughly US$26, or around A$40. The NZ Herald, also citing The Sun, quoted a higher figure in New Zealand dollars, so the sums differ between outlets and the pound figure is the one to rely on.

The source told The Sun that Brand "wants to get a job in the prison", because earning would raise the amount he can spend. That is a claim about his motives made by an anonymous person, and none of the outlets has verified it independently.

## How he reportedly spends his allowance

[Yahoo's report](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/russell-brand-applies-26-week-193329129.html), which first ran on InTouch Weekly, says most of Brand's weekly money goes on phone credit. The source said he puts speaking to his family ahead of filling himself up.

The rest reportedly buys eggs, dates and bananas. The [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/world/russell-brand-applies-for-jobs-in-jail-new-details-of-life-in-prison-revealed/MXQAYX6PN5EVBAGSYEN34SBQCU/) adds that Brand, previously a vegan, is said to cook his eggs in a kettle in his cell.

Other details in the coverage, all attributed to The Sun:

- He has access to a landline phone.
- He listens to Radio 1 reports about himself.
- He takes part in group prayer sessions in the prison chapel with other prisoners.

## The conviction behind the headlines

Brand was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, relating to incidents between 2004 and 2009, according to Yahoo's summary of the case. He was sentenced to 10 years and six months. The NZ Herald reports he is expected to serve at least two-thirds of that before he can be considered for parole, and that he will be on the sex offenders register for life.

According to [The Cut](https://www.thecut.com/article/russell-brands-wife-is-sorry-for-sharing-his-message.html), the sentencing judge, Joel Bennathan, described Brand as a "sexual predator" with a "casual and arrogant disregard" for women. Brand has repeatedly denied the allegations. His wife, Laura, has stood by him and testified in his defence during the trial.

Our earlier coverage looked at how [Russell Brand could still earn money from prison](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-prison-income/), which sits oddly beside a report of a £20-a-week job application.

## The message that led to an investigation

The prison job story lands days after a separate controversy. Brand's wife read a handwritten note from him on their Christian podcast, Sunday Service, on 15 November. In it he said he was not guilty of the offences he was jailed for, according to The Cut.

A spokesperson for Prime Minister Andy Burnham told Reuters the message was "clearly appalling and is being urgently investigated", adding that prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. The Cut reports that Laura returned to the podcast the following Sunday and said she would "absolutely honor" the prison's rules. We covered that in more detail in [Russell Brand's wife vows to honour prison rules](https://moduscarry.com/laura-brand-prison-rules/).

Page Six also reports that six more women have come forward since the verdict, and that London police are looking at newer claims received during the trial. Those are allegations, and none has been tested in court.

## What happens next

Nothing has been announced about whether Brand has been given a prison job. The prison service has not commented in any of the coverage we read. The investigation into the podcast message is the more consequential open question, since it could affect the conditions of his sentence.
