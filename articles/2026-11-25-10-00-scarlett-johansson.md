**Quick summary**

- *Paper Tiger*, the crime drama starring Scarlett Johansson, debuted with a 72% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes, according to ScreenRant.
- Critics are warmer: the Tomatometer sits at 87%, a 15-point gap.
- The film took $616,551 at the domestic box office after a limited release on November 13, on a reported $15 million budget.
- Johansson's next release is Brad Bird's *Ray Gunn* on Netflix on December 18.

[Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson)'s new crime thriller *Paper Tiger* has opened to a mostly positive audience response. It holds a 72% audience score on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) ahead of the Thanksgiving week in the United States, [ScreenRant reports](https://screenrant.com/scarlett-johansson-paper-tiger-movie-rotten-tomatoes-audience-score/). That is a solid result, and it comes with a note of caution: the score is based on just over 50 verified ratings, so it may still move.

## How the audience score compares

The 72% is the highest audience rating for any film directed by [James Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gray_(director)), edging past his debut *Little Odessa*, which sits at 70%. Critics have been more generous. The Tomatometer is 87%, which leaves a 15-point difference between reviewers and ticket buyers.

ScreenRant says reviews since the film's Cannes premiere in May 2026 have been generally positive, and that the praise has gone to all three leads. [Adam Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Driver) is singled out as the standout for his intensity as Gary Pearl. [Miles Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Teller) completes the main trio. Reviewers also admired the cinematography and the production design of 1980s New York.

The complaints are consistent on both sides. Some critics found the pacing slow and the film less balanced than it could be, and reactions to the abrupt ending were mixed. According to ScreenRant, audiences echoed those points about pacing and story, yet still found the film riveting because of the performances and the emotional weight.

## What the film is about

*Paper Tiger* follows two brothers in the mid-1980s whose family runs into trouble with the Russian mafia. It reunites Johansson with Driver, her co-star in *Marriage Story*. Gray directed, wrote and produced it. ScreenRant describes it as a crime thriller that pairs its criminal plot with family stakes and a bleak look at the American Dream.

## The box office so far

The numbers are small but have a bright spot. *Paper Tiger* opened in limited release on November 13 and went wide the following week. It has taken $616,551 domestically so far, against a reported budget of $15 million.

The limited run set a personal record for Gray. In six theatres the film grossed $215,009 over three days, a per-screen average of $35,834. That beat the $27,543 average of *The Lost City of Z*, his previous best. ScreenRant expects the total to grow as the film reaches more cinemas during Thanksgiving week, though it faces competition from *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, *Hexed* and *Focker-in-Law*.

The film is also a modest awards contender. ScreenRant calls it a long shot for the big prizes, but it has two Gotham Independent Film Awards nominations: Best Feature, and Outstanding Supporting Performance for Driver.
