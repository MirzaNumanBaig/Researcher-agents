**Quick summary**

- A nationwide US flight ground stop left comedian Tom Papa stuck in Los Angeles, so John Oliver stepped in at the Michael J. Fox Foundation gala on Saturday, 14 November, according to The Hollywood Reporter.
- The Waldorf Astoria event in New York raised a record $6 million, the same outlet reported.
- Christopher Lloyd told People his friendship with Fox is a "lifetime love".
- Tracy Pollan said shared meals are the couple's favourite thing to do together.

A grounded flight handed John Oliver an unexpected night on stage at the [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) Foundation gala in New York on Saturday. The Last Week Tonight host replaced comedian [Tom Papa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Papa), who was stuck in Los Angeles after the Federal Aviation Administration halted flights across the US, according to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/john-oliver-tom-papa-michael-j-fox-gala-faa-ground-stop-1236756623/).

## How John Oliver ended up on the bill

The FAA's nationwide ground stop on Friday lasted four hours. By Saturday, air travel had resumed, but the knock-on effects were still being felt. Host [Denis Leary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Leary) told the crowd at the [Fox Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation) benefit that few of the booked stars had been affected. Papa was the exception. He had boarded his plane before the stop was ordered, The Hollywood Reporter said.

The foundation found a replacement in New York. Oliver was a surprise addition, and he opened by joking that he was a disappointment to a room that had already been told the scheduled comedian was not coming. Leary had also nudged the audience to think [Jon Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Stewart) might be the stand-in, since he pointed out that Oliver knows Stewart.

Fellow comedian Josh Johnson used the disruption in his own set, according to the same report. He also joked about a Microsoft outage that ran from Thursday midday into early Friday. The FAA has said the pause was caused by air traffic control communication problems.

## A record night for Parkinson's research

The gala, the foundation's 26th annual "A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to Cure Parkinson's" event, raised a record $6 million, The Hollywood Reporter reported. Former late-night hosts [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert) and [David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Letterman) made separate appearances and joked about their CBS shows coming to an end. Colbert introduced a video about scientists working on Parkinson's treatments.

The night closed with a tradition. Fox joined the headline musician onstage for a Back to the Future-inspired version of "Johnny B. Goode". This year that meant John Mayer and Brad Paisley, with No Doubt's Tom Dumont and Shoshana Bean. Mayer said the sound Fox made as Marty McFly had made him want to pick up a guitar as a child.

Fox then took the microphone. He joked that playing with that lineup was "definitely worth having Parkinson's for" and said he remained committed to finding a cure. ## Christopher Lloyd on a "lifetime love"

On the red carpet, Back to the Future co-star Christopher Lloyd, 88, spoke to People about his friendship with Fox, who is 65. "We kind of got together on Back to Future, the trilogy, and it's never really stopped," he said. He called it "kind of a lifetime love".

Fox marked Lloyd's birthday in October with an Instagram post that nodded to their 1985 hit, People noted. Lloyd replied in the comments: "We truly are timeless." Fox has said before that the pair connected most on Back to the Future Part III, where he came to see how much Lloyd loved acting, he told Variety at the 2023 Sundance Film Festival.

Friends also spoke about Fox's return to acting this year in Shrinking. Leary, who worked with him on Rescue Me, told The Hollywood Reporter he was thinking of writing a character for Fox in his next project. Spin City co-star Alan Ruck said treatment had improved a great deal since Fox stepped away from that show in 2000.

## Tracy Pollan on the simple things

Fox's wife, Tracy Pollan, attended with three of their four children, in what People called a rare family appearance. Asked about their favourite activity together, she said their tastes in books and television differ but they both love a great meal. Her advice for staying positive was to take it day by day.

The couple met on Family Ties, married on 16 July 1988 and have four children, People reported. Fox was diagnosed with early-onset Parkinson's in 1991, went public in 1998 and set up his foundation in 2000. Pollan said the gala celebrates everyone who has worked with the foundation for more than 20 years, and that the money goes directly to scientists.
