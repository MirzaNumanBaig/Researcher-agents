**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) received the Theatre Actress award at Harper's Bazaar's 2026 Women of the Year awards on Thursday evening.
- In her speech she said a "dangerous jackboot of misogyny" had weighed on women in theatre and might be beginning to lift.
- She urged the room not to relax, saying the alternative has to be expressed "radically and clearly".
- Elizabeth Debicki presented the award, recalling their 2013 production of The Maids at Sydney Theatre Company.

Cate Blanchett used her Women of the Year acceptance speech to describe what she sees as a slight shift in the climate for women in theatre, and to argue that nobody should treat it as a win yet. She collected the Theatre Actress award at Harper's Bazaar's ceremony on Thursday evening, according to [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/culture/a74364557/cate-blanchett-women-of-the-year-speech/).

## A serious message wrapped in jokes

Blanchett opened lightly. Asked about her bare feet, she said she is "obsessed with taking my shoes off". Closing the night, she joked that being the last award was "a new method for clearing the room". She also revealed that her speech began as a 3am note to herself, which included a line about the difference between power and force and, next to it, a reminder about cat litter bags.

The humour gave way to something firmer. Harper's Bazaar describes the turn as sudden, "like the flip of a coin", a nod to the play that has defined her autumn.

## 'A dangerous jackboot of misogyny'

Blanchett said the past couple of years had been a struggle. "I realised there has been a dangerous jackboot of misogyny sitting on the back of our neck," she said, "and I sense that maybe, just maybe, that jackboot is beginning to lift."

She framed that hope alongside anger, at what she called the waste of time and female energy the "destructive jackboot" represents. Her view, as quoted by the magazine, is that the anger should be fuel rather than something to put down.

"We can't take our eye off the ball, because that jackboot has not gone away," she said. "It's just been lifted slightly." In that small gap, she argued, women need to "radically and clearly express what the alternative is". She ended with a line aimed at the platform formerly called Twitter, saying the jackboot should be "blasted out into outer space, where it belongs, with all the people who run X".

## Why the rehearsal room matters to her

Blanchett also spoke about her craft. "The rehearsal rooms are messy. I'm very messy," she said, calling them "quite a female space" where people can expose themselves appropriately, laugh at one another and bring in many points of view. The work has an end point, she added, and it is "for somebody else's benefit", not just the performer's own.

That fits the run that earned the award. Harper's Bazaar notes it came weeks after *Electra/Persona* closed at London's National Theatre. Directed by [Benedict Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Andrews), it blended Sophocles with Ingmar Bergman's 1966 film. Blanchett and [Nina Hoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Hoss) decided each night, by coin toss, who would play Electra or Elisabeth and who would play Clytemnestra or Alma, alongside newcomer [Ella Lily Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Lily_Hyland). Simon Stone's *The Oresteia* was playing across the river at the Bridge Theatre, telling the same family story.

## A presenter with a long history

[Elizabeth Debicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Debicki) presented the award. The pair first worked together 13 years ago in Jean Genet's *The Maids* at Sydney Theatre Company, where Blanchett was then co-artistic director with her husband, [Andrew Upton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Upton).

Debicki joked that Blanchett played her maid and was "a little obsessed" with her. She also said she met on the first day of rehearsal "an actress whose photo I had cut out of a newspaper when I was a teenager and Blu-tacked onto my wall". What she remembered most was Blanchett's grace, which she said can hide the "brutal force" the art demands.
