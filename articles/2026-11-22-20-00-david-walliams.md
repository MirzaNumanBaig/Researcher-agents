**Quick summary**

- The Observer reports that HarperCollins started an independent internal inquiry in 2023 into a relationship between [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) and a junior employee, and into staff concerns about his alleged behaviour.
- According to the newspaper, one female employee left the company with a five-figure payout, as first reported by the Telegraph.
- HarperCollins dropped Walliams as an author in December 2025, then said it would stop selling his back catalogue.
- Walliams has consistently denied any allegations of inappropriate behaviour and told the Observer's reporters he was never informed of the inquiry.

## What The Observer reports about the 2023 inquiry

HarperCollins opened an internal inquiry into David Walliams two years before it ended its relationship with him, according to an Observer report published on 22 November 2026. The inquiry was independent and looked at a relationship between the author and a junior employee, as well as concerns raised by staff about his alleged behaviour.

The newspaper says the outcome was that one female employee left the company after receiving a five-figure payout. It credits the Telegraph with first reporting that detail. The Observer adds that HarperCollins then made efforts to keep some staff away from the author, advising them not to visit his home alone and to meet him in pairs.

Walliams later said he was never told the inquiry had taken place, the Observer reports. In the two years after it concluded, he went on to publish several new children's books.

## How the story reached the Observer

The report draws on earlier work by [Tortoise Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortoise_Media), now the owner of The Observer. In 2023 Tortoise published a podcast series about Walliams's work called Beastly. While reporting it, the team says it heard allegations of inappropriate behaviour and concerns from current and former HarperCollins staff about how the publisher dealt with criticism of him.

Tortoise put those points to HarperCollins and to Walliams at the time. According to the Observer, the publisher did not engage with the reporting and responded only with a short statement calling him "one of Britain's best-loved authors". Walliams has consistently denied the allegations.

## From dropped author to disappearing catalogue

In December 2025, HarperCollins dropped Walliams as an author. The Observer says the company, by then led by newly appointed chief executive Kate Elton, gave no detail about the earlier inquiry. It cited the importance of staff wellbeing and pointed to its "new leadership". A long-term HarperCollins insider told the paper that the move, made weeks after Elton took the job, was "a huge statement for everyone" given the potential cost to the business.

Within nine months, the publisher announced it would stop selling his back catalogue. The Observer calls that unusual and says it will cost the publisher millions, and that his books will vanish from shelves unless another publisher picks up the rights. The paper also understands Elton is overseeing a programme of cultural change at the company, although it received no details.

Author and podcaster [Richard Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Osman) described Elton as one of the "heroes" of the story on The Rest is Entertainment. "Nothing illegal has happened," he said, but a company can decide what culture it wants and protect the people it employs.

## What HarperCollins has not said

The Observer's central criticism is that HarperCollins has never explained publicly what led to such a drastic step. It places that gap against detailed reporting published earlier this month by the Guardian and ITV News about allegations concerning Walliams and young women, including alleged workplace harassment at HarperCollins. Those are allegations, and Walliams denies inappropriate behaviour.

## What happens next

HarperCollins has not published findings from the 2023 inquiry, and the Observer says it has given no detail on what prompted its later decisions. Whether another publisher takes on the rights to his books remains open. The sources available to us do not say how far the Metropolitan Police or any other body has taken the matter, so we make no claim on that point.
