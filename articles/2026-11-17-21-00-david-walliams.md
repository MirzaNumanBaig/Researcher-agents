**Quick summary**

- A Sydney Morning Herald opinion piece published on 17 November says the allegations against [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) did not surprise people in comedy and television.
- The writer points to old TV clips now circulating online, including jokes about Walliams made on air in 2004 and 2009.
- Walliams' lawyers told The Guardian the jokes are "not statements of fact". He denies the allegations reported by that paper.
- Separately, actor Jeremy Edwards has told the Daily Mail about a 2006 Comic Relief sketch he says left him humiliated.

An Australian comedy and television writer argues that the claims now surrounding David Walliams were hardly a secret in the industry. In an opinion piece for The Sydney Morning Herald on 17 November, the writer says the "whisper network" had talked about the former Little Britain star for years. The column also draws a line to [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), who was convicted of rape on 6 November and, according to the piece, sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison.

This article reports what has been alleged and said. Walliams denies the allegations, and none of the claims below has been tested in court.

## What the Sydney Morning Herald column says

The piece, [published by The Sydney Morning Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/culture/tv-and-radio/shocked-by-russell-brand-and-david-walliams-you-shouldn-t-be-20261117-p61dyf.html), says days after Brand's sentencing, The Guardian published a detailed investigation into Walliams. According to the column, it included claims that he groomed teenage fans and sexually assaulted a 15-year-old girl. The column states that he denies those allegations.

The writer, who works in Australian comedy and TV, says they have never been to the UK yet knew the rumours. Their argument is about culture rather than new evidence: that audiences, hosts and colleagues laughed along, and that women who raised concerns risked their careers.

## The clips that are circulating

The column lists several resurfaced moments. One shows [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) at the 2004 British Comedy Awards joking about Walliams and 15-year-olds. Another shows Walliams with [Jimmy Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carr) on a 2009 episode of *8 Out of 10 Cats*, making a joke about teenage boys. A third, from a 2007 episode of [The Big Fat Quiz of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Fat_Quiz_of_the_Year), involves rape jokes at the expense of [Lily Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Allen). The column also describes a radio exchange between Brand and [Jimmy Savile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Savile).

Those involved have responded, according to the column. Ross told The Guardian he was "horrified" and "genuinely unaware". [Noel Fielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Fielding), now a co-host of [The Great British Bake Off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_British_Bake_Off), told The Sun he was "appalled" at some of his own jokes and regrets them. Allen said she laughed along at the time and now finds it "really horrendous", because "it was the culture".

Walliams' lawyers took a different line. They told The Guardian the clips are jokes, not statements of fact, and that many were written or delivered by his friends.

## Lucy Beaumont and the whistleblower claims

The column also refers to comedian Lucy Beaumont, who it says came forward as one of the whistleblowers. According to the writer, Beaumont says a young man told her he was uncomfortable being asked to bring young women to Walliams, and that she spoke to a young woman who was in "a really bad way" and had allegedly signed an NDA. The column adds that Beaumont knew speaking up could cost her work. We have written separately about [Lucy Beaumont's book](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-allegations-lucy-beaumont-book/).

## Jeremy Edwards describes a 2006 stage sketch

A second report adds a different account. [Jeremy Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Edwards), now 55, told the Daily Mail that Walliams pulled down his trousers and simulated a sex act during a 2006 [Comic Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Relief) show at the Hammersmith Apollo, in front of about 3500 people, according to [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/world/uk-tv-star-jeremy-edwards-says-david-walliams-sexually-humiliated-him-during-sketch-on-stage/IDUUVNUX4FD67ET7FX5AEYQKOA/).

Edwards says he had not agreed to it and that Walliams has never apologised. He was a late replacement for [Duncan James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_James), and the sketch also involved [David Baddiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Baddiel). Edwards recalled that [Rufus Sewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_Sewell) asked if he was all right, while Walliams told him he had "got the biggest laugh of the night". Our earlier post covers the [Jeremy Edwards account](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-jeremy-edwards/) in more detail.

NZ Herald also reports that Walliams was recently unmasked as the celebrity who sought an injunction to stop media reporting on accusations of sexual misconduct brought by 30 women. The sources do not detail those accusations, and Walliams denies the allegations reported by The Guardian.
