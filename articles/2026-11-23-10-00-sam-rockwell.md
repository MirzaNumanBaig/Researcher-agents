**Quick summary**

- Editor Mikkel E.G. Nielsen spoke to The HoloFiles about cutting *Wild Horse Nine*, published on 22 November 2026.
- The film stars [Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell) and John Malkovich as CIA agents on Easter Island in the early 1970s.
- Nielsen says the tone came from "trial and error", building on his earlier work with director Martin McDonagh on *The Banshees of Inisherin*.
- Audience test screenings helped decide when laughs ran long and when the story needed room.

The editor of [Sam Rockwell](https://moduscarry.com/tag/sam-rockwell/)'s latest film says its shifting tone was found in the cutting room, not planned in advance. Mikkel E.G. Nielsen, who cut [*Wild Horse Nine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine), told [The HoloFiles](https://theholofiles.com/2026/11/22/wild-horse-nine-editor-mikkel-nielsen-exclusive-interview/) in an interview published on 22 November that the process was largely "trial and error".

## What Wild Horse Nine is about

The film comes from writer-director [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh), the filmmaker behind [*In Bruges*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Bruges). According to The HoloFiles, it is set in the early 1970s and follows CIA agents Chris, played by John Malkovich, and Lee, played by Rockwell. They travel to Easter Island for a holiday before the coup in Chile that would overthrow [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende).

The trip gets complicated. Lee has to cope with Chris's erratic behaviour and his argumentative wife, played by [Parker Posey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Posey). Their CIA boss, played by [Steve Buscemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Buscemi), then steps in. The outlet describes the result as a mix of outrageous humour and melancholy, moving towards a portrait of loss, regret and friendship.

## Balancing comedy, drama and thriller

Nielsen said the film asked more of the edit than their previous project together. "There was a lot of learning from our previous project, Banshees of Inisherin, where it was a comedy and drama," he told the outlet. This time the thriller element was added on top of the melancholy.

He described the approach as playful. As an editor, he said, he likes to "add in some balls" and juggle them, with the hope that the film feels whole, earned and honest by the end.

The edit, as Nielsen explained it, invests early in the bond between Chris and Lee. It then withholds more information as the darker material emerges. Chris's memoirs and montages of his past offer a window into his character. The bright imagery of Easter Island sits in sharp contrast to the atrocities linked to his career, according to the interview.

## Test screenings and the third act

Nielsen said audience screenings were especially useful. They showed him when a laugh went on too long, when a scene lost attention and when a new piece of information needed more space. The aim, as The HoloFiles summarises it, was for the warmth of the early scenes to deepen the impact of the later revelations, and for the third act to bring the separate threads together.

The HoloFiles also says the conversation covers preserving the musical quality of McDonagh's dialogue, which it describes as full of unusual repetitions and long pauses. We have not been able to read that section in full, so we do not cover it here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who edited Wild Horse Nine?

Mikkel E.G. Nielsen edited the film. He previously worked with Martin McDonagh on *The Banshees of Inisherin*, according to The HoloFiles.

### Who stars in Wild Horse Nine?

Sam Rockwell and John Malkovich play the CIA agents Lee and Chris. Parker Posey and Steve Buscemi also appear, per The HoloFiles.

### Where is Wild Horse Nine set?

The story takes place on Easter Island in the early 1970s, shortly before the coup in Chile that overthrew Salvador Allende.
