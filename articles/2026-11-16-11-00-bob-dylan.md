**Quick summary**

- *Saturday Night Live* made a sketch imagining [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan)'s recent private meeting with Pope Leo XIV, but it was cut for time from the live show on Saturday, 14 November.
- Host John Mulaney played the pope and [James Austin Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Austin_Johnson) played Dylan.
- The show posted the sketch to its YouTube channel late on Sunday afternoon (US time), according to Billboard.
- Little has been reported about what Dylan and the pope really said to each other.

The Bob Dylan SNL pope sketch never made it to air, but it did reach the internet. *[Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live)* wrote a parody of Dylan's Vatican meeting with Pope Leo XIV, dropped it from the live broadcast on Saturday, and uploaded it to YouTube a day later.

## What the sketch imagines

The meeting itself was widely shared online after the Vatican posted a photo of the pair on Instagram. [Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/culture/tv-film/snl-bob-dylan-pope-john-mulaney-1236382560/) reports that the conversation was described as brief, made up of greetings and a few light exchanges. The sketch's opening text plays on that gap, saying the photos went viral while the conversation stayed a mystery.

Johnson's Dylan arrives with what he calls questions of great spiritual importance, according to [Consequence of Sound](https://consequence.net/2026/11/bob-dylan-the-pope-snl/). The talk then drifts somewhere less holy. The two discuss where his hoodie came from (the joke answer is "Amazon Essentials"), and Dylan floats an idea for a new hymn, asks about buying Mother Teresa's bones and confesses a single sin.

Both outlets quote the same running gag: this Dylan has no memory of anything interesting in his own life, and he tells the pope that Dylan fans are his least favourite kind of person. Billboard adds that he wears a black hoodie under a black jacket and says he asked his stylist to make him look as if he were about to DJ his own funeral. Mulaney's pope spends much of the scene trying to keep the chat going.

## Why it was cut

Billboard's explanation is a guess: the episode ran long. Mulaney's monologue was lengthy, and a nine-minute Central Park horse-and-carriage musical sketch took up more of the hour. Consequence of Sound confirms only that the Dylan sketch was cut for time. The musical guest was [Phoebe Bridgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoebe_Bridgers), who performed "Bobby" and "Still Standing".

## What happens next

The sketch is already online, so there is nothing to wait for. Whether Dylan or the Vatican will comment on the parody has not been reported.
