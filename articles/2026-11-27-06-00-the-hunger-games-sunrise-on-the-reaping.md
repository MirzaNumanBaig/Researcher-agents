**Quick summary**

- A review in the University of Maryland's student paper The Diamondback says the prequel relies on nostalgia instead of its own story.
- The reviewer praises Joseph Zada's performance as the young Haymitch but finds the relationships underdeveloped.
- The film, directed by Francis Lawrence, has a 68% Rotten Tomatoes score and 55/100 on Metacritic, according to Wikipedia.
- It had earned about US$237.2 million against a US$150 million budget at the time of the latest recorded figures.

[The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games%3A_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping) gives Haymitch Abernathy the screen time he never had in the original films, but one fresh review argues it spends too much of that time looking backwards. Writing for [The Diamondback](https://dbknews.com/2026/11/25/sunrise-reaping-movie-review/) on 25 November, reviewer Justin Mehlbrech called the film a nostalgia exercise that skips the character work its own story needed.

## What the Diamondback review says

According to the review, the film is the second prequel and sixth entry in the series. It is based on Suzanne Collins's novel and set 24 years before Katniss and Peeta's story, following Haymitch through the 50th Hunger Games, a Quarter Quell with 48 tributes. Haymitch is played by Joseph Zada, and Whitney Peak plays his girlfriend, Lenore Dove.

The review's central complaint is pacing. It says the film "did not have time to build much of a relationship between the characters", so when allies die in the arena, including Maysilee Donner, played by Mckenna Grace, the losses carry little weight. It describes a story that feels like it is ticking off important plot points rather than giving them substance.

Spoiler warning: the review also covers the film's late events, including the fate of Haymitch's family and of Lenore Dove, so readers who have not seen it may want to stop here.

## Where the film earns praise

The reviewer is warmer about Zada. The performance, the review says, captures Haymitch's anxiety through small physical details, such as a shaking body and a twitching face as he rides into the arena. The film also offers a fuller picture of Haymitch before the games than the original trilogy ever did.

The closing epilogue, which features Woody Harrelson, Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson, is described as a fitting tribute to the earlier films. The same review is less kind to a scene in which the Everdeens appear with baby Katniss, which it says pushes the franchise connection at the audience instead of showing it with subtlety.

## How wider critics and the box office compare

The Diamondback's verdict sits close to the broader reception recorded on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping). The film holds 68% on Rotten Tomatoes and 55/100 from 43 critics on Metacritic, a middling score for a franchise of this size. The summary of critics there says the prequel benefits from spotlighting well-loved characters and retreads familiar ground, but does so sturdily enough to stay competitive.

On money, the same page lists a US$150 million budget and a worldwide gross of about US$237.2 million since its 28 October 2026 release. That is roughly 1.6 times the budget, which the data labels a modest result for a blockbuster of this scale. Box office figures change week to week, so treat the number as a snapshot.
