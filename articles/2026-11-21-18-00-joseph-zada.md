**Quick summary**

- Australian actor [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), 21, plays a young Haymitch Abernathy in the sixth Hunger Games film, *Sunrise on the Reaping*.
- He told FilmInk that building Haymitch's southern accent took months and that he stayed in it every day on set.
- [Woody Harrelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Harrelson), who plays the older Haymitch, visited the set midway and told him to watch *Cool Hand Luke*.
- Zada and co-star Mckenna Grace have matching tattoos, according to the same interview.

## A young Haymitch with a lot to carry

In an interview with [FilmInk](https://www.filmink.com.au/cool-hand-joseph-zada-enters-the-arena/), Sydney-born Joseph Zada described how he approached one of the franchise's best-known characters. He plays Haymitch Abernathy in *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, the story of the 50th Games, known as the Second Quarter Quell. According to FilmInk, that year the Capitol doubles its demand, so four tributes from each district are sent to the arena.

The film is set 24 years before [Katniss Everdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen) enters her own Games, and FilmInk notes that this Haymitch is not yet the bitter mentor audiences know. He is young, vulnerable and still able to believe that courage might change something.

## Months spent on the accent

Zada said the voice was the hardest part. His dark hair was dyed Haymitch blonde, but the accent took far longer. He told FilmInk that every accent feels daunting because the aim is to work until you stop thinking about it, and that he can tell when an actor has not done that work.

His method was to stay in the accent every day on set. He explained that emotional scenes are where an accent tends to slip, and he did not want to lose takes because of it. He also had to find how the voice changes when the character is drunk or exhausted, which he described simply as work.

## Woody Harrelson's one tip

Harrelson came to the set about halfway through filming. Zada said they talked about the character's stoicism, and he worried that his Haymitch was too emotional and close to breaking. He felt that was allowed given the character's age, but he wanted to find an everyman quality.

Harrelson's suggestion was to watch [*Cool Hand Luke*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cool_Hand_Luke), which Zada called a great film and a useful reference. In the final act, he said, he worked to pick up the "Woody-isms" so viewers could believe this character becomes the Haymitch of 24 years later.

## Off-screen friendships

FilmInk says the story rests on Haymitch's bonds with Lenore Dove Baird, played by [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)), and fellow tribute Maysilee Donner, played by [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace). Away from the cameras, Zada and Grace bonded in the way the original trio did, and FilmInk reports that both have matching tattoos, as their predecessors did. We covered that detail in [Mckenna Grace and Joseph Zada Get Matching Ladybug Tattoos](https://moduscarry.com/mckenna-grace-matching-tattoos/).

Zada also said he ran into [Liam Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_Hemsworth), a veteran of the earlier [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) films, at the Australian Open after filming wrapped. Hemsworth hugged him, congratulated him and said he was looking forward to seeing the film. Zada now has his number.

## What happens next

Zada expects things to get "a bit crazy" once the film arrives, and says he may text Hemsworth for advice.
