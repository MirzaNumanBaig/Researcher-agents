**Quick summary**

- [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson), 59, writes a weekly Substack newsletter called The Open Journal, according to an exclusive interview with People.
- The newsletter goes out each Monday to more than 159,000 subscribers and has run for about two and a half years.
- Her son Dylan edits the writing and adds nature sounds to the audio version for paying subscribers.
- Anderson says her sons want her to write a book, and she has short stories on her desktop.

Pamela Anderson has turned a lifelong journaling habit into a weekly newsletter, and her son Dylan is the person who made her start it. The actress told [People](https://people.com/pamela-anderson-channels-her-wild-free-spirit-into-her-substack-exclusive-12261190) that The Open Journal, her Substack, is meant to offer "a little bit of positivity" when there is "so much negativity" around.

## How the Pamela Anderson Open Journal works

According to People, Anderson has written the newsletter for the past two and a half years. It lands in inboxes every Monday, reaching more than 159,000 subscribers, and the entries are free-flowing and poetic. Topics range from gratitude to manifestation and love.

The Substack is free to read. Subscribers who pay $5 a month receive extras, including Anderson's recorded narration of each entry. Dylan layers in nature sounds such as birdsong, so it sounds as though she is reading aloud from her garden.

Her process is old-fashioned. She writes most mornings, handwrites everything first, then types it up and sends it to Dylan, who gives feedback, edits and adds the finishing touches.

## Dylan's idea, and a journal written in the future

Before the newsletter, Anderson says she sent her sons long emails full of quotes and philosophy. Dylan, 28, told her they were not reading them. His suggestion was a Substack, and he signed on as editor. She joked that he now has to read what she writes.

Anderson shares Dylan and his brother Brandon Thomas Lee, 30, with her ex-husband, musician [Tommy Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lee). She told People the habit began long before fame. As a girl on Vancouver Island, she kept a "five-year journal" and wrote as if she were five years older.

Her reading list shaped the tone. She credited the 1990s self-help she devoured after arriving in Los Angeles, naming writer [Louise Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Hay) and psychotherapist [Carl Jung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Jung) among her inspirations. Anderson, who found fame on Baywatch more than 30 years ago, said writing "has always made me understand what I feel."

## What happens next

The next step may be a book. Anderson told People that her children "really want me to write a book" and that she has short stories on her desktop. She is undecided on the form, and said a novel or a collection is possible. As for genre, she described a mix of the romantic and the humorous.

She also pointed out that she had "nothing to do" with the novels that carried her name decades ago. Her published books so far are the 2023 memoir Love, Pamela and the 2024 cookbook I Love You: Recipes from the Heart. No publication plans have been announced.
