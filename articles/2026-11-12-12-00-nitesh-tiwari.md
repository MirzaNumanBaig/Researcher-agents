*Ramayana: Rise of a Legend*, the first chapter of [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari)'s planned trilogy, has earned a warm review from [In Review Online](https://inreviewonline.com/2026/11/11/ramayana-rise-of-a-legend-review/), which calls it loud, fast-paced and emotionally potent. The review, published on 11 November 2026, describes the film as the kind of grand roadshow entertainment that Hollywood has largely stopped making.

## The scale behind Ramayana: Rise of a Legend

According to the review, the film is the most expensive Indian production ever made and is co-produced by the visual effects company DNEG. It adapts the *Ramayana*, an ancient Sanskrit poem of 24,000 verses, and the reviewer says the budget for the whole project could reach US$480 million.

The cast is described as large and exciting. The score comes from [Hans Zimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Zimmer) and A.R. Rahman, and the film runs for 197 minutes. The follow-up is projected to arrive at Diwali in 2028, according to the same review.

## A comparison with Nolan's The Odyssey

The reviewer frames 2026 as a year of two mega-budget adaptations of world literature. The other is Christopher Nolan's *The Odyssey*, which the review notes was the first film shot entirely on 70mm IMAX film. *Rise of a Legend* is playing on far fewer screens, the critic says, yet finds it the more enjoyable of the two despite its longer running time.

This is one critic's view, and the comparison is a matter of taste rather than a measure of box-office or audience response.

## What the review says about the effects

The verdict on the visual effects is mixed but admiring. The reviewer says the photorealism is stretched thin across such a long film and occasionally slips into the "uncanny valley". The film's fantastical register, with vaporous giant deities and worlds within worlds, turns those moments into something dreamlike instead, the review argues, particularly on the biggest screens.

Pacing gets similar treatment. The critic says the film is at its best when dialogue and montage move together, and that it "does drag, here and there". When the momentum slackens, the review adds, the film's open melodrama becomes a matter of each viewer's sensibility.
