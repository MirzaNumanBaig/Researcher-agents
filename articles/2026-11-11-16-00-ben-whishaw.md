**Quick summary**

- The Hindu published its review of *Black Doves* Season 2 on 10 November 2026, calling it a spy thriller that still swings between gore, grit and giggles.
- The critic finds the new run better plotted and tighter than Season 1.
- The chemistry between Ben Whishaw and Keira Knightley is judged less forced than before, though still not fully convincing.
- The fourth episode, set in 1988, is singled out as the backbone of the season.
- The series is streaming on Netflix.

[Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw) and [Keira Knightley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keira_Knightley) are back as rival-turned-partners in [*Black Doves*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Doves) Season 2, and the first major review to land is a mixed one. Writing for [The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/entertainment/movies/black-doves-season-2-review-keira-knightley-ben-whishaw-spy-thriller-netflix-swings-between-gore-grit-and-giggles/article71685412.ece) on 10 November 2026, the reviewer says the second outing is an improvement on the first, yet still cannot settle on a tone.

## What the review says about Season 2

According to The Hindu, the show remains "handsomely mounted" but frenetic. The critic describes Season 2 as better plotted than Season 1, with outrageous shoot-outs and hit-and-miss humour still part of the package. The quieter moments, the review argues, make up for some of that.

The main complaint is about commitment. The reviewer says the series cannot decide whether it wants to be a Spy vs Spy-style splatter-fest or a Le Carré-style study of moral decay, and that this indecision undercuts an otherwise polished production. That is a verdict on one critic's reading, and other reviews may weigh the balance differently.

## The fourth episode carries the season

The Hindu singles out the fourth episode, titled "The Opportunity to Become Savage", as the show's backbone. It is set in 1988 and explains how the secret organisation's Berlin operation led to the murders and betrayals playing out in the present day.

In that episode, Whishaw's assassin Sam is arrested after a hit and then freed by a mysterious man who offers him his freedom in exchange for four more killings. The review says this is the instalment where the series chooses realism over cartoonish style, and that the choice works. It is the episode, the critic writes, where people do foolish things for love and ideals and old sins cast long shadows.

## Plot, cast and tone

The story picks up as Wallace, played by [Andrew Buchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Buchan), is about to become Prime Minister. His wife Helen, Knightley's long-time Black Dove operative, is in Paris stealing a file from a Russian oligarch played by [Goran Kostic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Kosti%C4%87). When her handler Elizabeth Reed, played by [Sarah Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Lancashire), goes missing, Helen gets a new handler in [Babou Ceesay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babou_Ceesay)'s Mr Conteh, whom she does not trust.

The supporting cast gets a nod. The reviewer calls [Ambika Mod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambika_Mod) a fun addition to the group of killers that includes [Ella Lily Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Lily_Hyland) and [Gabrielle Creevy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabrielle_Creevy). [Neve Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Campbell) plays a Defence envoy and, in the critic's words, delivers a respectable version of the annoying American. [Kathryn Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathryn_Hunter) gets one of the quotable lines, telling Sam, "These are strange times to be nefarious."

The review is less kind about the violence. It says the casual approach to gunplay lowers the stakes, since villains lose their heads while Sam and his allies walk away unscratched. It also questions how the Prime Minister's wife can roam freely without security or press attention, and says the inner lives of killers are becoming a tired idea unless they feed the story.

The soundtrack draws praise for its mix of pop favourites, including Billy Joel, Peter, Paul and Mary, Nina Simone and Nancy Sinatra.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who stars in Black Doves Season 2?
Keira Knightley plays Helen and Ben Whishaw plays Sam. The cast also includes Andrew Buchan, Sarah Lancashire, Babou Ceesay and Neve Campbell, according to The Hindu.

### Is Black Doves Season 2 better than Season 1?
The Hindu's reviewer says it is better plotted and tighter, and that the lead chemistry is less forced. The same review says it still lacks a firm tone.

### Where can I watch Black Doves?
The series is currently streaming on Netflix.
