**Quick summary**

- The showrunner of Prime Video's *Blade Runner 2099*, Silka Luisa, says [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s *Blade Runner 2049* was her model for the series.
- She told SlashFilm the 2017 film created its own take on the franchise while still feeling like an evolution of Ridley Scott's original.
- The show is set after a Replicant uprising and stars Michelle Yeoh and Hunter Schafer.
- All eight episodes of *Blade Runner 2099* are now streaming on Prime Video.

The team behind Prime Video's new *Blade Runner 2099* looked to [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s 2017 film *Blade Runner 2049* when deciding how to continue a famous story. Showrunner [Silka Luisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silka_Luisa) explained the thinking in an interview with [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2322392/blade-runner-2099-denis-villeneuve-sequel-inspiration-explained/), published this week as all eight episodes arrived on the streaming service.

## Two classics to live beside

The series follows [Blade Runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_Runner), the 1982 film directed by [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott), and Villeneuve's sequel, which SlashFilm describes as highly acclaimed. That is a lot to follow. Luisa says she had two obvious options: stay tightly faithful to the look and tone of the films, or deliberately head the other way.

She described a third route. The title itself, she said, helps, because the story is set so many years past 2049 that it leaves "enough space" for the show to carve out its own identity.

## What Luisa took from 2049

Luisa pointed to the way Villeneuve handled the first sequel. According to her, he did not simply rehash what Scott had done. He made a version of *Blade Runner* that was clearly his own aesthetic, yet it still felt like a continuation of Scott's conversation.

That balance became the target for *2099*. Luisa said the example freed her team from repeating the same material over and over, and gave them permission to take bold new directions.

SlashFilm's writer goes further, calling *2049* the poster child for legacy sequels done right. The outlet contrasts it with films that retread old ground and cites *Star Wars: Episode VII, The Force Awakens* as an example from the wider [Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars) franchise. That comparison is SlashFilm's opinion, not Luisa's.

## A world turned upside down

The biggest change in the series is its setting. According to SlashFilm, *Blade Runner 2099* takes place after a Replicant uprising, which has put humans at the bottom of the food chain.

The two leads have to find their way through that shift. [Michelle Yeoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Yeoh) plays Olwen, a Replicant Blade Runner. [Hunter Schafer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Schafer) plays Cora, a human posing as a Replicant. Both characters are described as having to navigate the new order throughout the season.

For readers following Villeneuve's career, it is a reminder of how far his work reaches. He did not make the series, and the sources do not suggest he has a role in it. The story is about his film's influence on others.
