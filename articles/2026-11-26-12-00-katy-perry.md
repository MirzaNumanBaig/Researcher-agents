**Quick summary**

- [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) shared a photo and video carousel on Wednesday from a Europe trip with former Canadian prime minister [Justin Trudeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Trudeau), according to Yahoo Lifestyle Canada.
- The post includes her new bob haircut, a selfie with French President Emmanuel Macron and apparent shots from the Venice-Simplon-Orient-Express.
- The pair were photographed in Venice on 21 November and at the city's airport on 23 November.
- Separately, the Australian trademark fight between Perry and designer Katie Perry is back in court for the unresolved parts of the case.

Katy Perry has given fans a look inside her latest European holiday with Justin Trudeau. On Wednesday the singer posted a carousel of photos and videos, and the Katy Perry Justin Trudeau trip it shows runs from Spain to Paris, Milan and Venice, according to [Yahoo Lifestyle Canada](https://ca.style.yahoo.com/katy-perry-and-justin-trudeau-take-their-romance-across-europe--and-enjoy-some-luxury-travel-on-an-iconic-train-222030288.html).

## What Katy Perry posted from Europe

Yahoo reports that the first image is Perry, 42, in a bathtub in front of an ornate green and yellow wall. Other pictures and clips appear to have been taken on the Venice-Simplon-Orient-Express, the Belmond train the couple likely took from Paris to Venice, where they arrived on 20 November.

The carousel also shows the pair playing card games, sharing dinners and posing in a hallway. One image is a selfie with French President Emmanuel Macron. Perry captioned the post "The bob's Euro debut" and used her holiday track "[Cozy Little Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozy_Little_Christmas)" as the soundtrack, per Yahoo.

Fans were warm in the comments. One wrote that Europe, "especially Spain", suited her, and another wished the couple well.

## The new bob

The haircut is the other talking point. Yahoo says Perry first showed it at the Baby2Baby Gala in West Hollywood on 14 November, so the Europe post is its first outing abroad, as her caption jokes. A separate InStyle report, which we could not open in full, also highlights the bob and a makeup-free look from the same set of photos.

## Where the couple were seen

Photographers spotted the pair in Venice on 21 November. Yahoo describes Trudeau, 54, in a black hat with a Canadian maple leaf and Perry in a multi-coloured headscarf. On 23 November they were photographed leaving Venice's Marco Polo Airport, with Perry in a Ralph Lauren hat and AirPods Max headphones and Trudeau in a shirt reading "Will yap about books".

Trudeau's own posts show a working side to the trip. On 20 November he wrote that it was "great to see new and familiar faces in Barcelona, Milan and Paris", and shared pictures with former Italian prime minister [Paolo Gentiloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Gentiloni) and Catalan leader Salvador Illa, according to Yahoo. He said he had spoken at conferences and held meetings with other politicians.

## Meanwhile in Australia: the Katie Perry case

Away from the holiday pictures, Perry's long legal fight with an Australian designer is back before a court. [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/katie-perry-vs-katy-perry-heads-back-to-court) reports that Thursday's listing appears to deal with the "unresolved" aspects of the case.

The dispute is over who may sell clothing under the name Katie Perry or Katy Perry. The singer was born Kathryn Elizabeth Hudson and adopted her stage name around 2001. The designer was born Katie Perry and has also used other names, including Katie Taylor.

On 11 March 2026 the High Court found, by a 3:2 decision, that the designer's trademark could stay on the register. It held that her use of the name on clothing was unlikely to deceive or cause confusion. Mediaweek says the designer had not known of the singer when she first applied, though she had become aware by the time the mark was registered.

Perry's representatives told Rolling Stone AU/NZ, as quoted by Mediaweek, that the singer offered "coexistence" in 2019 and never sought to shut the business down. They said the High Court sent the case back to the Full Federal Court to decide issues Perry raised, including the designer's ten-year delay in bringing her case. The designer called the outcome proof that trademarks should protect businesses of all sizes.
