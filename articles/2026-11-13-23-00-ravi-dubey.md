**Quick summary**

- [Ravi Dubey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Dubey) plays Lakshmana in *Ramayana: Part 1*, which Outlook India says is his big-screen Bollywood debut.
- Outlook India's review calls his performance one of the film's most memorable, mainly for its humour.
- Veteran actor [Sunil Lahri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunil_Lahri), who played Lakshmana on television in 1987, and filmmaker Karan Johar have both praised him.
- The film was released on November 5. Its second part is where the story reaches the epic war.

Ravi Dubey is emerging as one of the surprise winners of [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari)'s *Ramayana: Part 1*. In a review of the film's supporting cast published on 13 November, [Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/art-entertainment/beyond-rama-and-ravana-how-nitesh-tiwaris-ramayana-reshapes-its-supporting-cast) singles out his Lakshmana as one of the most memorable parts of the picture.

## Why Ravi Dubey's Lakshmana stands out

The film is led by [Ranbir Kapoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranbir_Kapoor) as Rama, with Yash and [Sai Pallavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Pallavi) among the main cast. Against that line-up, Outlook India says Dubey, a television star making his big-screen Bollywood debut, has drawn a surge of new admirers.

According to the review, Tiwari and co-writer [Shridhar Raghavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shridhar_Raghavan) use Lakshmana as the main human counterpoint to Rama's divine restraint. Kapoor's Rama is played with deliberate stoicism and spiritual distance. Dubey's Lakshmana is described as grounded, witty and biting, and his humour is called a welcome break from the solemnity of the royal court and the forest exile.

The review points to a few moments. After a battle with demons in a forest, Lakshmana jokes to the sage Vishvamitra that he is not half a bad warrior himself, just as Rama receives a boon. The bond between the brothers is played as easy and playful during their training under the sage, then turns fiercely protective whenever Rama or Sita is threatened.

## A tempered Lakshmana in the Shurpanakha scene

Outlook India also notes how the film handles the confrontation with Shurpanakha, played by [Rakul Preet Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakul_Preet_Singh). Lakshmana's impulsive anger is toned down compared with traditional tellings. Rather than cutting off her nose, his strike leaves a slash across her face. The reviewer says the wound still hits at her vanity and triggers Ravana's wrath. It also sets up a moment in which Sita scolds Lakshmana for the violence.

The review adds that his resentment towards Kaikeyi, played by [Lara Dutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Dutta), reads as more human than Rama's calm acceptance of his fate.

## Praise from Sunil Lahri and Karan Johar

Outlook India reports that Lahri, who played Lakshmana in Ramanand Sagar's 1987 television series, wrote that the innocence and intensity the character needs is visible in the new film. Karan Johar also posted his support, calling Dubey's performance brilliant and saying the loyal, loving brother on screen made him smile.

Both reactions matter because Lakshmana is a role many Indian viewers associate with a single television performance. Approval from the actor who defined it for an earlier generation carries weight.

## The limits of Part 1

The review is not all praise. Outlook India argues that the film moves so quickly towards the war in Part 2 that some of its supporting cast lose out. It names [Lara Dutta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lara_Dutta), [Sheeba Chaddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheeba_Chaddha) as Manthara and [Kajal Aggarwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kajal_Aggarwal) as Mandodari as actors whose arcs are trimmed.

For Dubey, the same pacing means the deeper side of Lakshmana gets little room. The review says his separation from his wife Urmila, played by [Sonia Balani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonia_Balani), and his legendary fourteen-year sleepless vigil receive short shrift in this first instalment.

## What happens next

*Ramayana: Part 1* arrived in cinemas on 5 November, and the story now builds towards the war in the second part. Whether the quieter, more emotional chapters of Lakshmana's journey get their space there is something the sources do not confirm.
