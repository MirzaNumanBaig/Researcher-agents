**Quick summary**

- [Charlie Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Cox) says he asked to audition for [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond) and was told he was "too old".
- He made the comments on Virgin Radio UK, as reported by Deadline and Complex.
- Cox turns 44 next month. [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson), who got the role, turned 40 this year.
- Cox says he expected Jack Lowden or Callum Turner to be named, and praised Pattinson's career.
- Denis Villeneuve is set to direct the next Bond film for Amazon MGM Studios.

The *Daredevil* star has joined the long list of actors with a Bond story to tell, and his version comes with a pointed question about arithmetic. Cox says he asked to audition for 007 and was turned down on age, even though the actor who won the part is only a few years younger than he is.

## What Charlie Cox said about the Bond audition

Cox spoke on Virgin Radio UK's *The Chris Evans Breakfast Show*, according to [Complex](https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/a/helen-storms/charlie-cox-bond-age-rejection), which credited Variety for the quote. "I asked to audition for that," he said. "Do you know what they told me? 'Too old.'"

He then turned to the numbers. "Rob is the same age as me," Cox joked, before conceding that Pattinson "does look good for it". He added that he turns 44 next month and asked how that could count as too old, a line [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/daredevil-charlie-cox-audition-james-bond-robert-pattinson-age-1237232024/) used in its headline.

Strictly speaking, the two are not the same age. Deadline notes that Pattinson turned 40 this year, while Cox turns 44 in December. Complex puts the gap at three years, based on Cox being 43 now.

## Who else was in the frame for 007

Cox says he did not see Pattinson coming. "I thought it was going to be Jack Lowden or maybe Callum Turner," he told the show. Deadline adds that other names who reportedly tested for the part included Lowden, Josh O'Connor, Turner and Nicholas Hoult. That list is the outlet's wording of earlier reports, not a confirmed shortlist from the studio.

The remarks are not sour. Cox described Pattinson's career as "amazing" and pointed to the directors he has worked with, several of whom went on to become major names after making early films with him. Complex says Cox has known Pattinson for years.

## Pattinson's own reaction to the role

When Amazon MGM Studios announced the casting, Pattinson called Bond "one of the most legendary characters in cinema" and said being trusted with 007 was "the honour of a lifetime", according to Deadline. He thanked Villeneuve and the studio team for their belief in him, and said the role was a dream come true.

Deadline describes Pattinson as the *Dune: Part Three* actor, a reminder that he and Villeneuve already share a franchise. Pattinson succeeds Daniel Craig, whose last outing as Bond was 2021's *No Time to Die*, Complex notes.

## Why the age question keeps coming up

Cox's anecdote lands in a week when Bond casting chatter has not slowed. Readers of Deadline's comment section were split, with some arguing that a 44-year-old is a risk for a franchise that plans years of films, and others saying Pattinson's bigger name settled it. Those are reader opinions, not reporting, and neither studio nor Cox's team has said anything about how the audition process was run.

It is also worth keeping the scope in check. We have only Cox's account of what he was told, and no detail on who told him or when. Amazon MGM Studios has not been quoted in either report.

## What happens next

No release date for the Villeneuve-directed Bond film appears in the sources read for this article, so audiences have no confirmed date to plan around. Pattinson is the actor to watch, with the studio expected to share more as the film moves forward. Cox, for his part, is turning 44 in December and has made clear he would have liked the chance to try.
