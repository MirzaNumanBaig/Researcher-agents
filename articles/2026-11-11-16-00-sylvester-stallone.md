**Quick summary**

- *I Play Rocky*, a film about how [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) wrote the original *Rocky*, has an unsettled Oscar screenplay category, Variety reported on 11 November 2026.
- Studios must submit their Official Screen Credits forms for the 99th Academy Awards by Thursday 12 November.
- The script was written as an original, but it uses established characters such as Rocky Balboa, Adrian and Apollo Creed.
- The decision rests with the Academy's Writers Branch executive committee, because the Writers Guild of America (WGA) will not classify the film.
- The film expands nationwide on 20 November.

Sylvester Stallone reportedly wrote *Rocky* in three days. Deciding which Oscar screenplay category a film about that feat belongs in is proving much slower. According to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/awards/i-play-rocky-oscars-original-adapted-screenplay-1236944423/), the Academy has yet to settle whether *I Play Rocky* counts as an original or an adapted screenplay, and the awards season clock is already running.

## Why the category matters for I Play Rocky

The deadline is close. Variety reports that Thursday 12 November is the last day studios can submit Official Screen Credits forms to the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences for the 99th Academy Awards in the best picture, animated feature and general entry categories. No film is considered after that.

Screenwriter Gamble, named by Variety, conceived and wrote the script as an original. It is not based on a book, article, memoir or any single published work. Variety says it was drafted on spec and shared with Christian Baha of Baha Films, who came aboard as a producer alongside Toby Emmerich. Peter Farrelly, who directed best picture winner *Green Book* (2018), eventually signed on to direct. It is Gamble's second produced feature script, after *Office Uprising* (2018).

The complication is the franchise. Variety notes that *Rocky* is among the most established pieces of intellectual property in Hollywood, and sources say some people within each organisation are weighing whether the script fits adapted, the category for scripts built on earlier material. Rocky Balboa, Adrian and Apollo Creed are existing characters, and Farrelly's film restages some of the best-known scenes from the 1976 original.

Amazon MGM and the Academy declined to comment, according to Variety.

## Who decides, and by what rules

*I Play Rocky* expands nationwide on 20 November, the same day WGA submissions close. The guild will not weigh in, however. Variety reports the film was not made under a WGA signatory agreement, so the guild cannot classify the screenplay or consider it for the WGA Awards, even though Gamble is a member.

That leaves the call to the Academy's Writers Branch executive committee, which makes its own determinations and has broken with the WGA before. Under the Academy's screenplay FAQ for the 99th Oscars, as summarised by Variety, films do not pick a category. The committee reviews each eligible title and generally follows the WGA, except where the guild has not classified a film or the classification "has been called into question."

The rules cut both ways. A script is generally treated as adapted if it is based on a sequel, a remake, an existing film or "pre-existing characters." It is treated as original if it tells a true story and the writer "performed their own research and used multiple sources." Once the committee rules, the decision is final, with no appeals or extra materials accepted.

Elsewhere on the circuit the question carries less weight. Variety says the Golden Globes put all screenplays in one category, while BAFTA and the Critics Choice Awards, which split original and adapted, generally go with the category the studio submits.

## The *Barbie* precedent

Variety points to a recent example. In January 2024, Greta Gerwig's *Barbie* was moved to adapted after Warner Bros. campaigned it as an original. The script earned an adapted nomination and lost to Cord Jefferson's *American Fiction*.

Reversals are not unusual. Variety lists *Moonlight*, *Whiplash* and *The Ballad of Buster Scruggs* as films that carried original labels from the WGA before the Academy moved them to adapted. The Academy has also long treated sequels as adaptations, which is how *Before Sunset*, *Toy Story 3*, *Top Gun: Maverick* and *Glass Onion* ended up in that category.

The same rule is being tested this season by Aaron Sorkin's *The Social Reckoning*. It was first marketed as a sequel to *The Social Network*, and the WGA has classified it as an original. There is no word yet on whether the Academy will agree.

## Stallone's own telling of the Rocky story

The film arrives as the legend behind the original is being re-examined. Slate has published a piece arguing that a story Stallone has long told about the making of *Rocky* does not hold up. In an email to the writer in 2026, Stallone said he put trainer Jimmy Gambina's father in the movie as a favour. Slate says that overlooks Gambina's own work in film and his connections to the production. According to Slate, Gambina trained Stallone for four months alongside Carl Weathers, who played Apollo Creed, with early starts, long runs and bag work.

Stallone is also still on screen in *Tulsa King*. Comic Book Resources argued this week, in an opinion piece, that season four is giving more room to the supporting cast than to Stallone's Dwight Manfredi. That is one critic's view, not a change the show has announced.

## What happens next

The Academy's committee may ask the film's credited writers for a statement while it reviews the script. Its ruling will then stand. The next fixed dates are 12 November for Academy submissions and 20 November for the nationwide release.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is *I Play Rocky* about?

Variety describes it as a film about Sylvester Stallone writing the original *Rocky*. Peter Farrelly directs, and it restages some scenes from the 1976 film.

### Why is its Oscar category disputed?

The script was written as an original, but it uses characters from an established franchise. The Academy generally treats scripts with "pre-existing characters" as adapted.

### Will the WGA decide the category?

No. Variety reports the film was not made under a WGA signatory agreement, so the guild cannot classify it. The Academy's Writers Branch executive committee will decide.
