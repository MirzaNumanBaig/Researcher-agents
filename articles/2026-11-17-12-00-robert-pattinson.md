**Quick summary**

- The Robert Pattinson Bond casting is official: [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson), 40, was confirmed as the next [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond) on Monday through the franchise's social media channels, according to ABC Audio.
- Denis Villeneuve will direct the 26th official Bond film, and Steven Knight will write the script, according to PerthNow.
- Australian columnists at The Sydney Morning Herald and Nine argue his offbeat indie career could make for a stranger, more unpredictable 007.
- Online reaction is split, with some fans threatening to boycott the franchise.

The Robert Pattinson Bond casting has barely settled, and Australian commentators are already arguing about what kind of spy he will play. The 40-year-old was confirmed on Monday as the successor to Daniel Craig, and the loudest question now is not whether he can do it, but whether he will make 007 weird.

## What has been confirmed

The franchise's social media channels announced the news, according to [ABC Audio](https://digital.abcaudio.com/news/robert-pattinson-new-james-bond). Villeneuve, the Dune director, will helm the film, the 26th in the official series. It is the first Bond adventure since longtime producers Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson ceded creative control to Amazon MGM Studios in 2025, with Amy Pascal and David Heyman now producing.

[PerthNow](https://www.perthnow.com.au/entertainment/james-bond-new-007-star-robert-pattinson-once-said-hed-be-pretty-bad-bond-shoes-too-big-to-fill-c-23171646) reports that Steven Knight, the creator of Peaky Blinders, will write the screenplay. Pattinson called it "the honour of a lifetime" in a statement and thanked Villeneuve, Pascal, Heyman and Tanya Lapointe.

## The case for a weirder Bond

The Sydney Morning Herald's opinion column leans on his smaller films. It points to The Lighthouse, The King and Mickey 17 as proof he can disappear into very different oddballs, and suggests a little of that unpredictability could freshen a franchise more than 70 years old.

[Nine's opinion piece](https://www.nine.com.au/entertainment/celebrity/new-james-bond-007-announcement-opinion-wrong-choice-in-defence-of-robert-pattinson-20261117-p61eqm.html) makes a similar argument and reaches for history. Craig was also an unpopular pick in 2006, the column notes, with some fans running online campaigns against him. His debut, Casino Royale, then broke series records in the UK and earned nearly $900 million worldwide, according to Nine.

Marie Claire Australia describes Pattinson as an "unexpected" choice with a fondness for the strange and the awkward. It also notes the casting makes him the franchise's first Millennial Bond.

## What fans are saying

Not everyone is convinced. Nine reports that reactions to the official 007 Instagram announcement ranged from jabs at his Twilight and [Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter) past to threats to boycott. One commenter called him the "pale, skinny, sparkly vampire". These are social media comments, not a measure of wider opinion.

Twilight fans, meanwhile, are celebrating. Marie Claire says the saga's official X account dug up a 2008 scene mentioning "Bond, James Bond" just before Edward Cullen appears, and cites search data from Third Marble Marketing showing interest in Twilight tripled after the announcement.

## What he said before

Pattinson has been asked about Bond before, and his answers have aged well. In 2010, promoting Remember Me with former 007 Pierce Brosnan, he told the BBC it was "quite big shoes to fill" and that doing it then would be "probably pretty bad". In 2012, he told Shortlist he would "definitely" consider it in about 20 years, and that a "fresh-faced Bond" would be a terrible idea after Craig, according to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/robert-pattinson-manifested-james-bond-casting-2012-1236756679/).

The same outlet reports he was among the finalists alongside Callum Turner, Jack Lowden, Josh O'Connor and Nicholas Hoult. He arrives on a strong run: PerthNow says The Drama, The Odyssey and Primetime have all done well this year. Primetime, directed by Lance Oppenheim, has a reported 86% on Rotten Tomatoes and earned about US$62 million against a US$14.5 million budget, per the figures on record.

## What happens next

Dune: Part Three, which PerthNow says is due in December, will be the next big look at Pattinson on screen. His thriller The Take, directed by Fernando Meirelles, is listed for 15 January 2027.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is the new James Bond?

Robert Pattinson, 40, was confirmed on Monday through the franchise's official social media channels. He replaces Daniel Craig, who last played the role in No Time to Die (2021).

### Who is directing the new Bond film?

Denis Villeneuve, who directed Dune, will direct. Steven Knight is writing the script, according to PerthNow.

### Did Robert Pattinson ever talk about playing Bond?

Yes. In 2010 he said he would be "probably pretty bad" if he did it then, and in 2012 he said he would "definitely" consider it in roughly 20 years.
