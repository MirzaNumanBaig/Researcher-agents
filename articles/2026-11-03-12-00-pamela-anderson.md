## From pencil-thin to soft and feathered

Yahoo's beauty feature describes how far her style has travelled. In the 1990s, it notes, her messy bun and razor-thin, pencil-drawn eyebrows defined a decade, and they are still copied each Halloween. Today, the piece says, she has moved into a "softer, fresh-faced era", and it points out that she has been willing to appear on the red carpet without makeup.

## Why light brows work in your 50s

The argument is practical. According to Yahoo, skin loses collagen and facial structure shifts with age, so dark or densely filled brows can look drawn on against a mature complexion. Heavy formulas can also be harder on ageing skin.

The feature quotes esthetician Jenneane Rocha, speaking to Vogue: women over 50 often keep using "the same hard, waxy pencils they used in their 30s", which she says drag across skin and tug on fragile hairs. Yahoo reads Anderson's feathered arches as a gentler alternative that still gives a lifting effect.

## The routine the feature recommends

Yahoo stresses that the goal is not to copy her thin shape or to drop product altogether. It recommends lightweight formulas and careful placement that follow the natural contours of the brow bone rather than carving a new, stark outline.

Its examples are specific:

- Thinning brows: fill only the sparse gaps in the mid-arch with a fine-tip pen such as Glossier Brow Flick, keeping the front feathered.
- Coarse or wiry brows: use a conditioning product such as Kosas Brow Pop Dual Action instead of a hard pencil.
- Grey brows: choose cool taupes or slate shades to avoid a warm contrast against silver hair.

These are the publication's suggestions, not advice from Anderson herself, and she has not commented on them in the sources we read.
