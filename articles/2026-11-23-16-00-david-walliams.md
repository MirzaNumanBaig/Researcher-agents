**Quick summary**

- [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), 55, denies allegations in a joint ITV News and Guardian investigation.
- Passages from his 2012 memoir, *Camp David*, are being re-read in light of those claims, according to NZ Herald.
- The Metropolitan Police said last week it is investigating non-recent allegations involving a man in his 50s, separate from the media reports.
- In Malta, a children's book he wrote for the Film Commission has not yet sent any proceeds to the president's charity, Times of Malta reports.

David Walliams is facing fresh attention this week as his own words from more than a decade ago are read against serious allegations, and as a charity book he wrote for Malta draws questions. Walliams has denied the allegations. This article sets out only what named outlets have reported.

## What the investigation alleges

ITV News and the Guardian published the result of a three-year investigation featuring accounts from several women, [NZ Herald reports](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/david-walliams-on-sex-addiction-shame-and-friendship-with-russell-brand/NDSSPKKIBZFQHJTCZRST4N6PFA/). They claim he used his fame to pursue teenage girls and young women.

According to the Guardian, as relayed by NZ Herald, several women said the comedian built relationships through phone calls, emails, social media messages and offers of career help before seeking sexual relationships. Photographer Lilly Creightmore alleges he pursued her for sex after they met when she was 18. Walliams rejects her account and says he never behaved inappropriately towards her.

In a statement to the Guardian and ITV News, Walliams said he regretted times when he acted "thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual sexual relationships with. He denied abusing any power imbalance created by his fame and said he had not broken the law.

## Police and the legal background

The Metropolitan Police confirmed last week that it is investigating a number of non-recent serious sexual offence allegations involving a man in his 50s. The force said these are separate from the claims reported by the Guardian and ITV News. It did not name anyone in that statement, as quoted by NZ Herald, and no link between the two should be assumed.

Times of Malta adds that Walliams was named last week after losing a legal bid to stop publication of the allegations. For earlier coverage, see our report on the [HarperCollins inquiry that preceded his exit](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-harpercollins-inquiry/).

## Why the memoir is back in focus

*Camp David*, published in 2012, includes diary extracts he kept from 1997. NZ Herald says passages are now being "looked at in a new light". The Sunday Mirror reported that Walliams wrote of "meaningless encounters" and described himself as a sex addict.

He wrote that he began keeping the diary as a record of therapy and of his struggle with shame. In one extract he said he believed sex could lift his sadness, but that it deepened his despair. He also wrote that a former girlfriend read his journals and found details of past relationships.

The book also covers his friendship with [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand). Walliams wrote that he first disliked Brand, then changed his mind after they worked together on projects including the comedy film *Cruise of the Gods*, and that the two became close.

It matters to read these extracts for what they are: his own account of his private struggles, published years ago. They are not evidence of the allegations now made, which he denies.

## The Malta charity book

Separately, Times of Malta reports that *The Golden Bee Adventure*, written by Walliams, was launched in 2024 by Malta's Film Commission, with proceeds meant for the Malta Community Chest Fund (MCCF), the president's charity. The President's Office recently told the paper it had received no donations from the book.

Culture Minister Malcolm Paul Agius Galea defended Film Commissioner Johann Grech. He said Grech told him the proceeds would be passed to the fund "at the opportune moment" once received, and that Grech never said there would be no proceeds. "I received a reply, and because I trust my CEOs, I will accept that reply," the minister said, adding that he will keep following the issue.

The MCCF helps people facing serious illness, disability and hardship, and is best known for its annual Boxing Day telethon, L-Istrina.

## What happens next

The Metropolitan Police inquiry is ongoing, and Walliams continues to deny the claims. Whether the Film Commission sends money to the MCCF, and when, remains unanswered. ## Frequently asked questions

### Has David Walliams responded to the allegations?

Yes. He has denied them. He told the Guardian and ITV News he regretted acting "thoughtlessly" with casual partners but did not abuse his fame or break the law.

### Is the Metropolitan Police investigating him?

The force confirmed it is looking into non-recent allegations involving a man in his 50s. It said these are separate from the Guardian and ITV News reports and did not name anyone, according to NZ Herald.

Not yet, according to the President's Office. The Film Commission says it will pass on proceeds when it has them, Times of Malta reports.
