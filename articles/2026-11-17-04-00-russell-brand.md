**Quick summary**

- A message from jailed comedian [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), claiming "I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for", was read out by his wife on their YouTube show on Sunday 15 November.
- On Monday, Downing Street called it "clearly appalling" and said it is being urgently investigated.
- Justice Secretary Alex Norris said he expects action if Brand broke prison rules.
- Brand is serving 10 and a half years after being convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

The Russell Brand prison message has drawn an urgent government response. According to the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm9d7pe8y559o), Downing Street said on Monday that a statement the convicted comedian sent through his wife is "clearly appalling" and under investigation, with action expected if he is found to have broken the rules.

## What the message said

Laura Gallacher, who married Brand in 2017, read the note from a notepad on the couple's Sunday Service YouTube show. [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/russell-brand-appalling-prison-message-investigated-1237225025/) reports that it began: "As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening."

He confirmed he is held at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire and joked about the name, calling it the biggest sex offenders' prison in Europe. He described the staff as strong, kind and dedicated. He added that he is "in no position to judge anyone" among his fellow prisoners, and asked followers to support his "treasured wife and family".

Gallacher, according to [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/world/uk/2026/11/16/russell-brand-criticised-for-message-shared-from-prison/), said her husband "is OK and is praying", and that the couple trust God's plan. Brand has presented himself as a Christian influencer since 2023, and the BBC notes he has a large following among believers, including in the United States.

## The government response

A spokesperson for Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: "Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims and speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances. Prisoners cannot get around these rules by having someone else publish their comments."

[Variety](https://variety.com/2026/tv/global/russell-brand-prison-message-under-investigation-breaking-rules-1236948824/) reports that the Ministry of Justice is carrying out the inquiry. Alex Norris called the broadcast "completely unacceptable" in a post on X. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told Sky News it was "revolting" that Brand "shows no remorse".

Andrea Simon, the Victims' Commissioner for London, told BBC Radio 4 that the claims will "certainly retraumatise his victims and other survivors of rape". She argued that platforms' terms of service should regulate how such messages are shared, and that prison rules should bar convicted prisoners from sending messages for broadcast unless the governor agrees.

## What punishment could follow

The BBC lists the sanctions open to governors if a breach is found. They include limits on social visits, loss of privileges such as extra gym time or television, and proportionate restrictions on communication. That could mean blocking particular phone numbers, closer monitoring of calls, or limiting contact with certain people to protect victims.

The statement is still on YouTube, [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/british-authorities-investigate-russell-brand-statement-1235658924/) reported on Monday.

## The conviction and the move to Littlehey

Brand, 51, was convicted at Southwark Crown Court on 5 November. Rolling Stone says the jury found him not guilty of two other rape counts and one of indecent assault. The Irish Times reports he is expected to serve two-thirds of the term before release on licence and will stay on the sex offenders' register for life.

Over the weekend he was moved from HMP Wandsworth, a category B prison, to category C HMP Littlehey, which mostly houses sex offenders and runs programmes tailored to them. Foreign Office minister Stephen Doughty told LBC it was not "a good look" that he had gone to a lower security jail.

## The wider fallout

Netflix released the documentary Russell Brand: Open Secret last Friday. The Guardian's review calls it a tribute to the Sunday Times and Channel 4 journalists Rosamund Urwin and Charlotte Wace, and to the women who spoke out. [SheKnows](https://www.sheknows.com/entertainment/articles/1235093327/katy-perry-russell-brand-prison-response/) adds that The Times and Sunday Times published accounts from six more women on 13 November, which could not be reported until the criminal case ended. Those are allegations, not findings.

The same outlet says Katy Perry, Brand's ex-wife, has not commented publicly on the sentence.

## What happens next

The Ministry of Justice has not said when its investigation will finish. Prison governors, not courts, decide any penalty under the prison rules. The BBC adds that Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy met industry leaders on Monday, who are due to report back within weeks on how they challenge abuse. ## Frequently asked questions

### What did Russell Brand say in his prison message?
He said he is not guilty of the offences for which he was jailed, praised the staff at HMP Littlehey and asked followers to support his wife and family. The message was read by Laura Gallacher on YouTube.

### Why is the message being investigated?
Prisoners are banned from making statements that cause distress to victims, and Downing Street says they cannot get round the ban by having someone else publish their words.

### How long is Russell Brand's sentence?
Ten and a half years, for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He is expected to serve two-thirds before release on licence.

### Could Brand be punished?
If a breach is found, governors can restrict visits, remove privileges or limit his contacts and calls.
