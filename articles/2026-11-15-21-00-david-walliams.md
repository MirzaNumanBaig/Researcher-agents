**Quick summary**

- Comedian Lucy Beaumont says she feared she would never work in television again after speaking to journalists about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams).
- According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/nov/15/lucy-beaumont-work-tv-david-walliams), her book *Drinking Custard: The Diary of a Confused Mum* sold out online soon afterwards.
- Fans also posted that they had bought tickets to her current tour in support.
- Walliams denies any wrongdoing.

Lucy Beaumont expected her decision to back a junior TV worker's concerns about David Walliams to cost her a career in television. The reaction so far has gone the other way, with her book selling out and fans buying tour tickets, The Guardian reported on 15 November 2026.

## What Beaumont said and what happened next

Beaumont told Sky News this week that she did not think she would "work in telly again", and joked that she might become a pilates teacher. She said she was speaking out even though she believed many people in the industry already knew about the concerns and were not talking about them.

The response suggests the public sees it differently. Her book sold out in both paperback and hardback on Amazon soon after she discussed her fears. Many fans also posted online that they had booked tickets for her shows, some citing her decision to speak up as the reason.

Beaumont wrote in a Guardian article this week that she had received thousands of messages. "I've been flooded with support because people felt something," she wrote. She added that many women reading about the case recognised the position the young women were in.

## The background to the investigation

Beaumont has said she spoke to The Guardian and ITV News for their three-year joint investigation. Walliams is one of Britain's best-known television personalities and top-selling children's authors. According to The Guardian, multiple women have accused him of using his power and celebrity status to prey on young women and teenagers for sex. He denies any wrongdoing.

Beaumont's role was limited. She said a young TV worker approached her and alleged he had been asked to take young women to Walliams' dressing room. The Guardian notes that similar allegations emerged during its investigation, and that Beaumont did not witness any such behaviour herself.

## Why she says people stay quiet

Beaumont's career is well established. She has appeared on the BBC's [Celebrity Traitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Celebrity_Traitors), *Taskmaster* and [Michael McIntyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McIntyre)'s *The Wheel*. Even so, she said high-profile people stay silent because they fear losing work or being labelled a "trouble-causer". "We're all paid really well … no one wants to mess that up," she said.

She said she did not believe harassment was a widespread problem across television. She did say she went to journalists because there is "no HR department in TV". She said she used to work in schools and was trained in safeguarding, and that she acts whenever she hears a concerning rumour.

This is not the first time she has raised the issue. In 2023 she told a podcast that "predatory male behaviour" had held her back in comedy. The Guardian reports that many industry insiders share her belief that speaking out against powerful figures still carries a professional price.
