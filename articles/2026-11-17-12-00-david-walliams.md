A woman who has accused David Walliams of grooming her has said she backs a change in the law that would set a staggered age of consent, according to a report published by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/nov/16/woman-accusing-david-walliams-grooming-backs-staggered-age-of-consent-call) on 16 November. Walliams denies all the allegations against him.

## What Alex says about the law

The Guardian and ITV News carried out a joint investigation into claims that Walliams used his power and fame to pursue young women and teenagers. The woman, who is known only as Alex, told them she was 15 and studying for her GCSEs when she first met him. She alleges they began having sex when she was 17 and he was 34.

Speaking this week, Alex said a staggered age of consent would have spared her "a lot of trauma". As a parent herself, she said she would be proud to see such a change pass. In her view it would protect young people from adults who exploit the current law, while still letting teenagers form relationships with each other.

She also described growing up in a culture where this kind of behaviour was normalised. Had it been clearly illegal, she said, she believes she would have been warier and would have recognised it earlier for what it was.

## Why the debate has reopened

Alex spoke after Mia Latter waived her right to anonymity. Latter told The Sunday Times she was in an abusive three-month relationship with Brand when she was 16 and he was 30, and that she alleges he raped her. She has asked the UK government to make it illegal for adults above a certain age to have sex with 16- and 17-year-olds.

Under current law, sex with a 16- or 17-year-old is legal in the UK in most circumstances. Alex acknowledged this, but argued that context matters: a partner who is double your age, who has known you since you were young and who holds far more power in work, money and stature is, in her words, not acceptable.

## Walliams' response

The Guardian reports that Walliams' lawyers say the suggestion he groomed Alex is false. They say she did not raise any concerns about the sex at the time, and that he did not believe she had any. They add that he was mortified to learn their brief, consensual relationship had left her sad. Walliams has denied all the allegations in the investigation.

None of the claims has been tested in court, and this report relies on the Guardian's account.

## What happens next

The Guardian's report does not say whether the government will consider any change to the age of consent. Alex said she supports a law change that would "make it a lot harder for predators to act", and Latter's campaign is continuing in public.
