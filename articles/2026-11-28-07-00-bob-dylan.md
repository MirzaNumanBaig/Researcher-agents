**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) played BP Pulse Arena in Birmingham on 26 November 2026 as part of his Lights of Autumn Tour of the UK and Europe.
- KLOF Mag reports a 100-minute set with no phones, no introduction and very little lighting, ending in a standing ovation.
- Sister Rosetta Tharpe's "How Far From God" made its debut in the set that night, according to the review.
- The tour finishes with five nights at London's Royal Festival Hall: 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 December.

Bob Dylan has again turned a big arena into something closer to a lounge room. In KLOF Mag's Bob Dylan Birmingham review, the 85-year-old is described as a hooded, barely visible figure at his keyboard who held the crowd for 100 minutes without saying a word to them.

## Bob Dylan Birmingham review: what the night looked like

According to [KLOF Mag](https://klofmag.com/2026/11/live-review-bob-dylan-26-nov-2026-at-bp-pulse-arena-birmingham/), the show started at 8pm sharp. There was no support act or background music, and phones were not part of the evening. The stage was lit by a handful of standard lamps and some fairy lights scattered around the monitors, which the reviewer likened to the lighting of an ordinary living room.

Dylan walked on in a light-coloured hoodie with the hood up, moved the microphone and began with "Watching the River Flow". He moved on to "Love Sick" and "It Ain't Me, Babe", which drew cheers from pockets of the audience, then "Tryin' to Get to Heaven". The reviewer says his voice improved as the set went on, singling out a sensitive "Sugar Baby".

Other highlights were "Black Rider" from Rough and Rowdy Ways and "Every Grain of Sand" from Shot of Love. The second of those closed the night and was one of two harmonica moments.

The review is not all praise. It notes several brief piano passages that felt hammered, though the band pulled things back on track. Those readers who have followed our earlier coverage of [Bob Dylan opening the UK tour in Bournemouth](https://moduscarry.com/bob-dylan-uk-tour-bournemouth/) will recognise the same unshowy approach.

## A debut cover and a steady band

The covers are a running feature of this tour. KLOF Mag says Bo Diddley's "I Can Tell" and "I'll Make It All Up to You", written by Charlie Rich, have stayed in the set for roughly 18 months. They were joined in Birmingham by "How Far From God", a Sister Rosetta Tharpe song the reviewer describes as 80 years old, which was played for the first time that night.

Behind Dylan was his quartet: guitarists Julian Lage and Joel Paterson, bassist Tony Garnier and drummer Anton Fig. The reviewer credits Fig's subdued, expressive drumming with holding the pace together.

The reviewer says the evening was less about individual songs than about building a world, a pace that stays steady and a cumulative effect they call deeply mesmerising. Nobody, they add, goes to a Dylan show for the greatest hits or the spectacle.

When it ended, Dylan turned his back to the crowd, stretched his legs, bowed, briefly clapped and walked off to a standing ovation.

## A tour with very little downtime

The workload behind this show is considerable. KLOF Mag notes that the extended Rough and Rowdy Ways World Wide Tour wrapped up in Texas in May. Within weeks Dylan was back on the road for The Long Hot Summer '26 Tour, a US run of 35 dates that continued to August. By mid-October he was in Norway to start the Lights of Autumn Tour in the UK and Europe.

For comparison, many artists his age have long since stopped touring at all. Dylan turned 85 in May, according to NewsBreak.

## The old songs are getting a fresh look

The Birmingham show landed in a week of Dylan retrospectives. [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/1988-the-traveling-wilburys-wrote-a-song-bob-dylan-couldnt-remember/) revisited the Traveling Wilburys, recalling that [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison) watched "Tweeter and the Monkey Man" take shape in 1988 and said Dylan changed the song after it was first written down. Harrison said he and Jeff Lynne remembered a passage Dylan had forgotten, and it became the chorus.

[Classical-Music.com](https://www.classical-music.com/rock/bob-dylan-self-portrait) looked back at Self Portrait, the double album released on 8 June 1970 that bewildered critics. It recalls Greil Marcus opening his Rolling Stone review by asking, "What is this s***?"

[That Eric Alper](https://www.thatericalper.com/2026/11/27/how-bob-dylan-turned-folk-music-into-a-voice-of-protest/) retraced how Dylan, at 22, sang "Only a Pawn in Their Game" in Greenwood, Mississippi, after the murder of Medgar Evers. The piece also notes that Peter, Paul and Mary's version of "Blowin' in the Wind" reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1963.

## What happens next

The Lights of Autumn Tour concludes at London's Royal Festival Hall on 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 December, according to KLOF Mag. Based on Birmingham, audiences can expect no phones, little light and a set that favours deep cuts and covers over the hits. The setlist has shifted during the tour, so the London shows may bring more surprises.
