**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) paid musical tribute to John Lennon on "Roll On John", released on the 2012 album Tempest.
- Lennon died in 1980, so the song arrived more than 30 years later, according to American Songwriter.
- The lyrics weave in Lennon's own song titles, including "A Day in the Life", "Come Together" and "The Ballad of John and Yoko".
- A public tour of Lennon's childhood home in Liverpool, a few years before the release, helped spark the song.

When John Lennon died in 1980, plenty of musicians rushed out a farewell. Bob Dylan did not. More than three decades later, on the 2012 album Tempest, he finally answered with "Roll On John", a tribute that [American Songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Songwriter) recently revisited in a feature on the song's origins.

## Why Dylan waited so long

The magazine notes that Dylan and Lennon met at the peak of their fame in the 1960s. Its account says Dylan introduced The Beatles to marijuana when he first met them in 1964, and points to well-known footage of the two trading one-liners in the back of a cab a few years later. How close they really were as friends is, in the magazine's words, hard to say.

By the 1970s they had largely gone separate ways. American Songwriter says Lennon often commented on Dylan in interviews, sometimes unkindly, and even recorded an unreleased satire of Dylan's "Born Again" period called "Serve Yourself".

After Lennon's death, others moved quickly. The magazine lists Elton John, [Paul Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Simon), [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney) and [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison) among the artists who wrote and recorded songs about Lennon not long after he died. Dylan stayed out of that line, which the piece describes as typically counterintuitive.

## A rare tribute from a writer who rarely did them

Dylan was not known for songs about people who had died. According to the report, the few he did write were about colourful and somewhat controversial figures, such as the gangster Joe Gallo and the comedian [Lenny Bruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Bruce). That makes a song for Lennon notable on its own.

What finally prompted it? The article says Dylan took a public tour of Lennon's childhood home in Liverpool a few years before Tempest came out. Few of the biographical details he may have picked up made it into the lyrics, but the visit's timing clearly set him thinking about the former Beatle.

## What the song actually says

Anyone expecting a plain, sentimental eulogy would have been surprised. American Songwriter describes "Roll On John" as offbeat, with Dylan slipping in fragments of Lennon's lyrics and nods to his songs. The magazine names "A Day in the Life", "Come Together" and "The Ballad of John and Yoko" among the allusions.

The song also runs on a sailing metaphor. As the magazine reads it, Dylan suggests Lennon was a prisoner of the fame his music brought, comparing him to a galley slave and urging him, as if he were still alive, to shove off quickly. One line the article quotes is "Leave right now, you won't be far from wrong."

Dylan does not look away from how Lennon died either. The magazine argues that Lennon, who never sugar-coated things, would probably have appreciated the honesty. The song ends gently, imagining a peaceful final destination with the lines "Shine your light" and "You burned so bright".

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Bob Dylan's song about John Lennon called?

It is called "Roll On John" and appeared on Dylan's 2012 album Tempest, according to American Songwriter.

### Why did Dylan wait so long to write about Lennon?

The sources do not give a firm reason. American Songwriter says he did not rush to follow the tributes released soon after Lennon's death in 1980, and that a tour of Lennon's childhood home in Liverpool a few years before 2012 helped inspire the song.

### Does the song quote Lennon's music?

Yes. The lyrics reference Lennon songs including "A Day in the Life", "Come Together" and "The Ballad of John and Yoko", the magazine reports.
