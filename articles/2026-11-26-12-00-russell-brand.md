**Quick summary**

- Laura Gallacher visited [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) at HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire on Wednesday, 25 November, with the couple's three children, according to Page Six.
- It was her second visit in about a week, after Brand was sentenced to 10 years and six months earlier this month.
- A prison statement Brand wrote is being "urgently investigated", the Prime Minister's Office said, calling it "appalling".
- Herefordshire Council says its libraries hold no Brand books, and the publishers have withdrawn those still on sale.

Russell Brand's wife brought their three young children to see him in prison on Wednesday, the latest chapter in a fortnight that has moved from verdict to sentence to a row over what he is allowed to say from behind bars. [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/25/celebrity-news/russell-brands-wife-three-children-visit-disgraced-comedian-in-prison/) reported that Laura Gallacher was photographed arriving at HMP Littlehey, a low-security prison in Cambridgeshire, with two daughters and a 3-year-old son.

## What happened at HMP Littlehey

According to Page Six, Gallacher was also accompanied by Brand's security guard, Joe McCann, and an unidentified woman. McCann reportedly drove her home after the visit, as he did when she went to the prison alone the previous week. On that earlier trip she was pictured carrying a folder of paperwork.

A source told The Sun at the time that Gallacher "looked a bit shaken" leaving the jail, adding that the reality of her husband's situation seemed to have begun to sink in. That is one anonymous account, and Page Six did not independently confirm it.

The couple married in 2017. Gallacher sat through the trial, which ended with Brand, 51, convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women. When he was sentenced to 10 years and six months, she was reportedly heard telling him, "I love you, I'll be OK."

## The statement under investigation

The visits come amid a dispute over a message Brand wrote in prison. Gallacher read it aloud during a recent episode of the couple's online programme, "Sunday Service with The Brands", and in it Brand maintained his innocence. Page Six quotes him saying he did not know which other men there were not guilty, and that those who were guilty were people "that Christ died to save".

The Prime Minister's Office later called the message "appalling" and said it was being "urgently investigated". A source told Page Six that Brand had reportedly faced "punishment" for releasing it without permission. Gallacher has since said she "understands" the rules and would "absolutely honor" them, adding that she would remain "proud to be a wife, a mother and a woman defending her family".

## Brand's books come off the shelves

The fallout has also reached the library shelf. [The Hereford Times](https://www.herefordtimes.com/news/26759644.herefordshire-libraries-decide-brand-walliams-books/) reported that Herefordshire Council confirmed its libraries hold no books by Brand. They do hold titles by [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), who is the subject of separate allegations and a police inquiry.

The council said its stock follows guidance from the Chartered Institute of Library and Information Professionals, which backs access to published material unless the content is prohibited by law. It added that having a title in stock "should not be taken as an endorsement" of an author or their alleged actions.

Brand published eight books between 2007 and 2019. The first, *My Booky Wook*, sold more than 600,000 copies, the paper said. Those still on sale have been withdrawn by their publishers since the conviction.
