**Quick summary**

- [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson) has turned her childhood home on Vancouver Island, Canada, into a working farm, according to a Yahoo report published on 26 October 2026.
- The vegetable beds reportedly cover about 15,000 square feet.
- Surplus produce is said to go to food banks, soup kitchens, churches and neighbours.
- The account comes second-hand, via the community site Localize, which cited Hola, and has not been confirmed by Anderson in the sources reviewed.

Pamela Anderson's return to her family home on Vancouver Island has grown into a large vegetable garden that reportedly feeds people well beyond her own household. [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/pamela-andersons-15-000-square-021500766.html) reported on 26 October 2026 that the plots now cover roughly 15,000 square feet, and that the extra harvest is shared with local charities and neighbours.

## From family home to working farm

According to Yahoo, the project began after Anderson moved back to her family's Vancouver Island home in 2020. The property is the place where she grew up, and the report says the move changed how the land is used.

For scale, 15,000 square feet is a little under 1,400 square metres. That is several times the size of a typical suburban Australian backyard vegetable patch, though the sources do not say how many beds or crops are involved.

Yahoo credits the detail to Localize, a community food-sharing site, which in turn cited Hola. That chain matters. The figures have not been independently measured in the material reviewed here, so they are best read as reported rather than verified.

## Where the harvest goes

The most striking line in the report is about the surplus. Localize said the extra harvest goes to food banks, soup kitchens, churches and neighbours, as relayed by Yahoo. No tonnage, weekly volumes or named charities were given, so the scale of the giving is unclear.

Readers responded warmly. One commenter on the original post wrote that a garden can help many people, and another said they had worked with Anderson and found her gracious. Those are personal comments rather than evidence, but they show how the story landed.

## Part of a wider movement

Yahoo placed the story alongside other community growing projects. It pointed to neighbours in Seattle who turned 7 acres of lawn into a free food forest, and to Browns Mill Park in Atlanta, described as America's largest free food forest. It also mentioned [Ron Finley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Finley)'s neighbourhood gardening push in Los Angeles as a response to food deserts.

Against that backdrop, Anderson's garden is one more example of private land being used to share food. The difference is the name attached to it.
