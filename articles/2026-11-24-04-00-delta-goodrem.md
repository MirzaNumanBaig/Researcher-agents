**Quick summary**

- Australian music broadcaster Molly Meldrum died early on Monday 23 November at a hospital in Melbourne, according to a statement from his family.
- [Delta Goodrem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Goodrem) paid tribute on Instagram, writing that waking up to the news "on the other side of the world" was hard.
- [Peter Andre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Andre) and [Brian McFadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_McFadden) also posted messages crediting him with helping their careers.
- Goodrem is in London for the current series of [Strictly Come Dancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strictly_Come_Dancing) and is through to week ten.

[Delta Goodrem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_Goodrem) has led the tributes to [Molly Meldrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molly_Meldrum), the broadcaster and music promoter who died in Melbourne early on Monday 23 November. Goodrem, who is in Britain for Strictly Come Dancing, shared photos of the two together and called him "a rock and roll rebel", according to the [Manchester Evening News](https://www.manchestereveningnews.co.uk/news/showbiz-news/strictlys-delta-goodrem-says-this-34952261).

## What Delta Goodrem wrote

Goodrem posted her message on Instagram on Monday morning, UK time. "This is hard to wake up to on the other side of the world," she wrote. She said she knew "there is an angel, a rock and roll rebel in the sky watching over us all", and called Meldrum "truly one of a kind" who was "so loved by us all". She added that she would treasure the memories forever.

The timing made it a difficult morning. Goodrem had spent the weekend in Blackpool, where Strictly broadcast week nine from the Tower Ballroom. She was away from home and, as her post made clear, away from the people who were grieving him in Australia.

## How Molly Meldrum's family announced his death

In a statement quoted by the Manchester Evening News, Meldrum's family said he "passed away peacefully" at a Melbourne hospital in the early hours of Monday. It described him as "affectionately dubbed 'the Guru'" and said he was "the heartbeat, the champion and the spirit of Australian music".

The statement came from his partner, Yan Wongngam, and the wider family. They thanked Wongngam for caring for Meldrum "with great love and devotion", particularly in his final years, and thanked his friends and the staff at Epworth Richmond.

The same report notes that Meldrum was known for backing local artists such as INXS and [Kylie Minogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kylie_Minogue), as well as international acts including ABBA. He also interviewed a long list of stars, among them King Charles.

A Herald Sun headline says Goodrem, Minogue and Barnes all honoured him. The full article was not available to us, so we can't report what Minogue or Barnes said.

## Other stars who paid tribute

Peter Andre said the news "really hurts". He wrote that without Meldrum he would not have had his chances in the entertainment industry "when it was near impossible to get noticed", and that Meldrum "believed in me from day one". Andre also said he had visited Meldrum at home recently to talk over old times.

Brian McFadden, formerly of Westlife, described himself as devastated. He wrote that Meldrum was one of the first people to interview him in Australia and to champion his career there, and called him "a true rock n roll legend".

## Strictly, meanwhile

Goodrem's tribute landed the morning after a tense night on the show. She and her partner [Kai Widdrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Widdrington) scored 36 for a quickstep on Saturday 21 November, their highest mark of the series. On Sunday they faced their first dance-off against [Jaime Winstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Winstone) and [Carlos Gu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Gu), and the judges saved them unanimously, according to the [Manchester Evening News](https://www.manchestereveningnews.co.uk/news/tv/strictlys-delta-goodrem-speaks-out-34951839).
