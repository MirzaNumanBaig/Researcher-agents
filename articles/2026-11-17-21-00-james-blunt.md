**Quick summary**

- [James Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blunt) posted a screenshot of a deed poll application to change his name from James Hillier Blount to Ryan Bel Air.
- It follows his pledge to become "Ryan Air" if his album Airplane Mode reached number one, which it did, according to the Official Charts Company.
- He captioned the post "This cost me £53.05"; no outlet has confirmed the application was approved.
- He will headline Lincoln Castle on Wednesday 16 June as part of his European tour.

James Blunt appears to have kept his word. After Airplane Mode went to number one in the UK, the singer shared a screenshot of a deed poll form asking to swap James Hillier Blount for Ryan Bel Air, a nod to the budget airline Ryanair. Reports on Tuesday said it was the **James Blunt name change** his fans had been demanding for a week.

## What Blunt actually posted

According to [The Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/music/news/james-blunt-name-change-airplane-mode-album-b3086232.html), the Celebrity Traitors finalist posted the screenshot to Instagram with the caption "This cost me £53.05". Nine.com.au converted that to about A$103 and noted he had edited out his address, email and phone number.

The papers stop short of calling it done. Outlets including the Somerset County Gazette said he "appeared" to have confirmed a legal change, and rova reported that Blunt had not said whether the application was approved or whether he plans to revert. Nine's own report said it would keep calling him James Blunt for now.

## The bet that started it

Blunt made the pledge in a video before the album came out. The 52-year-old reminded viewers that he had promised in 2024 to become Blunty McBluntface if the 20th-anniversary edition of [Back To Bedlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_to_Bedlam) hit number one. It peaked at number seven, so he stayed Blunt. "Now, I'm giving you a second chance," he said, according to rova.

Ryanair's reply under the video was short: "Our lawyers will be in touch."

Nine.com.au traced part of the gag to an X user, JoeBangles11, who has long asked Blunt for his favourite cheese. Last week he offered £1000 to charity if Blunt answered, and said he would change his own name if the album topped the chart. Blunt replied "Deal", and the cheese turned out to be Délice de Bourgogne. When the album won, the fan reportedly donated and renamed himself after the cheese. Blunt then posted a weekend Instagram Reel in which a call from "Ryanair" interrupts him. "You won't sue me. You're too cheap," he told the caller.

## A number one 19 years in the making

The stunt rests on a real chart result. The Official Charts Company says Airplane Mode is Blunt's first UK number one album in 19 years. His previous chart-topper was All The Lost Souls in 2007, while Back To Bedlam spent 10 weeks at number one in 2005.

"I can't tell you how grateful I am to every single person who has gone out there and downloaded, or bought, or streamed my album," Blunt told the Official Charts Company.

Lincolnshire County Council describes the eighth studio album as heavily influenced by 90s Britpop, with tracks including Circles, Oh Daisy and Weight Of It All. It is his third chart-topper. The record also carries the singles Tastes Like Summer and Weight of It All, according to rova.

## Celebrity Traitors and a Lincoln date

Blunt's profile has been boosted by The Celebrity Traitors, where he reached the final before being banished by fellow faithfuls Sebastian "Bash" Croft and Romesh Ranganathan, who went on to win. He told BBC Radio 2 he felt "betrayed" by Ranganathan but insisted they remain close friends, according to The Independent.

On the touring side, Lincolnshire County Council announced that Blunt will headline Live at Lincoln Castle on Wednesday 16 June, with Nathan Evans & The Saint PHNX Band as special guests. Tickets went on sale at 10am on Friday through lincolncastle.com and Ticketmaster UK. The council said the show is part of his Airplane Mode European tour and could be his first in the city. Blunt's hits, including [You're Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You're_Beautiful) and [Goodbye My Lover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodbye_My_Lover), are expected alongside new songs.

Entertainment Weekly reported that he told The Times on 14 November that being linked online to an anonymous musician in Hayden Panettiere's memoir was "deeply troubling".
