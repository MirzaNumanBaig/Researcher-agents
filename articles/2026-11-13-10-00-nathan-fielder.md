[Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder)'s documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes is still prompting close readings, and two new pieces published on 12 November focus on very small details: a single word and a pair of shoes. The film, made with [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim), follows Holmes in the weeks before she went to prison. Here is what each piece says, and why the details matter.

## The film in brief

*You Can See Everything* follows Holmes during her final weeks of freedom, according to a column carried by [Wyoming News Now](https://www.wyomingnewsnow.tv/news/entertainment/what-nathan-fielder-s-you-can-see-everything-reveals-about-the-delicate-balance-between-ambition/article_df6fd5cd-2701-5fd3-b58a-2119affa0f17.html). [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/new-balance-nathan-fielder-992-new/) calls it a three-hour odyssey and says it ended with Holmes serving an 11-year sentence for defrauding investors.

Fielder and Oppenheim appeared at a Q&A for the film at the 29th SCAD Savannah Film Festival in Georgia on 27 October 2026, the Wyoming News Now photo caption notes. Its central question, per Highsnobiety, is who deserves to be believed and how people tailor a performance to the story they want told.

For readers who missed it, our earlier coverage of the film includes [Amanda Seyfried's reaction to *You Can See Everything*](https://moduscarry.com/nathan-fielder-documentary-amanda-seyfried/), and the full run of stories sits on the [Nathan Fielder tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/nathan-fielder/).

## One small word: "can"

The column, written from the perspective of V2 Communications for people who communicate technology for a living, centres on one exchange. Fielder questions Holmes about Theranos claims that its blood-testing technology could perform a wide range of diagnostic tests.

Holmes defends the wording, the column says, arguing the technology was capable of this with software that had not yet been developed. Fielder pushes back, suggesting that when someone says a machine can do something, most listeners assume it can do it now.

The author draws a wider lesson. Statements such as "an AI platform can detect anomalies" may rest on results from a controlled setting, not on proven real-world performance. The column argues that healthcare is especially exposed, because the gap between scientific promise and demonstrated clinical benefit shapes what patients and providers expect.

It stops short of saying ambition is the problem. Emerging companies must describe a future that does not yet exist, it says, but the bigger the promise, the easier it becomes to blur potential and proof. Its suggested test is simple: can the technology do this today, or is it a goal?

Worth noting: this is a communications-industry column using the film as an example, not a review of it.

## The sneakers on screen

Highsnobiety takes a lighter route into the same themes. Its writer points to the grey New Balance sneakers Fielder wears for the whole film, which it identifies as either the 992 or the 990v6. The outlet says it checked with sneaker enthusiasts who had seen the film, so the exact model is an informed guess, not a confirmed detail.

The piece links the shoes to Silicon Valley dress codes. It notes that the 990-series look is associated with late Apple chief executive Steve Jobs, and that Holmes herself once copied Jobs's black turtleneck. It also says fans on Reddit spotted Fielder in grey 990v6 sneakers in the latest season of *The Rehearsal* and at an airport with a fan.

Holmes appears with the brand too. Highsnobiety says a red [New Balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Balance) shoebox sits on the table in a scene where she chooses what to wear to report to prison, and that news clips show her in white 750s on the day she arrived at the federal facility in Bryan, Texas.

The writer does not claim any of this is product placement and calls the detail a likely happy accident. The argument is that the "humble" shoe suits a film so focused on the performance of honesty that viewers start to question the documentarian too. Highsnobiety adds that it has affiliate partnerships and may earn commission from purchases.
