## Miranda Kerr's tree gets the trend treatment

The Australian model and entrepreneur has put up a red and gold Christmas tree, and design site Homes & Gardens says it captures how people are decorating for the 2026 holidays. The publication's feature, published on 11 November, describes the tree as [Miranda Kerr's](https://www.mirandakerr.com) take on a combination that feels both traditional and fresh. The image comes from a post on her [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/mirandakerr/) account.

According to Homes & Gardens, the tree is a live one, wrapped in red and gold ribbon and lit with white lights. It carries a mix of gold and red glass ornaments, with sprays of red berries tucked in for a natural finish. The magazine calls the result inviting and festive without going over the top.

## Why ribbon is the detail to copy

The feature singles out ribbon as one of the season's biggest Christmas trends. Interior designer Kanika Bakshi Khurana, founder of Kanika Design, told the publication that oversized bows and statement ribbons are now doing more than finishing a tree. In her view, a large velvet bow or a cascade of ribbon can be the focal point, with at least one major decorative element repeated through the rest of the tree in simpler forms.

That matches what Homes & Gardens sees on Kerr's tree, where the ribbon runs through the branches rather than sitting as a single topper.

## Warm gold replaces cool chrome

The second trend the magazine ties to the tree is a move towards warm metallics. Nancy Soriano, a design expert at Perigold, said she is drawn this year to deep forest greens, burgundy and oxblood, warm chocolate browns and soft creams, finished with antique gold and brass. She described these colours as layered and lasting rather than trendy.

Soriano also advised building the look through texture as much as colour. Her suggestions include velvet ribbon in place of satin, detailed glass ornaments, wood or aged metal, and a tree skirt in mohair, cashmere or faux fur. On finishes, she recommended letting metallics glow rather than shine, since warm brass and antique gold read as more elevated than high-polish chrome, especially by candlelight.

Homes & Gardens notes that the gold on Kerr's tree reflects that retreat from the chrome tones of recent seasons.
