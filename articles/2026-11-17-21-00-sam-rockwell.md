**Quick summary**

- [Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell) and John Malkovich star as CIA agents in [Wild Horse Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine), written and directed by [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh).
- The film opened in the United States on 6 November 2026 and runs 118 minutes, according to Comic Book Resources.
- The reviewer calls it funny and meditative, with a strong final act and an ending that hits hard.
- Criticisms include a slow pace and a friendship dynamic that echoes McDonagh's earlier film *The Banshees of Inisherin*.

Sam Rockwell's new spy comedy is drawing warm notes from critics. In a review published on 16 November, [Comic Book Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_Book_Resources) says Rockwell and Malkovich bring easy chemistry to *Wild Horse Nine*, a story about two CIA operatives that is less about espionage than about friendship and mortality.

## What Wild Horse Nine is about

According to the CBR review, Rockwell plays Lee and Malkovich plays Chris, two agents working in Chile in the lead-up to the 1973 coup. Much of the story unfolds on Easter Island, where the pair travel to fulfil Chris's final wishes after he receives a fatal diagnosis.

Chris claims to have taken part in several coups, including a secret operation in Dallas that becomes important in the last act, and decides to write a memoir. Lee, meanwhile, appears to be working with their boss, MJ, against Chris's wishes. The reviewer says his choice between his friend and his job carries dangerous consequences for his family back in the US.

## The cast and the look of the film

Steve Buscemi plays MJ, Parker Posey plays Lee's wife, Marge, and Mariana di Girolamo plays Monica. CBR singles out all three for praise alongside the two leads.

The review also credits the cinematography, which mixes digital photography with Super 8 and 16mm footage. McDonagh produced the film with Anita Overland, Graham Broadbent and Peter Czernin.

## What the review criticises

The verdict is not unqualified. CBR says the pace is slow and that there is little action or tension for a film sold as a spy thriller. It also finds the dynamic between Chris and Lee close to the one in McDonagh's previous film, *The Banshees of Inisherin*, which earned nine Oscar nominations and three Golden Globes.

The reviewer describes the ending as violent and gut-punching, but does not spell out what happens, and neither will we.
