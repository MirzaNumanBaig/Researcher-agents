**Quick summary**

- [Druski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druski) hosted [Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live) on 7 November 2026, the first host whose career was built on social media.
- Yahoo reports that The Strokes were the musical guest and that Emma Stone appeared in his monologue.
- His Elizabeth Holmes spoof, "Dru Can See Everything", drew the most attention from critics.
- His semi-scripted Netflix comedy, Collect & Praise, premieres on 1 December, according to Deadline.

Druski, whose real name is Drew Desbordes, stepped onto the Studio 8H stage on Saturday 7 November as the first creator to host Saturday Night Live. The night brought a surprise from Emma Stone, a crowded curtain call and a sketch that critics were still discussing two days later. Here is what happened and why the booking matters beyond one episode.

## A curtain call full of famous faces

Yahoo reports that Chris Rock, Dave Chappelle and Michael B. Jordan joined the stage as the credits rolled, giving the debut host a send-off packed with star power. Emma Stone, who appeared during his opening monologue, stayed for the goodnights. According to Yahoo, she stars with him in the upcoming comedy The Catch.

Austin Butler and Walton Goggins were also in the building while working on Miami Vice '85 in the New York area, Yahoo says. Goggins joined The Strokes on bongos during their set. Texas Senator-elect James Talarico, who appeared in the episode's post-election cold open, rounded out the group.

## The sketch everyone talked about

In his monologue, Druski said the show had given him two rules: no cursing and no whiteface. Yahoo notes he broke both in "Dru Can See Everything", a spoof of the A24 documentary You Can See Everything, in which he played convicted fraudster Elizabeth Holmes.

Esquire called it one of the night's best sketches and compared it with Eddie Murphy's 1984 "White Like Me" bit. The premise swaps Nathan Fielder for Druski as the man filming Holmes and her partner at home. Esquire singled out cast members Jane Wickline and Ben Marshall for carrying the four-minute piece, and praised the production team for building a convincing copy of the documentary's oceanfront rental in under a week. The writer admitted to being unsure whether the makeup gag was acceptable, so reaction is not uniform.

## Politics and a fitness skit

Yahoo also describes a sketch in which Druski played a BET political pundit alongside Kenan Thompson and Saidah Belo-Osagie. Pajiba, meanwhile, wrote about a pre-taped piece in which Druski and other cast members played Republican candidates apologising for their campaign rhetoric after the midterm elections. The Pajiba writer said it appeared online after the broadcast and described it as a cut-for-time sketch. That review is openly personal and political, so treat it as one viewer's opinion.

A third piece, "My Corner Guy", drew trade attention. Athletech News said it sets blunt feedback from a real trainer against the AI coaching offered by fitness wearables. Only the introduction of that article was readable, so the detail is limited.

## Why a creator hosting matters

Deadline ran a guest column by [Adam Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Goodman), founder of the creator-focused studio Invisible Narratives and a former president of Paramount Pictures' film group. He argues the booking is a programming decision, not a punchline, and that creators now test characters and formats with audiences in days rather than years.

He cites Nielsen data showing YouTube reached a record 15.8% of US television viewing in August, more than double Netflix's share. He also cautions that 30 million followers do not equal 30 million ticket buyers, and that audiences are not the same as owned franchises. As an example of letting demand lead, he points to the release of [Paranormal Activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranormal_Activity), which expanded city by city. Remember that Goodman runs a company in this field, so his view is an interested one.

## What happens next

Druski's next big test is Collect & Praise, which Deadline says premieres on Netflix on 1 December. The Catch, in which Yahoo says he appears with Emma Stone, has no release date in the sources we read.
