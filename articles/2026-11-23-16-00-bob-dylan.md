**Quick summary**

- Far Out Magazine says Bob Dylan's 1970 album Self Portrait gave American listeners their first real taste of Gordon Lightfoot.
- Dylan covered Lightfoot's song "Early Morning Rain", first released in 1965.
- Dylan has called Lightfoot a long-time mentor and said he can't think of a Lightfoot song he dislikes.
- A second Far Out piece revisits Paul Simon's 1966 Dylan parody, which Simon says was satire rather than a put-down.

The **Bob Dylan Self Portrait** album has a reputation as one of his strangest records, yet two fresh pieces from [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/self-portrait-one-of-bob-dylan-most-puzzling-albums/) argue it did real good for other songwriters. [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan)'s 1970 release carried a cover that introduced [Gordon Lightfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Lightfoot) to US audiences, and a second article looks back at how [Paul Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Simon) once poked fun at Dylan in song.

## Why Self Portrait confused Dylan's fans

According to Far Out, Self Portrait baffled many listeners at the time. Dylan set aside the poetic style that had made his name and filled the album with covers and unfinished live songs. The magazine is blunt about the result: it is not considered his best work.

The wider backdrop matters here. Far Out cites [Steve Van Zandt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Van_Zandt), who has said that The Beatles' arrival in America made young musicians want to form bands. Van Zandt also credits Dylan and the rise of folk rock with pulling American artists back to the top of the charts. Even so, the magazine says it remained very hard for a solo act writing personal songs to break through in the United States.

## How Gordon Lightfoot reached America

That was Lightfoot's problem. Far Out reports that he had plenty of success in Canada through the 1960s but struggled to get a foothold in the US, where his songs about feelings largely slipped by.

Dylan's choice changed that. On Self Portrait he covered "[Early Morning Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Morning_Rain)", a Lightfoot song first released in 1965. It tells of a man down on his luck and far from home. Some people say it was inspired by Lightfoot seeing a friend off at Los Angeles airport, though the magazine notes the meaning matters less than the exposure it gave him.

Dylan was a genuine fan. Far Out quotes him as saying he can't think of any Lightfoot song he doesn't like, and that "Lightfoot became a mentor for a long time." Lightfoot's first official US hit single, "If You Could Read My Mind", followed afterwards, according to the magazine. Far Out suggests Dylan's endorsement put the Canadian's name on the lips of music fans well beyond North America.

## Paul Simon's 1966 Dylan parody

The second Far Out story goes back further. In 1966, Simon and Garfunkel released "A Simple Desultory Philippic (Or How I Was Robert McNamara'd Into Submission)", a track on their third album, Parsley, Sage, Rosemary and Thyme. The magazine describes it as a parody aimed squarely at Dylan, who was by then moving from acoustic folk to electric sound.

Far Out says the recording imitates the folk rock of the time, with swirling organ and psychedelic guitar. [Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Simple_Desultory_Philippic_(or_How_I_Was_Robert_McNamara'd_into_Submission)) also pokes at Dylan's habit of packing lyrics with literary and pop culture references. One line has the narrator describing someone so unhip that, when you say Dylan, he thinks you mean [Dylan Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Thomas).

Simon has long said it was satire, not an attack on someone he has often praised as an inspiration. He told Rolling Stone, as quoted by Far Out, that his own voice sounds sincere and he cannot sound ironic, while "with Dylan, everything he sings has two meanings."

The magazine adds a possible background: it describes an awkward first meeting between the two men the week before Simon and Garfunkel's debut at Gerde's Folk City in Greenwich Village. That is Far Out's reading of events, reported as "reportedly" cold, and not a confirmed motive for the song.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Gordon Lightfoot song did Bob Dylan cover?
Dylan covered "Early Morning Rain" on his 1970 album Self Portrait. Lightfoot's version was first released in 1965.

### Did Bob Dylan admire Gordon Lightfoot?
Yes. Far Out quotes Dylan saying he can't think of a Lightfoot song he doesn't like, and that Lightfoot was a mentor to him for a long time.

### Was Paul Simon's Dylan parody an insult?
Simon says no. He has described it as satire and has often called Dylan an inspiration, according to Far Out.
