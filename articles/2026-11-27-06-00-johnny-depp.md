**Quick summary**

- [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) is among the first names announced for a Terry Reid tribute concert at London's O2 Shepherd's Bush Empire on 12 February 2027, according to Contactmusic.com.
- [Graham Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graham_Nash) and Imelda May are also on the bill, and organisers say more performers will follow.
- General tickets go on sale at 10am on 27 November 2026, priced at £65 to £75 plus fees.
- Separately, a new biography by Mark Seal revisits Depp's time on "21 Jump Street", Page Six reports.
- His film "Ebenezer" has made US$79.6 million worldwide and is losing 365 US cinemas in its third weekend.

Johnny Depp will take the stage in London in February for a one-night tribute to the late singer [Terry Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Reid), Contactmusic.com reports. The show, called Seed of Memory – Celebrating The Life and Music of Terry Reid, is set for 12 February 2027 at the O2 Shepherd's Bush Empire.

## Who is playing the Terry Reid tribute

Depp is joining Nash, Imelda May, PP Arnold, Rumer, Spike of The Quireboys and blues guitarist Toby Lee, according to the report. Organisers have said further acts will be announced before the night. The concert is presented by Academy Events, DMP, Décor and War Child.

The title comes from Reid's 1976 album Seed of Memory, which Nash produced. Reid was born in St Neots on 13 November 1949 and was nicknamed "Superlungs" for his voice. He became part of rock folklore after turning down the chance to front both Led Zeppelin and Deep Purple, choosing to keep building his own solo career. He died from cancer in Rancho Mirage, California, on 4 August 2025, aged 75.

It is not Depp's first tribute project this month. He and May appear on 20th Century Paddy – The Songs of Shane MacGowan, a record honouring the late Pogues frontman that also features Bruce Springsteen, U2, Tom Waits and Hozier.

## A biography revisits the "21 Jump Street" years

Depp's name is also in the headlines because of "The Many Faces of Johnny Depp", a new biography by author [Mark Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Seal). Page Six, which reported on the book, says Seal describes Depp's efforts to escape the second season of the teen police drama, which ran from 1987 to 1991.

Seal writes that Depp arrived on set dressed as Gandhi, set his underwear alight in protest at an uncleaned trailer, and wore a Band-Aid over his face for the camera. The book also says he was arrested and charged with assault after a confrontation with a security guard at a Vancouver hotel party, yet kept his role. These are the author's claims as reported by Page Six, and we have not verified them independently.

Page Six also quotes a 1990 interview in which Depp said he disagreed with the idea of police officers in schools. His pay rose from US$6,000 an episode in season one to US$12,000 and then US$45,000 in season three, the book says. [Holly Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Robinson_Peete) told Seal she confronted him in his trailer and described him as having "a really great heart" who hated being a teen pin-up. He turned to John Waters' "Cry-Baby" to shake off that image.

## Ebenezer loses cinemas as the totals climb

The actor's latest film, [Ti West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti_West)'s "Ebenezer", is a new take on the [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens) story with Depp as Scrooge. According to Collider, citing The Numbers, it drops from 3,545 to 3,180 US locations heading into its third weekend. Its takings fell 59% after a US$19 million opening weekend.

The worldwide total stands at US$79.6 million, including US$33.7 million domestically, against a reported US$38 million budget. ScreenRant notes that Thanksgiving week brought a crowd of new releases, and that the film holds a 55% critics score on Rotten Tomatoes against 90% from audiences. It puts a rough break-even point near US$95 million, so the film's final result is still open.
