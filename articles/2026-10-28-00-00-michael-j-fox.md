**Quick summary**

- [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) announced his retirement from acting in his 2020 memoir, *No Time Like the Future*.
- He returned in season 3 of Apple TV's *Shrinking*, which premiered on 28 January 2026, playing a Parkinson's patient named Gerry.
- Fox turned 65 on 9 June and has lived with Parkinson's disease for 35 years, according to DoYouRemember?.
- [Tracy Pollan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Pollan), his wife of more than three decades, is a central figure in the coverage of his return.

Michael J. Fox is working in front of the camera again, six years after he said he was done. The Back to the Future star appears in the third season of *Shrinking*, and a new profile from [DoYouRemember?](https://doyouremember.com/238867/michael-j-fox-acting-health-update) traces how a man who walked away in 2020 ended up with one of the busier years of his later career.

## How the comeback started with a phone call

Fox announced what he called his second retirement in *No Time Like the Future*. He told People that his short-term memory for lines was fading, and he wrote that long days and pages of dialogue were "best behind" him. He also left the door open, adding that things can change.

They did. *Shrinking* gave Harrison Ford the role of Dr. Paul Rhoades, a gruff therapist who lives with Parkinson's. Co-creator Bill Lawrence had worked with Fox on [Spin City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_City) and describes him as his first mentor. Fox told People he rang to ask, in effect, why a show about Parkinson's hadn't called him. Lawrence asked if he wanted in, and Fox said he'd love to.

## What he plays in Shrinking

Season 3 premiered on 28 January 2026. Fox plays Gerry, another Parkinson's patient whom Paul meets in a doctor's waiting room. According to TVLine, as cited by DoYouRemember?, Gerry appears in the premiere, then returns in episode 4, "The Field", and episode 7, "I Will Be Grape". The role grew after Fox said he'd happily do it again, and Lawrence has joked that he books Fox back within two weeks of hearing that.

## A career that started young

Fox was born in Edmonton on 9 June 1961 and grew up mostly in Burnaby, outside Vancouver. He was acting on Canadian television by 16 and left school at 17, according to his foundation's biography, then moved to Los Angeles in 1979. He has called leaving school a "stupid youthful mistake".

He found his breakout role as [Alex P. Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_P._Keaton) on [Family Ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Ties), which ran on NBC from 1982 to 1989 and won him three Emmys. In 1983 he also led the NBC TV movie *High School U.S.A.*, alongside [Bob Denver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Denver) and [Dawn Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_Wells). Pollan joined Family Ties in 1985 as Alex's girlfriend Ellen. They married on 16 July 1988 in Arlington, Vermont.

## Parkinson's, privacy and the foundation

Fox was diagnosed with young-onset Parkinson's in 1991, aged 29. He told CBC News in 2023 that he kept it to himself for seven years. In 1998 he told People magazine while starring in Spin City, and in 2000 he left the show and founded the Michael J. Fox Foundation for Parkinson's Research. DoYouRemember? describes it as the largest nonprofit funder of Parkinson's research in the world. Its work is detailed on the [foundation's website](https://www.michaeljfox.org).

The article's headline says Fox believes Pollan has "gone through a lot" to support him. The portion of the piece available to us does not include that remark in full, so we have not quoted it here.
