**Quick summary**

- Wrexham manager Phil Parkinson says visits from owners Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney are "always very uplifting" for staff, players and supporters.
- The comments followed a 1-1 Championship draw with Wolverhampton Wanderers at the Racecourse Ground, as reported by Goal.
- Wrexham fell behind to an Adam Armstrong goal and levelled two minutes later through Lewis O'Brien.
- Wrexham are 15th on 18 points; Wolves are fourth on 28, one behind leaders Birmingham.

[Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob McElhenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney) watched their side hold a Premier League-relegated Wolves to a draw, and the club's manager has said their presence matters well beyond the result. [Phil Parkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Parkinson) told reporters after the match, as [Goal](https://www.goal.com/en-au/news/phil-parkinson-ryan-reynolds-rob-mcelhenney-wrexham-draw-/blt32464db53ed3a04b) reported, that the owners' trips to North Wales lift everyone connected to the club.

## What Parkinson said about the owners

According to Goal, Parkinson described the effect of the visits in plain terms. "It's always very uplifting, I feel, for the people who work at the club, the staff and the players when Rob and Ryan come over," he said.

He went on to say the pair understand when people need a boost. Parkinson said the crowd is lifted too when the owners are in town, and that he stays in touch with them while they are in the United States, with an in-person chat a welcome bonus when they travel over.

It is a small but telling detail about how the Hollywood ownership works day to day. The two actors are best known to many Australian viewers through their screen work, yet the manager frames their role as hands-on and personal. ## How the match played out

Goal reports that Wrexham went a goal down to an Adam Armstrong strike. The response came quickly: Lewis O'Brien equalised just two minutes later, and the owners were on their feet in the directors' box.

Wrexham then had the better of the chance to win it. Goal says [Kieffer Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieffer_Moore) had a second-half opportunity, and his shot trickled past the post. At the other end, goalkeeper [Anthony Patterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Patterson) had to make a series of important saves to keep the point.

The fixture was also historic in a modest way. Goal notes it was the first meeting between the two clubs at the Racecourse Ground since 1924.

## Wolves left frustrated

The visitors saw it very differently. Wolves manager [Cesar Peixoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Peixoto) said he was angry and upset not to win, arguing his side controlled the game with more possession, shots and corners. Goal reports Wolves tested Patterson repeatedly through Fer Lopez and Tommy Doyle in a strong second half.

Parkinson was candid about his own team's second-half energy. He said Wrexham looked tired but hung on, and rated their honesty "10 out of 10".

## A useful week for Wrexham

The point caps a recovery. Wrexham lost 1-0 to Sheffield United on 30 October, then beat Middlesbrough before drawing with Wolves. "To go toe to toe with Middlesbrough and Wolves and to take four points, we have to be pleased," Parkinson said, according to Goal.

The table shows the gap that remains. Wrexham sit 15th on 18 points, while Wolves are fourth on 28, a single point behind leaders Birmingham.
