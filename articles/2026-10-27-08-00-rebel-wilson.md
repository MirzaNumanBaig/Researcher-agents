**Quick summary**

- A New South Wales Supreme Court judge found that statements on a website about producer Amanda Ghost were made with malice on [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson)'s part, according to RadarOnline, which cites ABC News.
- The same ruling dismissed a $15 million claim by AI Film Production Limited, the company led by [Amanda Ghost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Ghost).
- Wilson's company, Camp Sugar Productions, was awarded $359,000 in unpaid producer fees plus interest, and its legal costs.
- A related defamation case in California is still scheduled, with the trial date under discussion.

Justice Elisabeth Peden has given Rebel Wilson a split decision over *The Deb*, her directorial debut. The judge threw out a $15 million claim against the actress and awarded her company $359,000, but also made a finding about a website targeting a producer that Wilson has previously denied writing, [RadarOnline reported](https://radaronline.com/p/judge-rebel-wilson-anonymous-websites-indian-ghislaine-maxwell-wins-359k-fees/) on 26 October.

## What the judge found about the website

According to the report, the judgment concluded that Wilson had asked for a website to be created about Ghost, one of the producers of *The Deb*. The site, amandaghostsucks.com, made grave allegations about Ghost and had already featured in earlier litigation between the two sides.

Justice Peden found the website statements "were actuated with malice on Ms Wilson's part," RadarOnline quotes ABC News as saying. The report adds that evidence in a separate California dispute included messages involving crisis publicist [Melissa Nathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melissa_Nathan) and a discussion of an "untraceable site" said to cost $110,000.

RadarOnline notes that Wilson has disputed aspects of the allegations and previously denied writing the websites. These are findings in a civil judgment as reported by one outlet, and we have not seen the full decision.

## Why the $15 million claim failed

AI Film had argued that Wilson's social media posts and other conduct helped turn *The Deb* into a financial disappointment. The film took about $335,000 at the Australian box office.

The judge was not persuaded. "I am not satisfied AI Film … have demonstrated a causal connection between the misleading or deceptive representations and any loss or damage," she wrote, according to the report. The court also heard that international distributors had worried the film felt "small and distinctly Australian," which points to commercial factors beyond Wilson's posts.

Another finding went in Wilson's favour. The judge found that some additional producer fees, totalling $900,000, had been added to the film's budget without Wilson being told.

## The money, and the $10

Camp Sugar Productions succeeded on its cross-claim for unpaid producer fees. The court awarded it $359,000 plus interest, and Wilson's side was also awarded its legal costs.

The other side did not go home empty-handed, but only just. A subsidiary linked to the production received $10 in nominal damages over technical breaches involving a social media contract. You can read how the figures were first reported in our coverage, [Rebel Wilson wins $359,000 as The Deb producers get $10](https://moduscarry.com/rebel-wilson-wins-359000-as-the-deb-producers-get-10/).

## Wilson's reaction and a busy year in court

Wilson marked the result on Instagram with a joke about her law degree: "This girl never knew how much she was going to need that law degree!!" She also said defending herself had come at "an enormous personal, emotional and financial cost" and that she was proud she had "stood my ground — for myself and for others I was trying to protect." Everything we have published on the dispute is under the [Rebel Wilson tag](https://moduscarry.com/tag/rebel-wilson/).

The ruling is her second major Australian court win this year. In July, Federal Court Justice Elizabeth Raper dismissed a defamation case brought against Wilson by Charlotte MacInnes, the lead actor in *The Deb*. MacInnes has appealed. Our report on that earlier decision is [Rebel Wilson wins second court case over The Deb](https://moduscarry.com/rebel-wilson-wins-second-court-case-over-the-deb/).

## What happens next

The fight continues overseas. Ghost, Gregor Cameron and Vince Holden are pursuing a separate defamation case against Wilson in California. The trial was set for January 2027, though the parties have recently asked about moving it to March, as covered in [Rebel Wilson's The Deb trial may move to March 2027](https://moduscarry.com/rebel-wilsons-the-deb-trial-may-move-to-march-2027/). The MacInnes appeal also remains open. RadarOnline does not say whether AI Film will challenge the latest ruling.
