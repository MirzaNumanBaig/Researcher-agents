**Quick summary**

- Sydney-born actor [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), 21, says he had "a breakdown" while shooting *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, according to USA Today.
- He describes five straight days filming an intense sequence in Germany, plus painful inflamed lymph nodes during the shoot.
- His mother and younger brother stayed with him for a month afterwards, he says.
- The film opens in US theatres on 20 November.

The Joseph Zada Hunger Games breakdown is now on the record. The young Australian carrying the next [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) film has been candid about what it cost him. In an interview published on 18 November, Zada told USA Today he was "emotionally and physically distraught" during the shoot and suffered "excruciating pain" while making *Sunrise on the Reaping*, in which he plays the young Haymitch Abernathy.

## What Zada says happened on set

According to [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/11/18/joseph-zada-hunger-games-sunrise-reaping-interview/92799177007/), the production was relentless. Zada says he fell sick several times but could "not miss a day" of filming, and he worked through inflamed lymph nodes under his arms. Lifting his arms hurt so much that he managed no more than four hours of unbroken sleep a night, waking in pain and taking medication to get back to sleep.

He told the paper that when he looks at the end of his tether on screen, that exhaustion is "very real". The low point came during a five-day stretch filming one intense sequence in Germany. "I called my mom," he said, and she arrived within a day with his little brother. They looked after him for a month, he says.

These are Zada's own accounts of the shoot, given in a promotional interview. Neither the studio nor his family has commented in the sources reviewed here.

## From pub shifts to Panem

The story lands harder because of how recent his break is. Zada is the son and brother of actors, and he says he had "no interest" in acting until his father, Jeremy Cumpston, made him perform in a small production of *Romeo and Juliet* as a favour to a friend. He dropped out of high school to pursue it, then went four years of auditioning without a single booked role, returning to pub work for a while.

His first recognition came on television, in *Invisible Boys* and *We Were Liars*, and more recently in Netflix's *East of Eden*. *Sunrise on the Reaping* is his first major Hollywood film. Director Francis Lawrence told USA Today the search for Haymitch covered "thousands and thousands" of candidates and that the team put Zada "through the wringer" with repeated auditions.

He learned he had won the part on 23 April 2025, he says, and woke "quite hungover" to a call from his manager asking him to accept an Instagram collaboration that announced the casting before he had told most of his friends. His phone rang 200 times that day.

## A franchise with a lot riding on it

The film is set 24 years before the original story and shows how Haymitch, the drunken mentor played by Woody Harrelson in the earlier films, won his games. Zada says he studied everything Harrelson has made and later fired "thousands" of questions at him. He also credits Harrelson with putting him at ease. See also [Joseph Zada Studied a Month of Woody Harrelson Films](https://moduscarry.com/joseph-zada-woody-harrelson/).

The pressure is commercial as well as personal. Reviewing the film for The Sydney Morning Herald, the critic notes it is the second instalment based on [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins)' novels to proceed without Jennifer Lawrence's Katniss Everdeen. The paper reports that studio Lionsgate badly needs a big hit to ease its debts, and that young Australian fans have been lining up for a glimpse of Zada and co-star [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace). We covered their dynamic in [Joseph Zada Praises Mckenna Grace at Hunger Games Premiere](https://moduscarry.com/joseph-zada-hunger-games-premiere/).
