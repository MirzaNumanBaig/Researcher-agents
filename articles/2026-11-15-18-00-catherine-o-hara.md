- [Parker Posey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Posey) became emotional on "The Kyle Buchanan Show" while remembering [Catherine O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_O'Hara).
- She spoke about the Christopher Guest films "Best in Show" and "A Mighty Wind", according to TheWrap.
- Posey singled out the bond between O'Hara and Eugene Levy, who worked together for about 30 years.
- O'Hara died in January at the age of 71 after a brief illness, TheWrap reports.

## Parker Posey on the Guest film sets

Parker Posey was visibly moved this week when asked about Catherine O'Hara, who died in January. Appearing on Friday's "The Kyle Buchanan Show", Posey was asked about working alongside O'Hara and [Eugene Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Levy) on [Christopher Guest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Guest) comedies, including "A Mighty Wind", [TheWrap reported](https://www.thewrap.com/culture-lifestyle/culture/parker-posey-remembers-catherine-o-hara/).

Posey said watching the two performers "made me sob". She described the love between them as beautiful and noted they had worked together for roughly 30 years. She apologised for crying, then carried on.

## The walk in "Best in Show"

Posey recalled O'Hara working from "a completely generous place". She pointed to the limp O'Hara's character has in "Best in Show", saying the actress came to Guest with an idea for how to walk into the arena. In Posey's telling, O'Hara found a way to make her kneecap pop out, and it was one of the funniest things she had seen.

The detail matters because Guest's films rely on improvisation. Posey's account suggests O'Hara arrived with physical ideas ready and offered them freely to the director and the cast, rather than guarding them.

## How the industry remembered O'Hara

TheWrap notes that tributes came quickly when O'Hara died at home at the start of the year. [Dan Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Levy_(Canadian_actor)), who played her son in [Schitt's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schitt's_Creek), wrote that he had been lucky to "dance in the warm glow" of her brilliance. He added that she was extended family to him long before she played his mother on screen.

Director Martin Scorsese said losing her felt "impossible". He noted that different audiences know her from different places: "Schitt's Creek", the "Home Alone" films, "Beetlejuice" or the Guest comedies. For him, and many of his friends, it is the sketch show SCTV, where she created characters such as Lola Heatherton and Dusty Towne. Scorsese, who worked with her on "After Hours", called her a true comic genius.
