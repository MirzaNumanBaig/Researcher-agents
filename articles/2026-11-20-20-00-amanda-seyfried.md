**Quick summary**

- [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) voices Cinderella in Netflix's animated film Steps, which Radio Times says is now streaming.
- She told Radio Times that singing for animation is "so much harder" than live action, and that she had to stop and cry while recording one song.
- Separately, she sang ABBA's The Winner Takes It All in a new video with two social media personalities, as talk of a third Mamma Mia film continues.
- Her December includes a Saturday Night Live hosting debut and the return of Ted: The Animated Series.

Amanda Seyfried says one song in her new Netflix film Steps hit her hard enough that she had to put the microphone down and cry. The actor voices Cinderella in the animated fantasy, and she described the experience to [Radio Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Times) as the toughest singing job of her career.

## Why the Steps song was so hard

Seyfried has plenty of screen singing behind her, but she told the magazine that a recording booth is a different test. "It's so much harder than anything else," she said, adding that she felt real anxiety walking into the booth for four-hour stretches.

Her explanation was simple. Voice work, she said, is a huge instrument with nobody around you, and you are hoping to carry the energy of the character and the story on your own. She noted she has not done much voiceover in her life.

Then came the song. "I cried when I recorded that. I had to take a break and cry," she said, according to Radio Times. She was still upbeat about it, calling the job a beautiful challenge and saying it is worth it for a film that puts adults and children in a cinema together.

## What Steps is about

Steps is directed by Alyce Tzue and John Ripa. It retells Cinderella from the point of view of her supposedly wicked stepsisters. In the official synopsis quoted by Radio Times, one sister, Lilith, steals the Fairy Godmother's wand and hijacks the Royal Ball with her sibling Margot. That wrecks the familiar story and leaves the kingdom under the rule of a schemer named Priscilla.

Lilith must then team up with Cinderella to take on biker trolls and escape the Screaming Woods, in a quest to save both the kingdom and their relationship.

The voice cast is crowded. [Ali Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Wong) and Stephanie Hsu play the stepsisters, Bette Midler is the Fairy Godmother, Nikki Glaser voices Priscilla, Daniel Radcliffe is the Prince and Peter Dinklage plays the henchman Roderick.

## A long run of musical roles

Radio Times points out how much singing Seyfried has already done on screen: Cosette in the 2012 version of Les Misérables, Sophie in both Mamma Mia films, and the founder of the Shaker movement in Mona Fastvold's The Testament of Ann Lee. Her acting résumé is just as varied. She earned an Oscar nomination for playing silent-film star [Marion Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Davies) in Mank, and won a string of television awards for playing Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in The Dropout.

She also appears in Nathan Fielder's documentary You Can See Everything, and the cwibc.co.id site reports she is part of an Oscar campaign for it.

## The Mamma Mia video

Seyfried has also revisited her best-known musical. According to [cwibc.co.id](https://cwibc.co.id/en/amanda-seyfried-mamma-mia-performance), she performed ABBA's The Winner Takes It All in a new video with social media personalities Anthony Gargiula and Jonathan Tilkin. In the 2008 film, Meryl Streep sang the song as Seyfried's on-screen mother, Donna, and earned a Golden Globe nomination for Best Actress.

Gargiula shared the clip with his nearly 5 million TikTok followers. "Living our Mamma Mia dreams," he wrote in the caption. The report links the video to renewed interest in a third Mamma Mia film, though no new film has been confirmed in the sources we read.
