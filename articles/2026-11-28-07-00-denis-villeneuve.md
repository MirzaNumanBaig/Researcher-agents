**Quick summary**

- [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve) told Time he was "deeply disappointed" that Disney placed *Avengers: Doomsday* on the same day as *Dune: Part Three*.
- Both films open on 18 December 2026, a clash fans have nicknamed "Dunesday".
- Villeneuve said moving his film was "impossible" because of its IMAX commitment, so he accepted his "fate".
- In the same interview he said he is "not here to change the DNA" of James Bond.

Denis Villeneuve has spoken openly about the biggest clash on the 2026 release calendar. In an interview with Time, published on 27 November, the director said he was "deeply disappointed" when *Avengers: Doomsday* moved onto the same date as *Dune: Part Three*, which closes his sci-fi trilogy.

## What Villeneuve said about Dunesday

According to [AOL's report on the interview](https://www.aol.com/articles/denis-villeneuve-puts-damper-dunesday-195834000.html), Disney moved [*Avengers: Doomsday*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avengers%3A_Doomsday) from its original 1 May 2026 date to 18 December, directly against *Dune: Part Three*. Villeneuve said his own film had already committed to that date and to IMAX screens, so shifting was not an option.

He framed it as the end of a bumpy run. The first *Dune* arrived at the end of the pandemic, and the second was pushed from autumn to late winter because of the two Hollywood strikes. This time, he recalled thinking, there was "no pandemic, no strike in sight". Then came the Marvel date.

His verdict, quoted by [CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/dunesday-denis-villeneuve-praying-dune-part-3-box-office-success-avengers-doomsday-numbers-say): the Avengers films are "a mammoth", while *Dune* is "a sexy little animal that has a lot of firepower". He said he is not competing with them, adding "It's a T. rex", but that he stands proud of the film.

## What the numbers say

The earlier films give some context. CinemaBlend reports the first *Dune* made US$410.7 million worldwide against a US$165 million budget, and the 2024 sequel took about US$714.9 million.

For the clash itself, CinemaBlend cites tracking reported by Deadline. *Avengers: Doomsday* is projected at about US$270 million for its opening, the highest figure the National Research Group has recorded. *Dune: Part Three* is tracking at around US$75 million, with talk that it could pass US$80 million. That would be on par with the previous *Dune* opening and double the first film's debut, according to the same report.

Villeneuve himself sees an upside. He said the combined weekend "could be one of the biggest box-office weekends of all time because of both movies".

## Why it isn't called Dune: Messiah

The Time interview also covered the title. As [AOL reports](https://www.aol.com/articles/dune-3-denis-villeneuve-good-185304000.html), the film adapts Frank Herbert's second novel, *Dune Messiah*, but Villeneuve said it departs so far from the book that using the name would have been "dishonest". He described the novel as a "chamber piece" of people talking in rooms, noting that the film's opening battle runs about 15 minutes, against a single sentence in the book.

The film is set 19 years after *Dune: Part Two*, with Paul ruling as emperor and waging a brutal jihad, while a conspiracy threatens his empire, according to the same report. Readers curious about the director's earlier comments can see our piece on [Denis Villeneuve calling Dune: Part Three his most personal film](https://moduscarry.com/denis-villeneuve-dune-part-three-3/).

## Bond: "I'm not here to change the DNA"

Villeneuve also addressed his next project, the new [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond) film. He said he is "a hardcore James Bond fan" and loves "the unapologetic way the character behaves, the panache", as quoted by [Film-News.co.uk](https://www.film-news.co.uk/news/UK/130530/Denis-Villeneuve-explains-what-he-loves-about-James-Bond-traditions). His aim is to "embrace" the character and "bring it to the world of today", though he admitted the film "will be tainted by my sensibility".

He kept details back, saying the project is "still in the oven cooking". Robert Pattinson was announced as the new Bond earlier this month. AOL notes the film has no release date yet, and that earlier reports suggested Pattinson may have signed for three films, which is not confirmed in the interview. For more on the casting buzz, see [our Bond film coverage](https://moduscarry.com/denis-villeneuve-bond-film-2/).

## What happens next

Both films reach cinemas on 18 December 2026. Tracking figures will keep shifting over the next three weeks, and the real test is the opening weekend.
