**Quick summary**

- India Today published an analysis of *Ramayana: Part 1* on 11 November 2026, calling the film grand and wildly ambitious.
- The outlet says director [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari) keeps to the epic's core while adding new questions and context.
- Yash's Ravan is the most talked-about performance, while Sai Pallavi's Sita is described as the most natural presence.
- The film is Part 1 of a larger retelling, so several characters are only partly explored so far.

The first instalment of [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari)'s *Ramayana* is drawing attention for how it handles a story most Indian viewers already know by heart. In an analysis published on 11 November 2026, [India Today](https://www.indiatoday.in/india-today-insight/story/how-nitesh-tiwaris-ramayana-raises-new-questions-adds-contexts-but-sticks-to-epics-core-3036885-2026-11-11) described *Ramayana: Part 1* as impressive, grand and wildly ambitious, and said it leaves room for debate.

## A risky story to put on screen

India Today argues that filming the Ram story is as attractive as it is risky. Audiences arrive with their own pictures of Ram, Sita, Lakshman and Ravan, shaped by the Valmiki and Tulsidas texts, folk tales, Ramlilas and the Ramanand Sagar television series. Against that backdrop, the outlet says Tiwari tries to pitch a cinematic language of its own.

The piece judges the film by two measures: how faithfully it repeats old memories, and how many new meanings it offers a new generation. It credits Tiwari with some bold attempts to bring out questions buried inside the tale.

## The performances getting attention

According to India Today, Ranbir Kapoor plays Ram as restrained, gentle and dignified. The reviewer says he is hard to accept at first, but that the Ram-Kaikeyi exchange is where the audience begins to see Ram in him. The piece adds that Kapoor seems visibly to be striving in places, given how large the expectations are.

[Ravi Dubey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Dubey) is called energetic, fluid and playful as Lakshman, who is shown with impulse, questions and some dissent. Sai Pallavi is named the most natural presence in the film. Her Sita is described as sharp and independent, shown running and wielding weapons, though the outlet says the full range of the character is yet to be explored.

Yash's Ravan is the performance most discussed. India Today notes that social media claims Ravan overpowers Ram, and rejects that reading. In its view Yash may outshine Kapoor as an actor, but the character does not dominate the story's central hero. [Sharad Kelkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharad_Kelkar), who provides the Hindi dubbing for Ravan, is singled out for praise.

Arun Govil, who once played Ram on television, appears as King Dashrath. The review calls his exile scene powerful. [Sheeba Chaddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheeba_Chaddha) is also praised as Manthara.

## Behind the camera

India Today calls Tiwari imaginative, experimental and a visionary of scale. It says his effort, together with producer Namit Malhotra's, to take an Indian story to a global stage largely succeeds. It also credits writers and advisors including Shreyas Jain, [Shridhar Raghavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shridhar_Raghavan) and Bodhisattva for the film's ideological foundation.

A separate IMDb headline this week put Tiwari's cumulative worldwide box office across five films at 3,176 crore. That page could not be read in full, so the figure is unverified here.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who directed Ramayana: Part 1?
Nitesh Tiwari directed it, with Namit Malhotra named by India Today as producer.

### Who plays Ravan in the film?
Yash plays Ravan, and Sharad Kelkar provides the Hindi dubbing, according to India Today.

### Does Ravan overpower Ram in the film?
India Today says no. It argues that a stronger performance is not the same as a character dominating the story.
