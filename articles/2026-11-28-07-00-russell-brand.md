**Quick summary**

- A video shared on X on 25 November 2026, claiming to show [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) beaten by another inmate in a British prison, is fake.
- A fact check published by Yahoo says an AI tool placed his head on the body of a man in a cell fight clip first posted on 18 July 2026.
- The claim that TMZ published the footage is also false, according to the same fact check.
- Separately, Netflix's documentary *Russell Brand: Open Secret* is drawing fresh reviews in Australia.

A viral video claiming to show Russell Brand being attacked in a British prison is not real. A fact check carried by Yahoo found the clip is an older fight video altered with an AI tool, and that the "breaking news" label attached to it was misleading.

## What the fake video claimed

According to the fact check, the clip was posted by the account @upfrontmma on X on 25 November 2026. Its caption read "RUSSELL BRAND IN JAIL", and the accompanying text announced that he had been assaulted in prison. The post also suggested the footage had come from TMZ.

The fact checkers, from Lead Stories, say neither part holds up. A review of recent TMZ stories found no such video, and a search of the site for stories mentioning Brand and a fight returned nothing.

## Where the footage really came from

Lead Stories traced the original to the @fistfightreels account on X, where it was posted on 18 July 2026, more than four months before the fake appeared. That version carried a caption about one man staring as if he wanted a second round, with no connection to Brand.

The fact check concludes that the July video was edited so that Brand's head sits on the body of the inmate being beaten. In other words, the viral post took a real clip of an unrelated fight and used AI to make it look like news about a celebrity. It is a reminder to check where a dramatic clip first appeared before sharing it, particularly when it carries a "breaking" banner.

## Open Secret reviews continue

The false clip arrived in the same week Australian critics were weighing in on the Netflix documentary *Russell Brand: Open Secret*. In a TV round-up, The West Australian's Screen Queen column describes the film as an excellent documentary built around the investigation by two journalists at The Times, who spent years working on their reporting.

According to the review, the film asks how Brand stayed "hidden in plain sight" for so long, and includes interviews with women who describe the impact of his behaviour. The columnist, who writes that their own path crossed with Brand's during years in entertainment journalism, calls it sobering viewing and urges readers to watch it. That is one critic's opinion, and the column does not add new claims beyond the film itself.
