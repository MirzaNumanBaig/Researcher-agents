**Quick summary**

- *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story*, a documentary narrated by [Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook), began streaming on Netflix in the United States on 6 November 2026, according to What's on Netflix.
- It covers the theft of at least $12 million from Cook by his half-brother and former business manager, Darryl McCauley.
- It is a licensed title rather than a Netflix Original, and went to digital storefronts on 29 September.
- Cook says the real total may be higher, and some of the money has never been found.

Dane Cook has turned the worst financial chapter of his career into a feature documentary, and it is now on Netflix in the United States. *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story* reached the streamer on Thursday 6 November 2026, following a short run on digital platforms, What's on Netflix reports. Cook narrates, and the story is one he has been telling in pieces for some time, as our earlier coverage of [his brother and the pasta sauce bags](https://moduscarry.com/dane-cook-funny-money-documentary/) shows.

## What the documentary covers

The film looks back at Cook's peak around 2005 and 2006, when he was one of the biggest stand-up acts in the US. What's on Netflix points to his best-selling album *Retaliation* and repeated sell-outs at Madison Square Garden. Behind the scenes, the outlet says, money was leaving his accounts in withdrawals of $20,000, $50,000 and sometimes $100,000 without him noticing.

The scheme came to light after Cook hired a new business manager. The man responsible was his half-brother, Darryl McCauley, who had run his finances. ABC affiliate WJLA reports that McCauley had earlier worked as a corrections officer, and that Cook gave him the job after he was attacked by an inmate.

## The money and the case

Both outlets put the amount at $12 million. WJLA reports that McCauley pleaded guilty in 2010 to larceny, embezzlement and forgery, and was sentenced to five to six years in prison and ordered to repay the $12 million. His wife, Erika, received a sentence of two and a half years, according to the same report.

The detail that drew the most headlines is where the cash went. WJLA says it was hidden in household items, including potted plants, boxes of bagel bites and bags of frozen pasta sauce. That story has been told before; see how [Cook has described the betrayal on a podcast](https://moduscarry.com/dane-cook-funny-money-podcast/). What's on Netflix says the film reportedly shows some of it.

## Who made it

Brett Harvey wrote and directed. His earlier credits include *Breaking Olympia: The Phil Heath Story*. Rocky Mudaliar and Adam Scorgie produced, and Cook is an executive producer. The film premiered on 18 September at the Aero Theatre in Los Angeles, with a Q&A with Cook.

It began life as *Brace for Impact: The Dane Cook Story*, commissioned by Super Channel, according to What's on Netflix, which links that detail to a Deadline report. UpStreamFlix distributes it in the US.

## What Cook says about it

In the WJLA report, Cook calls the theft "humiliating, frustrating, and disgusting", yet he can now laugh about it. He describes the film as a "love letter" to stand-up comedy. He believes the true total taken may be higher than $12 million. WJLA reports that at least $1 million is still unaccounted for, and Cook has joked about splitting any of it with whoever finds it.

What's on Netflix adds that Cook felt relief and closure after finishing the project, and that questions remain about where the money went and what became of McCauley after his sentence. Those are the threads Cook has hinted could lead to more.

## What happens next

Cook has said there is more to the story and has hinted at a possible sequel, WJLA reports. No date or format has been announced. Viewers outside the US should check local availability, as the Netflix date reported so far applies to the United States only.
