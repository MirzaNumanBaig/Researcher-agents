**Quick summary**

- [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson), 40, was announced on Monday as the new [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond), according to Page Six.
- Amazon founder Jeff Bezos says he was "heavily involved" in the choice and that the team first wanted someone "a little more unknown".
- A UK source told Page Six that casting director Nina Gold was asked for an actor under 40, and producers then asked for Pattinson anyway.
- Twilight director Catherine Hardwicke says his offbeat humour is the reason he suits the part.

[Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson) is Hollywood's most unlikely 007, and that is the point, according to a new Page Six report published on 21 November. The 40-year-old, already a lead in the Harry Potter, Twilight and Batman films, was announced on Monday as the next [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond). The outlet describes a star known for deadpan wit rather than polished suavity.

## A star who was not on the original list

Page Six reports that Amazon MGM Studios executives first pictured a younger, lesser-known actor in the role. Bezos said at Semafor's Silicon Valley & The World conference this week that Pattinson was not initially considered "because he's already so famous".

The shortlist, according to the report, included [Nicholas Hoult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Hoult), [Callum Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callum_Turner), Jack Lowden and Josh O'Connor, all aged 36.

A UK source familiar with the process told the outlet that executives asked casting director [Nina Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Gold) to find someone under 40. When she returned with three names, Lowden, O'Connor and Turner, the source says producers replied by asking, "Can we have Rob?" That, the source said, "threw the gates wide open".

Page Six also reports that producer [Amy Pascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Pascal) seriously considered [Tom Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Holland), who she worked with on the Spider-Man films. That casting did not happen, but the idea reportedly led director [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve) to see the value of another big franchise name in Pattinson. The outlet attributes this to its own reporting and does not name a source.

## Bezos on the screen tests

Bezos said it was "so obvious he is James Bond" after watching the screen tests, adding that there was "something special about Rob". Page Six notes that, according to Variety, the tests cost the studio between $300,000 and $500,000. A separate insider told the outlet that rumours linking Bezos' wife, Lauren Sánchez, to the casting are "complete nonsense".

## Why the quirks count in his favour

Pattinson's predecessors [Sean Connery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Connery), [Pierce Brosnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierce_Brosnan) and [Daniel Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Craig) built a cool, debonair image, Page Six writes. Pattinson is better known for awkward, self-deprecating humour.

[Catherine Hardwicke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Hardwicke), who directed Twilight, told the outlet she spotted his potential at his first audition in 2007, when he was 21. She said he is "funny and hilarious and offbeat and very original", and also "so elegant in a way", with a frame that "looks great in a tux". In her view the eccentricity "adds to the richness of a character".

She recalled his audition opposite [Kristen Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristen_Stewart) for the role of [Edward Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cullen) as "supercharged", with an electricity that made him impossible to look away from. A Hollywood source also told Page Six that Pattinson is "definitely quirky" but "super talented" and will "elevate" the franchise.

## A famously unreliable interviewee

The report says the same playfulness shapes his press appearances. The Hollywood source said he "hates doing p.r., but plays with it" and likes to invent anecdotes.

Page Six points to two examples. In a 2011 interview with [Matt Lauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Lauer) on the Today show, he made up a story about watching a clown die in a car explosion. In a 2009 appearance on The Late Show With David Letterman, he claimed he had taken a crazed stalker fan to dinner while filming [Little Ashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ashes) in Spain.

Despite his fame, the source described him as "nervous" and "shy", and said that "getting him to be comfortable is tough". That will be a test for a Bond press tour, which is a far more commercial exercise than most of his past films.

The report does not give a release date or a start of filming for his first Bond film, and we have not found one in the sources read. What is confirmed is the casting itself, announced on Monday.
