**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) met Pope Leo XIV at the Vatican on 4 November, and photos of him in a pulled-up hood spread widely online.
- Antonio Spadaro, the Holy See official who arranged the meeting, told Rolling Stone the hood distracted from the real story.
- The pope quotes Dylan's unpublished 1963 piece "Go Away You Bomb" in the preface to his 2026 book.
- Dylan played Milan on the Monday and was due in Rome on the Thursday, which left Wednesday free.

The Bob Dylan Pope Leo meeting at the Vatican on 4 November became one of the most discussed moments of the singer's recent years, mostly because of what he wore. According to [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/bob-dylan-pope-leo-meeting-handshake-photo-pen-1235655979/), photos of the 85-year-old with his hood up beside the pope in white were turned into memes within hours. The priest who brokered the encounter says that reading misses the point.

## Why the hood took over the conversation

Rolling Stone reports that the contrast of black and white sent social media into a caption contest, with some users comparing the scene to [Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars) and [Emperor Palpatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpatine). Several outlets, including GQ and The Wall Street Journal, picked up the story.

Antonio Spadaro, a Jesuit priest and undersecretary for culture and education at the Holy See, sees it differently. "People talked a lot about what Dylan was wearing that afternoon and very little about what the pope had written," he told the magazine.

Spadaro said he asked Dylan's team at St. Anne's Gate whether the hood might be lowered. They told him it is part of how Dylan moves through the world, onstage and off, and he left it there.

## A 1963 page and a pope's preface

What the pope had written is the detail Spadaro wants noticed. Leo XIV quotes "Go Away You Bomb", an unpublished 1963 Dylan work, in the preface to his 2026 book, *Disarmed and Disarming: Peace Is a Gift*. In it, the young Dylan addressed the bomb directly and called it man-made and man-owned.

Spadaro called the handshake "a small parable". A man just past 20 told a bomb it was the work of human hands, he said, and 63 years later the author of that page, which nobody had wanted to buy at auction, shook hands with his most authoritative reader.

## The hands, not the hood

Spadaro posted a close-up of the clasped hands on Instagram. Dylan had taken off his gloves to greet the pope, he explained, so the hands were bare while his face stayed in shadow. He compared the image to Albrecht Dürer's 1508 "Praying Hands".

He also drew a parallel with Dylan's earlier papal meeting. In 1997 in Bologna, Dylan performed in a cowboy hat but removed it before climbing the steps to Pope John Paul II. "In 1997 he bared his head; in 2026, his hands," Spadaro said.

Spadaro also named some of his own reading: his recent books include a collaboration with [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese), and one with a foreword by [Patti Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patti_Smith).

## How a meeting got scheduled

The wish was mutual, according to Spadaro. Dylan wanted to meet the first American pope, and Leo, who had already quoted him, needed little persuading. The hard part was the calendar. Dylan played Milan on Monday and was due to play Rome on Thursday, while the pope left for Latin America on Friday. Wednesday was the only free day.

## Two older Dylan stories back in circulation

Away from the Vatican, Far Out Magazine has revisited two pieces of Dylan history this week. In one, it recalls that in 1965 Dylan said he had been about to quit singing, feeling drained, until "Like a Rolling Stone" changed his mind. The quote it uses is that he could finally "dig" himself, which he described as the tiring part of being admired by others.

In the other, it revisits a 2013 New Yorker interview in which Dylan praised Leonard Cohen's "Hallelujah". He called it a beautifully constructed melody that "steps up, evolves, and slips back, all in quick time", and said its connective chorus has "a power all of its own". Both stories are archive material rather than new news, but they show how closely his own songwriting remains studied. ## What happens next

Dylan's tour moves on after Rome, and no further Vatican engagement has been announced in the sources reviewed. Spadaro says he will keep sharing photos from the day. Whether the pope's reading of "Go Away You Bomb" leads to wider attention for the 1963 text is not something the sources address.
