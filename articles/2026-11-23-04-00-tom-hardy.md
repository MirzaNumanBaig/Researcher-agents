**Quick summary**

- [Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy) said in September 2026 that a second season of *Taboo* is in active development, according to MovieWeb.
- No release date has been announced. MovieWeb reports speculation of early 2028, which it calls ambitious.
- Delays are blamed on scheduling clashes for Hardy and co-creator Steven Knight.
- *Taboo* ran for eight episodes on BBC One from 2017 and earned two Primetime Emmy nominations.
- Hardy's 2012 crime film *Lawless* airs on TV on Sunday 22 November, according to JOE.ie.

Nine years after it first aired, *Taboo* is still finding viewers, and Tom Hardy has confirmed that a second season is being developed. The actor shared the update in September 2026 while promoting the new season of *MobLand*, per [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/taboo-recommendation-genre/).

## What Hardy said about Taboo season 2

Hardy told interviewers during the *MobLand* season 2 press tour that more *Taboo* is in the works, MovieWeb reports. The outlet says a renewal could have come soon after the first season, given the viewership and positive reception, but the schedules of the people involved got in the way.

Hardy was busy with the *Venom* films and *MobLand*. Knight, who is also the creator of *Peaky Blinders*, has had his own commitments. According to MovieWeb, Hardy wants the return to feel as big as the original, which has meant patience rather than a rushed second run.

There is still no firm date. MovieWeb notes that speculation points to early 2028 at the soonest, though it says even that may be optimistic. Treat that figure as a guess, not an announcement.

## Why Taboo still has a following

*Taboo* was created by [Steven Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Knight) with Hardy and his father, [Chips Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chips_Hardy), with [Ridley Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridley_Scott) as an executive producer. Set in London in 1817, it follows James Delaney, a vengeful antihero who takes on the East India Company over a strip of land on the Pacific coast of North America.

MovieWeb describes the show as a mix of historical drama, crime thriller and Gothic horror. Delaney has terrifying visions, telepathy and even astral projection, which the series ties to his past trauma. It never says whether these are real or all in his head, and that ambiguity is a big part of its appeal.

The eight-episode run was nominated for two Primetime Emmys, including recognition for Max Richter's score, according to MovieWeb. The same outlet calls Delaney one of the defining roles of Hardy's career. The series is now free to stream with ads on Prime Video, Tubi and Pluto TV, among other platforms, per MovieWeb. Availability can differ by region, so Australian viewers should check local listings.

## Lawless puts Hardy back on screen tonight

*Taboo* is not the only Hardy title in the news this weekend. JOE.ie picked the 2012 crime film *Lawless* as its TV movie of the night for Sunday 22 November, airing on the channel Legend at 1.45am in Ireland.

Hardy plays Forrest Bondurant, one of three bootlegging brothers in Prohibition-era Virginia. The film is adapted from Matt Bondurant's novel *The Wettest County in the World* and is loosely based on his own family history, JOE.ie says. [Jason Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Clarke) plays Howard, and Shia LaBeouf plays Jack.

The cast also includes [Gary Oldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Oldman) and Australian actor [Guy Pearce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Pearce), who plays the sadistic lawman from Chicago. Australian musician [Nick Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Cave) wrote the screenplay, and John Hillcoat directed.

According to JOE.ie, the film drew decent reviews on release. Critics praised its cinematography, period detail and Pearce's villain, while some objected to the violence and bleak tone. It has since built a cult following and holds a 7.2 score on IMDb.
