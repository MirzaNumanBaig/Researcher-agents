**Quick summary**

- [Keanu Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keanu_Reeves) made his feature debut in the 1986 ice hockey drama Youngblood.
- A decade after its release, he called the film "seminal" when asked how important it was to his career.
- In the late 1980s he gave a blunter verdict: "It's an awful movie."
- Far Out Magazine revisited both comments in a piece published on 29 October 2026.

Keanu Reeves has described his first Hollywood film in two very different ways. According to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/seminal-role-keanu-reeves-wanted-delete-from-history/), the actor called Youngblood "seminal" about ten years after it came out, yet in the late 1980s he dismissed it as "an awful movie". The outlet's 29 October 2026 retrospective sets the two comments side by side.

## A newcomer with $3,000 and a Volvo

Far Out describes a young Reeves who had just driven from Canada to Los Angeles in an old Volvo with $3,000 and little else. His only screen experience was a handful of local TV shows and made-for-TV movies.

Youngblood changed that. The 1986 sports drama put him beside [Rob Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Lowe) and [Patrick Swayze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Swayze), who were arriving fresh from St Elmo's Fire and [Red Dawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dawn) respectively. For an unknown, that was a significant step up.

## Playing what he knew

The casting made sense on paper. Reeves had played ice hockey as a youth and, according to Far Out, was nicknamed "The Wall" as a goalkeeper for his college team. In Youngblood he played the goalie Heaver.

The one thing he had to learn was a French-Canadian accent. Looking back, he credited [Carl Marotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Marotte), who told him about the audition while Reeves was in a play, and who helped him with the accent. "I auditioned playing hockey. It was beautiful," Reeves said in the interview Far Out quotes.

## Seminal and awful

The two verdicts came at different moments. The "seminal" remark arrived roughly a decade after the film's moderately successful release, with distance and some nostalgia. The "awful" one came earlier, when Bill & Ted's Excellent Adventure was about to make him a star and Youngblood was still fresh.

Far Out's own view sits between the two. The writer argues the film is not the rare case of a picture that is both classic and terrible, and calls it "fairly mediocre". The outlet adds that Reeves did something more worthwhile the same year.

## The better film that followed

Far Out notes that [River's Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River's_Edge) premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival nine months after his first film was released. The outlet says it was not a great film either, but that it gave him a far better showcase for his screen presence and easy charm than a small role in goal.
