**Quick summary**

- Neighbours footage is being recut with AI into a vertical "microdrama" for phones, starting with Margot Robbie's character Donna Freedman, according to The Guardian.
- Two Robbie storylines will become about 150 minutes of short episodes on the Epis platform, run by RoseBerry Media.
- Former co-stars Stefan Dennis and Matthew Werkmeister have criticised the project; Fremantle says it uses only original footage.
- Guardian Australia has asked Robbie for comment, and no response was reported.

Neighbours is being reworked into a phone-first microdrama built around [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie), and some of her former castmates say nobody asked them. The Guardian reported on 26 October that RoseBerry Media is using an AI-assisted tool to turn archive episodes of the Australian soap into vertical clips, with Robbie's early storylines first in line.

## What is being made

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/oct/26/neighbours-soap-recut-ai-microdrama-mobile-series-feat-margot-robbie), RoseBerry Media specialises in "vertical television" and is releasing the material on its Epis platform. The tool is called RedSnapper. The first release centres on Donna Freedman, the character Robbie played from 2008 to 2011.

Two of her storylines will be cut into micro-episodes, adding up to roughly 150 minutes. Vertical serials usually run about a minute an episode. The Guardian says the global microdrama market, which began in China, was estimated at up to US$15 billion (A$21.4 billion) last year.

The format is not new for existing titles. Paramount Pictures released Mean Girls on TikTok as 23 episodes in 2023, the paper notes. Neighbours offers a deep library for the same treatment: more than 9,300 episodes aired between 1985 and 2025.

## Why the cast is unhappy

[Stefan Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Dennis) told the Herald Sun the project was "unbelievably invasive, morally and legally wrong". He said he had put his agent on it and expected other cast members, the Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance and the Australian Writers' Guild to do the same. "We're dealing with a very big machine," he said. His agent declined to comment further to Guardian Australia.

[Matthew Werkmeister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Werkmeister), who played Zeke Kinski and shared a love-triangle storyline with Robbie's character, said he learned of the deal only when journalists contacted him. He told Guardian Australia he was concerned about his image being used through AI "and a platform I did not consent to". He added that he signed his original contracts at 13, when AI and vertical streaming were not a consideration.

Werkmeister said neither producer Fremantle nor RoseBerry had told him whether residuals would be paid. Guardian Australia understands any residuals will go to writers and actors under their existing agreements. The agent for [Sam Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Clark), who played Donna's partner Ringo Brown, said his client was waiting to hear from the US union Sag-Aftra before speaking.

## Fremantle's position

Fremantle's commercial and international chief executive, Jens Richter, told Variety last month the project showed how technology can "unlock new value" from its catalogue, as quoted by The Guardian. The company told Guardian Australia it will use only "original, previously broadcast footage, dialogue and performances".

A spokesperson said RedSnapper does not generate new scenes or performances, alter faces or likenesses, or use synthetic voices. Whether that answers the consent questions raised by the actors is the open point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Margot Robbie in the new Neighbours microdrama?
The series recuts original footage of her as Donna Freedman, a role she played from 2008 to 2011. The Guardian reports no new scenes are being filmed.

### Is the AI generating new performances?
Fremantle says no. It says RedSnapper does not create new scenes, change faces or likenesses, or use synthetic voices.

### Who has criticised the project?
Stefan Dennis and Matthew Werkmeister have spoken publicly against it. Sam Clark's agent said he is waiting for union advice.
