**Quick summary**

- [Joan Baez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Baez) has described how years of resentment toward [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) disappeared.
- She made the remarks to MOJO in 2019, and Far Out Magazine revisited them on 23 November 2026.
- She said it happened while she painted a commissioned portrait of Dylan and listened to his music.
- She now says "nothing negative" is left.

The Joan Baez and Bob Dylan story is one of folk music's best-known and most complicated. According to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/joan-baez-resented-bob-dylan-gift-from-heaven/), which revisited the remarks on 23 November 2026, Baez told MOJO in 2019 that her long-held resentment of Dylan had simply gone.

## What Baez said about Dylan

Baez told the magazine that she put Dylan's music on and "any remaining resentment literally evaporated like a gift from heaven", according to Far Out. She said the shift came about eight months before the interview, while she was working on a commissioned portrait of him.

Afterwards, she said, she could feel only gratitude: for having been his friend, for having his music, and for having been part of what she called "those wild years". Far Out quotes her as saying there was "nothing negative left at all" and that Dylan "gave us the absolute best".

## How they met

Far Out says the two met around 1961 at Gerde's Folk City in Greenwich Village. Baez was not immediately won over. In her 1987 memoir, *And A Voice to Sing With*, she called the young Dylan an "urban hillbilly".

That first impression did not last. Baez first sang Dylan's songs on record in 1963, on *Joan Baez in Concert, Part 2*. The same year they shared the stage at the Newport Folk Festival and appeared at the March on Washington, and they toured together.

## Where the bitterness came from

The relationship eventually ended, and Baez addressed it in her own songs. Far Out points to "To Bobby" (1972) and "Diamonds and Rust" (1975) as two places where she did so. The article presents the later resentment as real and long-lasting, which is what makes her 2019 account notable.

Dylan has spoken warmly of her too. In a 2017 Rolling Stone interview, as Far Out recounts, he praised her voice and compared it to a siren.
