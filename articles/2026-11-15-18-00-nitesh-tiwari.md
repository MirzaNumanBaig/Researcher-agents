**Quick summary**

- Actor [Addinath Kothare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adinath_Kothare), who plays Bharat in the two-part *Ramayana*, says the film's sets made him feel he was in the forest of Chitrakoot.
- He made the remarks in an interview with the Hindustan Times, as reported by The Times of India on 15 November 2026.
- He also argued that even senior actors should audition, saying the process matches the right performer to the right role.
- The second part of the epic is set to arrive in 2028.

The sets of [Nitesh Tiwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitesh_Tiwari)'s *Ramayana* were detailed enough to make one of its cast forget he was filming. Addinath Kothare, who plays Bharat, told the Hindustan Times that during the emotional Bharat Milap sequence he "genuinely felt like I was in the forest of Chitrakoot", according to [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/hindi/bollywood/news/addinath-kothare-recalls-the-emotional-bharat-milap-sequence-in-nitesh-tiwaris-ramayana-i-genuinely-felt-like-i-was-in-the-forest-of-chitrakoot/articleshow/135849210.cms).

## Why the Nitesh Tiwari Ramayana sets stood out

Kothare said what impressed him most was the effort behind the film's world. He praised the detailing of the sets for Ayodhya and Chitrakoot, crediting the director for building them.

One small touch stayed with him. In a real forest, he explained, a pathway forms because people keep walking over the grass. The production team recreated that detail on set. "These are the little things that make the world look real," he said, as quoted by The Times of India.

That realism, he said, pulled him closer to Bharat. He described stepping onto the sets and feeling he was the character, interacting with Rama, Sita and Lakshman rather than with the actors who play them. Everyone around him, he said, was in the same energy and flow.

The scene itself draws on one of the best-known moments of the epic. The reunion of Bharat with Rama in the forest has also been the subject of an earlier Indian film, [Bharat Milap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Milap), from 1942.

## The case for auditioning at every level

The interview also covered how Kothare won the role. He said the experience reinforced his view that auditions are not only for beginners. In his words, many people wrongly believe a senior actor should be exempt.

He argued that audiences go to the cinema for the story, not just for familiar faces. With so much talent available, he said, even ten good actors will not all find the right opportunities. The point of an audition, in his view, is to help a director decide who suits a particular part.

He added that casting directors today are trying new and fresh faces, and that actors should help filmmakers push their boundaries. "Internationally, even A-list actors audition," he said.

Kothare also thanked casting director [Mukesh Chhabra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukesh_Chhabra) for inviting him to try out for many projects over the years. Even when a test does not lead to a role, he feels it helps, because casting teams see what an actor can do and may remember him later. NDTV's headline on a related interview says he first auditioned for Lakshman before landing Bharat, though that page could not be read in full for this report.
