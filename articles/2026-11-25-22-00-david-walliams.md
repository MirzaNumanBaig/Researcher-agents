**Quick summary**

- UK Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham) says he will go further on non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) after the Russell Brand verdict and the allegations against David Walliams.
- Walliams denies the allegations. No court has established them.
- Former HarperCollins staff told The Guardian and ITV News that the publisher's 2023 inquiry was handled badly. HarperCollins disputes that.
- A parliamentary committee is due to begin hearing evidence on NDA use in the industry in about two weeks.

The **David Walliams NDA row** has moved into government policy. Burnham has said his administration will look at tighter limits on gagging agreements and a staggered age of consent, citing the conviction of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) and the allegations against [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), according to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/russell-brand-andy-burnham-ndas-david-walliams-1237233158/) and the [Daily Star](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/news/uk-news/andy-burnham-change-age-consent-37911775). Walliams strongly denies all wrongdoing.

## What Burnham has promised

Burnham called the Brand case a "watershed moment" for the entertainment industry and for the country, Deadline reported. Laws stopping NDAs from gagging employees who have suffered harassment or discrimination have already been brought forward to January.

He now wants to go further. Writing in The Guardian, he said he wants protections extended to "freelance employees and fans who experience abuse", and that he will keep working so NDAs "can't be used to silence others", while still protecting the identities of victims who want that.

The Daily Star reported that Burnham wants to "draw a line in the sand" on the idea that powerful men can silence their victims. The government is also considering a staggered age of consent aimed at stopping much older adults from having sex with 16 and 17-year-olds. It says it will ask survivors, parents, young people and support services to contribute.

The detail is still missing. The Daily Star noted that no specific age limits or age gaps have been set, and the government has not said exactly how it will make gagging victims harder.

## Unions and campaigners respond

Phillipa Childs, head of broadcasting union Bectu, said the government must urgently reassure creative workers that freelancers will have the same protection as employees, or risk "a giant loophole", according to Deadline. Zelda Perkins, who founded Can't Buy My Silence UK, called the promises "significant", the Daily Star said.

Parliament is also watching. The Digital, Culture, Media and Sport Committee is examining how the industry uses NDAs, with evidence sessions due to start in around two weeks. Over the weekend the Channel 4 News editor emailed staff to say ITN no longer uses the practice and has apologised for past cases, Deadline reported.

## The HarperCollins dispute

The policy push follows a joint Guardian and ITV News investigation published on 24 November, as summarised by [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/david-walliams-scandal-deepens-former-110000417.html). Former HarperCollins employees questioned how the publisher handled complaints about its bestselling author. One unnamed ex-employee called the process "not an inquiry" but "a cover-up".

According to that reporting, HarperCollins hired law firm Allen & Overy (now A&O Shearman) in 2023. At least five women reportedly took part, and Walliams himself was not interviewed. The review finished in early 2024, yet five more Walliams titles were published before December 2025.

Sources said the women who gave evidence were not told the findings and were asked not to discuss the process. One reportedly left with a five-figure settlement under a strict NDA. Former staff also said managers were alerted at least six times, with complaints dating to 2015.

HarperCollins told ITV News the external review ended in 2024, its recommendations were carried out, and further steps led to the company ending its relationship with Walliams. Walliams' lawyers said he did not recall one reported book-signing encounter from 2018 and had not known of a complaint. These accounts are untested in court.

Former HarperCollins chief [Victoria Barnsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Barnsley) criticised the reported use of settlements and confidentiality agreements.

## Where the legal case stands

Walliams was named on 10 November after losing a bid for an injunction to stop ITN and Guardian News and Media publishing allegations, the [Daily Express](https://www.express.co.uk/celebrity-news/2268660/david-walliams-behaviour-shame-diaries) reported. He denied the claims. On 13 November the Metropolitan Police said it had received allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man in his 50s. It said they were separate from the media reports, and that detectives were also reviewing that reporting. No charges have been reported.

## What happens next

The government must turn its announcements into detail: who NDA limits cover, and what a staggered age of consent would look like. The committee hearings in about two weeks should show how widely NDAs are used. The Met inquiry continues.

## Frequently asked questions

### What has Andy Burnham promised on NDAs?
He says NDAs must not be used to silence people who report abuse. Rules for employees take effect in January, and he wants to extend protection to freelancers and fans.

### Has David Walliams been charged with anything?
No charge has been reported. He strongly denies all allegations, and the Met Police is assessing separate reports.

### What does HarperCollins say about its inquiry?
It says an external law firm finished the review in 2024, recommendations were implemented, and further steps led to the company ending its relationship with Walliams.
