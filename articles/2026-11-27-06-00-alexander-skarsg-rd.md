**Quick summary**

- [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) plays a husband made of woven material in the fantasy romance Wicker.
- Instinct Magazine reports the costume took nearly two hours to put on each day.
- Wētā Workshop spent about 18 months on a suit of roughly 50 pieces, per the same report.
- Collider says the film has now arrived on digital platforms to rent or buy.
- Co-star [Olivia Colman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Colman) leads the cast alongside [Peter Dinklage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Dinklage).

[Alexander Skarsgård](https://moduscarry.com/tag/alexander-skarsgard/) did not become a walking basket through computer effects. According to Instinct Magazine, the Wicker star spent close to two hours every day being fitted into a handmade costume, and the film reached digital rental and purchase this week.

## What the Alexander Skarsgård Wicker role involves

Wicker is based on Ursula Wills' short story The Wicker Husband, as Instinct Magazine describes it. Olivia Colman plays a lonely fisherwoman who commissions a basketmaker, played by Peter Dinklage, to build her the perfect husband. Skarsgård plays that husband.

Collider, which covered the film's home release, describes it as a romantic fantasy that puts its own spin on the Frankenstein idea, and calls it a sleeper hit headlined by an Oscar winner.

## How the costume was built

The practical suit came from Wētā Workshop, which spent about 18 months developing it, according to Instinct Magazine. It is made of around 50 individually crafted pieces. The team worked from digital scans of Skarsgård's body and 3D modelling, then produced the components with 3D printing.

The face got the same attention. Citing [Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly), Instinct reports that the makeup team used flexible prosthetic plates that moved with his expressions rather than a fixed mask. They also added flax and muka hair and carefully made eyelashes.

That detail matters for a romantic lead, because his performance has to read through the prosthetics.

## Intimate scenes and broken beds

The film includes intimate scenes between Skarsgård and Colman. Instinct, citing Variety, says the suit was built from flexible materials rather than real wicker or straw, so the filming was more comfortable than it might sound. Colman is also reported to have said the lack of direct skin contact made those scenes easier.

Skarsgård has also said the production broke a few beds during filming, according to the same report. The article does not say why, and the claim comes from a single outlet's summary of interviews, so treat the details lightly.

## Where the film stands now

Collider's weekly roundup places Wicker among several recent theatrical releases that became available to rent and buy at home. Its headline says the film was removed from about 2,000 theatres before the digital launch. The roundup gives no box office total for Wicker in the text we could read, so we cannot say how it performed.

The same roundup notes that the A24 documentary You Can See Everything has earned nearly US$100 million in cinemas and also arrived on digital this week.

No, according to Instinct Magazine. Wētā Workshop built a practical suit of about 50 pieces over roughly 18 months, using body scans, 3D modelling and 3D printing.
