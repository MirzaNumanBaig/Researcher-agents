**Quick summary**

- Variety reports that Amazon MGM's Bond screen tests cost between US$300,000 and US$500,000, according to sources familiar with the process.
- Two late additions, Nicholas Hoult and Robert Pattinson, reportedly tipped the decision, with director Denis Villeneuve's voice carrying the most weight.
- Pattinson's casting as 007 was announced on 16 November 2026 via the official James Bond account on X.
- His next release is Dune: Part Three on 18 December, followed by The Take, with The Batman Part II set for February 2028.

The Robert Pattinson Bond screen test was a pricey, supposedly secret affair, according to a new Variety report. Amazon MGM spent between US$300,000 and US$500,000 testing its shortlist before choosing the British actor as the next 007, the trade publication says, citing sources familiar with the process. The casting was announced on 16 November, and the details of how it happened are now coming out.

## How the Robert Pattinson Bond screen test played out

Variety describes a crowded field. After Amazon MGM bought control of the franchise from the Broccoli family in 2025, producers Amy Pascal and David Heyman brought in [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve) to direct. Together with Amazon executives Courtenay Valenti and Mike Hopkins, they tested a long list of actors earlier this year, including Callum Turner, Tom Francis, Jack Lowden and Josh O'Connor.

Two late tests changed the picture, Variety reports: Nicholas Hoult and Pattinson. Turner had been the early favourite, but came under scrutiny against the more established names. Insiders told the outlet that Villeneuve's opinion became the most trusted in the room, and that his working shorthand with Pattinson on Dune: Part Three made the choice feel close to inevitable.

A separate Variety report earlier this month had Harry Styles and Theo James in talks for the role. One executive told the outlet that so many names leaking publicly makes studios look indecisive.

## A career already on a run

Variety argues the job cements [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson) among the top tier of movie stars. It points to a run that includes Primetime, The Odyssey, The Drama, Die My Love and The Batman, alongside his earlier indie period with Good Time and The Lighthouse. It also credits his agent of more than 20 years, Stephanie Ritz of WME, for balancing big franchises with riskier work.

Primetime has been well received. Film data supplied with this brief lists a score of 86 per cent on Rotten Tomatoes and 68 out of 100 on Metacritic, and a reported US$62 million at the box office against a US$14.5 million budget.

## The Oscars angle

Gold Derby offers a more tongue-in-cheek reading of the timing. It reports that Pattinson dropped out of public view after the announcement, despite booked appearances, and that his Primetime performance is competing with Ryan Gosling's Project Hail Mary role for the final spot in its Best Actor top five. Its experts currently place Pattinson fifth in the combined standings.

Gold Derby also notes there is little evidence that a "Bond bump" helps at the Oscars, and says the casting means Bond questions will follow Dune: Part Three on its press tour. Its first New York and Los Angeles screenings are on 30 November. Meanwhile, [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) is being campaigned for You Can See Everything by the same distributor, A24.

## Why the internet is joking about it

The announcement post drew more than 69 million views and 1.35 million likes within days, according to Yahoo News Singapore, citing Know Your Meme. Parody posts then spread, with users inventing roles for him from Snoopy to an NFL quarterback. None of it is real, and the outlet frames it as a meme trend. We also covered [the UK government joining the Bond memes](https://moduscarry.com/robert-pattinson-james-bond-memes/).

## What happens next

Startefacts lists the calendar. Dune: Part Three opens in US cinemas on 18 December, with Pattinson as the shapeshifting Face Dancer Scytale. Four weeks later comes Netflix's The Take, a heist film directed by [Fernando Meirelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Meirelles) with [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington) and [Daisy Edgar-Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Edgar-Jones). Wikipedia lists its release as 15 January 2027.

Then there is [The Batman Part II](https://moduscarry.com/robert-pattinson-james-bond-batman-future/), scheduled for 18 February 2028. Startefacts says [Matt Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Reeves) stepped away for personal reasons and [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh) helped finish filming. [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond) has a director and a screenwriter, [Steven Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Knight), but no release date yet.
