**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) and [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) star as a married couple in *Fjord*, written and directed by [Cristian Mungiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Mungiu).
- The story follows a family with five children who move to Norway and face a child protection investigation after their eldest daughter arrives at school with bruises.
- A review published by AOL on 26 October describes Reinsve as the emotional centre of the film.
- IMDb also ran a story on 26 October about Reinsve's press appearances for the film.

Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan are drawing attention for *Fjord*, a drama from Romanian filmmaker Cristian Mungiu that puts a religious family at the mercy of Norway's child protection system. A review on AOL, published on 26 October, calls it a challenging watch that asks viewers to test their own assumptions.

## What Fjord is about

According to [AOL's review](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/sebastian-stan-renate-reinsve-face-141503000.html), Stan plays Mihai and Reinsve plays Lisbet. They are a married couple who have just moved back to Lisbet's home country of Norway. Their five children were all born in Romania, Mihai's homeland.

Trouble starts almost at once. Their oldest daughter, Elia, played by Vanessa Ceban, turns up at school with bruises. Child protection services step in, and the children are removed from the home while the parents are investigated.

The review says Mihai and Lisbet acknowledge some minor physical punishment but deny causing bruising. It adds that the possible failings of the system are mostly brushed aside, even though there is little hard evidence.

## A story told in three languages

Mungiu uses language as a source of tension. AOL notes that characters move between Norwegian, English and Romanian, and that the meaning given to particular words and phrases shapes how people and their actions are judged.

The family belongs to an unspecified Evangelical Christian sect. The review says Mungiu is deliberately vague about the religion, though it leans conservative and puts the couple at odds with a liberal Norwegian society. In the reviewer's reading, the film turns the lens on those who see themselves as most progressive, while Mihai and Lisbet rarely get the chance to object to their treatment.

The cold landscape adds to the mood. Mungiu also weaves in side stories about the family's neighbours, including a rebellious daughter and an ailing father. The reviewer says these strands do little for the plot but add complexity.

## Reinsve and Stan on screen

AOL points to Stan's recent work in *A Different Man* and *The Apprentice*, and describes a less showy performance here, with a shaved head and a stoic but firm manner. The reviewer says Reinsve, described as coming off the success of *Backrooms*, anchors the film emotionally and shows real chemistry with Stan. The two previously co-starred in *A Different Man*.

The review also singles out the supporting cast, particularly the actors playing lawyers, for bringing a very different legal system to life compared with the one viewers in the United States would know.

The verdict is that *Fjord* holds back where many western films would push, leaving audiences to decide who is right. In AOL's words, the debate over the family continues after the credits.
