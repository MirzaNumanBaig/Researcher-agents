**Quick summary**

- A parent in Cambridgeshire has started a petition asking the county's libraries to stop displaying and promoting children's books by authors facing serious sexual allegations, according to the Cambridge Independent.
- The petition follows allegations about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), who has denied them.
- Cambridgeshire Libraries told the parent it has "no legal basis" to remove the books and is following publisher guidance.
- A separate opinion piece in The National says Walliams tried and failed to use an injunction to stop reporting of the allegations.

A petition in Cambridgeshire is the latest sign of how far the allegations around [David Walliams](https://moduscarry.com/tag/david-walliams/) have travelled, reaching the shelves of local libraries. According to the [Cambridge Independent](https://www.cambridgeindependent.co.uk/news/parent-s-petition-calls-for-cambridgeshire-libraries-to-remo-9493173/), a parent has asked Cambridgeshire County Council to rethink how its libraries handle children's books by authors who face serious allegations. Walliams has denied the accusations.

## What the David Walliams books petition asks for

The petition was launched by Clare Gray, who says she acted after the allegations about Walliams became public. It calls on the council, which runs the library service, to adopt "a precautionary safeguarding policy" on the promotion and display of children's books by authors who are the subject of allegations of sexual assault or other serious sexual or violent offences.

Gray had earlier asked the library service to put Walliams' books into storage rather than keep them on display. The paper reports that the service replied: "We have no legal basis to remove these items from stock. We are continuing to follow guidance and advice from the publishers regarding this matter."

The Cambridge Independent also reports that Walliams has denied accusations that he used his celebrity status to target people for sexual purposes when they were teenagers or young women. None of the allegations has been tested in court, and the sources reviewed here do not report any finding against him.

## The parent's case

Gray told the paper she was pleased with her child's school, which removed his books from its library and classrooms. She was disappointed by the county library team's answer and decided to petition the council instead.

She said the petition "is not about censorship" and that the books would remain available to anyone who wants to borrow them. Her argument is that libraries are trusted public spaces used by children every day, and that public institutions should act without waiting for allegations to be proven in court.

County councillor Elliot Tong, an independent member for Abbey, supports the petition. He said ensuring books by authors facing allegations of serious misconduct are not actively promoted would be "a reasonable and proportionate policy" for the library service to consider.

The distinction matters. The petition does not ask for books to be banned or destroyed. It asks about promotion and display, which is a narrower request than removal from stock.

## The injunction angle

Separately, a column in [The National](https://www.thenational.scot/comment/26766846.journalism-vital-functioning-democracy/) by a former editor of the Sunday Herald uses the Walliams case to discuss press freedom. The writer says Walliams "tried unsuccessfully" to use a court injunction to stop reporting of allegations that he harassed young women.

The writer recalls ignoring a superinjunction in 2011 over footballer [Ryan Giggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Giggs), arguing that it did not apply in Scotland because a Scottish court had not granted it. He stresses that this protects a publisher from prosecution for breaching an injunction, but not from a defamation claim if an allegation cannot be proven true. He also cites the saying, credited to both [William Randolph Hearst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Randolph_Hearst) and [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell), that journalism is printing what someone does not want printed.

This is a comment piece, so it carries the author's views rather than a news finding. It is useful mainly as context for why the allegations were reported at all.

## What happens next

The petition now rests with Cambridgeshire County Council, which has not yet changed its position according to the report. Libraries say they are waiting on publisher guidance. ## Frequently asked questions

### Has David Walliams been charged with anything?
The sources reviewed for this article report allegations, which Walliams has denied. They do not report any charge or court finding.

### Are David Walliams books being removed from Cambridgeshire libraries?
Not at present. Cambridgeshire Libraries said it has no legal basis to remove the items and is following publisher advice. One school has removed the books, according to the petitioner.

### Does the petition call for a ban?
No. The organiser says the books would stay available to borrow. The petition targets how they are promoted and displayed.
