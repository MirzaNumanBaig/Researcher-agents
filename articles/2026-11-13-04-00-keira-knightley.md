**Quick summary**

- Christie's has announced it will auction the green dress [Keira Knightley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keira_Knightley) wore in the 2007 film *Atonement* on 9 December, according to the New York Post.
- The auction house estimates the gown at US$130,000 to US$160,000. It sold for US$35,000 in 2008.
- The dress is on free public view at Christie's in Rockefeller Center, New York, until 17 November.
- Separately, Knightley told the Arizona Daily Star she enjoyed the stunts in season 2 of Netflix's *Black Doves*, which is now streaming.

The green silk dress that made *Atonement* one of the most talked-about costume moments of the 2000s is going back under the hammer. The [New York Post reports](https://nypost.com/2026/11/11/lifestyle/keira-knightleys-atonement-dress-could-fetch-166k-at-auction-but-new-yorkers-can-see-it-for-free/) that Christie's announced the sale of the Keira Knightley Atonement dress on 11 November, with bidding set for 9 December. Knightley is also in the news for her action work in *Black Doves*, and the two stories show how far her screen range runs.

## What Christie's expects the Atonement dress to fetch

The gown last came up for auction in 2008, when it sold for US$35,000. The Post puts that at roughly US$55,000 in today's money once inflation is counted. Christie's now estimates a final price between US$130,000 and US$160,000, which is more than double the inflation-adjusted figure at the top end. All prices here are in US dollars, as reported.

The dress was designed by [Jacqueline Durran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Durran), who later won Oscars for her costume work on *Little Women* and *Barbie*. It has an open back, a gathered waist with a drape at the centre and a flowing skirt. According to novelist [Ian McEwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McEwan), who wrote the book the film is based on, the skirt was meant to suggest a mermaid.

## Why the green dress still sells

Thais Hitchins, head of sale at Christie's London, told the Post the design stays relevant because the bias cut and drop back give a classic shape a modern edge. She added that the dress captures the "desire and lust" that never go out of style.

The fashion world agrees. The Post notes that the look has been referenced officially twice: by Alexa Chung in a custom Nensi Dojaka gown in 2024, and by designer Tory Burch at Vogue World in 2025. Victoria Beckham and Chanel have also echoed the slip silhouette in recent collections. ## How to see it before the sale

New Yorkers can view the original at Christie's in Rockefeller Center until 17 November, free of charge. The Post says it hangs a few feet from a Basquiat painting, Warhol screenprint paintings and a Monet valued at US$12 million to US$18 million. Anyone outside the city will have to wait for the auction results, or settle for the high-street green slips the paper lists at under US$300.

## Knightley kicks down doors in Black Doves

While the dress makes headlines, Knightley has been promoting the second season of [*Black Doves*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Doves), now streaming on Netflix. In an interview carried by the Arizona Daily Star, she said the best part of playing a spy was simple: "I got to kick a door down." She said she did not realise it was what had been missing from her life, and joked about opening a club where people can kick doors in.

Her character, Helen Webb, is a politician's wife who lives a double life as a member of the Black Doves, a spies-for-hire organisation. In season 2, according to the interview, a fellow member of the group (played by [Ben Whishaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Whishaw)) returns as her secrets are in danger of coming out. Knightley called the characters "morally complicated creatures" and said she would not want to meet Helen, but loves how strange she is.

## The stunts were touch and go

The action did not come easy. Knightley said the cast rehearsed with stunt coordinators, but with less preparation time this season it was "a little touch and go". The sequences had been choreographed, she said, but the actors had not had time to learn them fully. One art gallery scene was rehearsed in pieces, and then the director wanted it filmed in a single shot. "And we did it," she said.

Whishaw described the pair's relationship as a love story under extreme pressure, with both wanting to stay loyal while Sam is tormented by what he has done. Knightley said the fascination lies in how people can be loving and also "horrific killers" who lie constantly.
