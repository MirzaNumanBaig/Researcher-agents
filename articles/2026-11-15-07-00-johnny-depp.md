**Quick summary**

- Ebenezer, Ti West's take on A Christmas Carol, earned $6.7 million in North America on Friday, including Thursday previews, according to Variety and The Hollywood Reporter.
- That put it first on the day. The Hollywood Reporter expects roughly $20 million, or just under, for the weekend.
- Audiences gave it an A- CinemaScore, while critics are split: 53 percent on Rotten Tomatoes.
- It is Johnny Depp's first major studio film since 2018's Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald.

[Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) has opened his Scrooge film at number one. Ebenezer took $6.7 million at the North American box office on Friday, previews included, and led the day's chart, Variety reported. For a $38 million production, it is a solid start, and the first real test of whether audiences still turn out for the actor.

## How big is the Ebenezer opening?

Paramount released the film in 3,535 theatres. The Hollywood Reporter says the weekend is likely to land around $20 million or slightly under, which would comfortably cover the first frame against the film's $38 million budget.

The competition is thin. Variety puts Amazon MGM's heist comedy How to Rob a Bank at $2.2 million on Friday, heading for about $4.75 million over the weekend against a budget above $40 million. Sony's Ghost Soldier took $1.5 million, and holdover The Cat in the Hat is tipped for second place with roughly $10 million.

## Who is buying tickets?

The audience data is the most telling part. The Hollywood Reporter says 62 percent of the Thursday night crowd was over 30, and 50 percent of those polled on PostTrak named Depp as their reason for going. The film scored 4.5 out of 5 there. The trade paper reads that as a sign that older viewers who grew up with his films are the core of the turnout.

Paramount's marketing leaned on that, according to the same report, showing Depp in character as Scrooge to recall other transformations such as Captain Jack Sparrow and Willy Wonka. The film is rated PG-13 but is aimed at families.

## A comeback after eight years away from big studios

Variety describes Ebenezer as Depp's first major movie in eight years. Warner Bros. removed him from the Harry Potter spinoff series in 2020 after he lost a libel case against the British tabloid The Sun. He later won a defamation trial in the US against his ex-wife Amber Heard, who had accused him of domestic abuse. The Hollywood Reporter notes that Depp has denied her allegations.

In the gap he made smaller films, including Minamata (2020) and Jeanne du Barry (2023), and directed the 2024 biopic Modì, Three Days on the Wing of Madness, Variety says. The cast around him includes Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Sam Claflin, Daisy Ridley and Ian McKellen.

## What the cast says about the transformation

Depp is heavily made up as the old miser, and the look is the film's main talking point. Speaking to [Cinemablend](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/nothing-prepared-sam-claflin-seeing-johnny-depp-ebenezer-nothing), [Sam Claflin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Claflin), who plays Fred, called the first sight of him in character "terrifying". He had seen a prosthetics makeup test beforehand, but said the shuffle, the voice and the odd little laugh were what stayed with him. He also said Depp kept spirits high between takes.

The ghosts needed just as much work. [Andrea Riseborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Riseborough), the Ghost of Christmas Past, said she spent six and a half hours in the makeup chair, plus two and a half to get out of it. [Tramell Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramell_Tillman), the Ghost of Christmas Present, put his own time at five and a half to six hours, and both named their eyesight as the hardest part.

## How does it compare with other Scrooges?

Decider's Version Control column lined Depp up against Bill Murray in Scrooged, Jim Carrey in the 2009 animated version and Ryan Reynolds in Spirited, with Michael Caine's Muppet Christmas Carol as the benchmark they are all chasing. Its writer calls the story one of the most adapted in literature, which is exactly the challenge: how far a star can bend a familiar part before it stops being Scrooge.

Reviews are mixed. The Hollywood Reporter's critic Frank Scheck wrote that the film "fulfills its ostensible goal of restoring the actor to A-list status", while the 53 percent Rotten Tomatoes score shows how divided other critics are.
