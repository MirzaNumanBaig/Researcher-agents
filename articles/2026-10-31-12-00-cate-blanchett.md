**Quick summary**

- The Human Consent Foundation, a nonprofit backed by [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett), filed an amicus brief in the New York Times lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft, Variety reported on 30 October.
- The brief argues that a working licensing market already exists for creative work, contradicting claims that clearing rights for AI training is too costly.
- Blanchett and [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh) gave statements saying permission should not change in the AI era.
- OpenAI says fair use benefits everyone and points to briefs supporting its position. Microsoft had not commented.
- Separately, Blanchett's cast list for Brady Corbet's *The Origin of the World* has reportedly grown again.

Cate Blanchett has put her name behind one of the more pointed filings yet in the copyright fight over artificial intelligence. Her nonprofit, the Human Consent Foundation (HCF), has joined a group of Hollywood companies and stars in an amicus brief supporting the New York Times in its case against OpenAI and Microsoft, [Variety reported](https://variety.com/2026/biz/news/cate-blanchett-hollywood-stars-brief-nyt-openai-lawsuit-1236935578/).

## What the brief argues

The central claim is simple. According to Variety, the defendants have argued that licensing creative works for AI training is cost-prohibitive and that no settled method exists to value training data. The brief says the facts show otherwise.

"The creative industries already have in place a proven infrastructure and workflows to clear rights for dispersed works at scale and cost-effectively," the filing reads, as quoted by Variety. In other words, the signatories say the tech companies cannot plead impossibility when studios, publishers and agencies clear rights every day.

HCF co-founder and CEO Nikki Hexum said the filing was meant to show the court how the creative industry actually works. "We are not starting from scratch," she said, adding that the aim is for people to keep a meaningful choice, including the choice not to take part.

## Blanchett's statement

Blanchett and Soderbergh, who also backs the foundation, each supplied a statement for the brief. Blanchett said she has worked in film and theatre for more than 30 years and that the principle has not changed in that time. If someone wants to use her face, voice or image, she said, they ask first, and there is a contract and a conversation. "Consent isn't a barrier to creativity, it is the foundation of it," she said.

The foundation is young. Variety reports it was founded as RSL Media in May by Hexum and Blanchett, and launched a registry in June where people can record what permissions they have given AI models to use their likenesses.

## Who else signed

The list of signatories is long. Variety names [Helen Mirren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Mirren), [Bette Midler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler), Julia Roberts, Robert De Niro, Scarlett Johansson, Rosario Dawson, Ariana DeBose, [Greg Berlanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Berlanti), [Christine Vachon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christine_Vachon), [Dave Eggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Eggers), [Francesca Amfitheatrof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Amfitheatrof), Paul Feig, Rian Johnson, Vince Gilligan and the Stan Lee Foundation, among others.

The filing landed a day after SAG-AFTRA, the Writers Guild of America and the American Federation of Musicians filed their own brief against OpenAI and Microsoft. The unions argued that accepting a fair use defence would "render moot" the terms they negotiated at the bargaining table.

## The other side

OpenAI did not answer the brief directly. A spokesperson pointed Variety to a blog post featuring briefs from several groups, including the US government as of last month, backing the company's view that its scraping is protected by fair use. The post says experts across many fields see fair use as a win-win. Microsoft did not respond to Variety's request for comment.

The Times sued in December 2023, alleging that OpenAI copied thousands of its articles without permission. It filed for summary judgment last month, according to Variety.

## What happens next

The court has not ruled on the fair use question, and the Times' summary judgment motion is pending. Amicus briefs do not decide a case, but they shape the record a judge reads. Watch for further filings from both sides and for any response from OpenAI or Microsoft to the creative industry's licensing argument.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Human Consent Foundation?
It is a nonprofit backed by Cate Blanchett and Steven Soderbergh that aims to protect people's likenesses from unauthorised scraping by AI models. Variety says it began as RSL Media in May and opened a permissions registry in June.

### What is an amicus brief?
It is a filing from a party that is not in the lawsuit but wants to give the court information or arguments relevant to the case.

### What does the New York Times accuse OpenAI of?
The Times alleges that OpenAI and Microsoft violated its copyright by scraping thousands of news articles without permission. The case was filed in December 2023.
