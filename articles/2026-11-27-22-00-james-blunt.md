**Quick summary**

- [James Blunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Blunt)'s charity single Another Year is released on Friday 27 November 2026, according to the Norwich Evening News.
- Profits go to Doctors Without Borders, which provides humanitarian medical care.
- [Kate Garraway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Garraway) says the video was filmed in a Christmas pub in London and that she had to sing for real.
- The BBC says Blunt also features in BBC Local Radio's Christmas programmes, across all 39 stations in England.

James Blunt has a busy festive week. His charity single Another Year arrives today, with a video full of familiar faces from Celebrity Traitors, and he appears in BBC Local Radio's Christmas schedule, where he talked to a community group founder about practical ways to get men to open up.

## How the Another Year video came together

Kate Garraway told Smooth Radio, as reported by the [Norwich Evening News](https://www.eveningnews24.co.uk/things-to-do/national/26768362.kate-garraway-reveals-stars-teamed-pub-james-blunt-charity-single/), that she turned up to film without knowing she would be asked to sing. The Good Morning Britain presenter, 59, said she had assumed a producer's joke about auto-tune was just that. "No, it's me for real," she said.

The cast list is long. Beyond Garraway, the paper names Richard E Grant and Jerry Hall, Blunt's Celebrity Traitors co-stars, along with Prue Leith, Clare Balding, Denise Welch and Spencer Matthews. Amanda Holden, Bimini Bon Boulash and Fearne Cotton complete the group.

Garraway described the setting as a "gorgeous Christmas pub" in London. She said she did not know who else would be there until they arrived, and that the mood was one of "fancy seeing you here". Seeing Grant again was a highlight, she added, as she had not met him since the show aired.

## What happens in the video

According to Garraway, the story is simple. The celebrities are sitting at the bar when Blunt begins to play. They wonder what the song is, then drift towards the piano and sing along one by one.

She also settled one question about the shoot. She had assumed the drinks would be "water with colouring in", but they were real, and the glasses were topped up between retakes. "The merriment you see on screen is pretty genuine," she said, adding that it may have helped loosen up the singing.

The single is for a cause. The Norwich Evening News reports that profits will go to Doctors Without Borders. For more on how the project began, see our earlier report, [James Blunt Charity Single Another Year Reunites Traitors Cast](https://moduscarry.com/james-blunt-another-year/).

## Blunt on BBC Local Radio at Christmas

Separately, the BBC has announced its [Christmas schedule for BBC Local Radio](https://www.bbc.co.uk/mediacentre/2026/bbc-local-radio-christmas-featuring-take-that-james-blunt-and-more), which includes Blunt alongside Take That. The programmes will run across all 39 local stations in England.

The centrepiece is a Make a Difference special hosted by [Gaby Roslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaby_Roslin), in which winners of this year's BBC Make a Difference awards meet musicians they admire. Blunt met Graham Porter, who won the Community Group Award at BBC Radio York. Porter founded Dad's Behaving Madly after his own children were diagnosed with ADHD and autism and he found little support aimed at fathers. The group began as a breakfast meeting with one other dad and now runs monthly meet-ups, day trips and sporting activities for families.

Blunt praised the way the group frames its work. "I love the way you present it as problem solving," he said, explaining that a man can see that as practical rather than emotional.

In the same programme, Donna Aldred, a fundraiser award winner at BBC Radio Kent, met Take That. [Gary Barlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Barlow) and [Howard Donald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Donald) spoke about her son Ben's cardiac arrest and the heart screening fund he set up with the charity Cardiac Risk in the Young.

## The wider Christmas line-up

The BBC lists several other programmes. [Mark Benton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Benton) hosts Driving Home for Christmas, marking a year since Chris Rea's death, on Christmas Eve from 9am to 10am and again on Christmas Day from 7am. Roslin's Make a Difference specials air on Christmas Eve from 6pm to 8pm and on Christmas Day from 12pm to 2pm. The schedule also promises highlights of past interviews with [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton), who the BBC says died in August this year.

The release does not give a broadcast slot for the Blunt segment in the part of the schedule available to us, so check your local station's listings.
