**Quick summary**

- A new biography, "The Many Faces of Johnny Depp" by [Mark Seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Seal), is due out on 24 November, according to Page Six.
- It says [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) earned about $265 million from the Pirates of the Caribbean films.
- The book cites 2017 court documents that put his career losses at $650 million, plus about $100 million in back taxes owed.
- Depp's reported pay for his Ebenezer Scrooge role is $5 million, well under his usual asking price.

A new Johnny Depp biography sets out the money behind his 40-year career, and the numbers are striking. According to [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/20/entertainment/the-staggering-amount-johnny-depp-has-earned-and-lost-during-his-decades-long-career/), Seal's "The Many Faces of Johnny Depp" arrives on 24 November. It describes an actor who earned a fortune as [Jack Sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Sparrow) and then watched much of it disappear.

## What the Johnny Depp biography says he earned

Page Six reports that Depp "amassed $265 million" from the Pirates of the Caribbean franchise. The series began in 2003 with "The Curse of the Black Pearl" and ended after five films with "Dead Men Tell No Tales" in 2017.

Seal writes that the role changed his life: Depp "didn't move without an entourage" and could no longer fly on a commercial airline.

The report adds that Depp allegedly had to make at least two major studio films a year, earning at least $40 million annually, to fund his lifestyle.

## The $650 million figure

The most eye-catching claim concerns what went out. Page Six quotes the biography as saying court documents revealed in 2017 that Depp had lost "an astounding $650 million" over his career. It adds that he owed another $100 million for 17 years of late or unpaid back taxes.

Seal describes a fortune "gutted" by excessive spending and financial mismanagement. Depp sued his money managers, who countersued. According to Page Six, the case settled out of court in 2018, before trial.

## Spending, a sold yacht and a missed film

The book gives several examples of the spending. By 2015, Page Six reports, Depp had to sell his yacht to J.K. Rowling for a reported $27 million, although he kept his private island in the Bahamas and 14 homes. We have covered [his life on that Bahamas island](https://moduscarry.com/johnny-depp-bahamas-island/).

Emails cited in the book reportedly list 10 cases of wine said to total $290,000 plus tax, and $500,000 spent at Fred Leighton Jewelers. He also reportedly spent an estimated $3 million to $5 million in 2015 on a monument and cannon that fired the ashes of his friend, the journalist Hunter S. Thompson, into the sky.

His agent at the time, [Tracey Jacobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracey_Jacobs), reportedly emailed his business manager in September 2015 asking whether anyone had told Depp he had to make $25 million by the end of that year. She had also brought him "The Grand Budapest Hotel", directed by Wes Anderson. The fee was low, but Depp would have co-owned the film. He chose "Transcendence" instead, and Jacobs estimated he lost out on about $12 million.

## A smaller pay cheque for Scrooge

The report says Depp recently returned as Ebenezer Scrooge in a screen version of [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' "A Christmas Carol". His reported pay was $5 million. That is a big drop from the $16 million he received for his last studio role, in 2018's "Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald".

Page Six says he filmed only one scene before Warner Bros. let him go amid the publicity after he lost a libel case against The Sun, which had called him a "wife-beater" over his marriage to [Amber Heard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Heard). In 2022, the report adds, Depp won a separate defamation suit against Heard over an op-ed she wrote in The Washington Post.

## What happens next

"The Many Faces of Johnny Depp" goes on sale on 24 November. Its finance claims rest on 2017 court documents and Seal's reporting, so treat the figures as the author's account as relayed by Page Six.
