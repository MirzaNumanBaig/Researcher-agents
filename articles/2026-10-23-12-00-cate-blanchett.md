**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) appears on The Graham Norton Show on BBC One at 10.40pm on Friday 23 October.
- The episode is an Earthshot Prize special, and the Prince of Wales joins the guests on the sofa as a surprise.
- Hannah Waddingham and Robert Irwin are also on the couch, with Jorja Smith as musical guest.
- Separately, Blanchett spoke to the BBC's The Interview about the Displacement Film Fund she helped launch last year.

Cate Blanchett takes the sofa on The Graham Norton Show tonight, Friday 23 October, in an episode built around the Earthshot Prize and capped by a surprise visit from Prince William. The show airs on BBC One at 10.40pm and will be available on BBC iPlayer afterwards, according to [Beyond The Joke](https://beyondthejoke.co.uk/content/18015/william-graham-norton).

## A surprise guest on the sofa

Blanchett is joined by actress [Hannah Waddingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Waddingham) and conservationist [Robert Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Irwin). Singer [Jorja Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorja_Smith) completes the line-up and performs her song Dancing live in the studio.

Fan site [Cate Blanchett Fan](https://www.cate-blanchett.com/2026/10/22/cate-blanchett-on-the-graham-norton-show-23-october/) reports that host Graham Norton chatted with the three guests about their individual work before revealing to an unsuspecting audience what links them: all are involved with the Earthshot Prize. He then invited the Prince of Wales out to join them.

## What the prince said

Asked how the prize began, William said he had grown tired of the negativity around environmental stories and wanted to pair urgency with optimism, according to Beyond The Joke. He described wanting to highlight people already protecting the planet and help them scale up.

The conversation was not all climate policy. William said he is a fan of The Celebrity Traitors, that his children are "obsessed" with it, and that he had once wanted to be a policeman and later a DJ. He also named [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) as a favourite filmmaker, praising the psychological depth of The Dark Knight films. Both outlets mention his long-time support for Aston Villa.

## Blanchett on swapping roles every night

Blanchett spoke about her National Theatre run in Electra/Persona, in which she and Nina Hoss alternate parts. She said they toss a coin each night to decide who plays which role, and neither the cast nor the audience knows the result beforehand. That meant learning the whole play, which she said made rehearsal very hard. Cate Blanchett Fan lists the production as running at the Lyttelton Theatre from 19 August to 10 October.

## The Earthshot Prize in numbers

William founded the prize in 2020, taking inspiration from John F Kennedy's Moonshot challenge. It has five goals: Protect and Restore Nature, Clean Our Air, Revive Our Oceans, Build a Waste-Free World and Fix Our Climate. Each winner receives £1 million. William has committed to the prize until 2030, and this year's winners will be announced at a ceremony in Mumbai in November.

## A second BBC conversation on displacement

The Norton appearance follows a BBC World Service interview. On The Interview, presenter Katie Razzall spoke to Blanchett, described by the BBC as a 57-year-old double Oscar winner, about her humanitarian work. She was appointed a Goodwill Ambassador for the UN refugee agency UNHCR a decade ago and addressed the UN Security Council two years later on Rohingya refugees.

Last year she helped launch the Displacement Film Fund, which backs filmmakers who have experienced displacement and films about people forced from their homes. Five filmmakers received grants, with stories from places including Ukraine, Somalia and Iran. One of them, Hasan Kattan, joined her to discuss fleeing Syria.

Blanchett told the programme that there is a "toxic narrative around displacement" that has been politicised, and that art can help people see how much they share with those who have fled. The fund's aim, she said, is to re-humanise that conversation, [the BBC reports](https://www.bbc.com/audio/play/w3ct98kv).
