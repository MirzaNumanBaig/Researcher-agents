**Quick summary**

- The Michael J. Fox Foundation's annual gala took place on 14 November at the Waldorf Astoria in New York and reportedly raised $6 million, a record for the 26-year-old organisation, according to USA Today.
- [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox), 65, attended with his wife, [Tracy Pollan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Pollan), and three of their four children.
- Pollan told People the couple copes by taking life day by day, and that shared meals make them happy.
- The foundation has reportedly funded nearly $3 billion in Parkinson's research since 2000.

The Michael J. Fox Foundation held its biggest fundraising night yet on Saturday, with a gala in New York that reportedly brought in a record $6 million for Parkinson's research. [Michael J. Fox](https://www.michaeljfox.org) walked the red carpet beside his wife and three of their children, and the family's appearance drew as much attention as the money.

For readers following the story, our earlier report on the [Michael J. Fox gala and the family's appearance](https://moduscarry.com/michael-j-fox-foundation-gala/) has the first details. This piece adds the fundraising total, the line-up and what Pollan said on the carpet.

## A record night for the foundation

The event, titled A Funny Thing Happened on the Way to Cure Parkinson's, was the foundation's 26th annual gala. USA Today reported that the $6 million raised was a record for the organisation, which Fox founded in 2000.

Fox told Getty Images on the red carpet that he had not realised how much talent was willing to give its time and resources to the cause. "It surprised me, the scope of the help," he said.

The same report says the foundation has funded nearly $3 billion in research since 2000. It credits the organisation with winning federal resources and with helping to advance a spinal fluid test for people showing symptoms of the disease. Put another way, one evening's total is a small slice of a much larger effort, but a record all the same.

## Music, comedy and a Chuck Berry finale

Denis Leary hosted, according to Female First. John Mayer and [Brad Paisley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Paisley) headlined the music, pairing up for acoustic versions of Mayer's songs and a cover of [Free Fallin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Fallin'), per USA Today.

Comedy came from [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert), [David Letterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Letterman), John Oliver, Josh Johnson and Kylie Vincent, among others. Tony-winning singer [Shoshana Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshana_Bean) and No Doubt's Tom Dumont, who announced his own early-onset Parkinson's diagnosis earlier this year, joined Fox to close the night with [Johnny B. Goode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_B._Goode).

## The family at his side

Fox and Pollan, 66, were joined by twin daughters Aquinnah and Schuyler, both 31, and Esmé, 25. Their 37-year-old son, Sam, was not photographed at this year's event, though USA Today notes he was at the 2025 gala. E! News, relayed by Ukrainian outlet UNN, described the full-family turnout as a rare sight.

Pollan, who met Fox on the set of [Family Ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Ties) and married him in 1988, spoke to People. "I think you just have to do it day by day," she said. "You have to look at what's in front of you... and you have to stay in the moment."

Her answer to what keeps them happy was simpler still. The two have very different tastes in books and television, she said, but "we both always love a great meal."

## What Fox said about early detection

On the carpet, Fox returned to the point he has made for years: catching the disease early. "When I was diagnosed, my pinky was twitching," he said, adding that 70 per cent of the dopamine-producing cells in his brain were already gone. If doctors can test for it and treat it sooner, he said, "it would save a lot of grief."

Fox was diagnosed in 1991 at age 29. By his own account to USA Today in July, he hid it for seven years before deciding, after long talks with Pollan, to go public.

The Independent notes that he received the Bob Hope Humanitarian Award at this year's Emmys, and that he has appeared in the third season of Apple TV+'s Shrinking despite announcing his retirement from acting in 2020. He told The Hollywood Reporter he is "not retired from acting".
