**Quick summary**

- [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) believes someone privately kept the cherry-red Gibson ES-345 he played in *Back to the Future*.
- The guitar vanished after the 1985 shoot, and Gibson's search, launched in June 2025, has not found it.
- Investigators did locate the second guitar used in *Back to the Future Part II*.
- A Gibson documentary, *Lost to the Future*, is reportedly tied to the guitar being recovered.

Michael J. Fox has a theory about where the guitar from the "Enchantment Under the Sea" dance went: someone took it home and has stayed quiet since. The actor shared the idea in his memoir, and it resurfaced this week, according to [a report on cwibc.co.id](https://cwibc.co.id/en/michael-fox-missing-back-future-guitar), as the franchise marked the anniversary of its sequel.

## What Fox says happened to the guitar

The instrument is the cherry-red Gibson ES-345 that Marty McFly plays through Chuck Berry's "Johnny B. Goode". It disappeared after production on the 1985 film wrapped, and nobody has publicly accounted for it since.

In his memoir *Future Boy: Back to the Future and My Journey Through the Space-Time Continuum*, Fox wrote that he would love to claim he owns it but never imagined how valuable it would become. Someone else did, he suggested. "Somebody did anticipate that and snagged it for themselves," he wrote, as quoted by Parade, adding that it could be in an active collection, an attic or a home studio.

He also offered a dry guess about why the holder is silent: they may be "waiting for the statute of limitations on vintage guitar theft to expire." That is Fox's own speculation, not a finding, and the report names no suspect.

## Gibson's search so far

Gibson started looking in June 2025, ahead of the original film's 40th anniversary. The company brought in the Lost Bass Project team, which previously helped trace [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney)'s Höfner 500/1 bass after it had been missing for 51 years.

The results so far are partial. Investigators found the secondary guitar used in *Back to the Future Part II*, but not the original ES-345. According to [Guitar Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Player), the guitar was loaned to the production by Norman's Rare Guitars, after the props team dropped an earlier plan involving a Stratocaster or a Gretsch with a Bigsby. The report says a block parallelogram inlay at the 12th fret could help identify it among similar models.

Gibson is also developing a documentary called *Lost to the Future* about the hunt. Fox, quoted in the report, said its release will come after the guitar is found, "if that ever happens."

## Why the scene still matters to him

Fox has described the scene as a chance to pay tribute to his heroes. He said he sat down with the cinematographer and choreographer and asked to work in moves from [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix) (playing behind the head), [Pete Townshend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Townshend) (the windmill) and [Eddie Van Halen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Van_Halen) (the hammer-on). He said the crew were open to it.

## The sequel's anniversary

The attention landed alongside the 37th anniversary of *Back to the Future Part II*, which opened in US cinemas on 22 November 1989. The original premiered on 3 July 1985.

The report recounts several casting changes. [Crispin Glover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crispin_Glover) did not return as [George McFly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Back_to_the_Future_(franchise)_characters) after a pay dispute, and [Jeffrey Weissman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Weissman) took over, wearing prosthetics. According to the Hollywood Reporter, as cited there, Glover reportedly asked for US$1 million to return. [Elisabeth Shue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elisabeth_Shue) replaced [Claudia Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Wells) as Jennifer Parker; Wells had stepped away from acting to care for her mother.

Critics were cooler on the sequel. [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) gives it 63%, against 93% for the original. It still did well at the box office: US$332.8 million worldwide according to Box Office Mojo, compared with US$391.84 million for the first film. Among 1989 releases, only [*Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Jones_and_the_Last_Crusade) (US$474.1 million) and *Batman* (US$411.56 million) earned more.

## What happens next

Nothing is scheduled. Gibson's search remains open, and the documentary depends on a discovery that has not happened.
