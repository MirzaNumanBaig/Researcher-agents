**Quick summary**

- The Independent's review of *Ebenezer* says [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) is "a problem" the film cannot overcome.
- The reviewer calls the film mostly well made and surprisingly traditional, with director [Ti West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti_West) praised for its horror-tinged ghost scenes.
- Depp's performance is described as stylised and cartoonish, close to what he gave 15 years ago.
- The paper says the film's commercial result will show how much pull Depp still has, including for a rumoured *Pirates of the Caribbean* return.

The Independent has delivered a pointed verdict on *Ebenezer*, the new Charles Dickens adaptation that marks Johnny Depp's return to a leading role. In a review published on 13 November, the paper says the film is competently made but that its Scrooge is the weak link, and that the star's past controversies cannot be separated from how the film is received.

## A traditional Christmas Carol with a few new touches

According to [the review](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/reviews/johnny-depp-ebenezer-review-b3084292.html), *Ebenezer* is far more conventional than its marketing suggests. One trailer tells viewers to "think again" about the story, but the reviewer argues there is little to rethink. The main change is a prologue in which Jacob Marley, played by Sir [Ian McKellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McKellen), is still alive and working alongside Scrooge. The reviewer suggests it exists mainly to set up a shot of Marley's corpse.

The film runs 116 minutes and carries a 12A rating in the UK.

## Where the film works

The Independent gives most of the credit to West, who has shown "real promise" in the horror film *The House of the Devil* and the *X* trilogy, though it calls that trilogy a run of diminishing returns. His handling of the ghost-story elements gives the film a confident but restrained style, the review says.

Two images get particular praise. One is [Andrea Riseborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Riseborough)'s opaque-eyed makeup as the Ghost of Christmas Past. The other is a crow emerging from the mouth of Christmas Future.

The supporting cast is called "perfectly serviceable". [Tramell Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramell_Tillman), known for *Severance*, stands out as a gluttonous, decaying Ghost of Christmas Present. [Rupert Grint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Grint) plays Bob Cratchit and [Sam Claflin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Claflin) plays Scrooge's nephew Fred. [Daisy Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Ridley), as Fred's wife Clara, gets very little dialogue, the reviewer notes.

## The verdict on Depp's Scrooge

The review is harshest on the lead. It describes Depp's work as "a liability", stylised and cartoonish, with lines often delivered in a "piqued, squeaky" register that only sometimes lands as funny. The reviewer compares him unfavourably with the great screen Scrooges, naming [Alastair Sim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alastair_Sim) in 1951 and Michael Caine in the Muppet version from the Nineties. In the paper's view, a stronger Scrooge might have lifted the film into the canon of lasting adaptations.

The reviewer also writes that Depp, once an A-lister, has been absent from the American film industry for eight years. The paper links that gap to domestic abuse claims made by his former wife, [Amber Heard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Heard). It notes that Depp denied the allegations, sued for defamation and ultimately came out of the Heard trial with a ruling in his favour. It adds that he had earlier lost a separate lawsuit against *The Sun*, which had called him a "wife beater". These are the reviewer's characterisation of events, and the paper argues they shape how many viewers will see his speech about repentance in the film's final act.

## What happens next

The Independent says the question of whether Depp deserves a comeback is "slightly moot", since he already has one. What matters now is whether audiences turn up. It calls the film a bellwether for his Hollywood future and says a rumoured return to the *Pirates of the Caribbean* franchise will not depend on *Ebenezer* alone, but the box office will indicate how much popular clout he has kept. That return is a rumour and has not been confirmed in the source.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who directed Ebenezer?
Ti West directed the film, according to The Independent. The paper credits him with its stylish, horror-inflected ghost scenes.

### Who stars in Ebenezer alongside Johnny Depp?
The cast includes Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Sam Claflin, Daisy Ridley, Tramell Tillman and Ian McKellen.

### How long is Ebenezer?
It runs 116 minutes and is rated 12A in the UK, per the Independent's review details.
