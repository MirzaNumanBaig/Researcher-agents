**Quick summary**

- A new documentary, *SCTV: The Greatest Show You've Never Heard Of*, covers the full run of the Canadian sketch series, which aired from 1976 to 1984.
- [Martin Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Short) appears in a group interview filmed in July 2025 with Catherine O'Hara, Eugene Levy, Dave Thomas and Andrea Martin.
- According to What's on Netflix, O'Hara died about six months after that session, which gives the film a bittersweet weight.
- Director Jason Hehir also interviews Rick Moranis and the late Joe Flaherty.

[Martin Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Short) is back with his old [Second City Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_City_Television) colleagues in a new documentary that traces how a low-budget Canadian sketch show became one of comedy's great launchpads. *SCTV: The Greatest Show You've Never Heard Of* has been reviewed by [What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/what-to-watch/sctv-the-greatest-show-youve-never-heard-of-netflix-review/), which calls it a bittersweet reunion anchored by the late Catherine O'Hara.

## The couch reunion at the centre

The film's emotional core is a conversation filmed in July 2025. Short, O'Hara, [Eugene Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Levy), [Dave Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Thomas_(actor)) and [Andrea Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Martin) sit together on a couch and watch old clips and behind-the-scenes footage while swapping memories.

The reviewer writes that it is hard to watch the cast look back at their younger selves, with some of them tearing up. O'Hara is quoted describing the show as the ideal outlet for your 20s, because you are cocky at that age and the troupe gave you a place to express it in a funny way.

That July session took place roughly six months before O'Hara's death, according to the review. The film also includes interviews with [Rick Moranis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Moranis) and [Joe Flaherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Flaherty), who died in 2024.

## A show that outgrew its budget

The documentary follows the series across its whole life, from 1976 to 1984. The review says SCTV began as a direct rival to *Saturday Night Live*, then took John Candy and Dave Thomas from the early cast mix.

When money ran short, the broadcaster and surgeon [Charles Allard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Allard) stepped in, and production moved to a studio he owned in Edmonton. Bob and Doug McKenzie became breakout characters, and Moranis and Thomas went on to make [*Strange Brew*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Brew).

The review says the story ends after NBC picked the show up and moved it to New York, stretching the format from 30 to 90 minutes. Executive plans to make it more family-oriented were, in the reviewer's account, the final straw that sent the cast their separate ways.

## Why Short's presence matters

The reviewer notes that this is the second SCTV-linked documentary of the year. Earlier in 2026 came *Marty, Life Is Short*, an intimate portrait of Short, whose career began on the series. The two films give audiences a close look at the same comedy family from different angles.

Fans of the show's alumni will recognise the names. The review lists *Home Alone*, *Schitt's Creek*, *Father of the Bride*, *My Big Fat Greek Wedding* and *Honey, I Shrunk the Kids* among the later work that grew out of SCTV. [*Schitt's Creek*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schitt's_Creek) in particular ties back to the cast through Levy and O'Hara.

## Fans and new viewers

Younger viewers are a target. The reviewer, who had only seen a handful of sketches, says the film works as an introduction for people who never grew up with SCTV. [Bill Hader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Hader) and [Bob Odenkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Odenkirk) appear as fans, with Odenkirk calling the series brilliant and Hader recalling being shocked as a child by the dark "Yellowbelly" sketch.

The reviewer also says the film made them feel the loss of a time when sketch comedy was widespread on television across many audiences.
