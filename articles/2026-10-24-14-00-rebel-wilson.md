**Quick summary**

- A Los Angeles Superior Court judge, Thomas Long, denied the producers' request for sanctions against [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) on 22 October 2026, according to Law Commentary.
- The producers had sought to dismiss her claims or impose more than US$474,000 in penalties over alleged false testimony and evidence manipulation.
- The judge left the door open to jury instructions on suppressed evidence, or an amended complaint alleging evidence destruction.
- Wilson disputes the allegations, and none of them has been proven.
- A trial is currently set for 19 January 2027, though both sides have asked to postpone it.

A California judge has refused to punish Rebel Wilson over claims that she lied under oath and tampered with evidence in her legal fight with the producers of her film *The Deb*. Los Angeles Superior Court Judge Thomas Long denied the sanctions motion on 22 October 2026, [Law Commentary reported](https://www.lawcommentary.com/articles/rebel-wilson-deb-lawsuit-judge-denies-sanctions). The underlying allegations remain unresolved.

## What the producers asked for

Producers Amanda Ghost, her husband [Gregor Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Cameron) and Vince Holden asked for terminating sanctions, which would have ended Wilson's claims outright. As a fallback they sought financial penalties of more than US$474,000, plus an instruction telling a future jury that Wilson had deliberately destroyed evidence and repeatedly given false testimony.

The motion was prepared by attorneys [Camille Vasquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Vasquez) and Samuel Moniz. It accused Wilson of fraud on the court, a serious form of alleged misconduct aimed at undermining the judicial process.

At the hearing, Judge Long noted how high the bar is for throwing out a party's claims, calling that kind of penalty "very difficult, right out of the box," according to the report.

## The allegations at the centre

One claim concerns a demand letter sent early in the dispute by Wilson's lawyer, Bryan Freedman. The producers say Wilson denied under oath that she gave it to a reporter. Their lawyers pointed to a 12 July 2024 message, produced in discovery, in which Wilson allegedly told her publicist, Marcy Engelman, to send the letter "immediately" to a journalist.

The second concerns anonymous websites aimed at Ghost, including one called amandaghostsucks.com. The motion said communications involving Wilson's crisis public relations team discussed sites that would depict Ghost as a criminal. It also cited a proposed US$110,000 fee for an allegedly untraceable site, and document metadata the producers said named Wilson's company, Camp Sugar, as the author.

Wilson denies orchestrating the websites or manipulating evidence. Her attorney, Allyson K. Thompson, argued that the producers wanted extraordinary penalties while discovery was still under way and before Wilson had finished her deposition. Thompson said a jury should weigh the competing accounts and decide on credibility, and accused the producers of using the motion to damage her client's reputation.

## What the judge left open

The ruling is not a clean win for either side. Judge Long's written order allows the producers to seek jury instructions about the intentional suppression of evidence. They may instead ask permission to amend their complaint to add allegations of evidence destruction or manipulation.

## How the case got here

The litigation began in July 2024, after Wilson publicly accused the producers of financial misconduct and inappropriate behaviour on *The Deb*. Ghost, Cameron and Holden sued for defamation, alleging her statements followed disputes over creative control and writing credits. Wilson countersued, alleging theft, bullying, intimidation and breach of contract. Her claims included allegations about Ghost's conduct towards lead actress Charlotte MacInnes, who disputed Wilson's account of an incident during filming.

The California decision came a day before the Supreme Court of New South Wales awarded Wilson's production company about A$359,000 in unpaid fees, and a subsidiary of AI Film just A$10 in nominal damages over breaches of a social media agreement. Justice Elisabeth Peden found Wilson had asked for a malicious website targeting Ghost, but said the other company had not shown the site caused its claimed losses. That Australian finding does not decide liability in the California case. We covered the result in [Rebel Wilson wins $359,000 as The Deb producers get $10](https://moduscarry.com/rebel-wilson-wins-359000-as-the-deb-producers-get-10/).

In July, an Australian court also dismissed MacInnes's defamation claim against Wilson, finding the statements were not defamatory and that serious reputational harm had not been shown. MacInnes has appealed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did the judge find that Rebel Wilson lied or destroyed evidence?

No. Judge Long only denied the request for sanctions. The allegations are unresolved, and Wilson disputes them.

### What penalties did the producers want?

They sought dismissal of Wilson's claims or more than US$474,000 in financial penalties, along with a jury instruction about deliberate evidence destruction and false testimony.

### Can the producers still pursue these claims?

Yes. The ruling lets them seek jury instructions on suppressed evidence or ask to amend their complaint to allege evidence destruction or manipulation.

### When is the trial?

It is currently set for 19 January 2027, though both sides have sought a delay, with Wilson's lawyer requesting March.
