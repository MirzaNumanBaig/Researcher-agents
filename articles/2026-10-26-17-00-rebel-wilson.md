**Quick summary**

- The NSW Supreme Court has dismissed a $15 million claim by UK company AI Film against [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) over her film *The Deb*, according to ABC News.
- Wilson's company, Camp Sugar, was awarded $359,000 in unpaid producer fees, plus her legal costs.
- Justice Elisabeth Peden also found Wilson asked for anonymous websites targeting producer Amanda Ghost and that the statements on them were made with malice.
- The judge was not satisfied the websites caused AI Film financial loss. A related US defamation case is still running.

A Sydney judge has handed [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) a clear financial win in her fight with the producers of *The Deb*, while making findings about her own conduct that she will not welcome. The NSW Supreme Court ruling, reported on 23 October 2026, dismissed a $15 million claim against her and awarded her company $359,000.

## What the court decided

Justice Elisabeth Peden dismissed the claim brought by AI Film, a UK production company, according to [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-23/rebel-wilson-awarded-359-000-in-legal-win-the-deb-co-producers/107303226). AI Film argued that false statements by Wilson, made on her social media accounts and through a fake takedown website, had made the film unprofitable.

The judge said she was "not satisfied" that AI Film had shown a causal link between the alleged misleading representations and its losses, ABC reported. A subsidiary funded by AI Film received $10 in nominal damages for technical breaches of its social media contract. It was ordered to pay Wilson's company, Camp Sugar, $359,000 in unpaid producer fees, along with Wilson's legal costs.

The scale of the gap is the story: the producers sought $15 million and ended with $10, while Wilson's side recovered more than 35,000 times that figure.

## The findings against Wilson

The ruling was not one-sided. The [Star Observer](https://www.starobserver.com.au/news/judge-finds-rebel-wilson-was-behind-targetted-smear-websites-following-court-win/244743) reported that the judge found Wilson paid a US crisis publicity firm, The Agency Group, to create websites targeting Amanda Ghost, AI Film's chief executive. According to the outlet, Peden said she was satisfied "the statements published in the websites were actuated with malice on Ms Wilson's part."

ABC reported that one of the sites, amandaghostsucks.com, made claims about Ghost, but that none of the people it named were AI Film producers and there was no proof it cost the company money. The Star Observer said Wilson had repeatedly denied any involvement and that the judge found that denial untrue.

## How the budget dispute fed in

ABC reported that the court found the UK producers added $900,000 in extra fees to the budget without telling Wilson. Wilson had publicly accused the producers of embezzling that sum in Instagram posts. Emails from October 2025 cited in the case showed international distributors worried the film felt "small and distinctly Australian."

*The Deb* premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival in September 2024 and reached Australian cinemas in April 2026, where it earned about $335,000, according to both outlets. That is a fraction of what the producers claimed in losses.

## Wilson's response

Wilson posted on Instagram thanking the judge and her legal team. The Star Observer said she described the defence as costly personally, emotionally and financially, thanked her two daughters, and wrote "Two lawsuits. Two wins."

The second win refers to July 2026, when a defamation claim by *The Deb* star Charlotte MacInnes over Wilson's posts was dismissed. ## What happens next

Costs in the fee case had not been finally determined at the time of the Star Observer report. MacInnes has lodged an appeal against her defamation loss, and AI Film's producers are suing Wilson for defamation in California, according to ABC.
