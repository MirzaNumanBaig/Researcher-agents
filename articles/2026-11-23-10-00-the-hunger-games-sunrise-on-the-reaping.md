**Quick summary**

- Australian actor [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), 21, plays a young Haymitch Abernathy in [The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games%3A_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping).
- He told Variety his first day on set was the opening cornucopia scene, and that he trained in scuba diving for an underwater sequence.
- The Heights, a student newspaper, says the film opened in theatres on Friday 20 November and rests on his performance.
- The same review faults the PG-13 rating for softening the violence of the Games.

Joseph Zada has spent the past week explaining how a young Australian actor ended up leading one of the biggest franchises in cinema. In *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, he plays the teenage Haymitch Abernathy, and both a Variety interview and a campus review published on 22 November put him at the centre of the film.

## A first day in the arena

Zada told [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/features/hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-joseph-zada-action-scenes-1236954627/) that his first day on set was the film's opening cornucopia scene, where nearly 50 children are released to fight to the death. The first five weeks of the shoot were spent inside the arena. He called that scheduling "genius" because it let him settle into Haymitch before facing the film's veterans, including [Glenn Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Close), [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes), [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons) and [Kieran Culkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieran_Culkin).

He said the order let him work with the younger cast first. The heavier dialogue scenes came later, once he felt confident.

## The audition that took five weeks

The role began with a tape. According to Variety, Zada auditioned before [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins)'s prequel novel was published. He was working on the Netflix series *East of Eden* at the time and flew home to Australia to record it.

The brief was to improvise a scene as a younger version of Woody Harrelson's Haymitch. Zada instead wrote four scenes, used a drone and found a stand-in for Maysilee, the character played in the film by [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace). His father and older brother, who are also actors, helped. He had been given two weeks, but he told the production he needed five.

"Took that risk, and it paid off!" he said.

## Stunts, scuba and a close call

Zada said he read the manuscript several times and studied Harrelson's performance, but avoided copying his mannerisms. He focused on the voice and trusted the scenes.

The physical work was heavy. He named an explosion shot filmed in slow motion as his favourite to watch back, and joked that he looks like an action star in it.

An underwater scene was shot on his final day. He took three weeks of scuba lessons on weekends while filming during the week. On set, the crew removed his air supply once the cameras rolled and switched on the jets. In one take the jets were set too strong and he was thrown into a pole. A crew member had to bring him air.

He also said the film's last 30 minutes, which deal with Haymitch's trauma, took an emotional toll. He shot at least one demanding scene a week.

## What reviewers are saying

The Heights, Boston College's student newspaper, [called the film](https://bcheights.com/234999/arts/the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-rests-on-the-steady-shoulders-of-joseph-zada/) a worthy return to Panem. It said Zada makes the role his own, playing Haymitch as vulnerable rather than as an action hero. It also praised [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)) as Lenore Dove and Grace as Maysilee. [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) directs, returning for a fourth Hunger Games film.

The review was not all praise. It argued that the PG-13 rating holds back the violence, and that the large ensemble gets little room beside Zada. It rated the arena, a bright green space with flower beds around the cornucopia, as the best-looking in the series.

On the wider reception, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping) lists a 68% Rotten Tomatoes score and a Metacritic score of 55 out of 100 from 43 critics. It also records a budget of US$150 million and a worldwide gross of US$237.2 million so far. Those figures come from a crowd-edited page, so treat them as a snapshot.
