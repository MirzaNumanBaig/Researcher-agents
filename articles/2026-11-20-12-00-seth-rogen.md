Seth Rogen Spielberg stories are back in the news: the filmmaking lessons he took from the director, and a 25-year-old grudge over a role he didn't get. The Spielberg comments come from a recent podcast interview.

## What Seth Rogen learned from Spielberg

According to [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/seth-rogen-steven-spielberg-directors), Rogen appeared on Josh Horowitz's Happy Sad Confused podcast at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival. He talked about acting for Spielberg in The Fabelmans and what he took away about directing and sustaining a long career. The site says most of the practical advice arrives around the 40-minute mark.

Rogen is a director himself, working alongside his regular collaborator [Evan Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Goldberg). That, No Film School suggests, is why he treated the job like a class. He said Spielberg loves talking about filmmaking more than anything, so he asked "a million questions" about how a scene was being shot, why it was framed that way and when the blocking was decided. He even pulled up scenes from Spielberg's earlier films to ask how they were done.

## The pick-up shots that changed his thinking

The biggest takeaway concerned mistakes. Rogen said Spielberg was constantly going back to collect shots for earlier days' scenes, such as inserts or fixes, and never seemed bothered by it. Rogen admitted he had always felt a little embarrassed when he had to return to something he hadn't nailed the first time.

Spielberg's attitude, as Rogen told it, was simple: he was editing in his head, he knew what he needed, and he would get the shot. No Film School reads that as a reminder that audiences never see how many takes it took, only whether the scene works.

## Patience at the top

Rogen also praised Spielberg's temperament. He called him a very nice man with a great deal of patience, and said that quality gets squeezed out of many people who reach his level of success. Staying that way, Rogen said, is "really amazing". No Film School describes the set as one with no fear, where a cheerful director encouraged his team.

## A different kind of story: the audition he never got over

Far Out Magazine, meanwhile, has revisited an older Rogen anecdote. Back in 2000, he was a teenager whose only major credit was Freaks and Geeks, the series that began his long working relationship with [Judd Apatow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judd_Apatow). Apatow later directed him in The 40-Year-Old Virgin and [Knocked Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up).

Rogen auditioned for Dude, Where's My Car?, the stoner comedy about two friends who can't remember where they parked. The parts went to [Ashton Kutcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashton_Kutcher) and [Seann William Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seann_William_Scott). Speaking to The AV Club in 2008, Rogen said he has never seen the film because he auditioned, lost and felt bitter. Far Out notes that this was while he was promoting Pineapple Express, and that the old film has since become a cult favourite.

Rogen also joked that he knew where his car was. Far Out's view is that he turned out fine, because he started co-writing the comedies he wanted to be in.

## Frequently asked questions

### What did Seth Rogen learn from Steven Spielberg?
He said Spielberg openly goes back to pick up shots when the edit needs them, and does so without ego. He also praised his patience and kindness on set, according to No Film School.

### Has Seth Rogen seen Dude, Where's My Car?
In a 2008 AV Club interview he said no, because he auditioned for it and didn't get the role. Far Out Magazine reports he has held that grudge for more than 25 years.

### Who got the roles Rogen auditioned for?
Ashton Kutcher and Seann William Scott played the leads, according to Far Out Magazine.
