**Quick summary**

- The Michael J. Fox Foundation has launched a US$2.5 billion campaign called All Grit. No Quit. to speed up Parkinson's research.
- The foundation says more than US$1.75 billion has already come from over 276,000 donors, so the campaign is past the halfway mark.
- It was announced publicly on 17 September but quietly began in 2024, according to The Times of India.
- The money is meant to back research at several stages rather than a single approach.

[Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox)'s charity has put a number on its biggest push yet. The [Michael J. Fox Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation) for Parkinson's Research is running a US$2.5 billion campaign to accelerate new treatments and, ultimately, a cure, and it says it has already raised more than US$1.75 billion toward that goal.

## What the Michael J. Fox Foundation campaign is

Called All Grit. No Quit., the initiative is the foundation's first multi-year fundraising campaign, [The Times of India reports](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/michael-j-fox-foundation-launches-a-2-5-billion-parkinsons-research-campaign-after-raising-more-than-1-75-billion-from-276000-donors-worldwide-the-initiative-aims-to-accelerate-new-treatments-and-a-cure/articleshow/135923209.cms). The public announcement came on 17 September 2026, but fundraising had been running quietly since 2024.

By September, the total stood at more than US$1.75 billion from over 276,000 donors worldwide. That is roughly 70 per cent of the US$2.5 billion target. The campaign draws on patients, scientists, pharmaceutical companies, government partners and philanthropists.

The foundation's grassroots network is part of that base. According to the report, it includes more than 13,000 Team Fox events and fundraisers, along with 600 Legacy Circle members.

## Where the money is meant to go

The campaign is tied to the foundation's Strategic Research Agenda. The aim is to support research and drug development across multiple stages, building on earlier investments rather than betting on one route to a treatment.

The foundation points to its record as the case for continuing. It says its work contributed to the discovery and validation of a Parkinson's biomarker in 2023. It also notes that 23 new drugs have been approved over the past decade, with another 175 in clinical testing. It says it helped secure the National Plan to End Parkinson's Act, which was signed into law in July 2024.

These are the foundation's own figures, relayed by The Times of India, and they describe its contribution rather than sole credit for those advances.

## How donations are used

The foundation says gifts are put to work quickly rather than held in an endowment or excess reserves. An in-house team of 24 PhD neuroscientists and strategists reviews and funds projects in real time, it says, using global collaboration and input from people living with Parkinson's to set priorities.

The donor figures are worth noting. The foundation reported that 144,440 supporters gave during the previous year, with nearly half giving for the first time. The median gift was US$51.50, and the number of contributors has nearly doubled over the past decade, according to the report.

## Why the foundation says the need is urgent

The foundation estimates that about six million people worldwide live with Parkinson's, and that this number could double by 2040. In the United States alone, around 90,000 people are diagnosed each year.

Against that, the foundation says the campaign should speed up treatments that can improve lives now while keeping the longer-term goal of a cure in view. It also says it has helped expand treatment options by 200 per cent since it was established in 2000, with nearly two dozen treatments approved over the past decade.

## What happens next

The campaign is multi-year, and about US$750 million remains to reach the target, going by the foundation's figures. The sources do not give an end date or a breakdown of how that remaining amount will be allocated. The foundation's [official website](https://www.michaeljfox.org) is the place to look for updates.
