**Quick summary**

- Director [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh) gave an interview published on 11 November 2026 about his new film, [Wild Horse Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine).
- The film follows two CIA agents, played by John Malkovich and [Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell), on Easter Island days before the coup in Chile.
- McDonagh says the secrets of past US operations are public knowledge, yet few people react.
- He says he stands outside the Hollywood system and prefers stories about ordinary, flawed men.

Martin McDonagh used a new interview with El Mundo America, published on 11 November 2026, to explain what drives **Martin McDonagh Wild Horse Nine**, his latest film, and why he sees it as more than a period piece. The London-born writer and director described a story about two CIA agents, about friendship between men, and about a public that he feels has stopped reacting to the crimes of governments.

## A film about two agents and a coup

According to [El Mundo America](https://www.mundoamerica.com/entertainment/2026/11/11/6af43cbce85ece74488b4588.html), Wild Horse Nine follows two CIA agents on Easter Island (Rapa Nui) in the days before the 1973 coup in Chile. John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell play the pair, who look back on their sins, wounds and defeats. The outlet describes the film as a story of lost and ruthless men who are, above all, in love with their own failure.

The interviewer noted that McDonagh wrote his play The Beauty Queen of Leenane at 26 and has since become a major figure in cinema. The outlet counts 17 Oscar nominations across his five films, and says his short Six Shooter won the award for the director and for its stars, [Frances McDormand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_McDormand) and Sam Rockwell. His two previous features, Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri and The Banshees of Inisherin, led up to this one.

## "No one seems to react"

Asked whether the film is a history lesson showing that nothing has been learned, McDonagh agreed in part. He pointed to what the CIA and the US government tried to keep hidden in Chile in 1973, at the Bay of Pigs and in the Congo. Those matters, he said, are no longer secret, and still nobody seems to react.

His comment that governments no longer hide their crimes and people do not care became the interview's headline. He added that he trusts people will eventually see how absurd the situation is, and that this will give them more reason to push back. He also said he hopes viewers will go and read up on the history afterwards rather than be misled by far-right propaganda.

The interviewer recalled that, at the film's presentation in Venice, McDonagh had said the world knows a lot about KGB atrocities and little about those of the CIA. On who is to blame, he named no single culprit. In his view, everyone shares some responsibility, and his own job is to make the film as truthful as he can.

## Friendship between men

Pressed on why so many of his stories centre on two men, McDonagh joked that it may be time to find a therapist. His simpler answer is that he finds close, loving relationships between men interesting, and that cinema rarely explores them. He said he has never been drawn to Hollywood's usual brilliant, unique heroes, calling those stories similar and dull. In his words, most people are not brilliant, and sad, awkward or problematic stories are closer to what most of us live.

On toxic masculinity, he said he detests it but does not set out to change opinions. He would rather show that men do not have to be that way, and that subtlety and love can sit at the centre of their relationships. He stressed that the film's characters are not role models, though he sees an unspoken love between the two leads that is palpable on screen.

## Outside the system

McDonagh also said he does not see himself as part of the Hollywood system and feels clearly outside it. He added that he has no interest in offering an outsider's view of Americans just because he was born in London. Standing a little to the side, he suggested, is a privilege that lets a filmmaker look for a kind of truth that might otherwise be out of reach. The published text of the interview breaks off there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Wild Horse Nine about?
It follows two CIA agents on Easter Island, played by John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell, who reflect on their sins and defeats in the days before the 1973 coup in Chile, according to El Mundo America.

### Who directed Wild Horse Nine?
Martin McDonagh directed it. He previously made Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri and The Banshees of Inisherin.

### Does McDonagh consider himself part of Hollywood?
No. He says he is clearly outside the Hollywood system, and values the distance it gives him.
