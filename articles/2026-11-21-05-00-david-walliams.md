**Quick summary**

- A 2004 joke by [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) has resurfaced online and drawn criticism, according to the London Evening Standard.
- Ross has apologised, saying the line was written for him by the awards' scriptwriting team and that he thought it referred to Walliams dating younger women.
- The Times reports that Ross's ITV work will continue, including The Masked Singer UK and The Jonathan Ross Show.
- Walliams strongly denies the allegations made against him, and says he is "not a sexual predator" and has "not broken the law".

The row over a decade-old awards-show joke has pulled [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) into the allegations surrounding [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams). The London Evening Standard reports that Ross has apologised for the line, while sources told The Times his ITV schedule is unchanged.

## What the 2004 joke said

The clip comes from the 2004 British Comedy Awards. Ross remarked that the ceremony was 15 years old and, in the Standard's account, added that like all 15-year-olds it was drunk, unpredictable and "probably going home with David Walliams".

After the footage circulated on social media, critics described it as "disgusting" and "inappropriate", the Standard reports. Ross made a public apology for what he called a "tasteless" joke.

## Ross's explanation

In a statement quoted by the Standard, Ross said he recognised how shocking it was to revisit the footage. He said the joke was submitted by the Comedy Awards scriptwriting team and that he took the punchline to refer to the fact that Walliams had publicly dated younger women, exaggerated for comic effect.

He added that, had he suspected it referred to real events, he would have refused to use it and would not have been comfortable continuing a personal or professional relationship. The Times, as cited by the Standard, says it has seen a programme from the 2004 awards that lists Ross as one of four writers. The same report says Ross strongly denies writing the joke or having any prior knowledge of Walliams' alleged behaviour.

## What is alleged, and what Walliams says

The Standard says Walliams was named last week in an investigation by the Guardian and ITV News, after he tried to obtain an injunction to stop the allegations being reported. According to that report, one woman described an encounter at 15 as a "sexual assault", and several women accused him of grooming them when they were teenagers. The investigation also reported allegations of inappropriate conduct during his time on Britain's Got Talent and at the publisher HarperCollins. The Standard says the comedian is the focus of a Metropolitan Police investigation into non-recent allegations.

Walliams strongly denies the allegations. In a statement quoted by the Standard he said he had at times "behaved thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual relationships with and regretted it. He also said abusing a position of fame was something he never did, and that he was "devastated" that some women he met 10 to 20 years ago now felt hurt or anger.

These are allegations only. Nothing in the sources reports any finding or charge.

## The wider debate about children's authors

A separate piece in The Irish Times, a review of the filmed play Giant about [Roald Dahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roald_Dahl), opens by referring to the way Walliams "blew up" the children's books industry. The review is about Dahl's own controversy, but its framing shows how far the story has reached into publishing and the arts.

## What happens next

The Standard says it has contacted ITV for comment and has asked Ross for further remarks. An insider told The Times that both of Ross's shows are in process, with "no change on either" and a plan for them to return. Further developments in the police inquiry have not been reported in these sources.
