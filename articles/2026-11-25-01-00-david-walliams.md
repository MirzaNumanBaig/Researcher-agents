**Quick summary**

- Former HarperCollins staff say the publisher tried to hide complaints about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), according to reporting by The Guardian and ITV News published on 24 November 2026.
- The claims include an alleged "inappropriate" relationship with an intern and an approach to a girl who appeared to be in her teens at a book signing.
- The Metropolitan Police says it is investigating separate non-recent allegations of serious sexual offences.
- Walliams has previously denied all allegations, including any criminality. HarperCollins says it ended its relationship with him last year.

Former employees of David Walliams' publisher have accused HarperCollins of a "cover-up", saying repeated warnings about his behaviour were met with lawyers and a non-disclosure agreement rather than action. The claims were published on Tuesday 24 November by The Guardian and ITV News, and summarised by [Metro](https://metro.co.uk/2026/11/24/harpercollins-accused-trying-cover-up-david-walliams-behaviour-29816372/) and the [Daily Mail](https://www.dailymail.com/news/article-16318129/David-Walliams-publisher-HarperCollins-accused-former-staff-cover-claims-inappropriate-behaviour-women-girls.html). These are allegations, and Walliams denies them.

## What former staff allege

According to Metro, former staff told the two news organisations that Charlie Redmayne, who was chief executive from 2013 to 2025, and senior management were repeatedly told about concerns and that nothing was done.

The concerns reportedly include an "inappropriate" sexual relationship with a woman who was an intern when they first met, and inappropriate interactions with at least three other women connected to the company, including asking two of them on dates. The Daily Mail reports that the intern was in her 20s in 2017, when Walliams was 45, and that she told colleagues the relationship was consensual but, looking back, the result of "an abuse of power".

The third strand concerns a book signing. A former employee told the reporters that Walliams approached a girl who appeared to be in her mid-teens and seemed to ask for her Instagram details. The employee described being alarmed because the event was in a children's bookstore.

## The 2023 inquiry

In 2023, HarperCollins brought in the City law firm Allen & Overy to look into the allegations, Metro reports. Several former employees told ITV and the Guardian they believed the process was meant to close down complaints. One said: "It was not an inquiry, it was a cover-up."

An author who is still represented by the publisher gave a similar account. She said she believed there had been a cover-up, that the company "tried to shut women up", and that Walliams was protected "because he was a valuable asset".

## What Walliams and HarperCollins say

Walliams has not yet responded to the Guardian and ITV reporting, Metro says. In a letter understood to have been sent to journalists several months ago, he denied being a "sexual predator". He said he recognised he had "behaved thoughtlessly" at times and regretted not taking people's feelings "properly into account", but insisted he had not broken the law.

HarperCollins dropped him last year. Its statement, issued last week after the first allegations emerged, said the company had previously ended its publishing relationship with him and that it takes the safety of its people "incredibly seriously". It added that, to respect individuals' privacy, it does not comment on employee matters. Metro said the company had been contacted for comment on the latest claims.

The Metropolitan Police has said it received a number of non-recent allegations against the former Britain's Got Talent judge. Metro reports these are separate from the claims in the ITV and Guardian stories.

## The fallout for his books

Walliams has sold more than 60 million books worldwide, according to [Grazia](https://graziadaily.co.uk/life/in-the-news/culture-debrief-david-walliams/). Grazia reports that several schools and libraries have announced plans to remove his titles, and that Waterstones will return its remaining stock to the publisher. Its columnist argued that parents are being left to decide on their children's behalf, since young readers cannot weigh the moral questions themselves.

The case is also being discussed alongside other recent media controversies. Deadline reported that a Channel 4 News boss emailed staff about non-disclosure agreements after the issue resurfaced in the wake of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) and Walliams. ## What happens next

The police inquiry is the main thing to watch, and no charges have been reported. HarperCollins has yet to answer the cover-up claims in the sources we read, and Walliams has not publicly responded to the latest reporting. Further decisions by retailers, schools and libraries over his books are likely to follow.
