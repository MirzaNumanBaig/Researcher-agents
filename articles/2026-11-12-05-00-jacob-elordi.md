**Quick summary**

- [Jacob Elordi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Elordi) told Vogue Australia that filming in the dark British winter left him "base-level depressed", according to The Independent.
- He also said he doubted he was "good enough" to play Heathcliff in [Wuthering Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuthering_Heights).
- The 29-year-old Australian is set to play killer Gary Gilmore in a remake of The Executioner's Song and will produce for the first time.
- [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists) has acquired the rights to the project.

Jacob Elordi has spoken candidly about the toll of making Wuthering Heights, telling Vogue Australia that the cold, dark winter shoot left him feeling "base-level depressed". The comments, reported by The Independent and Film-News.co.uk this week, arrive just as the Australian actor lines up his next major role.

## What Jacob Elordi said about the Wuthering Heights shoot

According to [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/arts-entertainment/films/news/jacob-elordi-wuthering-heights-london-weather-depression-b3083264.html), Elordi, 29, played Heathcliff in Emerald Fennell's adaptation of Emily Brontë's novel. The film was shot mainly across the Yorkshire Dales from mid-January to late March 2025.

Elordi said he began work in January and had never experienced London winter like it. "I didn't expect to get so out of sorts," he told the magazine. "Waking up every day, it's so dark and so cold. I think I was base-level depressed."

The contrast with his earlier work with Fennell was stark. Saltburn was filmed from July 2022 at the University of Oxford and in Northamptonshire, and Elordi remembered the sun shining for what felt like the whole shoot. Wuthering Heights, by his account, was the opposite experience.

## Why he questioned whether he was good enough

The weather was not the only pressure. Elordi said he wondered whether he was "good enough" to play Heathcliff. On Saltburn, he explained, the script left room for improvisation and he could be "whoever I wanted". Brontë's material offered less freedom.

"There's a language to [Wuthering Heights] you can't really step around," he said. He added that he knew what Fennell wanted from the character and badly wanted to deliver it. Fennell had simply texted him to ask if he would do the film, and he described it as the first time he had thought he might not be up to a role.

## Jacob Elordi's next role: The Executioner's Song

Elordi's next project is a remake of The Executioner's Song, based on Norman Mailer's 1979 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel. The Independent reports that he will play convicted murderer Gary Gilmore, who insisted on being executed after killing two men in Utah.

Film-News.co.uk adds that the story was first filmed in 1982, with [Tommy Lee Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lee_Jones) as Gilmore, and that Gilmore was executed in 1977 after becoming a media sensation. That earlier film's director, Lawrence Schiller, will serve as an executive producer on the new version.

It is also a first for Elordi behind the scenes. He will take a hands-on producing role, working alongside [Scott Stuber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Stuber) and Nick Nesbitt of United Artists, his manager Matt Vioral and Sherry Marsh. The outlets describe the film as a journey into American violence, media frenzy and loneliness.
