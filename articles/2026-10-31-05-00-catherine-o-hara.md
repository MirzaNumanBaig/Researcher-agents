**Quick summary**

- *SCTV: The Greatest Show You've Never Heard Of* arrived on Netflix on 30 October 2026.
- The documentary includes footage of [Catherine O'Hara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_O'Hara) recorded months before she died in January 2026, aged 71.
- O'Hara sits on a couch with [Eugene Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Levy), Martin Short, [Andrea Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Martin) and [Dave Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Thomas_(actor)) to revisit old clips.
- Jason Hehir directed the film, and Jimmy Kimmel is an executive producer.

Catherine O'Hara has one more screen appearance, and it is with the people who started her career. *SCTV: The Greatest Show You've Never Heard Of*, a documentary about the Canadian sketch series, began streaming on Netflix on 30 October, and it carries footage of O'Hara filmed in the months before her death in January 2026, according to [Yahoo, republishing a Parade report](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/catherine-oharas-last-documentary-features-134815334.html).

## A reunion on a couch

The centrepiece is a conversation recorded in July 2025, about six months before O'Hara died. [What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/what-to-watch/sctv-the-greatest-show-youve-never-heard-of-netflix-review/) reports that O'Hara, Eugene Levy, Martin Short, Andrea Martin and Dave Thomas sat together and watched old clips and behind-the-scenes footage, trading memories about the show and about each other.

Parade says O'Hara described her years at Second City and on SCTV as formative. The What's on Netflix review quotes her saying the show suited people in their twenties because "you're cocky at that age", and that Second City gave performers a funny way to show it.

The review's writer calls it gut-wrenching to watch the cast members look back at their younger selves, with some visibly tearing up.

## What the film covers

[Second City Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_City_Television) ran from 1976 to 1984. It parodied the television of its day, including commercials, popular films, genre shows and late-night talk hosts. According to What's on Netflix, the documentary follows the whole life of the series. It starts as a rival to *Saturday Night Live* and covers the cast's growth, a funding crisis that ended with Edmonton businessman [Charles Allard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Allard) offering the show a studio, and the later move to NBC in New York.

It also looks at the breakout success of Bob and Doug McKenzie, and at [Rick Moranis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Moranis) and Dave Thomas, who took that success into the film [Strange Brew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strange_Brew). The review says the show ended when NBC executives wanted to make it more family friendly and the cast left.

Moranis and the late [Joe Flaherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Flaherty), who died in 2024, give interviews too. [Bill Hader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Hader) and [Bob Odenkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Odenkirk) appear as fans of the show, according to the same review.

## Friendship that outlasted the show

Parade notes that Levy worked alongside O'Hara for more than five decades. The pair became familiar to audiences through SCTV, then worked together again on several Christopher Guest films and on [Schitt's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schitt's_Creek).

Parade also lists the roles that made O'Hara internationally known: Delia Deetz in *Beetlejuice*, Kate McCallister in the *Home Alone* films, and Moira Rose in *Schitt's Creek*, which earned her an Emmy Award.

Near the end, the documentary pays tribute to castmates who have died, Parade says, naming John Candy, Harold Ramis and Joe Flaherty. What's on Netflix points out that the film is not the only SCTV project this year. It follows *Marty, Life Is Short*, a documentary on Short released over the summer.
