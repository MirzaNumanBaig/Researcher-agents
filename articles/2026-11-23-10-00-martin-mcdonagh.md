**Quick summary**

- Editor Mikkel E.G. Nielsen told The HoloFiles on 22 November 2026 how he shaped the tone of Wild Horse Nine.
- The film, written and directed by [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh), blends comedy, political thriller and drama.
- Nielsen previously cut McDonagh's The Banshees of Inisherin, and says that experience informed this film.
- Wild Horse Nine is in cinemas through Searchlight Pictures, according to the interview.

The comedy in [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh)'s latest film lands because someone decided exactly when to cut. In an interview published on 22 November by The HoloFiles, editor Mikkel E.G. Nielsen described how he and the director found the rhythm of [Wild Horse Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine), a film that moves from irreverent humour to thriller to grief.

## What Wild Horse Nine is about

According to The HoloFiles, the film is set in the early 1970s and follows two CIA agents, Chris (John Malkovich) and Lee ([Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell)), on a holiday to Easter Island. It is the lead-up to the coup in Chile that overthrew [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende). The trip gets harder for Lee as he deals with Chris's erratic behaviour, his argumentative wife (played by [Parker Posey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker_Posey)) and the arrival of their boss, played by [Steve Buscemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Buscemi).

The outlet describes the result as McDonagh's usual mix of biting humour and melancholy, built around morally compromised men facing their pasts. McDonagh's earlier films include [In Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Bruges). We covered the film's cinema run in [Wild Horse Nine Loses 175 Cinemas as Martin McDonagh Eyes Oscars](https://moduscarry.com/wild-horse-nine-theatres-mcdonagh/).

## Trial and error in the cutting room

Nielsen said the balance came from experimentation. He pointed to The Banshees of Inisherin, which combined comedy, drama and melancholy, and said Wild Horse Nine added a thriller on top. He described the work as adding more elements to juggle while trying to keep the film playful, so that it feels whole and honest by the end.

Audience screenings helped. According to the interview, they showed Nielsen when a laugh ran too long, when a scene lost people's interest and when a new piece of information needed more room.

## Dialogue treated like music

McDonagh's writing is known for repetition and pauses, and Nielsen said the rhythm is already in the script. The actors build on it with their delivery, and the director and cinematographer Ben Davis shape it further through the angles they choose. Nielsen said he thinks of scenes as musical pieces, then looks for the best version of each character.

## Why the darkness hits harder

The film spends its early scenes on the playful friendship between Chris and Lee, so viewers grow fond of them before the details of Chris's past surface. His memoirs and montages of his earlier work give a window into his character. They sit against the bright, colourful images of Easter Island.

Nielsen also described how transitions carry feeling, including a cut from Lee crying straight into a flashback. In another example, calm aerial shots of the island are broken by the sound of an aeroplane, pulling the viewer back toward the film's unsettling side. He called finding that balance the real balance of a film.
