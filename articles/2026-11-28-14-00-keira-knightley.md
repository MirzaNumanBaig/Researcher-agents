**Quick summary**

- The emerald-green gown from the 2007 film *Atonement* is heading to auction at Christie's London.
- The sale, "Love & Lust: A Literary Guide", takes place on 9 December, according to Canadian Press reporting.
- The estimate is up to £120,000. The dress last sold in a 2008 charity auction for £35,000 (US$46,000), ARTnews reports.
- It is being sold alongside early film posters and letters from Horatio Nelson.

The **Keira Knightley Atonement dress**, the backless emerald silk gown that became one of the most recognisable costumes of the 2000s, is going back under the hammer. [Keira Knightley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keira_Knightley) wore it as Cecilia Tallis in the 2007 film, and [Christie's](https://www.christies.com/) will offer it in London on 9 December, according to [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/entertainment/2026/11/10/keira-knightleys-unforgettable-green-atonement-dress-is-going-up-for-sale/).

## The dress and the film

The gown was designed by [Jacqueline Durran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Durran) for [Joe Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Wright)'s adaptation of [Ian McEwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McEwan)'s novel, as [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/market/keira-knightley-green-atonement-gown-christies-1234808139/) notes. It appears in the film's 1930s library scene.

CP24 reports that Wright wanted something showstopping: green, full in the skirt and moving at the hem. Durran used a very fine, light, unstructured fabric to get that effect. She has said the dress's lasting impact came as a surprise.

Christie's head of sale, Thais Hitchins, described it as one of the most instantly recognisable dresses in fashion history. For more on the actor's career, see everything we publish on [Keira Knightley](https://moduscarry.com/tag/keira-knightley/).

## What it is expected to fetch

The estimate is up to £120,000, according to CP24. The dress has been through the auction room before. ARTnews says it was sold at a charity auction in 2008 for £35,000 (US$46,000). Christie's told CP24 that it has stayed in an American private collection since.

Christie's says it is hard to predict who will bid, but it expects interest from private collectors, museums and institutions.

Screen costumes can outperform their estimates. CP24 points to Audrey Hepburn's dress from *Breakfast at Tiffany's*, which Christie's sold in 2006 for £467,200, more than six times its estimate. A Vivien Leigh dress from *Gone With the Wind* made US$137,000 in 2015. Those are different stars and different decades, so they are a guide rather than a forecast.

## What else is in the sale

The Atonement gown sits among objects about love and desire. ARTnews lists rare film poster art for *Metropolis* and *Dracula* (1931), with estimates of £100,000 to £130,000 and £80,000 to £120,000 respectively. There are also early 19th-century love letters from Horatio Nelson to [Lady Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma%2C_Lady_Hamilton), and original sketches by [Chris Foss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Foss) made for *The Joy of Sex*.

Specialists Thais Hitchins and Mark Wiltshire said the sale brings together an eclectic group of objects that capture many facets of love and desire.
