**Quick summary**

- [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) voices Cinderella in Netflix's animated film *Steps*, which is streaming now.
- She also sings in it: she performs "Smile And A Song", one of several original tracks by Justin Tranter and collaborators.
- Seyfried told People the gentle role was "a relief" after playing Elizabeth Holmes again in the documentary *You Can See Everything*.
- Separately, her "Am I too old?" comment on a Variety post about the next Bond film's leading lady has fans talking. No casting has been announced.

Amanda Seyfried has spent the week moving between a fairytale princess, a convicted tech founder and a joke about James Bond. The 40-year-old actress voices Cinderella in *Steps*, Netflix's new animated retelling, and Deadline published the film's full song list on 20 November.

## Steps turns Cinderella into the side character

*Steps* tells the old story from the stepsisters' point of view. According to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/steps-songs-soundtrack-netflix-cinderella-movie-animated-1237229486/), the film follows Lilith, voiced by [Ali Wong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Wong), and Margot, voiced by [Stephanie Hsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephanie_Hsu), the two sisters usually written off as "wicked" and "evil".

[People](https://people.com/amanda-seyfried-says-playing-cinderella-is-a-relief-after-elizabeth-holmes-exclusive-12262562) describes the setup as a misunderstanding. The sisters are not villains, only outsiders in a village that adores Cinderella and scorns them. That makes Seyfried's Cinderella something close to an antagonist, even though she is kind.

Seyfried said she did not want to play her as a pushover. "She needs a little bit of cynicism maybe," she told People, adding that the character could use some of her sisters' edge. In her view, the characters "rub off on each other" over the course of the film.

## The songs: from Olivia Rodrigo to Seyfried herself

Seyfried does not only talk in the film. Deadline reports that she sings, and that her song is "Smile And A Song", written by Justin Tranter, Rosie Hudson-Edmonds and Eren Cannata. Tranter is behind several of the original songs.

The soundtrack also leans on well-known names. Deadline's list includes:

- "brutal" by [Olivia Rodrigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Rodrigo), written with Daniel Nigro
- "Beautiful Things" by [Benson Boone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benson_Boone)
- "I'll Be There", in full and acoustic versions, by [Jess Glynne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Glynne)
- "Someone To Hold" by BlackPink's JENNIE, written by [Justin Tranter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Tranter), [Julia Michaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Michaels) and Jason Gill
- "Sabotage" by the Beastie Boys, plus original tracks from Rose Gray and Emei

For an earlier look at her recording sessions, see our post on [Seyfried recording her Cinderella song](https://moduscarry.com/amanda-seyfried-steps-netflix/).

## Why Seyfried calls Cinderella "a relief"

The lighter role arrives right after a heavy one. Seyfried recently returned as Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in *You Can See Everything*, a documentary by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim about the 34 days before Holmes reported to prison. People says she reenacted moments the filmmakers could not film, and that her involvement stayed secret until release. The film is in cinemas now.

By comparison, Cinderella was easy. "It's always a relief to play someone more like Karen in Mean Girls than anything else," she told People, referring to her 2004 character. She called the princess "pure and guileless".

The Holmes project felt risky, she told Fielder in a Rolling Stone conversation. She said she worried about hurting her career or being drawn into a "wasp's nest", but that her drive to take the job won out.

## The Bond joke that took off

Seyfried also made headlines for a one-line reply on Instagram. Variety posted a list of actresses who could play the next Bond's love interest, including Florence Pugh, Margot Robbie, Zendaya and Sydney Sweeney. Seyfried wasn't on it and commented: "Am I too old?"

According to [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/english/hollywood/news/amanda-seyfrieds-cheeky-am-i-too-old-comment-on-the-bond-girl-shortlist-goes-viral-fans-point-out-she-is-only-six-months-older-than-robert-pattinson/articleshow/136026453.cms), Amazon MGM Studios has confirmed Robert Pattinson as the seventh actor to play 007. Denis Villeneuve will direct from a script by Steven Knight. The studio has not named a co-star, title or release date. [Yahoo Style Canada](https://ca.style.yahoo.com/amanda-seyfried-poses-introspective-seeing-142146166.html) notes that the film is expected in 2028 and that Seyfried has not said she is pursuing the part.

Both outlets treat it as a joke that touches a real debate. Yahoo points out that Bond's leading ladies have usually been much younger than the actor, with Monica Bellucci in *Spectre* a rare exception. We covered the exchange in more detail in [Seyfried's "Too Old" Bond post](https://moduscarry.com/amanda-seyfried-bond-girl/).

## What happens next

*Steps* is streaming on Netflix and *You Can See Everything* is in cinemas, so both are available now. On Bond, there is nothing official: Variety's list is speculative and the studio has said nothing about the female lead.
