**Quick summary**

- British comedian Lucy Beaumont, who identified herself as a source in The Guardian's investigation, appeared on Channel 4's The Last Leg and thanked people for their support.
- David Walliams denies all allegations. UK police are investigating, and he has not been charged.
- The UK government says it will bring in a ban on NDAs that silence harassment victims from January, according to the Morning Star.
- Herefordshire libraries will keep their David Walliams books, citing professional guidance on access to published material.

British comedian Lucy Beaumont, who said she would probably never work in television again after going public as a whistleblower, has returned to the screen. She appeared on Channel 4's The Last Leg this week to talk about the messages she has received since the [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) allegations became public, [Nine.com.au reported](https://www.nine.com.au/entertainment/celebrity/david-walliams-whistleblower-lucy-beaumont-speaks-out-after-allegations-20261126-p61hdy.html).

## A warm welcome on The Last Leg

Beaumont told the hosts she was still working through a large pile of messages from strangers around the world. She said she may never get through all of them, but some had stayed with her. Her main takeaway, she said, was how kind people can be and that others want to see positive change.

Australian comedian [Adam Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Hills), one of the hosts, said the team wanted her on the show partly to show their support. He reminded her of her own recent words: "I'll probably never work on television again." Beaumont answered, "I'm alright for now, aren't I?", and the studio audience responded with loud applause, according to Nine.

## How Beaumont came forward

On November 11, Beaumont identified herself as one of the previously unnamed sources in The Guardian's investigation. She told Sky she took information to the paper after a former TV staff member confided in her about Walliams' alleged behaviour. She said she had done all she could privately to support the women involved.

She went public one day after The Guardian and ITV News published their joint investigation. The outlets said they spoke with multiple women who accused Walliams of preying on them, and one alleged he sexually assaulted her when she was 15, per Nine.

Walliams, 55, denies the allegations. In a statement to the two outlets he said he had at times "behaved thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual sexual relationships with, and that he regretted it. He also said: "I am not a sexual predator" and "I have not broken the law." A judge rejected his bid for an injunction to stop publication but protected his identity until early November.

## Publisher accused of silencing complaints

The row has also reached his former publisher. According to the [NZ Herald](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/cover-up-publisher-harpercollins-accused-of-silencing-complaints-about-david-walliams/AS7TWNBXH5ATZKG6BOJMV27MJI/), citing British reports, HarperCollins UK is accused of using a law firm to draw up non-disclosure agreements that stopped people raising complaints. The Guardian reported that concerns from staff were dismissed for nearly a decade.

In 2023 managers hired the law firm Allen & Overy to look into the claims. Several women were interviewed, but Walliams was never spoken to and the inquiry closed quietly the next year, the Guardian reported. One person described it as "a cover-up". One woman who took part reportedly received a five-figure payout and signed an NDA. HarperCollins cut ties with Walliams last December and said in September it would stop publishing his back catalogue.

## The UK moves on NDAs

The government said on Tuesday that it would bring forward plans to curb NDAs to January, the [Morning Star reported](https://www.morningstaronline.co.uk/article/unions-welcome-government-ban-use-ndas-silence-victims-sexual-harassment-following-brand). Clauses would be void when used to silence workers over harassment or discrimination, or to stop victims reporting criminal conduct. It applies only to new agreements.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham wrote in the Guardian that victims would be free to tell their story to whoever they choose. Unions welcomed the move, with TUC general secretary Paul Nowak urging that freelancers, interns and volunteers be covered. Equalities minister [Bridget Phillipson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridget_Phillipson) acknowledged gaps for freelancers, fans and people not employed by the company. The paper linked the debate to [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand)'s rape conviction and the Walliams allegations. For more on the policy angle, see [our report on Burnham and the NDA row](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-nda-row/).

## Books stay on some shelves

Walliams has published 24 children's novels, which together with his picture books have sold more than 50 million copies worldwide, according to the Hereford Times. UK and Australian bookstores have pulled his titles. Yet [Herefordshire Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herefordshire_Council) said its libraries still hold his books, citing professional guidance that supports access to published material unless it is prohibited by law. It added that a title's presence is not an endorsement of the author, and that stock will be reviewed regularly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Lucy Beaumont?

### Has David Walliams been charged?
No. He denies all allegations, and UK police are investigating.

### What is changing for NDAs in the UK?
