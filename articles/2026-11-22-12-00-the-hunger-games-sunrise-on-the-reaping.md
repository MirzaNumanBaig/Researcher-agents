**Quick summary**

- [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace) plays Maysilee Donner, a District 12 tribute, in *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*.
- Speaking at the film's press conference, she said Maysilee is driven by a refusal to let her personality and humanity be stripped away.
- Grace said the character is still afraid of dying, noting the tributes are only 16.
- The film, directed by [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence), opened on 28 October 2026 and has taken about US$237.2 million against a US$150 million budget, according to Wikipedia.

Mckenna Grace says the defiance of her *Sunrise on the Reaping* character is less about believing she will survive than about staying herself, according to an interview published by [The AU Review](https://www.theaureview.com/watch/interview-mckenna-grace-hunger-games/) on 21 November. The actress plays Maysilee Donner in the latest chapter of the [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) franchise, and her comments offer a closer look at one of the film's most talked-about characters.

## A prequel built around the 50th Games

*[The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games%3A_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping)* takes audiences back to Panem 24 years before the original story. It follows the events that shaped a teenage Haymitch Abernathy into the bitter mentor fans meet later, The AU Review reports.

The story is set during the 50th Hunger Games, the Second Quarter Quell. The Capitol adds a cruel twist: twice as many tributes must enter the arena. Maysilee, played by Grace, is among those chosen from District 12, and The AU Review describes her as a fiercely outspoken young woman.

The film is based on the world created by [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins), the author whose series began with [Katniss Everdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen) volunteering in place of her sister.

## Fear and humanity in Maysilee's story

At the press conference, an interviewer from The AU Review asked Grace whether Maysilee's biggest fear was dying, or surviving at the cost of the person she believed herself to be. Grace said the question captured what she had been exploring.

"I think that that was very extremely well put because I feel like that was kind of my whole thing with her," she said.

She did not play Maysilee as fearless. "There's obviously a fear of dying. They're 16," Grace said, adding that being terrified in such dire circumstances is an inherently human reaction. The AU Review makes the same point: the character's strength comes from refusing to let fear define her, not from feeling no fear.

## Standing up for herself and others

Grace said much of the role is about determination. "So much of her character is trying to be... determined to not let her personality and her character and her humanity be stripped away from her," she explained.

She also spoke about what it meant to play such a character as a teenager and as a woman. In her words, it was "really cool" to play someone so adamant about standing up for herself and speaking out for the people around her.

The AU Review ties this to a long-running theme in Collins' work: the Capitol does not only demand obedience, it tries to control how its citizens are seen and remembered. Tributes are given identities and packaged for public viewing, and the outlet notes that Haymitch's own acts of resistance are reshaped by the Capitol's version of events.

## How the film is performing

The film's release date was 28 October 2026. According to [Wikipedia's entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping), it has grossed about US$237.2 million against a reported budget of US$150 million, roughly 1.6 times its cost. Wikipedia lists a Rotten Tomatoes score of 68 per cent and a Metacritic score of 55 out of 100 from 43 critics.

The critical consensus summarised there is that the prequel gains from focusing on some of the series' best-loved characters, even as it covers familiar ground.
