**Quick summary**

- WildBrain, the rights holder, posted Nathan Fielder's 2008 documentary *Love and Cameras in America* on YouTube, then removed it in under a day.
- A WildBrain spokesman told The Globe and Mail the company is now "looking at different distribution options".
- The film follows the 2008 US presidential race and was made while Fielder worked on CBC's *This Hour Has 22 Minutes*.
- Interest surged after *You Can See Everything*, which opens in UK cinemas on 20 November.

[Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder)'s long-lost first feature documentary appeared online this week and was gone again within a day. WildBrain, the company that holds the rights to *Love and Cameras in America*, took the 2008 film off YouTube after a rush of attention, according to [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/culture/film-and-tv/article-nathan-fielder-documentary-pulled-off-youtube-after-brief-online/).

## Why the film disappeared so fast

The Globe reports that WildBrain spokesman Shaun Smith said there was "significant interest in the film after posting", and that the company is weighing other ways to release it. No date or platform has been given. For now, nobody outside the rights holder can say where fans will be able to watch it legally.

The upload was a surprise to the people who had been hunting for it. Globe journalist Tom Cardoso spent 18 months searching for the film, a project the paper first wrote about in early 2025. He spoke to a camera operator who had worked with Fielder and to his former executive producer, and he received a steady stream of emails from fans asking for a copy. When WildBrain told him it had digitised the film and posted it, he announced it on social media, as [Exclaim!](https://exclaim.ca/film/article/obscure-nathan-fielder-documentary-found-after-almost-20-years) notes.

## What the film is

*Love and Cameras in America* screened at a Halifax film festival in 2008 and was never distributed after that. Exclaim! says it follows the contest between [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and John McCain, and that Fielder made it while appearing in his "Nathan on Your Side" segment on CBC's *This Hour Has 22 Minutes*.

The official description, quoted by Exclaim! from Vulture, asks what happens when a man with almost no filmmaking experience and no interest in politics is sent to make a documentary about a US presidential election. That is a fair preview of the humour he later built a career on.

## Why everyone wants it now

Both outlets tie the scramble to the success of *You Can See Everything*, which Fielder co-directed with [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim). The Globe says the film has turned him from a cult favourite, known for *Nathan For You*, *The Rehearsal* and *The Curse*, into a far bigger name, and pushed fans back to his earlier work.

The numbers back that up. *You Can See Everything* holds a 97 per cent score on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), according to [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/bulletin/culture/you-can-see-everything-review-uk-elizabeth-holmes-b3088007.html). Wikipedia's entry for the film lists a budget of about US$7 million and a worldwide gross of about US$93 million, roughly 13 times what it cost. Those figures come from a crowd-edited source, so treat them as indicative until a trade tracker confirms them.

Exclaim! also frames the film as part of a strong year for Canadian screen work, alongside [Heated Rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heated_Rivalry) and *Nirvanna the Band the Show the Movie*.

## The critics on the Holmes film

The Independent reports that the documentary gives an up-close look at the convicted Theranos fraudster Elizabeth Holmes at home, shortly before she began an 11-year prison sentence in Texas. It was filmed in secret over three years. [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of The Guardian gave it five stars and called it "thrillingly reckless, deliciously irresponsible and as gripping as any thriller". Robbie Collin of The Telegraph also gave it five stars and wrote that it is "the most essential film of the year".

The film had a secret premiere at the Telluride Film Festival and opened in the US on 16 October. ## What happens next

*You Can See Everything* reaches UK theatres on 20 November. WildBrain has not said whether *Love and Cameras in America* will return to YouTube, move to a streaming service or get a limited release. Fielder has not commented in the coverage we read. Until the company announces a plan, the film remains out of reach again, which will only feed its legend among fans.
