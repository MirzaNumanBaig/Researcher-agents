**Quick summary**

- A Parkinson's study launched in 2010 by the [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) Foundation has had its data downloaded more than 50 million times, according to The Times of India.
- The study, first called the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI), was renamed the Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative in May 2026.
- It now covers over 4,200 clinic participants at 50 sites in 12 countries, plus more than 47,000 online participants.
- Researchers hope the data will help design more targeted clinical trials.

A study that [Michael J. Fox](https://www.michaeljfox.org)'s foundation started 16 years ago has become one of the most widely used resources in Parkinson's research. [The Times of India reports](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/us/michael-j-foxs-foundation-launched-a-parkinsons-study-in-2010-16-years-later-its-data-has-been-downloaded-over-50-million-times-and-is-helping-researchers-design-more-targeted-clinical-trials/articleshow/135238462.cms) that data from the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative has now been downloaded over 50 million times.

For a project that began as a search for measurable signs of disease, that figure shows how far the data has travelled beyond the original research teams.

## How the study began

The Michael J. Fox Foundation for Parkinson's Research launched the study in 2010. The aim, as the outlet describes it, was to help scientists understand the disease better and find markers that could improve diagnosis and treatment.

Those markers are biomarkers: measurable biological indicators that help researchers identify and track a condition. To find them, the study collected information from people with Parkinson's, people considered at risk, and control participants. That allowed scientists to follow how the condition develops and changes over time.

Fox has spoken publicly about living with Parkinson's since he disclosed his diagnosis in 1998, and the foundation has stayed focused on research and better options for people affected. Check the [foundation's official site](https://www.michaeljfox.org) for its own account of the work.

## From a single study to a shared resource

The initiative has grown well past its original scope. According to the foundation's May 2026 announcement, as reported by The Times of India, it now includes more than 4,200 participants attending clinics across 50 sites in 12 countries. A further 47,000-plus people contribute through an online platform.

The result is a collection of data, biological samples and findings that scientists anywhere can use. Instead of each team building a study from scratch, they can start with PPMI and put their questions to it. The report says the thousands of participants who have given their time, health information and samples made that possible, and that the dataset would be hard for any single laboratory to assemble.

The foundation has also opened a remote option through its digital research hub, my PPMI, so people can take part without visiting a clinic.

## Why the focus is shifting to precision medicine

The May 2026 rename to the Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative reflects a growing view that Parkinson's does not follow the same biological course in every person. Precision medicine looks at the characteristics of individual patients rather than treating everyone as if they have the same form of a disease.

According to the report, the foundation says PPMI data can help researchers spot disease-related changes earlier and build more sensitive ways to measure change in clinical trials. It could also help investigators choose participants whose biology suits a particular treatment, and judge whether a therapy is working.

The foundation's Chief Scientist, Mark Frasier, is quoted as calling it "a tremendously exciting time in Parkinson's research." The outlet points to work with the alpha-synuclein seed amplification assay, which can detect Parkinson's-related biological activity in samples. PPMI samples have helped advance that approach, including the possibility of identifying disease biology before symptoms appear.

## The scale of the condition

The figures behind the research explain the urgency. The report cites a study prepared for the foundation, Economic Burden of Parkinson's and Atypical Parkinsonism in the United States, which estimates that the disease affects about 1.2 million Americans and costs the US federal government more than $82 billion a year. The foundation has called Parkinson's one of the fastest growing neurological diseases in the world.

Existing treatments can ease some symptoms, the report notes, but the larger goal remains finding ways to address the disease itself.

## What happens next

The renamed initiative is expected to keep building on its data and samples as researchers use them to design trials aimed at specific patient groups. The Times of India does not give a timeline for new trials or results. People interested in taking part can look at the my PPMI hub through the foundation.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is PPMI?
PPMI stands for the Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative, a study launched in 2010 by the Michael J. Fox Foundation to find biomarkers that help diagnose and track Parkinson's. It was renamed the Parkinson's Precision Medicine Initiative in May 2026.

### How many people take part?
According to the foundation's May 2026 announcement, over 4,200 participants attend clinics at 50 sites in 12 countries, and more than 47,000 contribute online.

### Why does precision medicine matter for Parkinson's?
Researchers increasingly believe the disease does not progress the same way in everyone. Understanding individual biology could help match people to suitable trials and show whether a treatment works.
