**Quick summary**

- [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) spoke about his film Wicker on Saturday at Deadline's Contenders Film Los Angeles event, according to Deadline.
- He said the script, and Olivia Colman's involvement, drew him to the role of the straw-plaited husband.
- He called the wicker suit "horrible" to wear but said it shaped the character's stiffness and voice.
- Cinemablend reports he has joked to Entertainment Weekly about a sequel called Corduroy.
- Collider notes his 2011 thriller Straw Dogs is streaming free on Tubi.

Alexander Skarsgård says he was hooked on Wicker from the first page. Speaking on Saturday at Deadline's Contenders Film Los Angeles panel, the Swedish actor described the script as funny, romantic and irreverent, and said the chance to work with Olivia Colman sealed the decision, [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/11/alexander-skarsgard-wicker-interview-1237224217/). It is a notable turn for an actor better known lately for darker material, and his comments show how much of the film he built from the outside in.

## Why Skarsgård said yes to Wicker

Wicker is written and directed by Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson and based on a short story by Ursula Wills. Skarsgård plays a husband made of straw.

He told the panel the story felt like a fable and a saga at once. He pointed to its commentary on patriarchy, marriage, jealousy and social hierarchy, and said the filmmakers handled those themes so lightly that it "never felt too preachy or annoying." In his words, the message was "subtly inserted" and the film stayed entertaining, sweet and funny throughout.

The panel also included Fischer, Wilson and makeup-effects artist and character designer Joe Dunckley, per Deadline. The outlet said the full video would follow on Monday.

## Inside the wicker suit

The costume was not comfortable. Skarsgård joked that it was "horrible" to wear, but he said the first time he stepped into it helped him find the character's physicality.

He and the filmmakers wanted a clear arc. The husband starts out rigid and stiff, and his voice was a point of discussion from the start. As the character grows into himself, Skarsgård said, the rigidity eases and his speech gains a little more fluency. He was careful with the wording, saying the character does not become "nimble."

There was also a lighter admission. He said he cracked up laughing with Colman during some takes, and that co-director Fischer described Colman as "anti-fuss." Those moments, Skarsgård said, were "surgically removed" from the edit. Wearing a mask, he added, nobody can tell what is going on underneath.

## The Corduroy sequel pitch

The film has clearly stayed with him. [Cinemablend](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/alexander-skarsgard-reveals-hilarious-pitch-wicker-sequel) reports that Skarsgård told Entertainment Weekly a follow-up called Corduroy is being pitched around Hollywood this week, and that financing has been hard to find. He described the new character as "delicate and soft" and an "attentive lover."

Cinemablend itself could not tell whether he was serious, noting his deadpan style during press tours. It also says he raised the idea of continuing the story earlier in the press cycle, when he was asked what happens after the family of three leaves the village. Treat Corduroy as a joke until a studio says otherwise. No production has been announced.

## A darker side worth revisiting

Collider used the Wicker buzz to point readers back to Straw Dogs, the 2011 remake in which Skarsgård plays Charlie, the charming local who becomes a threat. It co-stars James Marsden and [Kate Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Bosworth). Collider says it is streaming free on Tubi.

The 1971 original starred [Dustin Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Hoffman) and [Susan George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_George_(actress)), and it arrived the same year as [Dirty Harry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirty_Harry). According to Collider, the remake drew mixed reviews and unfavourable comparisons with that film, but most critics singled out Skarsgård's performance. Collider also says he called the shoot "emotionally, pretty draining."

The outlet traces a line from that role to Hold the Dark, The Northman and Big Little Lies, and calls Pillion, from 2025, part of his current run of unusual romantic leads.
