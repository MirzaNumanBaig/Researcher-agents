**Quick summary**

- A review published on AOL UK judges *Fjord*, starring Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan, to be "anticlimactic" and "underpowered".
- The drama is directed by Cristian Mungiu, who won the Palme d'Or at Cannes in 2007.
- Reinsve plays Lisbet, a Norwegian woman whose family comes under child-welfare scrutiny in her home village.
- The film opened in the US on 9 October and in the UK on 13 November. Australian cinemas get it on 28 January.

**[Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve)'s new film *Fjord* has drawn a lukewarm verdict from a UK critic, who found the Cannes drama well made in places but short of the force of its director's earlier work.** The review, carried by [AOL UK](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/fjord-review-cristian-mungiu-sea-172647000.html) as the film reached British cinemas, describes a story about faith, language and the state stepping into family life.

## What the Fjord review says

Director [Cristian Mungiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Mungiu) won the Palme d'Or in 2007 for *4 Months, 3 Weeks and 2 Days*. The reviewer calls *Fjord* an "anticlimactic, underpowered movie" and says it is unmistakably his, with enigmatic long shots, a wariness of close-ups and one crowded dinner-table scene.

The critic's main complaint is that the pain in the story is not matched by the complexity audiences expect from him. By the review's account, the film neither delivers a compelling truth about its relationships nor teasingly holds one back.

The reviewer also places the film in a wider Cannes pattern. Directors such as [Kantemir Balagov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantemir_Balagov) and [Ryusuke Hamaguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryusuke_Hamaguchi) also brought films made outside their home country and language, with major foreign stars. The critic suggests the trend may cost these filmmakers some focus.

## The story and Reinsve's role

According to the review, [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) plays Mihai, a Romanian software engineer. Reinsve plays his Norwegian wife, Lisbet. The couple are strict, conservative Christians who move to the remote village where she was born, drawn by his IT job and a strong church community. Their neighbours, the school's headteacher and his wife, welcome them warmly.

The film opens just after Mihai has punished their teenage daughter. School staff then notice marks and bruises on the children. The review says the children are questioned, that language barriers may complicate what they say, and that Mihai gives police a damaging statement without a lawyer. The children are quickly placed in provisional care pending a hearing and criminal trial.

The critic credits Mungiu with an ingenious balancing act. The audience is first pushed to side with the children against a cold patriarch, then almost to side with him against officials the review calls smug. Mihai concedes he occasionally smacks or slaps the children, which the film presents as normal in Romania, but the bruises seem to suggest something worse.

The review says the matter is settled neither in court nor on screen. It describes a "strangely inert" finale at a ferry terminal, which hints at a relationship between two teenage girls, played by Vanessa Ceban and Henrikke Lund-Olsen, that the film has not fully explained. The review does not single out Reinsve's performance.
