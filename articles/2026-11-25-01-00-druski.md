**Quick summary**

- Comedian [Druski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druski) (Drew Desbordes) brings his megachurch pastor character to Netflix in the series "Collect & Praise", premiering Dec. 1.
- Mike Epps plays an unpredictable deacon opposite Druski's flashy Pastor Dru, according to Word In Black.
- The show grows out of a viral January sketch that reportedly drew more than 63 million views.
- Gospel and Christian figures are split on whether the humour exposes hypocrisy or mocks the Black church.

Druski's most talked-about character is getting a full series. "Collect & Praise" premieres on Netflix on Dec. 1, and it turns a preacher who cares more about offerings than souls into a streaming comedy for a worldwide audience, [Word In Black reports](https://wordinblack.com/2026/11/druski-takes-the-black-megachurch-to-netflix-whos-laughing/).

## What Collect & Praise is about

The series follows Pastor Dru, a showy preacher who dresses in designer clothes and puts money ahead of faith. Comedian Mike Epps joins him as a deacon described as erratic and willing to do almost anything, a pairing the outlet says promises more scandal than salvation.

Netflix pitches it as a comedy where faith takes a back seat to fortune. According to Word In Black, the trailer is built around cash-counting, outrageous behaviour and the trappings of religious wealth.

## From viral sketch to Netflix

The idea comes from a sketch called "Mega Church Pastors LOVE Money", posted in January. In it, Druski's preacher lowers himself into the sanctuary from overhead rigging, shows off red-soled Christian Louboutin shoes, demands lavish offerings and rides around in a luxury sedan.

The video reportedly passed 63 million views across platforms. Word In Black links that reach to Druski becoming the first internet personality to host [Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live). For more on his rise, see our earlier piece on [his broken home and how it shaped his comedy](https://moduscarry.com/druski-broken-home-comedy/).

## Satire or caricature?

The sketch also set off an argument among churchgoers over whether it called out hypocrisy or made fun of Christianity. Several well-known voices weighed in.

Grammy-winning gospel artist [Kirk Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Franklin) spoke about it in October on "The Baller Alert Show", saying the satire reflected something painful and real. He also said, "Let's stop giving him content."

Christian rapper Lecrae defended the parody, saying his first reaction was recognition rather than offence. He argued some churches and leaders have made themselves easy targets through manipulation and theatrical excess.

Pastor Mike Todd of Transformation Church in Tulsa, Oklahoma, called the sketch hilarious but questioned whether it matched reality. He told his congregation it was an attack on their focus, not on the church.

Word In Black's own view is that the tension is real. The Black church has long been a source of spiritual strength, political activism, education and community survival, yet it has also seen scandalous ministers and "prosperity gospel" scams. The outlet argues that a caricature of the pastor as a hustler and worship as a business risks implying the whole institution is fraudulent.

Druski sees it differently. He told BET that audiences were recognising behaviour that had always been visible but went unexamined, saying, "We're not noticing it unless someone waves a red flag." On NBC's "TODAY" this month, he said the character came from his childhood in a Black church and that the skit "was like putting the mirror up."

## A creator-built path to Netflix

Druski began posting Instagram sketches in 2017 and now has nearly 40 million followers across platforms, according to Netflix. His company, 4LIFERS Entertainment, has a multi-year Netflix partnership covering original programming and licensed existing projects.

Netflix executive John Derderian said Druski has a gift for building characters people cannot stop talking about, and that "Collect & Praise" is only the start of the partnership.

## What happens next

The series arrives on Dec. 1. Fans can also catch Druski on TV before the year ends, as he is set to host [ABC's Chicago New Year's Eve special](https://moduscarry.com/druski-chicago-new-years-eve/). More Netflix projects under the 4LIFERS deal are expected, though no titles were named in the source material.
