**Quick summary**

- Norwegian actor Anders Danielsen Lie has kept working as a general practitioner in Oslo between film roles, according to a Martin Cid Magazine profile published on 21 November 2026.
- He qualified as a doctor in 2007 and took a year out of his studies to make his first Joachim Trier film, Reprise.
- In 2021 he won the Amanda, Norway's national film award, for supporting actor for The Worst Person in the World.
- His most recent lead is the jazz pianist Bill Evans in a film directed by Grant Gee.

[Anders Danielsen Lie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anders_Danielsen_Lie) is one of the few actors to have competed at Cannes and then gone back to seeing patients. A new profile in [Martin Cid Magazine](https://www.martincid.com/people/anders-danielsen-lie/) traces how the Oslo doctor and actor has balanced both lives, and what that balance has meant for his screen career.

## A doctor who acts, and says it does not work

Born in Oslo on New Year's Day 1979, Danielsen Lie is the son of the actress [Tone Danielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tone_Danielsen). He was eleven when [Erik Gustavson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Gustavson) cast him in the title role of Herman in 1990. He then did not act again for sixteen years.

In that gap he studied Ancient Greek, then medicine, with a detour into musicology at the University of Oslo. He qualified as a doctor in 2007, according to the profile.

He is blunt about the arrangement. While promoting Bergman Island, he said it "doesn't really work" and that he would never recommend combining the two jobs to anyone else.

## The Joachim Trier years

The acting career restarted when [Joachim Trier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Trier) invited him to audition for Reprise (2006). He was still a student and took a year out of his degree to make it. As Philip, a friend whose debut novel is published just before his mind gives way, he set the template for the roles that followed.

Oslo, August 31st (2011) put him at the centre as Anders, a former heroin addict on day release from rehab. The film was selected for [Un Certain Regard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un_Certain_Regard) at Cannes. The profile notes that The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw named his performance among the ten best by a male actor that year.

The trilogy closed with The Worst Person in the World in 2021. Danielsen Lie played Aksel, an underground comic artist, opposite [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve). The National Society of Film Critics in the United States named him best supporting actor, and he took the Amanda in the same category.

## A French and international run

His career then widened. [Olivier Assayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Assayas) cast him opposite [Kristen Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristen_Stewart) in [Personal Shopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Shopper) (2016). [Jacques Doillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Doillon) had him play the poet [Rainer Maria Rilke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainer_Maria_Rilke) in a biopic of the sculptor Auguste Rodin. For the zombie thriller The Night Eats the World (2018) he shot every scene twice, once in English and once in French.

[Paul Greengrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Greengrass) then gave him a difficult part in the Netflix film 22 July (2018): Anders Behring Breivik, the far-right terrorist who killed 77 people in Norway in 2011. Danielsen Lie declined to describe it as an ordeal. He told Seventh Row that acting is "very technical work" and that he has never used a method approach.

In 2021 he was also in competition at Cannes in Mia Hansen-Løve's Bergman Island, playing Joseph opposite [Mia Wasikowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Wasikowska). After shooting his Trier scenes in autumn 2020, he had taken a post as medical supervisor for the COVID-19 vaccination and contact-tracing team in Oslo's Nordre Aker borough.

## What the double life has cost

That detail made him a headline, and Martin Cid Magazine argues it has not always helped him. Vanity Fair introduced him to American readers as doctor, COVID hero and movie star. The profile says he largely stayed away from the leading roles that build an international career so he could keep practising.

The credits reflect that, in the magazine's view. They include supporting parts in English-language films such as Mothers' Instinct, with [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway) and [Jessica Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Chastain), and a small role in Trier's Sentimental Value (2025), which won the Grand Prix at Cannes. This is the outlet's assessment, not a claim from the actor.

## Music, books and a new lead

Away from the screen, the profile describes a restless mind. He plays piano, guitar, bass and drums and has released two albums: This Is Autism (2011), built from cassette recordings of his childhood, and IDIOSYNCRASY (2024). With the psychologist Maria Øverås he wrote Sex og sånt (2007), a sex-education book for teenagers that won a Norwegian Ministry of Culture prize for non-fiction.

That musical training sits under his latest lead. [Grant Gee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Gee) directed him as the jazz pianist Bill Evans in Everybody Digs Bill Evans, a portrait in which, the profile says, the music mostly stops. The source text available to us ends before it finishes describing the film, so we have not added detail beyond that.

## What happens next

The profile does not announce new projects or a decision to leave medicine. For readers following his work, the next marker is how Everybody Digs Bill Evans is received. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is Anders Danielsen Lie really a doctor?

Yes. He qualified as a doctor in 2007 and works as a general practitioner in Oslo between films, according to Martin Cid Magazine.

### Which films did Anders Danielsen Lie make with Joachim Trier?

Reprise (2006), Oslo, August 31st (2011) and The Worst Person in the World (2021) make up Trier's Oslo trilogy. He also has a small role in Sentimental Value (2025).

### What is his latest leading role?

He plays the jazz pianist Bill Evans in Everybody Digs Bill Evans, directed by Grant Gee.
