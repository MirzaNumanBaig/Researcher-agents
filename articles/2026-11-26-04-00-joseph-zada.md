**Quick summary**

- Reviewers are praising [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) for his performance as a young Haymitch in *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*.
- Moviejawn calls the film "vibrant, violent" and worthwhile, but says the pace leaves little room for the deaths to land.
- The film runs 2 hours 38 minutes, the longest in the franchise so far, according to Moviejawn.
- Wikipedia lists a 68% Rotten Tomatoes score and a Metacritic average of 55 from 43 critics.

Joseph Zada is drawing the strongest notices of the film's cast. In a review published on 25 November, Moviejawn says the Australian actor "shines" as the young Haymitch Abernathy in *Sunrise on the Reaping*, the latest film set in the world of [The Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games). Not every critic is as warm about the film itself.

## What reviewers say about Zada's Haymitch

Moviejawn's reviewer says Zada captures the character's roots while showing a side audiences have not seen. The older Haymitch, played by [Woody Harrelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Harrelson) in the earlier films, is a bitter drinker. Zada's version is carefree and selfless, according to the review.

The review points to quiet moments as the best evidence: Haymitch hugging his mother and brother, chatting with the people of District Twelve and sharing tender scenes with his girlfriend, Lenore Dove, played by [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)). That softness matters, the reviewer argues, because the story then hardens him.

The same review highlights Zada's chemistry with [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace), who plays Maysilee Donner. The two start from very different backgrounds in District Twelve, and Moviejawn calls their reluctant alliance, and the friendship that grows from it, some of the best scenes in the arena.

## The story and the supporting cast

The film, directed by Francis Lawrence and written by [Billy Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Ray_(screenwriter)) and Michael Lesslie, is set at the 50th Hunger Games. Haymitch is pulled in as the final tribute when the reaping goes wrong, then has to keep his friends alive while working out that the arena is not what it seems, Moviejawn reports.

The review also praises [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons) as Plutarch Heavensbee and [Elle Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Fanning) as Effie Trinket, saying both make familiar characters their own. [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes) also stars. The film adapts the novel by [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins).

## Where critics split

Praise for the cast comes with caveats. Moviejawn says the film moves so quickly that neither Haymitch nor the audience gets time to absorb the deaths. With 48 tributes, double the usual 24, many are barely introduced. The reviewer names Ampert, played by [Percy Daggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_Daggs_III), as one of the few who register. Some returning characters, such as Beetee, Mags and Wiress, are described as feeling like cameos.

Wikipedia's summary of the critical response shows the same split. It lists a 68% score on Rotten Tomatoes and 55 out of 100 on Metacritic from 43 critics. The consensus line it quotes says the prequel retreads familiar ground sturdily. It also quotes a negative review from Brandon Yu of The New York Times, who found the film both bloated and limited.

## How the film is performing

Wikipedia puts the budget at US$150 million and the worldwide gross at US$237.2 million. That is about 1.6 times the budget. The film is in its first weeks of release, so the final total is still open. Wikipedia and Moviejawn also differ on the release date, so check local cinema listings.
