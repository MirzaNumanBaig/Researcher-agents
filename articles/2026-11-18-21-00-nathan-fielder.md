Days before its British release, [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder)'s documentary *You Can See Everything* has prompted a long explainer from [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/nov/18/nathan-fielder-you-can-see-everything), which argues the film makes the most sense if you know the whole Nathan Fielder career, from prank television to this. It opens in UK cinemas on Friday.

## What the film is, and why it matters

*You Can See Everything* runs for three hours and stars Fielder alongside his co-director Lance Oppenheim. According to The Guardian, it follows disgraced Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in the weeks leading up to the start of her prison sentence for defrauding investors.

The paper's critic describes it as more than a Theranos story, calling it a meditation on truth and on how little we can know about other people. The Guardian also says it has become an unlikely cultural phenomenon and a box office success in the US.

The numbers back that up. [Wikipedia's entry on the film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything) lists a US$7 million budget and a US$93 million gross, roughly 13 times what it cost. It records 97% on Rotten Tomatoes (229 reviews) and 97/100 on Metacritic.

## From Nathan for You to Dumb Starbucks

Fielder first became widely known through *Nathan for You*, a parody reality series in which he played a version of himself. The Guardian notes the setup: a man who claimed to have graduated from one of Canada's top business schools, helping struggling small businesses.

The paper says the show was always closer to situationist chaos than to simple pranks. It compares his approach to [Andy Kaufman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Kaufman) rather than to [Jeremy Beadle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Beadle), and counts 32 episodes in total.

Two stunts stand out in its account. One was a plan to save an electronics store with $1 televisions that customers could only buy after an obstacle course involving a live alligator. The other was the rebranding of a failing coffee shop as [Dumb Starbucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumb_Starbucks), which exploited a parody-law loophole and went viral.

## The turning point: Smokers Allowed

The Guardian singles out a third-season episode, *Smokers Allowed*, as a turning point. Fielder had 21 minutes to revive a bar. After learning that smoking was permitted if events were staged as a "play" for an audience of two, he filmed an ordinary night and hired actors to recreate it word for word.

The paper sees that episode and the feature-length *Finding Frances* as rehearsals for what came next.

## The Rehearsal and the "Fielder method"

*The Rehearsal* put what the paper calls the "Fielder method" at the centre: acting out absurd scenarios in advance to guess how a real person will react on camera. The Guardian says the first series helped a woman weighing up motherhood, while the second tackled airline crashes and aviation safety.

It also points to a little-known detail: Fielder was keen on stage magic as a teenager and worked in a magic shop in a shopping mall. According to the paper, he and Oppenheim came up with *You Can See Everything* while watching card tricks at the Magic Castle in Los Angeles. Fielder is also a high school friend of [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen). The piece opens with producer [Robert Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Evans)'s line that every story has three sides (yours, mine and the truth), an idea it says no one has probed more skilfully than Fielder.
