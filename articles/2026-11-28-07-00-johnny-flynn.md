- [Johnny Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Flynn), 43, has released *Beam Cottage*, his seventh album and a set of duets with his sister Lillie.
- He plays drug lord Frankie in the second season of Guy Ritchie's *MobLand*, which he says has brought him an unexpected fanbase.
- He recently filmed *If Love Should Die*, a drama about [Mary Wollstonecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Wollstonecraft), in Dublin.
- He told The Nerve that he has just played [Paul Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Simon) in a film linked to the making of *Graceland*.

## Johnny Flynn on Beam Cottage and a very busy year

Johnny Flynn has a lot on at once. In a new interview with The Nerve, the actor and folk musician described a run of work that spans television, film and a fresh record with his sister. The album, *Beam Cottage*, is a set of duets with Lillie Flynn. According to the interview, it sets the pastoral calm of the siblings' childhood home in England against the weariness that comes with getting older. It is his seventh album, and the pair performed at the Royal Festival Hall in London this month, [The Nerve](https://www.thenerve.news/p/johnny-flynn-interview-beam-cottage-folk-album-mobland-elsinore-hamlet) reports.

The record also reflects the family's ties to South Africa. Flynn was born there and moved to the UK aged four, and his mother grew up there. As a teenager he won a music scholarship to Bedales school in Hampshire, then became a leading name in London's mid-2000s folk scene. His screen breakthrough came in 2014 with the comedy *Lovesick*, followed by the 2017 film *Beast* opposite [Jessie Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessie_Buckley). He has moved between music and acting ever since.

## MobLand fame and an armed police fan

On screen, Flynn plays Frankie, a scooter-riding drug lord in a sheepskin coat, in season two of *MobLand*. He says the role has won him some surprising admirers, including an armed police officer who approached him and called it a nice character. "It's gone kinda nuts," he said. ## Playing Paul Simon

The most striking detail in the interview is about *Graceland*. Flynn says the album was played constantly in the family car when he and Lillie were children, and it carried particular weight given their South African roots. He recalls watching Nelson Mandela's release on the news with the record playing in the background.

On the very day he finished mixing a song for *Beam Cottage*, he says, a call came asking whether he would play Paul Simon in a film about the making of *Graceland*. He shot it this summer. He describes the story as centred on [Hugh Masekela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Masekela), the jazz trumpeter who backed Simon when the singer was accused of breaking the cultural boycott. Flynn called the timing fatalistic.

## Politics of kindness

Flynn says he does not see himself as very political. He describes a "manifesto of kindness" and sums it up as having no "us and them". He also calls himself a diehard socialist, and backs more funding for state schools paid for in part by private schools, despite having attended one himself.
