**Quick summary**

- *Paper Tiger*, a crime drama starring [Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson), opened in six US cinemas on 13 November.
- It took US$215,009 over the weekend, a per-screen average of US$35,834, according to Box Office Mojo figures cited by ScreenRant.
- That beats director [James Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gray_(director))'s previous best debut average of US$27,543, set by *The Lost City of Z* in 2016.
- The film has an 85% Certified Fresh score on Rotten Tomatoes and a reported budget of US$15 million.

*Paper Tiger* has made a strong start. The new film from writer-director James Gray, with Scarlett Johansson in a leading role, opened in just six US cinemas on 13 November and posted the best debut per-screen average of his career, [ScreenRant reports](https://screenrant.com/scarlett-johansson-paper-tiger-box-office-opening-weekend-domestic-result/).

## What the opening numbers show

According to Box Office Mojo figures quoted by ScreenRant, the film grossed US$215,009 across its first three days in the United States. Spread over six screens, that works out at US$35,834 a cinema.

The previous record belonged to *The Lost City of Z*. It debuted in 2016 with an average of US$27,543, which was also the only earlier Gray film to clear US$15,000 a screen on opening weekend, ScreenRant says.

A limited release flatters averages, so the figure is not a forecast. Because Gray's releases have varied so much in size, ScreenRant cautions that the record does not necessarily predict long-term performance. *The Lost City of Z* never played in more than 900 US cinemas in a single weekend, which capped its total. *Ad Astra*, Gray's highest-grossing film overall, opened on more than 3,000 screens.

## The story and the cast

The film is set in 1986 and follows two brothers who get tangled up with the Russian mob. [Adam Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Driver) and [Miles Teller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Teller) play the brothers. Johansson plays Hester Pearl, the wife of Teller's character, Irwin.

Driver and Johansson were previously paired in *Marriage Story*, ScreenRant notes. The film had its world premiere at the 79th Cannes Film Festival before reaching US cinemas.

## Reviews and the money

Reviews have been warm. ScreenRant puts the film's Rotten Tomatoes score at 85%, enough for a [Certified Fresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) rating.

The outlet compares it with Gray's 2007 crime drama *We Own the Night*, which starred [Joaquin Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaquin_Phoenix), [Mark Wahlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Wahlberg) and [Eva Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Mendes). That film drew mixed-to-negative reviews and a 57% score.

On cost, ScreenRant cites a reported budget of US$15 million and estimates break-even at around US$37.5 million. It suggests the film could become Gray's second to pass US$50 million worldwide since *We Own the Night*. That is the outlet's projection, not a reported result.

## Awards talk

ScreenRant also points to early Oscar buzz. If the film becomes a contender this season, it says, Driver or Johansson could earn a third nomination, Teller a first, and Gray a first. A nomination run could keep the film in cinemas well into 2027, the outlet adds.

## What happens next

The film moves to a [wide release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_release) after this limited debut, according to the ScreenRant report. The first wide-weekend figures will show whether the early enthusiasm carries beyond the six cinemas that opened it. No Australian release date appears in the sources we read.
