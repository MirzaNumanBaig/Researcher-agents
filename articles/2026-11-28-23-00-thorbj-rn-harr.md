**Quick summary**

- Thorbjørn Harr is billed second, after Sara Khorami, in Netflix's hospital drama Always on Call, according to Martin Cid Magazine.
- He plays a doctor in the same Oslo emergency room as Khorami's character, a banished surgeon.
- Born in Oslo on 24 May 1974, he has spent about 25 years in the ensemble of Nationaltheatret.
- Abroad he is best known for Vikings, Younger and Togo; at home he won the 2020 Amanda for best supporting actor.

Many viewers pressing play on Always on Call will meet [Thorbjørn Harr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorbj%C3%B8rn_Harr) as a new face: the tall, fair-haired doctor in an Oslo emergency room. Norwegian audiences know a different man, according to a [Martin Cid Magazine profile](https://www.martincid.com/people/thorbjorn-harr/) published on 28 November 2026. For them he is a long-serving stage actor who also sits on the theatre's board.

## From a schoolyard to the National Theatre

Harr was born in Oslo and, per the profile, was spotted in a schoolyard by NRK scouts looking for young actors. He made his television debut at 14 in Onkel Gustav og kassettmysteriet. His breakthrough came with two NRK films about a girl called Frida, the second reaching cinemas in 1991.

From 1993 he spent fifteen years in Teatersport Oslo, an improvisation group he ran with comedian [Harald Eia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harald_Eia) and two other performers. Martin Cid Magazine says that training in timing still shows in his comic work.

He studied at the Norwegian National Academy of Theatre from 1997 to 2000 and joined Nationaltheatret straight away, debuting in Ibsen's De unges forbund. The roles since include Figaro in 2004, Håkon Håkonssøn in 2005 and Tom in Tennessee Williams's memory play in 2007. A decade apart he played both men who corner Nora in [A Doll's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Doll's_House): Torvald Helmer in 2010 and Krogstad in 2020. In 2022 he was Rochester in Eline Arbo's staging of the Brontë novel, and Kurt in a Strindberg production directed by [Marit Moum Aune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marit_Moum_Aune).

## The work he built himself

The profile argues his most respected work is the work he wrote or shaped. In 2009 he adapted a text by [Lars Saabye Christensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Saabye_Christensen) into Chet spiller ikke her, performed it and played saxophone on stage. It won the Norwegian critics' prize for theatre, and his radio work brought NRK's Blue Bird award two years later.

In 2012 he shared a Hedda Award with [Mariann Hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariann_Hole) and Jan Gunnar Røise for Komilab, a comedy laboratory at Torshovteatret. In 2015 he dramatised and produced a Kafka evening with music by Röyksopp. It opened at the Bergen International Festival before moving to Nationaltheatret's main stage. He also sings in the jazz duo Harr & Hartberg.

## A slower screen career at home

His Norwegian film work built gradually. He led Mot Moskva in 2003, which earned an Amanda nomination, and Mars & Venus in 2007. The television lead in Kielergata in 2018 brought a Gullruten nomination.

The turn came through [Dag Johan Haugerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Johan_Haugerud). Harr won the 2020 Amanda for best supporting actor for Beware of Children. Four years later he and Røise played the two married men at the centre of Haugerud's Sex, the Berlinale Panorama film that opened the director's Oslo trilogy and won the Nordic Council Film Prize. On NRK he also played former prime minister Kåre Willoch in Power Play.

## What international audiences saw

The profile describes his overseas credits as parts at the edge of the frame. In Vikings, across 2013 and 2014, he played [Jarl Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Vikings_and_Vikings%3A_Valhalla_characters), whose betrayal ends in the blood eagle. In Younger he was Anton Bjornberg, a love interest for Hilary Duff's character. He also appeared in Paul Greengrass's 22 July and Netflix's horror film Cadaver. He played musher Charlie Olsen opposite Willem Dafoe in Disney's Togo, and a holidaying father in the volcano series La Palma.

In 2020 he played a jarl again in Norsemen, a comedy that parodies saga dramas of the kind that made him recognisable abroad. Martin Cid Magazine says that joke carries the critical point: travelling productions have mostly asked him for a type, such as the Nordic authority figure or the man in uniform, while his prizes at home came from other kinds of work.

## What happens next

Always on Call is the clearest sign yet that the export market has noticed more of his range, the profile says. The article did not report further screen projects. At Nationaltheatret, Harr is also the staff's elected board representative and is arguing for the rescue of the theatre's building.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is Thorbjørn Harr in Always on Call?
He plays a doctor working in the same Oslo emergency room as Sara Khorami's banished surgeon. Netflix bills him second, after Khorami, according to Martin Cid Magazine.

### Where do I know Thorbjørn Harr from?
International viewers may recognise him as Jarl Borg in Vikings, Anton Bjornberg in Younger or Charlie Olsen in Disney's Togo.

### Has Thorbjørn Harr won any awards?
Yes. He won the 2020 Amanda for best supporting actor for Beware of Children, plus a Hedda Award in 2012 and the Norwegian critics' prize for theatre in 2009, per the profile.
