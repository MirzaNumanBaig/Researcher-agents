**Quick summary**

- A former general manager of New York's Mark Hotel has described the night in 1994 when [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp) was arrested there.
- His account appears in Mark Seal's new book The Many Faces of Johnny Depp, as reported by [Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly).
- Raymond Bickson says Depp was "belligerent" after staff found the room badly damaged.
- Depp's charges were dismissed after he agreed to pay US$9,767 in damages, according to The New York Times.
- Entertainment Weekly has asked a Depp representative for comment.

A hotel manager who saw the fallout of one of Johnny Depp's best-known run-ins with the law has given his version of events. Raymond Bickson, who spent 15 years as general manager of the Mark Hotel in New York, recalls the 1994 night in The Many Faces of Johnny Depp, a new book by Mark Seal, [Entertainment Weekly reports](https://ew.com/manager-says-johnny-depp-was-belligerent-after-1994-hotel-room-trashing-12268363). The book is out now.

## What the manager remembers

Bickson says he saw Depp and his then-girlfriend, [Kate Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Moss), arguing as they came into the building. In the book he says Depp broke the mirror in the elevator on the way up to their room on the fourteenth floor, and that the argument carried on once they were inside.

Around 5 a.m., according to the book, several guests heard loud, destructive noises from the room. One of them was the Who singer [Roger Daltrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Daltrey). The hotel's head of security went to check, and Bickson says staff found the room "totally trashed".

## The damage on the police report

The book quotes a police report that lists what was broken. It names two seventeenth-century picture frames and prints, a lamp stand, a Chinese pot, a shattered glass tabletop, broken coffee table legs, broken wooden shelves, a shattered vase, cigarette burns on the carpet and a split red desk chair.

Depp was arrested on two counts of second-degree criminal mischief, per Entertainment Weekly's summary of the book. The allegations come from Bickson's recollection and the report as quoted by the author, and Depp has not responded in the coverage we read.

## "He was belligerent"

Bickson says the hotel did not want to press charges at first. His staff's offer, as he tells it, was for Depp to collect his things and leave. "But he was belligerent," he says.

Police then took the actor into custody, and word spread fast. Bickson says the street outside was crowded with media vans, so much so that the hotel practically had to close off Seventy-Seventh Street. Regular guests such as [Ali MacGraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_MacGraw) and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar had to walk in through the front door.

## A call from Marlon Brando

The most unexpected moment, by Bickson's account, was a phone call. His secretary told him [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando) was on the line, and he did not believe her at first. Brando, he says, described Depp as "such a good guy" and said it was surely a misunderstanding.

Brando had recently worked with Depp on Don Juan DeMarco. They teamed up again on The Brave, Depp's 1997 directorial debut, which was never released in the United States after poor reviews at Cannes.

Depp spent the night in custody and was released the next day. The charges were dismissed after he agreed to pay US$9,767 in damages to the hotel, according to The New York Times, as cited by Entertainment Weekly.
