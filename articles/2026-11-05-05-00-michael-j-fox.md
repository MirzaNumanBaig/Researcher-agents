**Quick summary**
- The [Michael J. Fox Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Michael_J._Fox_Foundation) (MJFF) and Medtronic announced a deep brain stimulation (DBS) education initiative on 4 November 2026.
- A free online hub, a practical guide and interactive webinars are planned; the hub is live at michaeljfox.org/dbs.
- Medtronic is the premier partner, and MJFF says it keeps editorial oversight of the materials.
- An estimated 6 million people worldwide live with Parkinson's, a figure expected to double by 2040.

The Michael J. Fox Foundation and medical technology company Medtronic have launched a joint education programme to help people with Parkinson's disease and their carers understand deep brain stimulation, according to a [Medtronic press release](https://news.medtronic.com/2026-11-04-Medtronic-and-Michael-J-Fox-Foundation-Partner-to-Help-More-People-with-Parkinsons-Understand-Deep-Brain-Stimulation) dated 4 November 2026. The foundation was set up by actor [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox), and its [official website](https://www.michaeljfox.org) now hosts the new material.

## Why the Michael J. Fox Foundation DBS hub matters

The announcement says about 6 million people worldwide live with Parkinson's, and that the number is expected to double by 2040. DBS has been used for nearly three decades to manage some symptoms, yet the release says awareness and understanding of the therapy remain barriers to people considering it.

The partners describe the programme as an effort to close that knowledge gap, so patients and families can have better-informed conversations with their care teams.

## What DBS is, and what it is not

The release describes DBS as a surgically implanted device that sends electrical signals to parts of the brain that control movement, often compared with a cardiac pacemaker for the brain. It is used alongside medication, and some patients may be able to reduce how much medication they take.

It is not right for everyone. The release also states that Parkinson's is progressive, that symptoms vary from person to person, and that there is currently no cure or treatment proven to stop its progression.

## What the education initiative offers

Resources are available now at michaeljfox.org/dbs, home of "The Live On with DBS Education Hub". The partners say a practical guide, interactive webinars and further programming will cover:

- how DBS works, and its potential benefits and risks
- preparing for surgery
- adjusting to life with a DBS device
- the latest research advances

## Who is involved

Medtronic is named as the premier partner. It says it has supported DBS therapy for over 30 years and served more than 200,000 people with movement disorders and other conditions worldwide. In 2025 it introduced what it calls the world's first FDA-approved adaptive DBS system for Parkinson's.

MJFF's chief program officer, Sohini Chowdhury, said industry partnerships let the foundation expand clear, practical resources. The release says industry support allows MJFF to keep editorial oversight while directing donor-raised money to research. Andrea Swain, president of Neuromodulation at Medtronic, said innovation only helps if people know it exists.

The release also quotes John L. Lipp, a writer, research advocate and person with Parkinson's who took part in the ADAPT-PD clinical trial. He described five years with DBS as meaningful, and stressed that the decision is personal and needs clear information.

This is a company announcement, so the claims come from the partners themselves. Readers weighing treatment should speak to their own care team.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Live On with DBS Education Hub?
It is the Michael J. Fox Foundation's online hub at michaeljfox.org/dbs, supported by Medtronic, with information on how deep brain stimulation works, its risks and benefits, and life after surgery.

### Who is Medtronic in this partnership?
Medtronic is the premier partner of the initiative. MJFF says industry support lets it keep editorial oversight of the resources.

### Does deep brain stimulation cure Parkinson's?
No. According to the release, there is no cure or treatment proven to stop progression. DBS can help some people manage symptoms and is used alongside medication.
