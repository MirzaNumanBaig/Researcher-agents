**Quick summary**

- U2 say a [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) song, "Most of the Time", helped shape the drumming on "Torn", their duet with Dolly Parton.
- Bono told iHeartRadio the idea came from Dylan's 1989 album Oh Mercy, according to a ClutchPoints report carried by Yahoo.
- "Torn" closes the new U2 album Carnaval de Luz, released on 13 November, and arrived as a single the same day.
- Separately, a Secret Berlin feature recalls Dylan's 1987 concert in East Berlin, which drew about 100,000 people.

## A Dylan track behind a country duet

One of Bob Dylan's best-loved late-80s songs has turned up in an unexpected place. U2 say "Most of the Time", from Dylan's album [Oh Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Mercy), gave their drummer a way into "Torn", the track they recorded with [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton) for the new record Carnaval de Luz.

According to [Yahoo, republishing a ClutchPoints report](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/music/new-albums/articles/bob-dylan-song-inspired-u2-135258071.html), Bono explained the link in an interview with iHeartRadio. The report does not suggest the songs sound alike. The Dylan track was a reference point for approach and feel, not a template.

## Why the drummer needed a reference

[Larry Mullen Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Mullen_Jr.) described the problem plainly. Working around a voice like Parton's, on her song, he did not feel he could simply play country, and said he did not know how to do that. The job was to interpret the song in a new way.

"Most of the time" gave him an idea of how to get into it, he said, and he and producer Jacknife Lee worked out the rest together. Mullen also admitted to some nerves. In his words, a drummer does not want to upset Dolly Parton.

Those nerves passed. The report says Parton gave his playing her "total blessing".

## The Oh Mercy connection

The thread runs through one person. [Daniel Lanois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Lanois), who has a long history with both Dylan and U2, played pedal steel on "Torn". He also produced Oh Mercy in 1989.

Bono said that album holds some of the band's favourite Dylan tracks, according to the same report. That makes the reference less surprising: the people in the room already knew that record well.

## About "Torn" and Carnaval de Luz

The report describes "Torn" as a meditative country song about a family being divided. It closes the album. It was also the last vocal recording Parton made before she died in August 2026, according to the same article.

Carnaval de Luz came out on 13 November and is U2's first collection of new material since Songs of Experience in December 2017, a gap of nearly nine years. Three singles preceded it: "Street of Dreams", "Silencio" and "The Greatest Show on Earth". "Torn" was released as a single on 13 November, with two alternative takes.

## Dylan in East Berlin, 1987

Dylan's name is also in the news for a different reason. A [Secret Berlin](https://secretberlin.co/en/bob-dylan-concert-east-berlin/) feature looks back at 17 September 1987, when he played East Berlin for the first time, in Treptower Park.

He was joined by [Tom Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty) and the Heartbreakers and [Roger McGuinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_McGuinn), and played to around 100,000 people, two years before the Berlin Wall fell, according to the feature.

The show was never meant for the East. Dylan was booked for the Waldbühne in West Berlin, but only about 2,000 tickets reportedly sold. Promoter Fritz Rau then offered the date to the GDR Artists' Agency. The Free German Youth organised it as part of Berlin's 750th anniversary celebrations and billed it as a peace concert. Some 81,000 tickets sold quickly.

The Stasi watched closely. The feature says it listed Dylan under his birth name, Robert Zimmerman, and planned to deploy around 1,200 Free German Youth students as so-called agitators, with People's Police officers and security teams also on duty. There were no major political incidents.
