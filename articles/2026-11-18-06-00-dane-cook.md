**Quick summary**

- [Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook) owns two homes across the street from each other in the Hollywood Hills, worth US$10.08 million (about A$15.4 million) combined, according to realestate.com.au.
- He paid US$7.08 million for the first house in 2008 and US$3 million for the second in December 2020.
- His half-brother and former business manager, Darryl McCauley, pleaded guilty in 2010 to 27 counts of larceny, embezzlement and forgery.
- Cook has told his story in the Netflix documentary *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story*.

Dane Cook has quietly built a Hollywood Hills compound worth about US$10.08 million (A$15.4 million), according to a report from realestate.com.au published on 17 November. The comedian bought two houses on the same street, years after the financial betrayal that nearly wiped him out.

## Dane Cook Hollywood Hills: the two houses

The report says Cook paid US$7.08 million (A$10.8 million) in 2008 for a four-bedroom contemporary home high above the Sunset Strip. In December 2020 he spent a further US$3 million (A$4.6 million) on the house directly across the street.

Realestate.com.au describes the purchases as a sharp turnaround for the 54-year-old, who it says built a US$35 million (A$53.5 million) fortune at the height of his 2000s stand-up fame. The outlet also notes his role in the 2007 comedy [Good Luck Chuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Luck_Chuck), in which he starred opposite [Jessica Alba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Alba).

The report gives no address and no current valuation, so the US$10.08 million figure is simply the two purchase prices added together.

## How his half-brother took the money

The first house was bought in the same year that the worst of the theft came to light. Cook hired his older half-brother, Darryl McCauley, as his longtime business manager and put him in charge of Great Dane Enterprises. According to the report, Cook had brought McCauley out of a prison job near Boston after an inmate attacked him.

Prosecutors found that McCauley began siphoning money from Cook's accounts around 2004. Realestate.com.au says the amounts grew from cheques of US$22,000 to nearly US$1 million at a time. In December 2008 McCauley walked into a Bank of America branch with a forged US$3 million cheque and left with the cash in duffel bags, Cook recalled in the documentary.

Police and FBI agents later raided McCauley's home and found hundreds of thousands of dollars hidden in jars of spaghetti sauce and in potted plants, the report says.

## What the court ordered

McCauley pleaded guilty in 2010 to 27 counts of larceny, embezzlement and forgery. He was sentenced to six years in prison and ordered to pay US$12 million (A$18.4 million) in restitution.

Cook has said he believes the real loss was between US$17 million and US$22 million. He was left with almost no cash and millions in unpaid taxes, according to the report, and spent years touring relentlessly to rebuild his finances.

Realestate.com.au reports that McCauley has paid back just US$50 a month, leaving more than US$12 million outstanding. Cook even offered to cancel the debt if his brother agreed to an on-camera interview for the documentary. McCauley refused.

## Cook on missing his brother

Despite everything, Cook told Rolling Stone that his life is now the best it has been, but that he still misses the brother he grew up with in Massachusetts. In the quote carried by realestate.com.au, he says that with a time machine he would go back and hug him one more time.

## What happens next

Nothing in the report points to a new sale or renovation. The open question is the US$12 million restitution, which McCauley has so far repaid only in tiny monthly amounts. Any change to that arrangement would come through the courts, and none has been reported.
