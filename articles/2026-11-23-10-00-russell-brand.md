**Quick summary**

- Laura Brand said in a YouTube episode published on Sunday 22 November that she will "absolutely honour" the rules of HMP Littlehey, where her husband is jailed.
- A week earlier she had read out a message from [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) in which he said he was "not guilty" of the offences he was jailed for.
- The UK government called that message "clearly appalling" and ordered an urgent investigation, according to the BBC and Page Six.
- Brand is serving 10 and a half years for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.
- She will keep hosting the weekly series, and the video remains on YouTube.

Laura Brand has told viewers she will follow the rules of the prison holding her husband, one week after she read out a statement from him that set off a political row in Britain. The pledge came in the latest episode of *Sunday Service with the Brands*, published on 22 November.

## What Laura Brand said on Sunday

She opened the episode by addressing the fallout. "Last week's episode drew a considerable amount of attention," she said, "and now that I understand the parameters set by the brilliant and professional staff at HMP Littlehey, I will absolutely honour that." The quotes are carried by the BBC, The Guardian and The Independent.

She is not stepping back. "I am choosing to continue Sunday Service because I really enjoy it," she said, according to The Guardian. She added that she would keep being "proud to be a wife, a mother and a woman defending her family" while the media's picture of her was "under so much scrutiny".

The BBC says she told viewers she had spoken to him at the Cambridgeshire prison and that he was "OK". Rolling Stone, citing the Daily Mail, reports that she visited him this week and met prison officials. What was discussed is unclear.

## The message that started the row

In the previous episode, titled "The Good Work Continues", she read out a note from her husband. It said: "As you know, I am not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for, so something strange is happening." It was the first episode published since he was jailed, according to The Guardian.

A spokesperson for Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham) called it "clearly appalling". The spokesperson said prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims, that speaking to the media is allowed only in exceptional circumstances, and that having someone else read a message does not get around the rule, as reported by Nine and Page Six.

Burnham urged social media companies to take the videos down but said he would not "leap" to change the law. Google, which owns YouTube, refused a government request to remove the episode, the BBC reports. The Guardian says it has passed one million views. Many of the channel's videos in the past year drew only 50,000 to 100,000.

## Why the prison rules matter

The Guardian reports that the Ministry of Justice is looking into whether Brand broke rules meant to prevent "serious distress" to victims. People reports that if he is found to have done so, he could face disciplinary action, including limits on phone calls and other contact with his wife. No outcome has been confirmed.

Brand was convicted at Southwark Crown Court on 5 November, according to The Guardian. He pleaded not guilty. The [Crown Prosecution Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prosecution_Service) says the offences took place between 2004 and 2009, as cited by People. He must sign the sex offenders' register for life.

Rolling Stone reports that his wellness brand Reborn began selling "Free Russell" shirts on Saturday, which we covered in [Russell Brand sells "Free Russell" merch from prison](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-merch-prison/).

## What happens next

The investigation into the first message is still open. People reports that an attorney for Brand said his legal team is advising him on a possible appeal of the conviction and sentence.

## Frequently asked questions

### What did Laura Brand say about the prison rules?
She said she now understands the parameters set by the staff at HMP Littlehey and will "absolutely honour" them. She also said she will keep hosting *Sunday Service*.

### Why was the earlier message controversial?
Russell Brand's note claimed he was not guilty. Downing Street said prisoners may not make statements that cause distress to victims, and that a relative reading a message aloud does not bypass the ban.

### Is the video still online?
Yes. The BBC says Google refused the government's request to remove it.
