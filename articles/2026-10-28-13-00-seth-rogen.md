- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) told the New York Times he doubted Jake Gyllenhaal's performance while making Donnie Darko, according to TheWrap.
- The comments are part of an oral history marking the cult film's 25th anniversary, which fell on Monday.
- Rogen played the bully Ricky Danforth, in what was his first film role.
- Separately, Collider reports his thriller The Invite is the most-watched film on HBO Max in the US and on Paramount+ in Australia.

[Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) has admitted he wasn't sure Jake Gyllenhaal's lead performance in [Donnie Darko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Darko) would work while cameras were rolling. The actor made the comments in a New York Times oral history of the film, published as it turned 25, as [TheWrap reported](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/seth-rogen-donnie-darko-jake-gyllenhaal-doubts/).

## What Rogen said about Gyllenhaal

Rogen, now the star and co-creator of The Studio, recalled watching Gyllenhaal on set and wondering how the film would land. "He wasn't playing this charming version of it," he said, according to TheWrap.

His doubt turned into respect. Rogen said he came to realise Gyllenhaal had put a level of thought into the role that he hadn't realised was even on the table for an actor. For a performer who was then just starting out, that was an education.

He wasn't only worried about one performance. Rogen also recalled thinking the project would either be really good or turn into "the weird B-movie of itself", TheWrap said.

## A first film role and a crowded cast

Donnie Darko was Rogen's first film part. He played the high school bully Ricky Danforth in a cast that also included [Jena Malone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jena_Malone), [Drew Barrymore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Barrymore), [Mary McDonnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McDonnell), [Katharine Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharine_Ross), [Patrick Swayze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Swayze) and [Noah Wyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Wyle).

[Richard Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Kelly_(filmmaker)) wrote and directed the science fiction thriller. It follows Gyllenhaal as a troubled teenager haunted by a figure in a rabbit costume who warns him the world is about to end.

## How a box-office miss became a cult classic

The film did not start well. TheWrap notes it underperformed at the box office, largely because its advertising was limited after the September 11 attacks. It found its audience later through DVD and VHS sales, midnight screenings and reissues.

Rogen's own explanation is simple. "If a thing is good and resonates, it will find its people," he told the Times, adding that Donnie Darko "very much was that."

## The Invite tops streaming charts

Rogen is also in the news for a very different film. Collider reports that Olivia Wilde's The Invite, a remake of the Spanish-language comedy The People Upstairs, is the most-watched movie on HBO Max in the US after arriving on the service. It also ranks as the most-watched film on Paramount+ in Australia, a useful sign for local viewers.

The film also stars Edward Norton and Penélope Cruz alongside Rogen. It is Wilde's third feature as director, after Booksmart and Don't Worry Darling. Collider describes it as one of the standout films of 2026 and suggests it is quietly gaining awards-season momentum, though that is the outlet's view and not a confirmed nomination.
