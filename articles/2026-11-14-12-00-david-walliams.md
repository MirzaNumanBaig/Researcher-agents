**Quick summary**

- London's Metropolitan Police confirmed on Friday 13 November that it has opened an investigation into "non-recent serious sexual offences involving a man now in his 50s".
- Police did not name anyone, but the reports on which this article draws link the inquiry to [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), 55, who denies wrongdoing.
- The Met says the complaints are separate from the Guardian and ITV News investigation published earlier this week. Detectives are also reviewing that reporting.
- Walliams has not been arrested or charged, according to the reports.

The David Walliams police investigation became a criminal matter on Friday, when the Metropolitan Police said detectives are examining several allegations of non-recent serious sexual offences. The force did not identify the man involved, in line with its usual policy. [Anadolu Agency](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/london-police-investigate-british-author-david-walliams-over-alleged-sexual-offenses/4124804), citing The Guardian, and [Rolling Stone Australia](https://au.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-news/david-walliams-london-police-investigation-sexual-misconduct-103364/) both tie the statement to the author and comedian. We have covered earlier steps in our [earlier report on the Met investigation](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-police-investigation/).

## What the police have said

According to Anadolu Agency, the Met received several allegations recently. The force said specialist officers are supporting those who have come forward. It declined to give locations or dates, to protect complainants, who are entitled to lifelong anonymity.

Rolling Stone Australia reports that the Met told the BBC the allegations "are separate from those reported by ITN and The Guardian". It added that detectives are also proactively reviewing that reporting to establish whether it shows evidence of offences.

Detective Superintendent Mike Cagney, whose complex sexual offences unit is leading the work, urged anyone affected to get in touch "no matter when it happened". He said specialist officers can explain the process and offer support.

The Guardian reported that some allegations date to the 2000s and that at least some complainants approached police this week, according to Anadolu Agency.

## What Walliams has said

The investigation follows a joint Guardian and ITV News report published on Tuesday, after Walliams failed in a legal bid to stop it. Rolling Stone Australia says more than 30 people were involved in that report. Anadolu Agency says it included accounts from two women who said they first met him at 15 and believed he had groomed them.

Walliams denies the allegations. In a written response quoted by the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c8r4g04904xqo), he said: "I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law." He also said he behaved thoughtlessly at times towards people he had casual relationships with, and that he regrets not always considering their feelings. He drew a line between that and abuse of power, which he said he never committed.

An allegation is not a finding. No charge has been laid, and he is entitled to the presumption of innocence.

## The fallout so far

The consequences have come quickly. The Guardian reported, via Anadolu Agency, that his literary agency dropped him, the BBC began removing some of his programs from iPlayer, and the Labour Party suspended his membership.

His books are also under scrutiny. The BBC reports that the Guille-Alles Library in St Peter Port, Guernsey, will keep lending them. Its spokesperson said books should stay available unless their content is illegal, while acknowledging it is "a sensitive and complex issue". Our coverage of the retail side is in [David Walliams books pulled by Dymocks and Big W in Australia](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-books-australia/).

## A wider debate on the age of consent

The case, alongside the allegations against [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), which he denies, has revived calls to change the law. The Guardian reports that some Labour MPs back a staggered age of consent, and Claire Waxman, the victims' commissioner for England and Wales, wants "serious conversations" about legislative change.

Barrister [Charlotte Proudman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Proudman) said the current law does not adequately recognise the power imbalance between children and much older adults. The Close the Consent Gap campaign proposes making it an offence for a 16- or 17-year-old to consent to sex with anyone more than three years older.

Researchers quoted by The Guardian were more cautious. Prof Roger Ingham of the University of Southampton asked what evidence shows such a change protects young people. Dr Sophie King-Hill of the University of Birmingham warned it could stop young people coming forward for fear of having broken the law.

## What happens next

Police inquiries of this kind can take months. The reports do not give a timeline, and no arrest has been reported. The Met has asked anyone with information to come forward. No. The Guardian reported that he has not been arrested, and Rolling Stone Australia says he has not been charged.

### Does the police investigation involve the Guardian and ITV News report?
The allegations being investigated are separate from that report, the Met said. Detectives are also reviewing the report to see whether it shows evidence of offences.

### What has David Walliams said?
He denies wrongdoing and says he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law. He also said he regrets behaving thoughtlessly in some casual relationships.
