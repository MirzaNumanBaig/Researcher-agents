**Quick summary**

- [Ravi Dubey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_Dubey) says the word "hamara" (our) in his wife's post is what stayed with him most.
- [Sargun Mehta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargun_Mehta) compared his 21 years of rejections and waiting to Lakshman's vanvaas, or exile.
- He plays Lakshman in Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana, and spoke to Garavi Gujarat and NDTV about the role.
- He says the couple celebrated every milestone, from early television work to becoming producers.

After his performance as Lakshman in Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana drew praise, Ravi Dubey says the moment that mattered most was not the applause but a single word in a post by his wife, Sargun Mehta. In comments reported by [Garavi Gujarat](https://www.gg2.net/ravi-dubey-sargun-mehta-hamara-vanvaas-ramayana/) on 13 November 2026, the actor explained why "hamara" stood out.

## What Sargun Mehta wrote

According to Garavi Gujarat, Mehta shared an emotional note on social media looking back on her husband's 21 years in the entertainment industry. She named the rejections, the struggles and the years of waiting, and drew a parallel with Lakshman's vanvaas, the exile in the epic. The outlet quotes her as writing that she had said this Diwali their vanvaas would be complete.

Dubey said the choice of "hamara", meaning "our", framed the wait as something the two had shared. "We've watched each other quietly go through so much over the years," he told the outlet, adding that he thought seeing him on screen was far more emotional for her than for him.

## A dream carried since day one

Dubey said that seeing himself on the big screen was a goal he had carried since he entered the industry. He described Ramayana as the greatest Indian film ever made, and said experiencing the moment through his wife's eyes made it especially meaningful.

He accepted that "vanvaas" was a fair word for the long stretch. There were rejections, he said, and years when the dream felt very far away. He was also quick to say those years were not only hard. The couple celebrated their first days as television actors and later their move into producing, and each felt like a complete victory when it happened.

In his view, her post held both things at once: the weight of the wait and the happiness of the life they built along the way.

## How he approached Lakshman

Garavi Gujarat also reports Dubey's comments, first made to NDTV, on playing a character defined by loyalty to Shri Ram. Asked whether he held back to keep the focus on Ram, he said he never did so consciously.

He said he tried to stay close to what he understands as Lakshman's deepest instinct: to walk a step behind Ram, as his shadow and shield, with no wish for the spotlight. He called that unconditional loyalty and sense of duty graceful and noble.

Dubey also said that in his reading, every character in the Ramayana is part of the Ram Leela, the divine play of the epic. The excerpt available to us cuts off partway through that answer, so we have not reported the rest of it.
