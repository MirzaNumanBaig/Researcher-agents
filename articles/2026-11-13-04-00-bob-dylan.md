[Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) is back in conversation this week for two reasons: a fresh live cover on a Brooklyn stage, and a run of features looking again at the songs he wrote, alone and with others.

## Orville Peck and Ziwe cover Dylan in Brooklyn

Orville Peck performed "It Ain't Me, Babe" with Ziwe on Tuesday at Brooklyn Paramount, [JamBase reported](https://www.jambase.com/article/orville-peck-ziwe-dylan-cover-brooklyn-video-setlist). The outlet says it was reportedly the first time Peck had played the song live. The night was the second of two at the venue on his Man Turned Mule Tour, which supports his recent album Mule.

Ziwe is a Brooklyn-based writer who created the Showtime variety series Ziwe. JamBase notes she also hosts a live "Pop Show". The two traded banter on stage before the duet, which sat between "Hexie Mountains"/"My Side Of The Mountain" and "Outta Time" in the set.

Covers are becoming a habit on this run. Peck was joined by The Cactus Blossoms for a Gram Parsons song in Minneapolis, and he played Dolly Parton's "I Will Always Love You" at a solo show in London in August, JamBase said. The Dylan track first appeared on Another Side Of Bob Dylan in 1964.

## The Tom Petty co-write behind the Wilburys

Far Out Magazine looks at a quieter piece of Dylan history: his habit of writing with the people who later became his bandmates. With [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison), he co-wrote "I'd Have You Anytime" at his Woodstock home around 1968. It became the opening track of Harrison's album All Things Must Pass.

In 1987, according to [Far Out](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/traveling-wilburys-bob-dylan-wrote-some-classic-songs-for-his-bandmate/), Dylan and [Tom Petty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty) wrote lyrics for a track that guitarist Mike Campbell had been working on. It opened Petty's album Let Me Up (I've Had Enough) as "Jammin' Me". Campbell told Songfacts in 2003 that the words were assembled from newspaper and television fragments.

Dylan reportedly suggested name-dropping [Eddie Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Murphy), [Joe Piscopo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Piscopo) and [Vanessa Redgrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanessa_Redgrave) in the lyrics. Far Out argues the session built a bond that carried into the Traveling Wilburys, which Harrison began assembling in 1988.

## Four Bob Dylan songs named after women

[American Songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Songwriter) published a short list of Dylan songs addressed directly to a named woman, leaving out narrative pieces such as "The Lonesome Death Of Hattie Carroll". Its picks are "To Ramona" from Another Side Of Bob Dylan, "Visions Of Johanna" from the 1966 album Blonde On Blonde, and "Isis" and "Sara" from 1976's Desire.

The magazine notes Dylan wrote eight of Desire's ten tracks with Jacques Levy, but "Sara" was a solo effort. It describes the song as a direct message to his wife at the time. That is the magazine's reading, as it also says most Dylan experts hear "Isis" as being about the same relationship.
