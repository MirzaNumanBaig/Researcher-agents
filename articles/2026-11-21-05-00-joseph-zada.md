**Quick summary**

- [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), 21, plays a 16-year-old Haymitch Abernathy in the Hunger Games prequel *Sunrise on the Reaping*, which opens in cinemas on 20 November.
- W Magazine says it is his first feature film.
- He also stepped into a role once played by [James Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dean) in Netflix's *East of Eden*, in the final two episodes.
- The Sydney-born actor says he auditioned for Haymitch just days after finishing *East of Eden*.
- He has finished filming the second season of *We Were Liars*.

Joseph Zada has spent the past few months taking over parts first made famous by screen legends. In a new interview with W Magazine, the 21-year-old Sydney actor explains how he went from Australian television to leading the latest [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) film, which opens on 20 November.

## Playing young Haymitch

*Sunrise on the Reaping* is based on Suzanne Collins's fifth book in the series. Zada plays Haymitch Abernathy at 16, the younger version of the character Woody Harrelson played in the original four films, according to W Magazine.

The magazine describes a very different Haymitch from the cynical, hard-drinking mentor audiences know. This one is in love and sober, and the story follows him as he unravels from a youthful "mama's boy" in neglected District 12 into the jaded mentor of the later films.

The story is set 24 years before [Katniss Everdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katniss_Everdeen) appears, during the 50th Hunger Games, the second Quarter Quell, when the Capitol picks twice the usual number of tributes. Haymitch is chosen from District 12 and has to leave his family and his girlfriend, Lenore Dove, played by [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)).

W Magazine says director [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) chose Zada to lead the film. Zada admits there was "a lot of pressure" in joining a series whose director and crew had worked together for more than a decade.

## A run of roles that came in sequence

Zada is known to Australian audiences for the drama [Invisible Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_Boys), where he played an aspiring musician who is outed as gay. Young adult fans saw him in the first season of *We Were Liars* on Prime Video.

His breakthrough into the mainstream came this autumn in Zoe Kazan's *East of Eden* for Netflix, according to W Magazine. He says his *We Were Liars* audition was the last one the casting team watched, and that his co-star [Mamie Gummer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamie_Gummer) helped push his tape through to Kazan.

He then auditioned for Haymitch days after wrapping the Steinbeck adaptation. "The role came at the perfect point," he told the magazine, adding that he would never have been as ready as he was straight after working with Florence Pugh.

W Magazine notes the franchise has a history of launching young stars, naming Jennifer Lawrence, [Rachel Zegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Zegler) and [Tom Blyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Blyth).

## An acting family from Sydney

Zada says his mother, film producer Jessica Brentnall, predicted from the time he was five that he would be an actor. He was not allowed to play rugby, he jokes, because "you can't have a broken nose as a movie star."

His father, [Jeremy Cumpston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Cumpston), is a director and actor, and several of the five Cumpston siblings now have IMDb pages. Zada was born Joseph Cumpston and took his grandmother's surname professionally at 18. W Magazine reports the name traces to his great-grandfather, an Afghan cameleer, and that Zada has described a "spiritual connection" to that history.

At 15 he agreed to star in his then-girlfriend's school short film. When she presented it over Zoom, her mother, agent Mags Stewart, glimpsed his performance and decided she wanted to represent him.

## What happens next

*Sunrise on the Reaping* is in cinemas now, and the interview frames it as the film that could make Zada a star.
