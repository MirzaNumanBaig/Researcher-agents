**Quick summary**

- [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) appeared on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon, which aired on Tuesday 25 November (US time), to discuss her surprise role in *You Can See Everything*.
- She said she never meant to play Elizabeth Holmes again, but Nathan Fielder asked to pick her brain about her earlier turn in *The Dropout*.
- She admitted she told "a lot of people" about the project, and says almost all of them stayed quiet.
- She was filmed in her own apartment, watching documentary footage with four cameras running.

Amanda Seyfried has explained how one of the year's best-kept film secrets nearly got out. Speaking to Jimmy Fallon on The Tonight Show this week, the actor said she joined Nathan Fielder and [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim)'s documentary *You Can See Everything* late, and that she is far from a natural secret-keeper. For readers following the film, this is the clearest account yet of how her role came together.

## How Amanda Seyfried ended up in the film

*You Can See Everything* is a documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, who is serving a 10-year, three-month federal prison sentence in Texas after a conviction for wire fraud and conspiracy, [The A.V. Club reports](https://www.avclub.com/amanda-seyfried-you-can-see-everything-role-told-people). Seyfried already played Holmes in the Hulu series *The Dropout*, so her name is tied to the story.

She told Fallon she had no wish to go back to it. "It was never my intention to recreate Elizabeth Holmes ever again," she said, according to [Harper's BAZAAR](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/celebrity/latest/a74533467/amanda-seyfried-carolina-herrera-floral-cutout-dress-photos/). Then Fielder, the film's director, got in touch to ask if he could pick her brain about the experience.

Seyfried said she assumed he was working on a fictional show. The two spoke on FaceTime for an hour. Fielder then revealed what the project really was and asked whether she would be comfortable being filmed while she watched the documentary footage.

## Four cameras in her apartment

"A couple weeks later, I was watching the footage with four cameras in my apartment," Seyfried said. "I was like, 'How did I get here?'" (Harper's BAZAAR).

The A.V. Club says she signed on at the last minute and that this is when the project began to change shape. She appears about halfway through the film, as herself, and also reenacts conversations Fielder had with Holmes before Holmes went to prison. Seyfried told Fallon she has not fully processed it. "It was meta, at a point," she said.

The secrecy was tight. She said she could not see the documentary footage or a script until she reached the set, and joked that it might otherwise have been "a five-factor authentication app" where she could read it the night before. She also previously told Rolling Stone that she started filming without telling her agent or her lawyer, a risky step legally, as The A.V. Club notes.

## "The most indiscreet person I've ever met"

Asked how the role stayed hidden, Seyfried was as surprised as anyone. "I'm the most indiscreet person I've ever met in my life," she said. "I told a lot of people. But I guess I could trust them all."

Nearly all of them did keep quiet. The exception, by her account, was one person who wrote something on X. Fielder raised it with her on air, and Seyfried played dumb, according to The A.V. Club. The outlet also notes that she passed on playing Gamora in *Guardians of the Galaxy*, and jokes that Marvel boss [Kevin Feige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Feige) would have had his hands full with her and fellow spoiler-prone actors [Tom Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Holland) and [Mark Ruffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Ruffalo). That aside is the outlet's own gag, not a claim from Seyfried.
