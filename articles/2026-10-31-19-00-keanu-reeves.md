**Quick summary**

- [Keanu Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keanu_Reeves) agreed to voice the lead in the Japanese stop-motion feature "Hidari" after watching its pilot film, director Kawamura Masashi told the Tokyo International Film Festival.
- His casting was announced at Cannes in May, but he had seen the pilot before the festival.
- The film is still raising money. A US company's offer to fund the whole budget has stalled, according to Variety.
- Separately, Reeves and Sandra Bullock told the "50 MPH" podcast they would both like a "Speed 3", Entertainment.ie reports.

Keanu Reeves said yes to a handmade Japanese samurai film because someone put a pilot in front of him, and he answered within about a week. Director Kawamura Masashi described the casting at the Tokyo International Film Festival, according to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/festivals/keanu-reeves-hidari-ss-rajamouli-1236935934/), in the same week Reeves was talking up a different kind of comeback: a third "Speed" film.

## How did Keanu Reeves end up in Hidari?

Kawamura told Variety the puppet for the lead was designed with a specific face in mind. "We were heavily influenced by Kurosawa's films," he said, describing a look made from Toshiro Mifune and Reeves "added together, divided by two and then aged".

That put Reeves at the top of his wish list for the lead voice. The producers found a way to reach the actor and arranged a screening of the pilot. About a week later, Kawamura said, the reply came back: a Zoom call was on. "Then we got on the call and Keanu was there on the other side," he said.

The casting was announced at Cannes in May, but Kawamura said Reeves had already watched the pilot before the festival. Our earlier report on [Hidari seeking a new backer](https://moduscarry.com/keanu-reeves-samurai-film-hidari-seeks-new-backer/) covers the funding side of the story.

## Why is a stop-motion film getting attention?

The proof-of-concept short was shot about three years ago, paid for by the team with help from crowdfunding, and posted to YouTube. Kawamura told Variety it has drawn roughly 5.3 million views and about 120,000 followers, many of them overseas.

At Annecy this year the team pitched the film at the Annecy Animation Showcase, part of the Cannes Film Market, and carried one of the hand-carved puppets with them. Kawamura said the puppet drew bigger crowds than its makers did. He also said a 30-minute standing conversation with "RRR" director S.S. Rajamouli ended with a simple question: how was this made?

Kawamura's own pitch is that the film is an action film that happens to be stop-motion. A bigger question is who pays for it.

## Where does the money stand?

The budget is large, Kawamura said, and the team is still seeking grants and overseas distribution partners. Earlier this month in Busan, Dwarf Studios producer Matsumoto Noriko said talks with a US company that had offered to fund the entire budget, in exchange for all the rights, had stalled.

The project has backing from Film Frontier, now in its third year. It is part of the Agency for Cultural Affairs' Creator Support Fund, which supports creators for around three years, from development through to overseas release, according to Variety.

## What did Reeves and Bullock say about Speed 3?

This year marks 30 years since [Jan de Bont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_de_Bont)'s original "Speed". Its sequel, "Speed 2: Cruise Control", starred [Sandra Bullock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Bullock) but not Reeves, and Entertainment.ie calls it a huge flop.

Both actors appeared on the "50 MPH" podcast and were asked about another film. "We'd freakin' knock it out of the park," Reeves said, according to [Entertainment.ie](https://entertainment.ie/movies/movie-news/keanu-reeves-sandra-bullock-speed-3-560112/). Bullock said she thinks the two of them "need to do something in front of the camera" before she dies, and joked about doing it in wheelchairs or on scooters at Disneyland.

Reeves added that there "is a siren call" to it, as if something was left undone. Neither actor announced a project, and the report does not mention a studio or script.

## What happens next

"Hidari" has no announced release date in the coverage. The next step is funding: Kawamura's team is still looking for grants and distribution partners.
