**Quick summary**

- [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh) is promoting his new film [Wild Horse Nine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine), now in cinemas in several markets, including Ireland.
- The film follows two CIA agents sent to Easter Island shortly before Chile's 1973 military coup. John Malkovich, Sam Rockwell and Steve Buscemi star.
- McDonagh told The Playlist his plays will stay on stage and that a film of "The Pillowman" is "not going to happen".
- He also said he hopes to bring "The Pillowman" back to a theatre in New York or London soon.
- Critics are split: No Film School calls it his saddest film, while HeadStuff finds it shallow.

Martin McDonagh has spent the past week talking up Wild Horse Nine, a darkly comic drama about two CIA agents on Easter Island, and the interviews say a lot about where the writer-director is heading. According to The Playlist, he is also drawing a firm line around his stage work: the plays are not going to the screen.

## What Wild Horse Nine is about

The story is set shortly before Chile's 1973 military coup. According to [The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/martin-mcdonagh-wild-horse-nine-interview-20261110/), two veteran agents, Chris (John Malkovich) and Lee (Sam Rockwell), travel from Santiago to Easter Island on orders from their bureau chief, played by Steve Buscemi. What looks like an odd assignment forces both men to revisit their pasts. Chris, whose health is failing, has to face his mortality and the things he has done.

[No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/wild-horse-nine-is-very-sad) saw the film at the Venice Film Festival and describes Chris as a dying man whose memory is fading, who bonds with Chilean university students on the island. The same review says the film "brought the house down" in Venice and calls Malkovich's turn a possible career best.

The historical backdrop is real. The Playlist notes that the United States, and the CIA in particular, spent years working to undermine Chile's elected socialist president, Salvador Allende, before the coup of 11 September 1973. Allende died during the overthrow. McDonagh's film sets a personal story of guilt inside that history.

## Why McDonagh's films are getting sadder

The Playlist's interview carries the line that his films are getting sadder, and No Film School agrees. Its writer calls this his saddest film "which is saying a lot", and points out that McDonagh is only 56. The review sees a filmmaker already reflecting on the later decades of his career, and compares him to Paul Thomas Anderson, also 56, whose recent work shares some of the same generational themes.

The comparison to his earlier films is deliberate. The Playlist reminds readers that [In Bruges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Bruges) and "The Banshees of Inisherin" also turned on fractured male friendships in distinctive, isolated places. Here the friendship is between Chris and Lee, and the isolation is the middle of the Pacific.

## Easter Island on screen

Cinematographer Ben Davis has shot four films with McDonagh, starting with "Seven Psychopaths" in 2012. He told Awards Watch their relationship has not changed much, but that both men have aged and the film reflects it, as it is about the end of life rather than the beginning.

Davis described Easter Island as even more isolated than the Irish setting of "The Banshees of Inisherin". He said a driver could cross the island in about 40 minutes, yet the ocean surrounds you in every direction. He also said the hotel rooms for Chris and Lee were built as extensions of the real hotel rather than on a stage, because McDonagh wants things to feel genuine. [Awards Watch](https://awardswatch.com/interview-wild-horse-nine-cinematographer-ben-davis-on-his-collaborations-with-martin-mcdonagh-shooting-easter-island-and-animal-magnetism/) says the recurring use of windows and mirrors was written into the script.

## The reviews are not unanimous

Not everyone is won over. JoBlo's interviewer calls it one of the best films of the year and singles out Malkovich, with praise too for Buscemi, Parker Posey and Tom Waits, in an interview with McDonagh and Buscemi on [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/interview-wild-horse-nine/).

[HeadStuff](https://headstuff.org/entertainment/film/if-nothing-else-wild-horse-nine-is-undeniably-a-mcdonagh-production-film-review/) is far cooler. Its Dublin-based reviewer says Malkovich succeeds while Rockwell does not, and argues the film's treatment of Chilean history is shallow beside the work of the late Chilean documentary maker Patricio Guzmán. The same review still expects Oscar nominations, noting McDonagh now ranks as a prestige director on the awards circuit. No Film School says it is too early to call the awards race but would back Malkovich on performance alone.

## The plays stay on stage

In a separate Playlist conversation, McDonagh said he has several unproduced screenplays but remains reluctant to give up creative control. Asked about a film of "The Pillowman", which premiered on Broadway in 2005, he said: "Not going to happen, but I'll try; I'm going to try and get it back on here or in London soon. It's been a while."

The Playlist reports that he has long opposed screen versions of works such as "The Beauty Queen of Leenane", "The Lieutenant of Inishmore" and "The Pillowman", even though some of them look cinematic on the page. No dates or venues for a revival have been given.

## What happens next

Wild Horse Nine is in cinemas now in Ireland and the United States, according to HeadStuff and JoBlo. The sources do not give an Australian release date, so check local cinema listings. No Film School expects Malkovich to feature in the leading actor conversation this awards season. A Pillowman revival remains a hope rather than an announcement.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Wild Horse Nine about?
It follows two CIA agents, played by John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell, who go to Easter Island shortly before Chile's 1973 coup. Their trip forces them to confront what they have done.

### Will The Pillowman become a film?
No, according to Martin McDonagh. He told The Playlist it is "not going to happen" and said he wants to bring the play back to the stage in New York or London.

### Who stars in Wild Horse Nine?
John Malkovich and Sam Rockwell lead, with Steve Buscemi as their bureau chief. Reviews also mention Parker Posey and Tom Waits.

### Has Wild Horse Nine been well reviewed?
Reviews are mixed. No Film School and JoBlo praise it, while HeadStuff criticises its treatment of history, though it still expects awards attention.
