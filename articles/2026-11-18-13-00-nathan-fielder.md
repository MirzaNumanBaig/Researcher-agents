[Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) has long been a cult favourite, but his latest film has turned admiration into something closer to a Nathan Fielder celebrity crush, according to a column published by [Psychology Today Australia](https://www.psychologytoday.com/au/blog/how-fantasy-becomes-reality/202611/offbeat-celebrity-crushes-obsessing-over-nathan-fielder) on 17 November 2026. The piece uses his new documentary to explain why people fall for stars who do not fit the usual Hollywood mould.

## What the column argues

The column's starting point is *You Can See Everything*, in which Fielder, as director, interviews Elizabeth Holmes, described in the piece as an infamous biotech fraudster, and her partner Billy Evans. The author notes that Fielder was given close access to the couple, as the title suggests.

The writer also points out that this is not the first time Fielder has been cast as an offbeat heartthrob. According to the column, he appeared shirtless on the cover of New York magazine in 2022 under the caption "Hot cat dad alert", in a story about his show *The Rehearsal*. The magazine, the column says, even questioned his "inexplicable attractiveness".

## Parasocial crushes explained

Psychologists call a celebrity crush a parasocial romantic relationship. The term describes an attachment that lives mostly in the imagination, because most fans will never meet the person they admire.

The author argues that these fantasies are not necessarily a problem. Imagining a bond can work as a way to rehearse real relationships, the column says, and real relationships also lean heavily on imagination, such as daydreaming about an office crush. It does name possible costs, including unrealistic expectations of romance and being underwhelmed by ordinary people.

On looks, the writer's view is that Fielder may not resemble [Chris Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth), but is still reasonably attractive, and that Hollywood beauty standards can skew our sense of what is normal.

## Why the documentary may add to the appeal

The more interesting argument is about the film itself. The column says the documentary is emotional, carries a noticeable soundtrack built to heighten feeling, and is full of cues for intimacy and self-disclosure, both of which build attraction.

Eye contact gets particular attention. The author says Holmes is known for an unusually intense stare, which an expert on lying has suggested may be a manipulation technique. Direct eye contact is usually read as honesty and closeness, the column adds, and in the film both Holmes and Fielder show that intensity. Sharing secrets while holding someone's gaze, the writer says, can make viewers feel close to Fielder regardless of his motives.

The column also floats the idea that a man being openly vulnerable on screen can read as attractive, given the familiar trope that men struggle to share emotions.

## How the film is doing

The column's critic and fan scores are in line with the wider picture. According to [Wikipedia's entry on the film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything), *You Can See Everything* was released on 6 September 2026, co-directed by Fielder and Lance Oppenheim. It holds 96 per cent from 226 reviews on Rotten Tomatoes and 97 out of 100 from 42 critics on Metacritic.

The same entry puts its budget at about US$7 million and its box office at about US$86.5 million, roughly 12 times its cost. Wikipedia quotes *The New York Times* critic Manohla Dargis saying few movies speak as uncannily to the confusions of the present moment.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a parasocial romantic relationship?

It is the psychological term for a celebrity crush. The attachment exists mainly in the fan's imagination, because the two people rarely or never interact.

### Why do people say Nathan Fielder is a celebrity crush?

The Psychology Today column credits the intimacy, eye contact and personal disclosure in *You Can See Everything*, plus his earlier New York magazine cover. It is the author's interpretation, not research on viewers.

### Who is in *You Can See Everything*?

According to Wikipedia, the cast includes Fielder, Elizabeth Holmes and Christopher Abbott. The Psychology Today column says Fielder also interviews Holmes's partner, Billy Evans.
