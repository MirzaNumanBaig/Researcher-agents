**Quick summary**

- [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) faces allegations of inappropriate behaviour towards young women and girls, published after a three-year ITV News and Guardian investigation. He denies them.
- His literary agent has dropped him, and his publisher HarperCollins will no longer print his books, according to the ABC.
- The Metropolitan Police says it has received no related reports, but would carefully assess any that come in.
- UK ministers will meet TV, film and publishing leaders over the coming week about protections for women and children.

The David Walliams allegations have moved from the front pages to a wider argument about power in British entertainment. According to the [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-12/uk-confronts-banter-culture-after-david-walliams-russell-brand/107376026), the claims, which Walliams denies, appeared after a three-year investigation by ITV News and The Guardian, and landed in the same week comedian [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) was sentenced to more than 10 years in jail.

## What has been alleged, and what Walliams says

The ABC reports that multiple people have alleged he used his fame to groom teenage fans, sexually assault a 15-year-old girl and harass women at work. Walliams has denied all of the allegations.

In his response to the investigation, quoted by the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c6vg233enxjro), Walliams said he "realised that at times I behaved thoughtlessly" towards people he had casual sexual relationships with. He also said: "I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law." His lawyers told the investigators he denied grooming or acting inappropriately.

The BBC's review of the morning papers shows how prominently the story ran. The Guardian gave it a full front page, and The Sun, Daily Mail, Metro and others led with it.

## The fallout so far

The consequences for his career have been quick. The ABC says Walliams was dropped by his literary agent and that his back catalogue is being removed from online platforms. Bookstores are returning unsold copies to HarperCollins, which will no longer print any of his books. His titles have been translated into more than 50 languages and reportedly sold 50 million copies.

On the legal side, the BBC says The Daily Telegraph reported that [Scotland Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Yard) approached the journalists so potential victims could be told they may report criminal allegations. A Met spokesperson said it had not received any related reports, and any it did receive "will be carefully assessed".

## The injunction and the NDA debate

Walliams tried unsuccessfully to block publication in London's High Court. That attempt has drawn sharp comment. The BBC notes The Times argued that had the injunction succeeded, it would have made coverage conditional on a mental health assessment of its subject, while the i Paper said it should never have been pursued.

[Zelda Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Weinstein_sexual_abuse_cases), a former assistant to [Harvey Weinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Weinstein) who broke a non-disclosure agreement in 2017, told the ABC that legal tools used to protect reputation "are really only open and available to those who have a lot of money and power". She now leads Can't Buy My Silence UK, a campaign group that has pushed for legislative change.

British Culture Secretary [Lisa Nandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Nandy) called the allegations "the latest in a long line of reported abuses of power" in entertainment, the ABC reports. The government tightened NDA laws in 2025 and is now considering going further, possibly even banning them.

## A wider look at 'laddie' culture

Attention has also turned to the people around him. The ABC says The Times described a "circle of enablers", and old jokes are being re-read. At the 2004 British Comedy Awards, [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) quipped about Walliams while the room laughed. Ross has since said he was "horrified" by the allegations and by Brand's conviction, and that he was "genuinely unaware of their conduct" at the time. Our earlier report covers [Ross's statement](https://moduscarry.com/jonathan-ross-russell-brand-statement/).

Comedian [Shappi Khorsandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaparak_Khorsandi), who performed with Brand early in his career, told Channel 4 that a "laddie" culture of misogyny was widespread in the 1990s and 2000s and pushed many women out of comedy. Professor [Rosalind Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalind_Gill) of Goldsmiths, University of London, told the ABC the culture was hard to challenge because it was framed as "ironic" or "just banter".

## What happens next

Ministers are due to meet industry leaders over the coming week. No police investigation into Walliams has been confirmed by the Met, which says it has no related reports. Any further action will depend on what, if anything, is formally reported.

## Frequently asked questions

### Has David Walliams responded to the allegations?

Yes. He denies all of them. He has said he behaved thoughtlessly at times but that he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law.

### Is there a police investigation?

The Metropolitan Police says it has received no related reports and would carefully assess any it gets. The Telegraph, as cited by the BBC, said he faces a police investigation, but the Met has not confirmed that.

### What is happening to his books?

According to the ABC, HarperCollins will no longer print them, bookstores are returning unsold stock and his back catalogue is being removed from online platforms.
