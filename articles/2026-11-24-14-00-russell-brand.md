**Quick summary**

- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) prison income may stay near £2m a year, as company records show about that much for five years, The Telegraph reported on 23 November.
- Brand was sentenced this month to 10 years and six months for rape and sexual assault.
- YouTube says he has not been able to earn money there since 2023, but he still posts on Rumble, which has refused calls to demonetise him.
- Separately, reports say [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) is weighing offers to tell her story of their marriage.

Russell Brand prison income could stay near £2m a year, according to The Telegraph on 23 November. The paper says accounts from his production company appear to show that income has run at roughly that level for the past five years, across several revenue streams.

## What the company records show

The Telegraph [reports](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/2026/11/23/russell-brand-still-making-2m-a-year-while-in-prison/) that Pablo Diablo's Legitimate Business Firm Limited held £3.3m in assets when plans to close it were published at the end of last year. The company is still being wound down.

The paper says the records indicate Brand could be making large sums from sources other than YouTube. That matters because his YouTube channel is not monetised. A YouTube spokesperson told the paper he has been unable to earn there since 2023, when he was first suspended from its Partner Programme.

These are a newspaper's reading of company records, not a confirmed statement of what Brand will earn in custody.

## The sentence behind the story

Brand was sentenced earlier this month for raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others between 2004 and 2009. The Telegraph quotes Mr Justice Bennathan, who said the offences showed Brand's "casual, arrogant disregard of any woman's right to say no".

Brand, 51, moved to the United States in 2024 while under Metropolitan Police investigation, the paper notes.

## Rumble, YouTube and the merchandise

Pressure on the platforms has grown. The Telegraph says a justice minister was understood to have met YouTube representatives last week, and that YouTube was asked to disable his account. Meta had already taken down his Instagram and Facebook profiles.

Brand still publishes on Rumble, where his account has 2.2 million subscribers. The platform rejected parliamentary calls to demonetise him in 2023. His wife, Laura Brand, has been uploading videos there since the sentencing, and one featuring the couple drew almost 75,000 views in eight days.

His official website also sells "free Russell" T-shirts and hats for US$35 (about £27).

## The YouTube message and prison rules

Last week Laura Brand used his YouTube account to deliver a message in which he said he was not guilty of the offences. Downing Street called the message "appalling", and the Ministry of Justice opened an investigation into whether he had broken prison rules.

Prisoners may maintain their innocence but cannot make statements from jail that distress victims, The Telegraph notes. In a video on Sunday, Mrs Brand said she now understood the limits set by staff at HMP Littlehey and would "absolutely honour that". The author and illustrator, 39, said she would keep recording the Sunday Service podcast without her husband. ## Katy Perry reportedly weighs her options

Brand's name is also back in the headlines because of his first marriage. Yahoo News UK, summarising reports from the Daily Mail and The Mirror US, says Perry has been in "active discussions" about telling her story in a book or series, though none of this has been confirmed by Perry herself.

A Daily Mail source said she had received several offers over the years but would wait until after the trial. PR experts quoted by the outlet estimated a memoir might fetch several million dollars, though one suggested silence may serve her better. These are estimates and unnamed-source claims, not facts.

According to People, cited by Yahoo, they married in October 2010 and the marriage lasted 14 months.
