**Quick summary**

- The BBC removed a clip of a Russell Brand prison message from BBC Sounds after Radio 4's Today programme played it on Monday, saying it was aired "in error".
- Brand's wife, Laura, read the message on their YouTube show on Sunday. In it he said he was "not guilty" of the offences for which he was jailed.
- Downing Street said the message is "being urgently investigated" and that action is expected if prison rules were broken.
- Brand is serving 10 years and six months after being found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

The BBC has deleted a clip of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand)'s prison message from BBC Sounds, after its flagship Radio 4 Today programme played an excerpt on Monday 17 November. The broadcaster told [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/bbc-deletes-russell-brand-clip-after-playing-in-error-1237226478/) the clip was played "in error, due to miscommunication within the team". The original message is still on YouTube.

## What the message said

On Sunday, Laura Brand read words from her husband on the couple's Sunday Service show on YouTube. According to Deadline, he said he was "not guilty for the offences I have been jailed for" and confirmed he is held at HMP Littlehey, a prison for men convicted of sexual offences.

The message was written in the language of his Christian faith. He said the staff were "strong", "kind" and "dedicated", and that he was in no position to judge other prisoners. He closed by asking supporters to look after his wife and family.

Brand was found guilty at Southwark Crown Court of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, [the BBC reports](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cqvg27zyrd99o). He received a sentence of 10 years and six months. He has long positioned himself as a Christian influencer, with a large following that includes the US.

Our earlier coverage of the message itself is in [Russell Brand Says He Is Not Guilty in Prison Message](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-prison-message/).

## Downing Street and the prison rules

A Downing Street spokesperson called the message "clearly appalling" and said it was being urgently investigated. According to Deadline, the statement added that prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims, and that they cannot get around the rules by having someone else publish their words.

The BBC says Justice Secretary Alex Norris made a similar point, saying prisoners are rightly barred from distressing victims. For the wider picture, see [Russell Brand Prison Message Under Urgent Investigation](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-prison-message-investigation/).

## An accuser responds

Mia Latter, 37, spoke to Today after the broadcast. She alleges Brand raped her when she was 16 and he was 30. She was not a complainant in his criminal trial, and he denies her claims, the BBC notes.

Latter called it "appalling" to hear "this Christian message alongside absolute lies". She said the women who gave evidence "got their verdict".

She was one of six women who made allegations as part of a 2023 investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches. She has waived her anonymity to campaign for a staggered age of consent in the UK, which would make sexual relationships between 16 and 17-year-olds and adults illegal. She told the programme such a law could have stopped her relationship with Brand "dead in its tracks".

## Katy Perry's father is asked about Brand

The conviction has also revived attention on Brand's past marriage to [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry). According to a Yahoo Style Canada report, her father, pastor Keith Hudson, was asked about Brand on Sunday as he headed to a service in Montecito, California. He did not criticise his former son-in-law and replied that people are "better off talking about the Lord".

Perry has not commented publicly on the conviction, the same report says. The pair married in 2010, and Brand filed for divorce in December 2011. Our report [Katy Perry Silent as Russell Brand Jailed, Docuseries Reported](https://moduscarry.com/katy-perry-russell-brand-docuseries/) covers what has been said about her plans.

The Guardian also ran a [cartoon by Ben Jennings](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/picture/2026/nov/17/ben-jennings-russell-brand-prison-cartoon) on Brand in prison, a sign of how widely the story has spread.

## What happens next

The government investigation into how the message was produced and published is under way, and no outcome has been announced. It is not yet clear whether any prison rule was broken or who would face action if one was. The BBC has not said whether it will take further steps over the clip.
