**Quick summary**

- [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) told [Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly) he is "already working on a sequel" to *Wicker*.
- He called the joke follow-up *Corduroy* and said financing has been hard to find.
- The film stars Olivia Colman and Peter Dinklage, with Elizabeth Debicki also in the cast.
- Critics have praised the film, and his basket-covered character has become a meme.

Alexander Skarsgård has answered the internet's obsession with his wicker costume the only way he could: by announcing a sequel. In an interview with Entertainment Weekly, published on 11 November 2026, the actor said a follow-up to *Wicker* is under way and that it is called *Corduroy*.

## What Skarsgård said about Corduroy

The sequel is a joke, but he delivered it with a straight face. "We tried to find financing for it, but, for some reason, it's been hard," he said, adding that the team was pitching it around Hollywood this week, according to [Yahoo News UK's republication of the Entertainment Weekly interview](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/alexander-skarsg-rd-reveals-material-153000112.html).

The new lead would be "made out of corduroy", he explained. That character would be "delicate and soft" and, naturally, an "attentive lover". The gag riffs on his role as the Wicker Husband, a partner woven from wicker for a lonely woman in a seaside town.

## The film behind the memes

*Wicker* is directed by married couple Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson. It adapts the short story "The Wicker Husband". Olivia Colman plays a fisherwoman who is shunned by her town and asks the local basketmaker, played by Peter Dinklage, to make her a companion. The result is Skarsgård, covered in wicker.

Elizabeth Debicki also appears. Entertainment Weekly notes that Colman and Debicki are both alumni of *The Crown*. The film has drawn warm reviews. The outlet quotes critics who describe it as "a quirky fable with a lot of heart" and a "funny, sexy, feminist drama".

## Why the costume took off

The attention has spilled beyond reviews. Entertainment Weekly reports a wave of memes and online comments about the character's appeal. Some of that reached cinemas, where moviegoers posed with the big cardboard cutouts of the character, and a few even tried to take one home.

The cast has its own theories. Colman credits the character's "soul" rather than his looks. Debicki points out that he truly listens, and says Skarsgård was "a very nice canvas".

Skarsgård, who is best known for playing villains and for [*True Blood*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/True_Blood), says he used a mental trick to play a gentler type. "In order to get through the scenes," he said, "I closed my eyes and thought, 'What would Tom Hanks do?'" The reference is to [Tom Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hanks), an actor known for warm, likeable roles.

## What happens next

Nothing suggests *Corduroy* is a real project, and the source gives no production details beyond the joke. What is confirmed is the film's reception: reviews are positive and the character remains a talking point, which may keep *Wicker* in conversation for some time. No release dates or further Skarsgård projects were mentioned in the interview coverage we read.
