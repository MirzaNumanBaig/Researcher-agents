**Quick summary**

- [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and co-owner Rob Mac watched Wrexham draw 1-1 with Wolverhampton Wanderers at the Racecourse Ground on Saturday.
- Reynolds wrote on Instagram that the new Kop Stand feels like "a living, breathing promise of the greatest days yet to come".
- The Kop is approved for 7,750 fans and lifts the ground's capacity to 18,000, according to Sports Illustrated.
- The club hopes to open the stand for the 2027-28 season.

Ryan Reynolds used a Saturday visit to the Racecourse Ground to say the best is still ahead for Wrexham, pointing to the rising Kop Stand as proof. The actor and club co-owner was in the stands with Rob Mac for a 1-1 Championship draw against Wolves, a day after the club announced chief executive Michael Williamson was leaving.

## What Reynolds said about the Kop Stand

After the match, Reynolds posted photos on Instagram, including one of himself and Rob Mac in front of the new stand. "I can't believe how far along the new Kop stand is," he wrote, as quoted by [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/soccer/ryan-reynolds-exciting-wrexham-prediction-dramatic-week). He added that "some of the best days of my life have been spent in this stadium, standing next to" Rob Mac, and that Saturday was one of them.

[Football Ground Guide](https://footballgroundguide.com/news/ryan-reynolds-delivers-new-verdict-on-wrexhams-kop-stand-after-recent-racecourse-ground-visit.html) reports that the 49-year-old also shared a picture with former Wrexham captain James McClean, who returned to the Racecourse with Wolves. Another image showed the owners presenting long-serving employee Geraint Parry with a shirt marking 25 years at the club.

On the pitch, Adam Armstrong put Wolves ahead before Lewis O'Brien levelled for Wrexham.

## A rough fortnight behind the optimism

The upbeat message lands after a bumpy stretch. Sports Illustrated says the club was fined by the EFL for fielding two ineligible players, and manager Phil Parkinson publicly questioned the fighting spirit of some of his squad. Williamson's exit followed on Friday.

The pitch told a different story. Wrexham beat Middlesbrough 2-0 in midweek and then took a point from Wolves three days later. Parkinson said there was an "uplift" around the club with the owners present, according to the same report, and acting chief executive Shaun Harvey received a standing ovation from parts of the home crowd.

When the owners announced Williamson's departure earlier in the week, they said making the Wrexham community proud remained their most important job as custodians of the club. ## Where the Kop Stand build stands

According to Sports Illustrated, the metal framework and roof structure are already in place. The club hopes the first seats will be installed before Christmas, although plenty of work will remain after that.

The reported timetable runs as follows:

- Work on the red-brick facade is due to start this month.
- Most of the external work is expected to finish by February.
- The stand should look close to complete from outside by spring.
- Work on the internal facilities is due to begin in March.

The club recently won approval for 7,750 fans in the stand, which takes the Racecourse Ground to 18,000 for home matches. Sports Illustrated describes the project as costing $95 million and calls the stand "Premier League-ready". It would also meet UEFA Category 4 standards, which would let the ground host full men's internationals. Wrexham hopes to open it at the start of the 2027-28 season, in either the Championship or the Premier League.
