**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) has signed the "Protect the Lungs of the City" open letter against removing 377 trees from Adelaide's Park Lands.
- The trees are due to make way for a MotoGP street circuit, with felling scheduled to start on Tuesday 1 December.
- Nearly 14,000 people had signed within two weeks, and the letter has been tabled in the state and federal parliaments, according to campaign material.
- The South Australian government says it will plant ten trees elsewhere for each one removed.

Cate Blanchett has added her name to a campaign trying to stop the removal of hundreds of trees from Adelaide's heritage-listed Park Lands, days before clearing for a MotoGP circuit is due to begin. The Oscar winner, who grew up in Melbourne, is among the signatories reported on 27 November 2026.

## What the campaign is asking for

The "Protect the Lungs of the City" open letter calls on the South Australian government to halt the removal of 377 trees and to commit to protecting the Park Lands and other green spaces. The [Daily Mail](https://www.dailymail.com/sport/othersports/article-16325487/cate-blanchett-adelaide-motogp-trees-parklands-backlash.html) reported that the letter asks for the Park Lands to be "fully protected" from development.

A campaign statement distributed through [Medianet's News Hub](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2026/11/lungs-of-the-city-campaign-backed-by-sia-cate-blanchett-and-paul-kelly-as-adelaide-park-lands-tree-removal-looms/184404/) says nearly 14,000 people signed within two weeks. It names Sia, Paul Kelly, [Peter Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Garrett), [Dev Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev_Patel), [Eric Idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Idle) and [Magda Szubanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magda_Szubanski) alongside Blanchett. The statement describes the campaign as uniting arts, music, cultural and sporting communities, and gives the campaign website as lungsofthecity.com.au.

Because it is a campaign release, the figures are the organisers' own. The Medianet text lists co-leads Naomi Keyte and Bea Jeavons as available for interviews until 1 December.

## The MotoGP plan

The trees stand in the eastern Park Lands, where the state government plans to host the Australian Motorcycle Grand Prix from November 2027, moving it from Phillip Island. The Daily Mail put the cost of the circuit at $96 million and said the government has pledged to plant ten trees at another location for every tree removed.

Streamline Feed, a smaller outlet, gave more detail. It reported that 8,895 trees stand inside the four-kilometre circuit precinct, so about 95 per cent would stay, and that 42 of the 377 trees are classed as significant. It also said 88 are marked for transplanting. We could not confirm those numbers in a second source, so treat them as that outlet's reporting.

## Court ruling and the government's position

According to Streamline Feed, the South Australian Supreme Court dismissed a challenge from the South East City Residents Association on 23 November. The group had argued that the Motor Sport Act 1984 did not allow parkland trees to be felled. The outlet says the judge found the law lets the Motor Sport Board remove trees where reasonably necessary for its functions.

The same report says Premier Peter Malinauskas welcomed the result and argues that many of the trees are dead, invasive or unhealthy, and that a $15 million Nature Positive Package will deliver a net gain for biodiversity. It adds that the residents' group is taking legal advice about an appeal. Campaigners quoted there argue young replacement trees take decades to match the shade and habitat of mature ones, and say they do not oppose MotoGP itself.

## Why Blanchett's name is drawing debate

The Daily Mail headline framed her involvement as divisive, quoting a critic who asked when she had last been in Adelaide. The paper's own reporting, as far as we could read it, confirms she was born and raised in Melbourne. The Medianet statement gives no comment from Blanchett beyond her signature.

## What happens next

Felling is scheduled to begin on Tuesday 1 December, straight after the Adelaide Grand Final, according to the campaign. The next test is whether any appeal or stay is lodged before then. Streamline Feed says the race itself is scheduled for 12 to 14 November 2027.
