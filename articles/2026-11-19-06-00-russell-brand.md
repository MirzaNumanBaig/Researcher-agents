**Quick summary**

- A message from jailed comedian [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) was read out on his wife Laura's YouTube show, and UK authorities are now investigating it.
- In it, he said he is "not guilty of the offences" he was jailed for, according to Mediaweek.
- Justice Secretary Alex Norris called the broadcast "completely unacceptable" and promised action if rules were broken.
- Brand is serving a sentence of 10½ years for one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.
- Refuge and the Victims' Commissioner for London say such public statements add to survivors' pain.

A statement from Russell Brand's prison cell, read aloud on his wife's online show, has set off an urgent government inquiry in Britain. The comedian, who is serving a 10½-year sentence, said he is "not guilty of the offences" he was jailed for, [Mediaweek reported](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/completely-unacceptable-video-message-from-russell-brand-urgently-investigated) on Tuesday. Ministers say prisoners may not use others to get around the rules on contact with the public.

## What was said in the video

According to Mediaweek, Laura Brand read the message on the couple's *Sunday Service* show on YouTube. In the episode, released on a Sunday, she sat with a Bible and a lit candle beside her. She described the prison staff as "strong", "kind" and "dedicated", and asked viewers to support his "treasured wife and family". She also said she had spoken to him and that he was "OK" and praying.

The part that drew the sharpest reaction was his claim of innocence, made after a jury convicted him earlier this month.

## How the government responded

Justice Secretary Alex Norris wrote on X that prisoners are rightly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims. He said the matter was being "urgently investigated" and that he expects action if rules were broken, according to Mediaweek.

The prime minister's official spokesman said prisoners are strictly banned from such statements, and that speaking to the media is allowed only in exceptional circumstances. A separate line from the Justice Secretary's post makes the intent clear: prisoners cannot get around the rules by having someone else publish their comments.

The inquiry will decide whether a message passed to a family member and then published counts as a breach. That question has not yet been answered publicly.

## Survivors' groups push back

Andrea Simon, the Victims' Commissioner for London, told BBC Radio 4's *Today* programme that the claims will "certainly retraumatise" his victims and other survivors who have to hear them. She said victims had already described at the sentencing hearing how his denials and two decades of media fame added to their trauma.

The domestic abuse charity [Refuge](https://refuge.org.uk/news/refuge-responds-to-the-trial-of-russell-brand/) described the guilty verdicts as long overdue justice. It also noted that not every survivor in the trial received a guilty verdict, and said every survivor deserves to be believed regardless of the outcome. Refuge cited Office for National Statistics data showing 9.6% of adults, about one in ten, experienced domestic abuse in the past year. It called for mandatory training for justice professionals and better funding for specialist services.

## Comedy's reckoning continues

Comedians have also been reflecting on the case. In [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/stage/2026/11/17/irish-comedians-react-to-russell-brand-sentencing-he-was-a-very-valuable-asset-thats-why-he-was-protected/), Dublin comedian David O'Doherty said he shared an Edinburgh venue with Brand in 2006 and last saw him in 2009, when they worked on a TV pilot that never went ahead. He said the "infrastructure" around Brand had changed by then, and that the stars of show business can be shielded by a level of mollycoddling that now needs tackling.

[Alison Spittle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Spittle), an Irish comedian based in London, said she first heard allegations from other comics' stand-up routines in 2018. Both she and O'Doherty rejected the idea that this is a uniquely British problem.

In City Journal, writer Christopher F. Rufo, who filmed Brand in 2014, offered a more contested opinion. He argued the case reflects broader confusion about sexual ethics. That is a commentary view, and it is not shared by the survivors' groups quoted above.

## What happens next

Authorities have not said how long the inquiry will take or what action could follow. Norris said he expects action "if he is found to have broken the rules". Whether the *Sunday Service* episode stays online is also unclear from the reports.
