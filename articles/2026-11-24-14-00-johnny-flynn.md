**Quick summary**

- [Johnny Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Flynn) and his sister Lillie Flynn have made an album called Beam Cottage.
- A review published on 23 November 2026 describes it as a set of songs anchored to their childhood and a family home they shared briefly with their father.
- The closing track, Sleepy Head, was written by their father, the actor [Eric Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Flynn).
- The record mixes traditional songs with a Paul Simon cover, according to the reviewer.

Johnny Flynn's latest music project is a family one. He and his sister Lillie Flynn have recorded Beam Cottage, an album that, according to a [review by Bernard Zuel](https://www.bernardzuel.net/post/radie-peat-no-human-tongue-can-tell-johnny-flynn-and-lillie-flynn-beam-cottage-review) published on 23 November 2026, looks back to the pair's childhood and the house the album is named after.

## What Beam Cottage is about

Zuel writes that the songs tie the siblings to their early years and to a home they shared too briefly with their father, Eric Flynn. Lillie is described as a member of her brother's wider band, The Sussex Wit.

The review says the record closes with Sleepy Head, a song by Eric Flynn, delivered with what the critic calls a sweet jauntiness. It is a small detail, but it gives the album a personal frame: the last thing a listener hears comes from the siblings' father.

## The sound: lightness with weight underneath

Zuel's central point is about tone. He argues that the Flynns bring a lightness of touch to the material, and that this keeps the songs from tipping into either heaviness or forced cheer. The emotional foundations of the songs, he says, do not lean towards humour, but they benefit from small deflections. In brighter songs the same approach keeps them steady rather than overdone.

Two tracks get specific mention. Paul Simon's The Boy in the Bubble is, in the reviewer's early listening, the standout. He says the siblings' vocals pull back into the murk, which lets the wistfulness settle into the song's disjointed emotions. On Tell Old Bill, a traditional song, Lillie takes the lead while Johnny hums behind her, adding what Zuel describes as both earnestness and sprinkles of joy. Idumea, another traditional, is also covered.

Zuel also connects the album to Flynn's screen work. He notes that Flynn wrote the theme tune to Mackenzie Crook's Detectorists, and that his song The Ghost of O'Donahue is revived on this record. The same reviewer had recently watched Flynn as the antagonist in the second season of MobLand and in a brief role as the Everest climber George Mallory in the film Tenzing.

## A shared review with Radie Peat

The piece is a double review. Zuel pairs Beam Cottage with the solo album No Human Tongue Can Tell by Irish singer Radie Peat, who is also part of the band Lankum. He calls both records proof that folk music resists limits of age, instrument or place. That framing is the reviewer's, but it places the Flynns' album in a wider run of folk releases this season.

For readers who know Flynn mostly from television, the album is a reminder of where he started. He is an actor and a musician, and his screen roles have often sat alongside his songwriting.
