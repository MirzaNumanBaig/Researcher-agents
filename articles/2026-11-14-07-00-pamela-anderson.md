**Quick summary**

- [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson) and her son Dylan Jagger Lee released "Where Does the Garden Go?", the first holiday film for her skincare brand Sonsie, on Friday 13 November, according to WWD.
- The 83-second stop-motion and live-action film took eight months and used 11 handmade puppets and 1,668 photographed frames, per People.
- It was made without AI. A miniature puppet of Anderson appears alongside her dog, Zeus.
- The film launched alongside the Sonsie Holiday Shop, with gift sets built from the brand's five core products.

Pamela Anderson has turned her Vancouver Island garden into a miniature paper world for Sonsie's first holiday campaign. The 83-second film, "Where Does the Garden Go?", went live on 13 November and was made entirely by hand over eight months, [People reports](https://people.com/pamela-anderson-turns-garden-into-stop-motion-for-sonsies-holiday-campaign-exclusive-12249466). The Pamela Anderson Sonsie holiday film is also a family project, led by her son Dylan Jagger Lee.

## What the film shows

The story asks a simple question: where does the garden go when winter arrives? According to People, the flowers fade, but squirrels, rabbits and foxes slip away to a greenhouse and set up a gift-wrapping workshop.

[ADWEEK](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/pamela-anderson-on-how-skincare-brand-sonsies-first-holiday-ad-reflects-her-world/) describes the opening: Anderson unfolds a book to reveal a paper rose, and the film moves into a papier-mâché wonderland. She then steps into the miniature world herself. Her dog, Zeus, carries her across the night sky to deliver Sonsie gifts.

The film will run on social media. It arrived alongside the Sonsie Holiday Shop, which [WWD](https://wwd.com/pop-culture/celebrity-news/pamela-anderson-son-dylan-sonsie-holiday-campaign-1239414034/) says includes skincare gift sets from a Hydration Duo to the Sonsie Bundle, which holds all five of the brand's essentials.

## The numbers behind 83 seconds

The scale of the work is the story here. [NewBeauty](https://www.newbeauty.com/view/pamela-anderson-holiday-interview-2026) says the team of more than 20 people made 11 puppets from tissue paper and wire, and photographed 1,668 frames, each one posed by hand. ADWEEK counts 23 creators across continents.

The film was animated at 12 frames per second, so every second on screen needed 12 separate adjustments. Over 10 days of shooting, artist Alix Bortoli animated 139 seconds of footage before it was cut down, according to NewBeauty. That is close to 170 hand-posed frames a day.

Some details stand out:

- The treehouse workshop set took three weeks to build.
- A single shot in the workshop scene took nearly four days.
- The miniature Anderson took four days to make, and the miniature Zeus took two.
- Bortoli, who is based in Paris, flew to Vancouver Island with the puppets, per WWD.

## Stepping into the set

Mixing live action with stop-motion was the hardest part. To let Anderson walk into the tiny world, the team rebuilt parts of it at full size in a real garden: a grass floor, a tree painted on wood and a greenhouse made from the same wood, raffia roof and clear windows as its miniature twin.

Lee told ADWEEK the blend was "much harder than I thought", because the camera had to move about an inch at a time to keep the perspective right. NewBeauty adds that every gift was made twice, once in miniature and once life-size, and that Anderson helped wrap the large ones.

The puppet version of Anderson wears a replica of her real sweater. "Quirkiness is part of my charm," she told NewBeauty.

## A family business and a makeup-free brand

Anderson took majority ownership of Sonsie in 2024, according to ADWEEK. Her sons Brandon Thomas Lee and Dylan Jagger Lee are co-founders, handling marketing and brand strategy. Lee came up with the concept and co-directed the film.

Anderson told WWD she gave up makeup in 2023 and that the choice was not meant as a statement. "My skin is better now than it was when I was in my 30s," she said. She also gave People a reason for the handmade approach: "There's nothing that can compare to human hands and human beings."

The garden is the brand's central metaphor. Lee told People that beauty, like a garden, "never ever truly goes away". It also fits Anderson's own life: [Hello!](https://www.hellomagazine.com/us/934918/pamela-anderson-winter-wonderland-new-update-home-vancouver-island/) reports she keeps a small winter garden of cabbages, kale, Swiss chard and herbs. ## What happens next

The film is live on Sonsie's social channels, and the Holiday Shop is open. Anderson told People she will spend the colder months "dreaming up the next garden" and planning bulbs for spring. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is "Where Does the Garden Go?"

It is Sonsie's first holiday campaign film: 83 seconds of stop-motion and live action starring a puppet Anderson and then the real one. It was released on 13 November, according to WWD.

### Was the Sonsie holiday film made with AI?

No. People and WWD both say it was made entirely by hand, without AI.

### Who made the film with Pamela Anderson?

Her son Dylan Jagger Lee, Sonsie's chief brand officer, produced it and co-directed it with Paris-based artist Alix Bortoli, who also animated it and built the sets and puppets.

### How long did it take to make?

Eight months, per People, NewBeauty and ADWEEK.
