**Quick summary**

- Hugh Jackman announced on social media that *The Prestige* returns to cinemas on 29 January 2027.
- The re-release marks the film's 20th anniversary.
- It will be available in 70mm and IMAX 2D for the first time, according to Jackman's post.
- Jackman's post tagged Disney Studios. No Australian release details have been reported yet.

Hugh Jackman has announced that Christopher Nolan's *The Prestige* will return to cinemas on 29 January 2027, with 70mm and IMAX 2D screenings offered for the first time. The news came in a social media post on 22 October 2026, as reported by [Social News XYZ](https://www.socialnews.xyz/2026/10/22/hugh-jackman-announces-the-prestige-return-to-theatres-for-its-twentieth-anniversary-in-imax-and-70mm/).

## What Jackman said

According to Social News XYZ, Jackman wrote that the film is "returning to theaters on January 29th, 2027" in celebration of its 20th anniversary, and that it will be available in 70mm and IMAX 2D for the first time. His post tagged Disney Studios.

The outlet's report is the only source for the announcement so far. It does not list cinemas, ticket details or whether the dates apply outside the United States, so Australian audiences should wait for local confirmation before planning around the date.

## The film behind the re-release

Released in 2006, *The Prestige* was Nolan's fifth feature. Jackman plays Robert Angier, one of two rival magicians in the late 19th century. Christian Bale plays the other, Alfred Borden. A shared tragedy turns their professional competition into a dangerous personal obsession, and each man tries to outdo the other with the ultimate illusion.

The supporting cast includes Michael Caine, Scarlett Johansson, Rebecca Hall and Andy Serkis. David Bowie appears as the inventor Nikola Tesla, whose scientific work becomes part of the plot. Wally Pfister was the cinematographer and David Julyan composed the score.

## Why the formats matter

The headline detail is the presentation. Social News XYZ notes that this is the first time the film will be available in 70mm and in IMAX 2D. Those are premium large-format options, and they suit a story built around spectacle and misdirection.

The report also describes the film as a favourite for repeat viewing. Its layered plot rewards a second look once the audience knows how the pieces fit together, which is the usual argument for revisiting it on a bigger screen.
