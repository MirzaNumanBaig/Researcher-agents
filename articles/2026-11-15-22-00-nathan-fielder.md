**Quick summary**

- Daily Sabah published a review of *You Can See Everything* on 15 November 2026, calling the film a "maze" of story-telling.
- The film is co-directed by Lance Oppenheim and [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder), and features Elizabeth Holmes.
- Wikipedia lists a 96% Rotten Tomatoes score from 226 reviews and 97/100 on Metacritic.
- The same page puts its box office at about US$86.5 million against a US$7 million budget.

A new review of Nathan Fielder's *You Can See Everything* argues that the film's real surprises sit in how it is told, not in what it reveals about its subject. Turkish outlet [Daily Sabah](https://www.dailysabah.com/arts/cinema/maze-of-story-telling-nathan-fielders-you-can-see-everything) ran the piece on 15 November 2026, and its reviewer says the film keeps shifting who is narrating.

## What the Daily Sabah review says

The reviewer admits to coming in with patchy background. They had seen the trailer, the online clips imitating Elizabeth Holmes's "why would I deceive you" line, and social media explainers on the Theranos scandal. They had not seen the 2022 series about Holmes, and say they had never heard of Fielder before.

That outsider view shapes the review. According to Daily Sabah, the film is "a documentary of sorts", and the writer questions whether the idea of a spoiler even applies to a story where the outcome is public record: Holmes lied to investors and went to jail. For this critic, the spoilers are in the structure, which is why the review stays vague about the film's various narrative layers.

The closest comparison offered is Kaouther Ben Hania's *Four Daughters* (2023), in which family members tell their story, watch actors perform it and sometimes step in to play themselves. The reviewer sees similar layers in Fielder's film and says describing them would be the biggest spoiler of all.

## Amanda Seyfried and the jail phone calls

One scene gets particular attention. [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried), who played Holmes in the 2022 series, plays Holmes speaking to Fielder by phone from jail. The reviewer says this is because Fielder apparently cannot legally show the real footage of those conversations. That is the reviewer's reading, and Daily Sabah does not cite a statement from the filmmakers.

The review also questions how much of the opening act can be trusted. It notes that the "holiday home" where Fielder stays with Holmes and her partner is a rental, and that the household appears to have no help despite having two children. For the writer, that was enough to doubt the film was a straight documentary.

In the second half, the review says, Fielder invites Seyfried to the same house and the two go over the footage from the first half. It describes this as a test case for the difference between acting and being yourself.

## How the film has performed

The review is one voice among many. Wikipedia's entry on the film, which first screened in September 2026, lists a 96% Rotten Tomatoes score from 226 reviews and a Metacritic score of 97 out of 100 from 42 critics. It also reports that PostTrak audiences gave the film a 98% positive score, with 91% saying they would definitely recommend it.

The critics' consensus on Rotten Tomatoes, as quoted there, describes Fielder's commitment to the bit as pushed to its limit in what it calls a Rorschach test of Holmes's psyche. *The New York Times* critic Manohla Dargis, quoted on the same page, wrote that few movies speak as uncannily to the confusions of the present moment.

The commercial numbers are unusual for a film of this kind. Wikipedia lists a budget of US$7 million and a box office of US$86.5 million, roughly 12 times the cost. Those figures come from Wikipedia and Wikidata and are not confirmed in the Daily Sabah review.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who directed You Can See Everything?
Wikipedia lists Lance Oppenheim and Nathan Fielder as the directors. Fielder also appears in the film alongside Elizabeth Holmes and Christopher Abbott.

### Is You Can See Everything a documentary?
Daily Sabah calls it a documentary "of sorts" and says it mixes narrative levels, including a scene played by Amanda Seyfried. The review argues it plays with the form rather than simply informing the viewer.

### What is the film's critical score?
Wikipedia lists 96% on Rotten Tomatoes from 226 reviews and 97/100 on Metacritic from 42 critics.
