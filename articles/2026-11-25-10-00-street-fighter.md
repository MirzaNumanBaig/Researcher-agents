**Quick summary**

- The new Street Fighter film reaches digital storefronts such as Amazon Video and iTunes on 1 December, according to FandomWire.
- Mortal Kombat II joins the Prime Video streaming catalogue on 21 December, months after its premium digital debut.
- FandomWire reports Street Fighter has made about US$102 million worldwide on an US$82 million budget.
- Mortal Kombat II has made about US$129 million on an US$80 million budget, the biggest total for a fighting game film, per the same report.

The new Street Fighter film is heading home. It becomes available on digital storefronts on 1 December, and three weeks later its old rival Mortal Kombat II lands on Prime Video, according to [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/prime-video-street-fighter-mortal-kombat-december/). For anyone who missed either film in cinemas, December offers the two franchises back to back.

Everything we have published on the film sits on our [Street Fighter tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/street-fighter/).

## Two release dates, two different routes

The two films are not arriving the same way. Street Fighter is a Paramount release, and its 1 December date is for digital purchase and rental through stores such as Amazon Video and iTunes. That is not the same as streaming for free with a subscription.

Mortal Kombat II is a different case. FandomWire says the Warner Bros. sequel reached premium digital platforms months ago, so 21 December marks its arrival in the Prime Video streaming catalogue, where subscribers can watch without a separate rental fee. The film was directed by [Simon McQuoid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_McQuoid) and stars [Karl Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Urban) as Johnny Cage. It opened on 8 May.

## How the box office compares

FandomWire reports that Street Fighter opened in theatres on 16 October 2026 and took first place at its first weekend, then cooled quickly. Its worldwide total is put at US$102 million against an US$82 million budget, citing The Numbers. Wikipedia's entry for the film gives a slightly lower gross of about US$100.8 million, and lists Kitao Sakurai as director with Andrew Koji, Noah Centineo, Callina Liang, Cody Rhodes and Orville Peck in the cast.

Mortal Kombat II took US$129 million worldwide on an US$80 million budget, according to FandomWire, which cites Box Office Mojo and calls it the highest-grossing fighting game film so far.

Put side by side, the numbers are close. Street Fighter earned roughly 1.2 times its budget. Mortal Kombat II earned about 1.6 times its own. Both clear their costs on paper, but neither is the kind of result a studio builds a franchise plan around without a second look.

## What critics made of them

Reviews are where the two films separate more clearly. FandomWire gives Street Fighter a Rotten Tomatoes score of 46%, while Wikipedia's film page shows 42% on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) and 43 out of 100 on Metacritic from 28 critics. The numbers differ because review scores keep moving as more critics weigh in. The critics' consensus quoted there says the film shows affection for the games and has plenty of visual style, but suffers from muddled storytelling and juvenile humour.

Mortal Kombat II sits at 63% on Rotten Tomatoes, which FandomWire describes as more divided. That is a clearer pass than Street Fighter managed, though not a glowing one.

## Why the home market matters here

FandomWire argues that the home release is a second chance for both films. Action movies built around choreographed fights, the outlet notes, often gain from being paused, rewound and shared with friends. That is opinion rather than data, and nothing in the report shows how either film has performed at home so far.

There is also a corporate angle. FandomWire says both franchises now sit under the same Skydance Group, which has fuelled talk of a crossover. It adds that members of the Street Fighter cast told ComicBook.com which Mortal Kombat characters they would most like to face. No crossover has been announced, and we have not seen any confirmation from the studios.

## What happens next

The dates to note are 1 December for Street Fighter and 21 December for Mortal Kombat II. Because the films arrive three weeks apart, many viewers will be able to watch them one after the other and judge for themselves. Whether strong home viewing changes any studio plans for sequels or spin-offs is something neither company has said. Pricing and Australian availability were not given in the report, so check your local store closer to the date.

## Frequently asked questions

### When can I watch Street Fighter at home?

Street Fighter is due on digital storefronts such as Amazon Video and iTunes on 1 December, according to FandomWire. It is a purchase or rental release, not a subscription streaming date.

### When does Mortal Kombat II come to Prime Video?

Mortal Kombat II joins the Prime Video streaming catalogue on 21 December. It was already available on premium digital platforms before that.

### Is there a Street Fighter and Mortal Kombat crossover film?

No crossover has been announced. FandomWire notes that both franchises now fall under the Skydance Group, which has prompted speculation, and that cast members have talked about which characters they would fight.

### Did Street Fighter make money?

On the reported figures, it grossed about US$102 million worldwide against an US$82 million budget, which is above cost but modest for a studio release.
