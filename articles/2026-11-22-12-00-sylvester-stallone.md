**Quick summary**

- [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) posted an Instagram video on Saturday, 21 November, to mark 50 years since Rocky premiered in New York City.
- He thanked fans and wrote that the character "belongs to all of YOU now", according to Complex.
- Philadelphia's Rocky Season festivities continue until 3 December, officially recognised as Rocky Day in the city.
- The anniversary arrives alongside I Play Rocky, a Peter Farrelly film about how the original movie got made.

[Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) has marked 50 years of Rocky with a thank-you to the fans who turned a small boxing drama into a global franchise. In a video shared on Instagram on Saturday, 21 November, the actor said he never expected the day to arrive, and credited audiences for keeping the story alive. It is the clearest sign yet of how much the Rocky 50th anniversary means to the man who wrote it.

## What Stallone told fans

According to [Complex](https://www.complex.com/pop-culture/a/markelibert/sylvester-stallone-rocky-50th-anniversary-instagram), Stallone opened by saying he could not believe it had been 50 years since the "birth of Rocky". He described the original plan as modest. "I just wanted to make a little movie about the things that we're going through," he said, adding that the film had clearly resonated with people far beyond what he imagined.

He also thanked fans on behalf of his family and finished with a line suited to the character: "remember to always keep punching." In the caption he went further, writing that [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa) "may have started with me" but now belongs to everyone who has taken him to heart.

The tone is a long way from the usual studio anniversary release. There is no trailer or merchandise push in the report, just a personal message from an actor who is 80 and has spoken recently about his late-career run on [Tulsa King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa_King).

## How a three-day script became a Best Picture winner

The numbers behind the anniversary explain the affection. Complex, citing TIME, reports that Stallone wrote the first draft in roughly three and a half days. He then turned down offers of up to US$360,000 for the script because he insisted on playing the lead himself.

That gamble paid off. Rocky was made for under US$1 million, according to Complex, while DW puts the budget at just over US$1 million. Either way, it grossed about US$225 million worldwide and became the highest-earning film of 1976. For scale, that is more than 200 times what it cost to make.

The film earned 10 Oscar nominations and won three, including Best Picture. DW adds that Stallone personally received two nominations, for best actor and best original screenplay. The story spawned a franchise that continues through the Creed films.

## Why the film still lands

DW's retrospective points to the mix that kept audiences coming back. Rocky is a sports story, but it is also a love story: the aspiring boxer's courtship of pet store worker Adrian, played by Talia Shire, carries as much weight as any round in the ring. The training run up the Philadelphia Museum of Art steps, set to Bill Conti's "Gonna Fly Now", is now so famous that visitors copy it, and the track is a staple at sporting events.

Stallone has also spoken about the craft behind the final fight. He told The New York Times, as quoted by DW, that what looked like wild punching was an exact ballet, with 14 pages of choreography.

## Philadelphia celebrates, and a new film arrives

Philadelphia has leaned into the milestone. Complex reports that the city's Rocky Run on 14 November drew roughly 40,000 participants, and the wider Rocky Season runs until 3 December, officially Rocky Day in Philadelphia.

The anniversary also coincides with I Play Rocky, directed by Peter Farrelly, which follows Stallone's fight to get the original film made. [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) plays Stallone. Stallone was not officially involved in the production, but Complex reports he endorsed Ippolito's performance after watching the audition tape. "This kid didn't impersonate me. He became me," Stallone said, according to Farrelly.

## What happens next

The Philadelphia celebrations run through 3 December. Complex also notes that Tulsa King is heading towards its fourth season, with Stallone describing acting as "like breathing". No new Rocky film has been announced in the sources we read, and we are not reporting any.
