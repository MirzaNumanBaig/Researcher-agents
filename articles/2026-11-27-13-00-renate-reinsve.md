**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) was close to leaving acting in 2020 and had planned to move to Italy to qualify as a carpenter, according to Far Out Magazine.
- The day after she settled on that plan, director Joachim Trier phoned to say he was writing a script with her in mind.
- That script became The Worst Person in the World, which won her Best Actress at the Cannes Film Festival.
- She has since been nominated for an Academy Award for Best Actress in 2026 for [Sentimental Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentimental_Value), per Contentory.

Renate Reinsve, the Norwegian actress now nominated for an Oscar, was one phone call away from swapping the stage for a workshop. [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/renate-reinsve-almost-quit-acting-for-carpentry-think-im-done/) reported on 27 November that in 2020 she had decided she was finished with acting and was preparing to train as a carpenter in Italy.

## Why she wanted out

Reinsve spent her twenties in Norway's theatre scene, working on experimental and demanding productions while picking up small film and TV parts. By the time she was 32, Far Out says, she felt she had reached the end of the road on stage, and the screen roles on offer felt superficial.

She has spoken about the toll in interviews. Far Out quotes her telling The Guardian in 2025 that she had done the work for many years, that it was hard and physical, and that she had worked with many great directors. "I think I'm done," she recalled thinking.

Carpentry was not a random escape route. Far Out notes her grandfather owned a hardware store in Oslo, and relatives all worked there at some point. Contentory also mentions a family hardware business in her background. She told an interviewer in 2024 that she had just finished renovating her house and loved it, which made building things for a living feel appealing.

## The call that changed the plan

The plan was already taking shape: Italy, a professional carpentry qualification, a new life. Then, the very next day by Far Out's account, Joachim Trier rang. The director, known for Louder Than Bombs, told her he was writing a script with her in mind.

"I had no hesitation," she said, adding that she loved everything he had made and called the timing a very strange coincidence.

The script became The Worst Person in the World, a darkly funny, anti-romantic romantic comedy. Contentory describes the 2021 film as the breakthrough in which she played Julie, a young woman working through relationships, career choices and the uncertainty of adulthood. Her performance won the Best Actress award at Cannes. "It changed my life completely," she told the interviewer quoted by Far Out.

## A familiar kind of turnaround

Far Out draws a comparison with [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford), who turned to carpentry in the late 1960s after struggling to find good roles. The magazine says [George Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lucas) cast him in [American Graffiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Graffiti), and [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola) later gave him a small part in The Conversation, before [Star Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars) made him a star. The parallel is the magazine's own framing, but the pattern of a trade career abandoned at the last moment is hard to miss.

## Where her career stands now

Contentory lists her recent credits as Armand, A Different Man, Presumed Innocent and Sentimental Value, the last of which brought the 2026 Academy Award nomination for Best Actress. Far Out also names the horror hit Backrooms among her best-known recent roles.

Her path began long before the breakthrough. According to Contentory, she was born on 24 November 1987 in Solbergelva, near Drammen, and graduated from the Oslo National Academy of the Arts in 2013. She then joined Trøndelag Theatre in Trondheim and won a Hedda Award for Best Supporting Actress in 2014 for The Visit. Her screen debut was a small part in Trier's Oslo, August 31st, released in 2011. Contentory also notes she is a mother who keeps her private life largely out of the press.

The long run-up matters to how her story reads. The Cannes win did not appear from nowhere; it followed more than a decade of stage work and modest screen credits, and nearly followed a career change instead.
