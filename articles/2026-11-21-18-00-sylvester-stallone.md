**Quick summary**

- Variety published a fact-versus-fiction look at *I Play Rocky* on 20 November 2026, as the original *Rocky* marks 50 years since its premiere.
- The film follows [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) as an unknown writer who insists on playing [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa) himself.
- According to Variety, the film hits most production events with some accuracy and polishes others for storytelling.
- The disputed points include the producers' offer, the three-day script and the sale of Stallone's dog, Butkus.

## What I Play Rocky gets right and bends

*I Play Rocky*, now in cinemas, tells how [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) went from unknown actor to writer and star of a film that won the best picture Oscar. [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) plays him. Variety's [fact-versus-fiction piece](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/i-play-rocky-fact-vs-fiction-butkus-dog-stallone-fee-1236952451/) says the film "effectively toes the line" between accurate events and added drama.

The story arrives half a century after the original *Rocky* premiered. We have covered the film from several angles, including [how Stallone responded to Ippolito's performance](https://moduscarry.com/sylvester-stallone-i-play-rocky/) and [early reviews of the biopic](https://moduscarry.com/i-play-rocky-review-stallone-biopic/).

## The $250,000 offer

The film shows Stallone sitting across from producers [Robert Chartoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Chartoff) and Irwin Winkler as they offer more and more money for him to give up the lead. Variety says the real story was more complicated. It reports that United Artists reportedly offered up to $250,000 if he would step aside, and that Stallone held his ground.

The studio, according to Variety, would have preferred [James Caan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Caan), [Nick Nolte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Nolte) or Ryan O'Neal. Variety also notes that the film makes Stallone look like a bigger unknown than he was at the time. Whether he ever spoke the title line to the producers is, in the outlet's words, unclear, because no one seems able to recall.

## The agents' dispute

The film leaves out the agents who usually shape such deals. Chris Rumar, son of early Stallone agent Craig Rumar, told Variety that his father and agent partner Larry Kubik were central to getting the film made. He said Stallone has been "on a tour" about inaccuracies in how *Rocky* came together, and that nobody challenges him in interviews.

Kubik said on the "Sexy Boomer" podcast that they introduced Stallone to producer Gene Kirkwood, who had a deal with Chartoff and Winkler. Their first attempt was Stallone's script *Hell's Kitchen*, which later became [Paradise Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Alley). Stallone had already sold it elsewhere, so he began a new screenplay. These are one side's recollections, reported by Variety.

## The 72-hour script

The film shows Stallone writing for 72 hours with his wife typing his handwritten pages. Variety says that part is broadly right. But he sketched only about the first third of the story in that stretch, then made dozens of revisions before it was ready to shoot.

## Butkus the dog

Stallone has often said he sold his bull mastiff Butkus for about $40 when he was broke, then paid thousands to buy him back so the dog could appear in the film. The price he paid varies in retellings from $1,500 to $15,000. Variety says Stallone has named a man called "Little Jimmy" as the buyer. Boxing trainer Jimmy Gambina, who worked on *Rocky*, recalls it differently, according to Variety. The excerpt available to us ends before his account.

## Casting the lead

Separately, a Deadline video shared on Facebook has director [Peter Farrelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrelly) discussing his choice of Ippolito. According to its headline, he said the casting was "100% there". We could not read the full interview, so we add nothing beyond that line.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Stallone really write Rocky in three days?

Partly. Variety says he worked for 72 hours on the basics, but sketched only the first third or so and revised the script many times afterwards.

### Did the studio offer Stallone money to step aside?

Variety reports that United Artists reportedly offered up to $250,000 for him to give up the starring role, and that he refused.

### Who else was considered for Rocky?

Variety says the studio would have preferred James Caan, Nick Nolte or Ryan O'Neal.
