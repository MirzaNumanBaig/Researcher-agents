**Quick summary**

- [Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook) spoke about his half-brother's theft on Jesse Tyler Ferguson's *Dinner's on Me* podcast, according to Netflix Junkie.
- The documentary *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story* reached digital platforms on 29 September 2026 and Netflix on 6 November, with availability varying by country.
- A forensic investigation put the theft at roughly US$12 million, the figure used for court-ordered restitution. Cook mentioned about US$15 million on the podcast.
- The film is directed by [Brett Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Harvey_(English_director)) and includes family, friends, managers and the lead prosecutor.

Dane Cook has made a joke out of the worst financial episode of his life, and a new documentary is giving the whole story a wider audience. Speaking on the *Dinner's on Me* podcast this week, the American comedian shrugged off the suggestion that his half-brother took "a s*** ton of money" by noting that the Boston police said much the same thing, as [Netflix Junkie reported](https://www.netflixjunkie.com/hollywood-news-ton-of-money-kid-dane-cook-reveals-how-his-brother-stole-15-million-from-him/amp/).

## What Cook said on the podcast

The host, [Jesse Tyler Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Tyler_Ferguson) of [Modern Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Family), put the question bluntly. Cook answered with the kind of deadpan he built a career on, recalling that the officers who handled the case used the same words.

Behind the joke is a serious allegation that has been on the record for years. According to Netflix Junkie, Darryl McCauley had managed Cook's finances since the 1990s, as the comedian moved from Boston clubs to arena stages. Investigators found that signatures were forged and money was moved through unauthorised transactions.

## The numbers behind the story

The scheme came to light in December 2008, when a forged US$3 million cheque helped expose it, Netflix Junkie reports. A forensic investigation then established about US$12 million in theft, and that is the sum used for restitution ordered by the court.

Cook's own estimate on the podcast was higher, around US$15 million. The outlet notes that the complete loss remains uncertain, so the larger number is best read as his recollection rather than a court finding.

## What the documentary covers

*Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story* follows Cook from working-class Boston to a comedy career that peaked in the mid-2000s. [SSBCrack](https://news.ssbcrack.com/funny-money-the-dane-cook-story-explores-dane-cooks-rise-and-family-betrayal/) says the film recounts his rise after the 2003 album [Harmful If Swallowed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmful_If_Swallowed), his two stints hosting [Saturday Night Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturday_Night_Live), and film roles including [Good Luck Chuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Luck_Chuck). It also notes he sold out Madison Square Garden, making him the second comedian to do so.

Then the tone shifts. The film examines why Cook handed his money to McCauley as his career took off, and how the losses ended the brothers' relationship.

## Who appears in the film

The interview list is long. Cook appears alongside his wife Kelsi, his sister Courtney Cook and half-sister Kelly Amann, according to both outlets. SSBCrack adds childhood friend Al Del Bene, former neighbour Richard Pollmann, McCauley's ex-wife Erika Montsesi and his former best friend Guy Venuti.

The investigative side is covered by business managers Richard Feldstein and Thomas Kingsley, lawyer Joseph Zwicker, detective lieutenant Bobby Irwin and lead prosecutor Richard Grundy. Netflix Junkie describes the result as a heist story in which the culprit sat at the family table.
