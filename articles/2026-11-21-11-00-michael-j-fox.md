**Quick summary**

- Guitar Player has picked apart the "Johnny B. Goode" scene in the 1988 film Back to the Future, starring Michael J. Fox.
- The scene is set in 1955, but the song was not released until 1958.
- The cherry red Gibson ES-345 Fox plays was not designed until 1958 and not sold until the following year.
- The guitar vanished after filming, and Gibson has launched a search for it, according to Guitar Player.

[Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) mimed one of cinema's most replayed musical moments when his character, Marty McFly, tore through a rock and roll number at a 1955 school dance. A new piece from [Guitar Player](https://www.guitarplayer.com/gear/i-guess-you-guys-arent-ready-for-that-yet-michael-j-fox-never-spoke-truer-words-heres-what-back-to-the-future-got-wrong-about-marty-mcflys-chuck-berry-inspired-guitar-show) published on 20 November 2026 asks what the film got wrong, and the answer comes down to a guitar.

## The scene and its song

In Back to the Future, McFly travels from the 1980s to 1955 and ends up on stage at a high school dance. He plays "[Johnny B. Goode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_B._Goode)", the hit associated with [Chuck Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry), who was by then well on his way to being called the Father of Rock and Roll, Guitar Player notes.

The magazine says the choice of song makes sense on the surface. Berry was already a star in 1955 after "Maybellene", and he went on to write "Roll Over Beethoven", "School Days" and "Sweet Little Sixteen". The double-stop opening of "Johnny B. Goode" became a rite of passage for young guitarists.

## Two anachronisms, one forgivable

The first slip is the song itself. The scene is set in 1955, yet "Johnny B. Goode" did not come out until 1958. Guitar Player treats that one lightly: McFly is simply giving the kids a preview of the music still to come.

The second slip is harder to explain away. Fox plays a cherry red Gibson ES-345 semihollow, a model Berry himself favoured. But according to Guitar Player, Gibson did not begin designing the ES-345 until 1958, building on the ES-335 launched that year, and the guitar was not on sale until 1959. In 1955 it could not have belonged to the dance band.

The article jokes that McFly would have had to bring the guitar with him, or that the band's guitarist is a time traveller too.

## The intentional errors

Not every anachronism was an accident. Guitar Player says Fox's performance deliberately borrows from players who came much later: [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix), [Angus Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_Young) and [Eddie Van Halen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Van_Halen). The scene includes feedback, power chords and two-handed tapping, none of which a 1955 crowd would have heard.

That is the point of the famous closing line. Fox turns to the stunned audience and says, "I guess you guys aren't ready for that yet."

The magazine adds that Berry did go on to inspire later guitarists, from [Keith Richards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Richards) to [Ace Frehley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ace_Frehley) and Young. It also says he was buried with a Gibson ES-355, the model that became a staple of his live shows, rather than an ES-345.

## The missing guitar

The guitar from the film has a story of its own. Guitar Player reports that after filming wrapped, the instrument disappeared, and Gibson has now started a search for it. We covered a related theory about where it went in [Michael J. Fox Has a Theory on the Missing Back to the Future Guitar](https://moduscarry.com/michael-j-fox-back-to-the-future-guitar/).

## What happens next

Guitar Player's report does not say whether the guitar has been found or give any timeline for the search. For now, Gibson's appeal is the thing to watch. ## Frequently asked questions

### Which guitar does Michael J. Fox play in Back to the Future?

He plays a cherry red Gibson ES-345 semihollow, according to Guitar Player. It was one of Chuck Berry's favoured styles of guitar.

### Why is the guitar an anachronism?

The scene takes place in 1955, but Gibson did not start designing the ES-345 until 1958, and it was not available to buy until 1959.

### Is the guitar from the film missing?

Yes, according to Guitar Player. The instrument disappeared after filming ended, and Gibson has launched a new search for it.

### Was "Johnny B. Goode" out in 1955?

No. The song was released in 1958, three years after the year in which the film's dance scene is set.
