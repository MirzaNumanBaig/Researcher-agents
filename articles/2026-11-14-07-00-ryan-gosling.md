**Quick summary**

- Cinematographer Greig Fraser told Deadline how 1980s pop culture shaped the look of *Project Hail Mary*.
- He spoke at the ToruńCamerimage Film Festival, which runs from 7 to 14 November 2026.
- Fraser shot scene transitions with a mis-timed shutter to create image streaking, a nod to the cartoon "wobbly lines" flashback.
- The film stars [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) and is described by Deadline as a summer box office hit.

Oscar-winning cinematographer Greig Fraser has explained how he and directors [Phil Lord and Christopher Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lord_and_Christopher_Miller) built the visual style of *Project Hail Mary*, the sci-fi film led by [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling). Speaking to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/project-hail-mary-greig-fraser-ryan-gosling-camerimage-1237221490/) at the ToruńCamerimage Film Festival, he said E.T., cartoons and other 1980s touchstones were part of the conversation from the start.

Fraser made the comments while taking a break from shooting [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes)' Beatles films, according to Deadline.

## A shared pop culture language

Fraser said he had known Lord and Miller in passing for years but had never worked with them until this film. He described them as filmmakers who keep switching formats, ratios and genres while keeping an underlying theme. What connected the three of them, he said, was growing up on the same films and cartoons.

That common ground shaped the early talks. Fraser told Deadline that Lord raised *Scooby-Doo*, specifically the wobbly lines that signal a flashback, as a way to think about moving between space and Earth. Both men agreed the finished film would never use wobbly lines. The task became translating that 1980s feeling, from films such as E.T. and from cartoons, into what filmmakers do today.

## The mis-timed shutter

To get there, Fraser said he shot with a mis-timed shutter, which creates streaking in the image as scenes transition. Fraser's credits include the first two *Dune* films and *The Batman*, per Deadline.

*Project Hail Mary* is an Amazon MGM film based on [Andy Weir's novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Hail_Mary). Gosling plays a science teacher who wakes on a spaceship light years from home and learns he may be humanity's only hope against a parasite dimming the sun. The story alternates between two timelines: the present in space and flashbacks on Earth.

## Cooler in space, warmer at home

Fraser said the two worlds needed different colour. Space is a bit cooler and Earth a bit warmer. He called it a fine balance, because it would be easy to fall back on the cold, fluorescent look of space films. Earth is what the character is fighting for, so the memories had to feel lovely.

There was a limit to the warmth, though. Earth is dying because the sun is dying, and Fraser said the audience should not sit through a film of roughly two and a half to three hours wanting to jump off a building. He wanted viewers to come out enjoying the character's experience and to see him grow.

## An opening that avoids the space-film look

The Australian cinematographer said the opening scene mattered. Gosling's character wakes without knowing where he is, and Fraser did not want it to feel like a space film. Rather than a laboratory, he wanted a high-end hotel or health retreat, so the audience feels as disoriented as the character.

He also pointed to a computer-generated shot the team designed in which the camera drifts past the character as a tiny figure in a small window of the ship. Fraser said it references the end of *The Empire Strikes Back*, where small figures stand in a window, and suits a character on a journey going past.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who shot Project Hail Mary?
Greig Fraser, an Oscar-winning Australian cinematographer, shot it with directors Phil Lord and Christopher Miller, according to Deadline.

### What 1980s influences did the filmmakers use?
Fraser cited E.T., cartoons such as *Scooby-Doo* and *The Empire Strikes Back*, which informed the transitions and a key shot of the spaceship.

### What is Project Hail Mary about?
Ryan Gosling plays a science teacher who wakes aboard a spaceship and learns he is humanity's sole hope to stop a sun-dimming parasite. It is based on Andy Weir's novel.
