**Quick summary**

- Billy Evans, partner of convicted Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, has responded to being seen as the villain of Nathan Fielder's documentary *You Can See Everything*.
- In a preview clip from CBS Sunday Morning, he said he would have preferred that not to be the case, but called the film "entertainment".
- Evans also posted on X that the idea he controls Holmes is a misconception.
- Amanda Seyfried, who appears in the film, has described a tense dinner-table exchange with him.

[Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder)'s Elizabeth Holmes documentary has put its most visible supporting figure on the defensive. Billy Evans, Holmes's partner since 2018, told CBS Sunday Morning he would rather not have been portrayed as the villain, according to [The Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/arts-entertainment/tv/news/billy-evans-you-can-see-everything-elizabeth-holmes-b3084765.html), which reported the preview clip on 13 November.

## What Billy Evans told CBS

The full interview airs on Sunday. In the preview, CBS correspondent [Matt Gutman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Gutman) asked Evans how he felt about being the villain of the film.

"I would have preferred for that not to be the case. But it's entertainment," Evans replied, as quoted by The Independent.

Gutman pressed him on whether he was comfortable being the subject of entertainment that makes him look "conniving" and "manipulative". Evans conceded the film made him look "crazy". He said his aim had been for audiences to see Holmes, and that when he watches the movie he sees "the person that I love and exactly who she is".

## Why viewers read him as controlling

*You Can See Everything* is a genre-bending documentary from Fielder and director [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim). It reached theatres last month and follows Holmes and her family in the month before she entered prison in 2023. Evans and Holmes have two children.

According to The Independent, some viewers came away believing Evans was controlling Holmes. Several scenes show him running her social media accounts while she is incarcerated. He also says he holds power of attorney, and he tries to negotiate a financial deal with A24 without her knowledge.

Evans has rejected that reading. In a recent post on X, he called the biggest misconception about the film "that I somehow control Liz", adding that anyone who actually knows her "would find that idea hilarious". The documentary's depiction and Evans's account sit side by side, and the reporting does not settle which is closer to the truth.

## Amanda Seyfried's tense dinner scene

The film also features [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried), who played Holmes in the Hulu series *The Dropout*. She acts out several reconstructed scenes and meets Evans at his home after Holmes goes to prison. Her involvement was kept secret until after the film's release.

Seyfried has since told Rolling Stone she was stunned by a dinner-table exchange with Evans about the late conservative activist [Charlie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kirk), who was assassinated at a speaking event in September 2025. "There was no reason for [Evans] to get that aggressive," she said, calling "aggressive" the more diplomatic word. She described her jaw as "on the floor" and said that at that point "the gloves are off".

## The background to the film

Holmes, 42, was convicted of fraud in 2022 after claiming her start-up Theranos had developed a revolutionary device that could diagnose a range of conditions from a few drops of blood. She was sentenced to 11 years and began serving her time at a Texas facility in May 2023.
