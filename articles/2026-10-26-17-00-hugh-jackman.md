**Quick summary**

- Asked by Variety whether he will appear in Avengers: Secret Wars, Hugh Jackman said he had "no idea" what the interviewer was talking about, according to IGN India.
- The question noted that Jackman has previously said he will not be in Avengers: Doomsday. He did not confirm or deny a Secret Wars role.
- Separately, BGR published a look back at The Fountain, the 2006 sci-fi romance Jackman made with Rachel Weisz.
- BGR says the film was poorly reviewed on release but has since built a cult following.

[Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) has given nothing away about a possible return to the Marvel universe, answering a direct question about Avengers: Secret Wars with a deadpan denial. At the same time, one of the most divisive films of his career is getting a second look from critics and fans.

## What Jackman said about Secret Wars

According to [IGN India](https://in.ign.com/avengers-secret-wars/273995/deadpool-wolverine-star-hugh-jackman-addresses-whether-he-will-return-for-avengers-secret-wars), Variety put the question to the actor plainly. The interviewer reminded him that he had said he would not be in Doomsday, then asked whether Secret Wars was a different story.

Jackman replied that he did not know what the interviewer was talking about and had no idea what they meant. That is the full extent of what the report quotes, and it is not a confirmation. It is not a denial either.

It is worth treating the exchange for what it is: a playful non-answer. Nothing in the report suggests a casting decision, a contract or a filming schedule. Anyone reading it as a hint is guessing.

## The Fountain gets a second look

The other story involving Jackman this week is about the past. [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2290415/hugh-jackman-rachel-weisz-sci-fi-movie-the-fountain-cult-classic/) revisited The Fountain, the 2006 film from director [Darren Aronofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darren_Aronofsky), made after his harrowing drama Requiem for a Dream.

Jackman and [Rachel Weisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Weisz) play characters across three timelines. In the first, a conquistador and a queen. In the second, a modern scientist and his dying wife. In the third, a space traveller far in the future who is trying to hold on to a lost love. BGR describes it as bold and experimental, and a much more hopeful film than Aronofsky's previous one.

## How critics reacted in 2006

BGR calls it Aronofsky's most poorly received film to date. It quotes [Michael Atkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Atkinson_(writer)) of Sight & Sound, who wrote that it was hard to recall another American film that pursues a personal vision and goes so maddeningly wrong. [Peter Bradshaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bradshaw) of The Guardian found a strange deadness in it, along with self-importance.

[Richard Roeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roeper) went further and called it one of the worst movies of the year, according to the same article.

Time has been kinder. BGR puts the audience score on the review aggregator it cites at 74 per cent, and argues that the film may eventually be seen as ahead of its time. That is the outlet's opinion, not a settled verdict, but the gap between the 2006 reviews and the current audience score is the clearest sign of a reappraisal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Hugh Jackman in Avengers: Secret Wars?

Nothing has been confirmed. When Variety asked, Jackman said he had no idea what the interviewer meant, per IGN India.

### What is The Fountain about?

It follows three linked stories across time: a conquistador and a queen, a scientist and his dying wife, and a future space traveller searching for a lost love, as BGR describes it.

### Did The Fountain do well with critics?

No. BGR reports that reviews in 2006 were harsh, though its audience score today is 74 per cent.
