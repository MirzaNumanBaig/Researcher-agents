**Quick summary**

- On Michael J. Fox Alex P. Keaton: [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) says he played his own brother to win the "Family Ties" role.
- He described the audition in a May 2001 interview with the Television Academy, as reported by TVLine on 14 November 2026.
- Series creator Gary David Goldberg initially did not want him for the part.
- Casting director Judith Weiner persuaded Goldberg to give Fox a second reading, and it worked.

## The brother who held the dinner table

The story of Michael J. Fox and Alex P. Keaton starts at an audition. When Fox walked in for [Family Ties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Ties), he already knew who he was playing. According to [TVLine](https://www.tvline.com/2311987/michael-j-fox-real-life-inspiration-family-ties-alex-p-keaton/), Fox told the Television Academy in May 2001 that he based [Alex P. Keaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_P._Keaton) on his own brother, "the smarta** in our family, who was so much smarter than everybody else".

Fox said the character clicked for him straight away. "I got it. I understood it. I knew how to make it funny," he recalled. He also admitted that his comedic timing was still developing at that point in his career, so he borrowed a few tricks from watching his brother at home.

One habit stuck with him. As Fox described it, his brother would put his glass of milk down at the dinner table, and from the moment it left his mouth everyone waited to hear what he would say. That pause, Fox said, became part of Alex.

## Why Michael J. Fox and Alex P. Keaton almost never happened

Getting the part was not simple. TVLine reports that Fox once told Route Magazine that series creator [Gary David Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_David_Goldberg) did not want to see him again after the first reading. The quote Fox attributed to Goldberg was blunt: "I'm a grown man. I know what I like, I know what I don't like. I didn't like him."

Casting director Judith Weiner disagreed. According to the same report, she convinced Goldberg to bring Fox back for another try. Fox has not described that decision as luck. By his account, the second reading went differently because he now had a clear read on what Goldberg wanted.

## Learning to feed the laugh

Fox told the Television Academy that he could tell early in the second audition that Goldberg was laughing. He said that taught him something he had not needed on smaller projects, because he had not done much sitcom work at that level. The more Goldberg laughed, the more Fox understood what he liked, and he pushed that harder on the next line.

It paid off. Fox recalled that Goldberg turned to Weiner afterwards and asked why nobody had told him how good the actor was. From that day, Fox said, the showrunner was his biggest champion.

## From sitcom to pop culture icon

"Family Ties" ran on NBC for seven seasons, from 1982 to 1989, and TVLine describes it as an era-defining series that made Fox a household name. The report notes that his fame climbed again when he landed the role of Marty McFly in 1985's "Back to the Future".
