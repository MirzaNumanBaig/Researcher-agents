**Quick summary**

- Russell Brand merch reading "Free Russell" is on sale through Reborn, the wellness business of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), according to The Hollywood Reporter and Page Six.
- Each item costs $34.99, and the site calls the range "fundraiser apparel".
- Brand was sentenced on 9 November to 10 years and six months in prison after a jury found him guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.
- A prison statement from Brand, read out by his wife, has drawn a government response and a Google review of the video.

Russell Brand merch calling for his release is on sale through his wellness company, less than two weeks after he was jailed for 10 years and six months. The Hollywood Reporter and Page Six both reported the range this weekend.

## Russell Brand merch: what is on sale

According to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/russell-brand-selling-free-russell-merch-in-prison-1236760597/), there are three products: a shirt printed "Free Russell Brand", a hat carrying the same words, and a second shirt reading "Free Russell" beside an image of his face. [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/21/celebrity-news/russell-brand-hawking-free-russell-merch-via-his-wellness-company-from-behind-bars-after-rape-conviction/) describes the first shirt as black with white lettering and says the second sits on a red and blue background.

Every item is priced at $34.99. The site labels the range "fundraiser apparel" and describes it as "silenced for speaking out against injustice and corruption",.

It is not clear how long the range has been available. The Hollywood Reporter found customer reviews dating back to 16 November, a week after the sentencing. Page Six says the shirts are also linked from russellbrand.com, where Reborn's supplements, including creatine powder and bovine colostrum, are sold. Page Six asked Reborn for comment.

## The verdict and the sentence

The trial ran for four weeks at Southwark Crown Court. The jury found Brand guilty on one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He was found not guilty on two other rape charges and on a charge of attempted indecent assault, The Hollywood Reporter reports. Page Six says he is being held at HMP Littlehey, a low-security prison in Cambridgeshire.

Earlier coverage looks at [how the conviction is being read as the end of Britain's "lad culture"](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-lad-culture-conviction/) and at [who spoke up before the verdict](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-warnings-before-verdict/).

## The prison message that came first

The merchandise follows a statement Brand wrote in jail. His wife, Laura Gallacher, read it aloud in a video posted to his channels, with Page Six placing it in a 15 November episode of their "Sunday Service with the Brands" podcast. In it he says he is "not guilty" of the offences for which he was jailed, and he asks supporters to pray for him and support his family.

The video drew a sharp response. Britain's Justice Secretary, Alex Norris, called the broadcast "completely unacceptable" in a post on X, saying prisoners are barred from making statements that cause distress to victims and that the matter was being urgently investigated. Page Six reports that the Prime Minister's office described the statement as "clearly appalling".

Google, which owns YouTube, told BBC News it was aware of the content and investigating. It added that it suspended Brand's channel from the YouTube Partner Program in September 2023, so the channel has not earned money from the platform since. According to the BBC, as relayed by The Hollywood Reporter, Google turned down a request from the Ministry of Justice to take the video down, saying none of its rules had been broken.

Page Six also quotes an unnamed source speaking to The Sun, who claimed Brand may face punishment inside prison for releasing the statement without permission. That claim is unconfirmed, and neither outlet reports any official finding.

## What happens next

The Ministry of Justice inquiry into the prison statement is still open, and Google says its own review continues. No outlet has reported a response from Reborn about the merchandise, or whether any money from the range is going to a legal fund.
