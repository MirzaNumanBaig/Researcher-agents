**Quick summary**

- [Tom Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hardy) lets his MobLand fixer Harry Da Souza smile for the first time in episode 9, "Nervous Breakdown", which aired on 13 November.
- FandomWire calls the pub scene the best acting of Hardy's run on the series.
- Last week's reports that Hardy was fired were challenged by Variety, which says he was not officially fired and that talks about a third season are under way, according to JoBlo.
- Season 2 has already been shot, but Hardy may still be needed for some reshoots, per the same report.

Tom Hardy has spent two seasons of MobLand playing a man who barely changes expression. In episode 9, which aired on 13 November, Harry Da Souza finally smiles, and [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/mobland-season-2-tom-hardy-episode-9/) argues it is the strongest work Hardy has done on the show. The timing matters, because the same week brought conflicting reports about whether he will be back for season 3.

## Why the episode 9 smile works

Harry has been the Harrigan family's fixer, a man who cleans up violence without visible feeling. In "Nervous Breakdown", FandomWire reports, he walks into an abandoned pub knowing he may not walk out. Frankie Cooper has kidnapped Harry's teenage daughter, Gina, played by [Teddie Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddie_Allen).

Harry explains that he timed the walk from the car and worked out that Frankie's men need seven seconds to reach him. That, he says, is plenty of time to do damage with the pint glass in front of him. As he talks, a grin flickers across his face.

FandomWire's reviewer says the power of the moment comes from restraint. Hardy barely raises his voice and delivers threats in the tone of someone discussing dinner. The grin, the review argues, shows a frightened father who has stopped hiding it, yet it makes Harry harder to trust, not easier.

The episode also has lighter beats. According to the same review, Harry races off to find Gina in a bathrobe, carrying coffee, toast and a submachine gun. He later shares a scene with Helen Mirren's Maeve Harrigan, where he appears to be holding back another smile.

## A worthy rival in Johnny Flynn

The scene depends on [Johnny Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Flynn), who plays Frankie. Flynn was born in Johannesburg in 1983 and fronts the folk band Johnny Flynn & the Sussex Wit. His screen career goes back to the 2013 film [Song One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_One), according to FandomWire.

Frankie is a London drug lord who first appeared at the end of the season 2 premiere. Flynn has said he based the character on the Gallagher brothers in the mid-1990s, and that Frankie is usually the smartest person in the room until Harry arrives. He has described pushing Harry as "poking the biggest bear in the woods".

As the episode closes, Kat McAllister, played by [Janet McTeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_McTeer), is making her move on the Harrigan empire. FandomWire expects the finale to show whether that bear has been pushed too far.

## The season 3 question

Behind the scenes, the picture is less settled. Last week, reports claimed Hardy had been fired from the Paramount+ series and was unlikely to return. [JoBlo](https://www.joblo.com/tom-hardy-mobland-update/) now says a new Variety report states he was not officially fired and that discussions about his return are under way.

The original reports described friction between Hardy and writer and executive producer [Jez Butterworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jez_Butterworth), as well as producer David Glasser. Variety's insiders, as relayed by JoBlo, believe there is fault on both sides. These are anonymous accounts, and one source disputed part of them.

According to those sources, Hardy was unhappy that scripts sometimes arrived a week before shooting, because he likes time to prepare and rehearse. They also say he proposed script changes that were hard to resolve because Butterworth was rarely on set. "Often his ideas are very sound," one source said. Butterworth has other commitments, including The Agency and [Sam Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Mendes)' Beatles films, and one source said he is "spread too thin".

Another source said the show could carry on without Hardy, though it would probably lose viewers. Neither Hardy nor the producers are quoted on the record in the material we reviewed, so none of this is confirmed.

## What happens next

The season 2 finale is the next event. Season 2 has already been shot, but JoBlo notes Hardy may still be needed for reshoots, which gives both sides a reason to settle things.
