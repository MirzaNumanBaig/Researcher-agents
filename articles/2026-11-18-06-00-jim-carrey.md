**Quick summary**

- A sequel to the 2000 film *How the Grinch Stole Christmas* is in development at Universal and Imagine Entertainment.
- [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey) is in talks to return as the Grinch, and [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard) is in talks to direct again.
- Variety and TheWrap both reported in June that the talks are ongoing and the project could still fall apart.
- A fresh People.com headline says the pair will not "go forward" unless the sequel is "great". We could not read that report.

The Jim Carrey Grinch sequel is a real project, but it is not a done deal. Universal and Imagine Entertainment are developing a follow-up to the 2000 live-action hit, with Carrey and Howard in talks to come back, according to [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/grinch-sequel-jim-carrey-ron-howard-37810/) and [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/?p=8035974). A People.com exclusive published on 17 November is headlined to say the two will only proceed if the film is "great". We could not open that article, so we are not reporting its detail.

## What has been reported about the Jim Carrey Grinch sequel

Both trade outlets describe the same set-up. Universal and Imagine are developing the film, and Carrey is expected to play the Grinch again. Howard is expected to direct, and Brian Grazer, who co-founded Imagine with Howard, is expected to produce.

A script already exists. Variety says Alec Berg, Jeff Schaffer and David Mandel are the writers, all of whom worked on *Curb Your Enthusiasm* and on the 2003 film *The Cat in the Hat*. TheWrap adds that the project will be overseen by Susan Brandt, chief executive of Dr. Seuss Enterprises, with Universal executives Britt Hennemuth and Christina Hoffrogge.

Neither outlet gives a release date or a timeline. The word used in both is "talks", which is why the People headline is worth noting: it suggests the filmmakers are being careful about quality before committing.

## Why the original film still matters

The 2000 film earned about US$346 million worldwide, according to TheWrap, which made it the sixth biggest release of its year. Variety puts the figure at more than US$350 million. Critics were unkind at the time, yet the film became a holiday regular, and Universal re-released it for its 25th anniversary last year, TheWrap notes.

There is also competition from the Seuss catalogue. Illumination's animated *The Grinch* (2018) grossed US$512.7 million worldwide, per TheWrap, so any live-action return would be measured against a bigger number than the first film's.

## The makeup question

Carrey's past comments explain why a return was never certain. Variety quotes a 2024 ComicBook.com interview in which he said "if we could figure out the Grinch", describing the heavy makeup on the first film as excruciating. He suggested motion capture could free him up for other work. Neither outlet reports how the sequel would handle the character's look, so that remains an open question.

Carrey has also scaled back his acting since the 2010s, TheWrap says, though he has found box office success again as Dr. Robotnik in Paramount's *Sonic the Hedgehog* films. He is due to return in the fourth instalment, which Variety places in 2027 and TheWrap in April 2027.

## What happens next

Nothing is confirmed until Carrey and Howard sign. If they do, a casting and production announcement from Universal would follow, along with a release window. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a Grinch sequel with Jim Carrey confirmed?
Not fully. Variety and TheWrap report that it is in development at Universal and Imagine, with Carrey in talks to return. They say the project could still fall apart.

### Who is directing the Grinch sequel?
Ron Howard, who directed the 2000 film, is expected to return, according to Variety. He is also in talks, not signed.

### When will the Grinch sequel be released?
No release date has been announced by the studio or reported by either outlet.

### Who wrote the script?
Alec Berg, Jeff Schaffer and David Mandel, per Variety and TheWrap.
