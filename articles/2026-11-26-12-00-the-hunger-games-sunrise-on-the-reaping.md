**Quick summary**

- A review published on 25 November by Mediaversity Reviews criticises the look and story of [The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games%3A_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping).
- The film opened on 28 October and has a 68% Rotten Tomatoes score and 55/100 on Metacritic, according to Wikipedia's record.
- It has grossed about US$237.2 million against a US$150 million budget.
- Opinion is split, with some critics warmer on the film than others.

The latest [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) prequel is drawing a more critical response than its box office suggests. In a review published on 25 November, [Mediaversity Reviews](https://www.mediaversityreviews.com/film-reviews/2026/11/25/the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping) argues that the film's garish arena and repetitive plot hold it back, even though director [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) delivers the series' usual pacing.

## What the review says about the look

The reviewer's biggest complaint is the arena. Where the earlier films leaned on natural landscapes, and the previous prequel on a rougher, grittier feel, this one uses bright, surreal colours and creatures that are plainly computer-generated. The review calls the result gaudy.

It does allow for another reading: the artificial glossiness could be a deliberate comment on a cruel contest built for entertainment. Cinematographer [Jo Willems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Willems), who has shot the series since [Catching Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catching_Fire), told [Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly), as quoted in the review, that the team wanted to push colour. The District 12 scenes, by contrast, keep the series' earthier look, which the reviewer says makes the arena seem cheaper by comparison.

## A prequel with a known ending

The film follows a teenage Haymitch Abernathy, played by [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), a character audiences first met as the mentor played by [Owen Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Owen_Wilson) alongside [Jennifer Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Lawrence) in the original four films. It is based on the novel by [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins), with a screenplay by [Billy Ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Ray_(screenwriter)) and [Michael Lesslie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Lesslie).

Mediaversity says the story suffers from the usual prequel problem: because viewers know where it ends, deaths and setbacks lose some of their sting. It also says similar grief scenes recur so often that they stop landing.

## Representation concerns

The review gives the film 3 out of 5 for gender and says it passes the [Bechdel Test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechdel_test). It finds the story far more male-centred than the previous film, which had co-leads in [Tom Blyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Blyth) and [Rachel Zegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Zegler). It describes [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress))'s Lenore Dove as an idealised figure who mainly serves Haymitch's story, while [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace)'s Maysilee Donner is capable but in a supporting role.

The reviewer also objects to how some characters of colour, including one played by [Ben Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Wang), are written and killed. These are the reviewer's views, and other critics may read the same scenes differently.

## How it compares with other reviews

Wikipedia's summary of the reception puts the film at 68% on Rotten Tomatoes and 55/100 from 43 critics on Metacritic. The Rotten Tomatoes consensus calls it a sturdy if familiar prequel that gains from its well-loved characters. The New York Times' Brandon Yu was among the harsher voices, calling it both bloated and limited in vision.

Audiences are still turning up. The film has taken around US$237.2 million worldwide, about 1.6 times its US$150 million budget, which Wikipedia's figures class as a modest result for a blockbuster. In Australia it set a franchise opening record, as we reported in [Sunrise on the Reaping Sets Hunger Games Australian Record](https://moduscarry.com/sunrise-on-the-reaping-australian-record/).
