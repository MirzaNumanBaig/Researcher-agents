**Quick summary**

- Sylvester Stallone has revisited his years of poverty before fame, according to a Fox News report published on 26 November 2026.
- He worked at a deli, as a cinema usher and cleaning lion cages at the Central Park Zoo while trying to break in.
- He credits Henry Winkler, his co-star on *The Lords of Flatbush* (1974), with urging him to keep writing.
- The account draws on Stallone's memoir *The Steps* and on comments from both men.

[Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) has spent decades as one of Hollywood's best-known action stars, but a new look back at his early years shows how thin things once were. According to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/entertainment/sylvester-stallone-endured-broke-lonely-days-cleaning-lion-cages-henry-winkler-changed-life), the actor remembers cleaning lion cages and living on very little before a friendship with [Henry Winkler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Winkler) gave him the confidence to push on.

## The odd jobs behind the Rocky legend

Before he left New York, Stallone held a string of low-paid jobs, Fox News reports. He worked at a deli, ushered at a movie theatre and cleaned lion cages at the Central Park Zoo. Those details come from his memoir, *The Steps*, which the outlet draws on for its account.

The work paid the bills, but writing was the real goal. The same report says Stallone had written about 15 screenplays by the time he and his then-wife, Sasha Czack, packed up for California.

## A blacked-out window and a visit from Winkler

Stallone and Winkler first worked together on *The Lords of Flatbush* in 1974, though Stallone dates their friendship to 1972, per Fox News. He says Winkler was the one who reached out first.

Winkler later visited the couple's flat on Lexington Avenue, where they lived with a bullmastiff. Stallone had painted the windows black so the time of day would not matter and he could keep writing. Winkler said the sight showed how driven his friend was.

In the memoir, Stallone describes Winkler stepping past a homeless man and climbing five flights of stairs to reach him. Winkler was struck by the shelves of books and the pile of scripts, and told Stallone to stay with it. He said writing was harder to break into than acting, according to the report.

## Starting again in Encino

Stallone and Czack moved west with $830 and had about $500 left on arrival, Fox News reports. They knew almost nobody. Winkler, by then a star of *Happy Days*, was the one person Stallone could call.

The couple rented a house in Encino for $300 a month. It was about 500 square feet, with no lawn and no air conditioning. Stallone also writes of reaching Hollywood and Vine and marvelling that he would have a star on the Walk of Fame less than a decade later.

## What each man says now

Stallone told the outlet that Winkler's encouragement mattered at a time when he kept hearing "no". He says Winkler remained supportive and often joked that *The Lords of Flatbush* had helped shape his own TV character. Stallone also recalls ringing Winkler years later to tell him he had matured like fine wine.

Winkler describes Stallone as something of a philosopher. He said Stallone's upbringing could have broken a person, but his motto was to keep punching. Winkler says he never doubted his friend would make it and calls the friendship an honour.

A second report, from Men's Journal, covers Stallone's first wife, Sasha Czack. We could not read it, so nothing from it is used here.
