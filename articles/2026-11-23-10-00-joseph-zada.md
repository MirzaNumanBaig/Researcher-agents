Joseph Zada Hunger Games stunts, and the risky audition tape behind the role, are the focus of a new Variety interview. The 21-year-old Australian plays the young Haymitch in *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*.

## A first day in the cornucopia

Zada's introduction to the set of the latest [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) film was the deadliest scene in the story. According to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/features/hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-joseph-zada-action-scenes-1236954627/), his first day was the opening cornucopia sequence, in which nearly 50 children are released to fight to the death.

The production then spent its first five weeks inside the arena. Zada called that scheduling choice "genius". It let him build the character on his own, he said, before he had to work alongside the veterans in the cast, among them [Glenn Close](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Close), [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes), [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons) and [Kieran Culkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieran_Culkin).
## An audition tape that took five weeks

Zada auditioned before [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins)'s novel was published. He was shooting Netflix's *East of Eden* at the time, so he flew home to Australia to record his tape. The brief was to improvise a scene for a younger version of Woody Harrelson's Haymitch.

He went well beyond that. Zada wrote four scenes, used a drone and found a stand-in for Maysilee, the character later played by [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace). His father and older brother, who are also actors, helped. He told Variety he never went to acting school, but the three of them spent about four years turning auditions into small short films at home.

The studio gave him two weeks. He told them it would take five. "Took that risk, and it paid off," he said.

## Playing Haymitch without copying Harrelson

Zada has been a fan of the franchise for as long as he can remember. He read Collins's manuscript several times and studied Harrelson's performance in the earlier films, but he deliberately avoided copying his mannerisms. His main concern was the voice. He said he trusted that the rest would follow if he played the truth of each scene. For more on that, see our earlier piece on [Zada's Haymitch accent and the tip he received](https://moduscarry.com/joseph-zada-haymitch-accent/).

## Explosions, scuba lessons and a loose pole

The film is physically demanding, with combat, explosions and other threats around Haymitch. Zada's favourite shot to watch back is an explosion in slow motion. He joked that he looks like an action star and found it hard to believe it was him on screen.

The underwater scene was harder. Zada filmed it on his final day of production and spent three weeks taking scuba lessons beforehand, training on weekends while shooting during the week. For each take he descended with an oxygen tank. When the crew called "Action", they removed his air supply and switched on the jets. On one take the jets were too strong and he was thrown into a pole, and someone had to swim down and give him air. "Pretty scary," he said.

The second half of the story is darker. Variety notes that Haymitch carries heavy trauma after the games, which the film covers in its final 30 minutes. Zada filmed at least one emotionally demanding scene a week and said it was unavoidable that this took a toll on him.
