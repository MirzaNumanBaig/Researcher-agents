**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) is overseeing Stories She Tells, a project funded by UNIQLO with the University of Tokyo as founding partner.
- The project launched on 29 October 2026, according to Japanese fashion outlet FASHIONSNAP.
- Essays by women will be chosen through an open call that starts this winter, with Blanchett chairing the selection committee.
- The first magazine issue, themed "Connection", is planned for autumn 2027.

The Cate Blanchett Stories She Tells project is a new literary venture from UNIQLO, the Japanese clothing retailer where the Australian actor serves as global brand ambassador. According to [FASHIONSNAP](https://www.fashionsnap.com/article/2026-10-29/stories-she-tells/), it launched on 29 October 2026 and will publish essays by women, with Blanchett chairing the committee that picks them. The newsletter [The Future Party](https://www.futureparty.com/p/uniqlo-launches-magazine-with-cate-blanchett) picked up the story on 3 November.

## What Stories She Tells actually is

FASHIONSNAP describes it as a global project meant to share the varied voices and stories of women with the wider public. UNIQLO ties it to its "LifeWear" philosophy, the idea that the brand's work extends from clothing to enriching daily life through words and stories.

The centrepiece is a literary magazine. Its debut issue, themed "Connection", is expected to carry essays by women, related research papers and literary works. The Future Party reports that the essays will be curated through open submissions.

Blanchett's role goes beyond lending her name. Per FASHIONSNAP, she will chair the selection committee. She said she was honoured to work with UNIQLO on an initiative to share the perspectives of women writers, and that she hopes it helps a new generation of writers find free expression and connection.

## The University of Tokyo link

The University of Tokyo is the founding partner. FASHIONSNAP says faculty from its Diversity and Inclusion Co-creation Center, known as IncluDE, will advise on themes and editing and help produce content. The project also plans to expand through partnerships with universities worldwide.

On launch day, a one-day book fair for University of Tokyo students was held in front of the university's Central Library. The Yamanashi Prefecture bookshop Nohohon Books & Coffee chose titles by women writers, artists and photographers from Japan and abroad.

## Support for writers

The Future Party says chosen writers will receive financial support and mentorship. FASHIONSNAP does not specify payment or other formal support, so the details of that package remain unconfirmed. Both outlets agree on the aim: giving emerging writers, particularly those with few chances to be heard, a place to publish.

## How it fits UNIQLO's print plans

The Future Party, citing Highsnobiety, notes that this is technically UNIQLO's second print venture. The company already backs LifeWear magazine, a semiannual fashion title edited by Takahiro Kinoshita, the former editor-in-chief of the Japanese menswear magazine Popeye. Fashion-funded literary magazines are not new, the newsletter says, but few have come from a modern low-cost retailer.

## What happens next

Submissions open this winter. FASHIONSNAP reports that eligibility and selection details are due to be announced around December 2026, and the first issue is slated for autumn 2027. Until then, the rules, the size of any writer support and the magazine's price and distribution have not been published.
