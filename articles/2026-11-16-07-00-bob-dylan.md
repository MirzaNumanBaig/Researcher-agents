**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) played "Honest With Me" in Salzburg on 14 November 2026, according to KarloBag.
- Setlist.fm data cited by the site puts its last performance at 8 December 2019 in Washington, almost seven years earlier.
- The Salzburg Arena show listed 18 songs, opening with "Watching the River Flow" and closing with "Every Grain of Sand".
- Far Out Magazine has also revisited "Great White Wonder", the 1969 Dylan bootleg that helped start the bootleg era.

Bob Dylan brought "Honest With Me" back into his live set at the Salzburg Arena on 14 November 2026, ending a gap of nearly seven years. The song, from the 2001 album *Love and Theft*, was played twelfth, according to a report on KarloBag.eu that draws on the official setlist.

## What Dylan played in Salzburg

The set ran to 18 songs. Dylan opened with "Watching the River Flow" and finished with "Every Grain of Sand". "Honest With Me" sat between "I Can't Seem to Say Goodbye" and "Soon After Midnight".

The night leaned on his early 2000s work. Two other *Love and Theft* songs appeared, "High Water (For Charley Patton)" and "Sugar Baby". Longer-standing favourites were there too, including "It Ain't Me, Babe", "Tangled Up in Blue" and "Not Dark Yet", along with "Goodbye Jimmy Reed".

KarloBag cites setlist.fm for the gap. The previous recorded performance of "Honest With Me" was at The Anthem in Washington on 8 December 2019. For a songwriter whose shows change constantly, that is a long absence for a song with a driving, blues-heavy arrangement.

Only one of the two reports for this story could be read in full, so details beyond the setlist, such as the crowd's reaction or how the song sounded, are not confirmed here.

## Why setlists matter to Dylan fans

Dylan's concerts rarely repeat themselves night after night, which is why fans track each change. A song returning after years is the kind of detail that circulates quickly among collectors and completists. It also fits a wider pattern in the coverage this week, where Dylan's catalogue and its history are drawing attention.

## The bootleg that started a craze

That history includes one of rock's best-known unofficial releases. In a new piece, Far Out Magazine retraces how "Great White Wonder" appeared in record shops around Thanksgiving 1969, in a plain white sleeve with only a faded stamp for a title.

According to Far Out, a shop in Indianapolis was selling it for $12.75 and claimed to have sold 30 copies on the first morning. The album was assembled by two Los Angeles friends, Ken Douglas and Michael Taylor, who ran an underground label called Trademark of Quality.

The double album held 24 tracks of uneven quality. Its centrepiece was material from the [Basement Tapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Basement_Tapes), recorded with the group that became The Band in Woodstock in 1967. Fans were hungry for it because Dylan had stayed largely out of public view after his 1966 motorcycle crash, and his 1969 release [Nashville Skyline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Skyline) did not deliver those songs. The Basement Tapes only came out officially in 1975.

## How the industry responded

Far Out reports that Columbia Records called the bootleg an abuse of an artist's integrity and said it defamed Dylan and defrauded his fans. Legal action followed, but the album kept returning with new artwork and sequencing.

The label eventually answered with its own official bootleg series. Columbia began the CD box sets in the early 1990s, and the Dylan series has reached 18 volumes, the latest being *Vol 18: Through the Open Window* in 2025, according to the same piece.

## Frequently asked questions

### When did Bob Dylan last play "Honest With Me" before Salzburg?
According to setlist.fm data cited by KarloBag, it was on 8 December 2019 at The Anthem in Washington.

### What is "Honest With Me" from?
It is a track from Dylan's 2001 album *Love and Theft*.

### What was "Great White Wonder"?
It was an unofficial 1969 double album of Dylan demos, outtakes and Basement Tapes recordings, and one of the first famous bootleg LPs, according to Far Out Magazine.
