Keanu Reeves has landed another action lead. According to [Men's Health UK](https://www.menshealth.com/uk/fitness/a69932791/keanu-reeves-action-training-62/), the 62-year-old will headline Netflix's Maelstrom, stepping in for Alan Ritchson. The report, published on 27 October 2026, uses the casting as a way to look at the training that keeps Reeves ready for physically demanding work.

## What Maelstrom is about

Men's Health describes Maelstrom as a thriller in which Reeves plays a federal marshal. His job is to protect the fiancée of an arms dealer while a hurricane isolates everyone from the outside world.

The outlet says there is no word yet on a training plan built for the film. It does note that the casting is a reminder of how long Reeves has remained one of Hollywood's go-to action stars. He first played Neo in The Matrix 27 years ago and has since spent years as hitman John Wick.

## Strength built for endurance, not size

According to the report, Reeves has never trained to simply add bulk. His focus has been on becoming stronger and more resilient so his body can cope with repeated fight scenes and stunt work.

That approach began with trainer Denise Snyder, whom he met while filming [Point Break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Break) in 1991. They kept working together through The Matrix and the first John Wick film. Reeves later brought in celebrity trainer [Patrick Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Murphy_(producer)).

Murphy told Men's Health that he built Reeves' strength with compound exercises in circuits rather than bodybuilding-style isolation work. "He wasn't lifting crazy heavy weights," Murphy said, adding that the aim was muscular endurance and stability. By his account, he fitted five to six circuits into an hour and a half, which he described as around a thousand reps a session with minimal rest.

Murphy also said the sessions were not where Reeves took the most punishment. That came on set, during filming of the fight scenes.

## Restorative training and martial arts

To handle that volume, Murphy added what he calls restorative training. Men's Health explains it as resistance work using cables and bands that targets stabilisation, balance and alignment. Murphy said the exercises were chosen to influence posture, alignment and stabilisation.

Martial arts sit alongside the gym work. The report says Reeves has developed real proficiency in kung fu, jiu-jitsu, wushu and judo over his career, starting with preparation for The Matrix. Before that film, he reportedly trained four hours a day for four months in kung fu and jiu-jitsu with choreographer [Yuen Woo-ping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuen_Woo-ping). Yuen told The Hollywood Reporter the team had to start from scratch with the basics, but that Reeves worked very hard.

Judo came later, for John Wick. Reeves told Men's Journal he had never done judo before and that you "really gotta throw someone". Director Chad Stahelski told Men's Health the team "threw away the actor thing and trained him as a stunt guy".
