- Separately, a Yahoo shopping piece drawing on Homes & Gardens praised the beige entryway she showed on Instagram.

Nicole Kidman says the secret to staying level-headed is simple: put the phone down. In an interview with Vogue Australia, reported by [The Independent](https://www.the-independent.com/life-style/nicole-kidman-phone-mental-health-b3074143.html) on 27 October, the Oscar winner said she is often slow to answer messages and does not check her email constantly.

## "I'm just not that person"

The format was a Q&A in which people close to Kidman sent in questions. [Shailene Woodley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shailene_Woodley), her Big Little Lies co-star, opened the topic by teasing that Kidman is "terrible" on her phone and asking what she gets in place of constant scrolling.

Kidman laughed that Woodley is the same, then explained. She can be late with texts and does not watch her inbox all day. According to The Independent, she said that approach lets her stay "emotionally present" and keep "pretty stable mental health".

She was candid that it takes effort. "I will choose to meditate rather than doomscroll," she said, adding that she has doomscrolled herself and that it takes discipline to tell yourself not to. When life gets overwhelming, she said, she heads for nature to realign, and the phone is not the answer.

## The no-phone bedroom rule

Her firmest rule concerns night time. Kidman said the phone never stays in the room while she sleeps. The trade-off, she acknowledged, is that she is not the first person to reply in every group chat, and friends can be annoyed when she misses a message.

She did share one small habit that helps: after reading a text, she swipes to put the blue "unread" dot back, so she remembers to reply later. It is a modest fix, and a relatable one for anyone whose replies live only in their head.

## Rest, film sets and the snooze button

[Troye Sivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troye_Sivan), the Australian singer and actor, asked how she gets enough rest while working so much. Kidman credited her years on film sets, where long waits of four or five hours taught her to switch off. She described lying on a bed and zoning out with minimal thoughts as a form of rest in itself.

She was frank that sleep is still difficult because she travels a lot. But she said she can sleep until about five minutes before she has to leave, does not need a slow coffee to wake up, and has never pressed a snooze button.

Another Big Little Lies castmate, [Alexander Skarsgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd), kept things lighter. He asked whether he should buy a combined washer-dryer or two separate machines. Kidman's answer: it depends on space, and with room to spare, go for separate units.

## A beige entryway draws attention

Away from the interview, Kidman's home has also been in the news. A Yahoo shopping feature published on 26 October looked at an entryway she shared in an Instagram post. It describes a tan Persian rug, a wooden bench, striped beige curtains and a pair of burlwood mirrors either side of a window.

The piece, which says it draws on Homes & Gardens, uses the space as an example of layered neutrals, a look it predicts will be popular in 2027. An interior designer quoted in it, Ellie Brown of Oak&More, advises mixing textures such as velvet and cotton without overloading a room. The article is a shopping feature and includes affiliate links, so treat its trend forecasts as commentary rather than news about Kidman herself.
