**Quick summary**

- [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney) watched Wrexham draw 1-1 with Wolves at the Racecourse Ground on Saturday 7 November.
- The visit came a day after chief executive Michael Williamson left the club following about two years in the job.
- [Shaun Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_Harvey), already a club director, is acting CEO while Wrexham look for a successor.
- Wrexham sit 15th in the Championship after 14 games, with manager [Phil Parkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Parkinson) under growing pressure.

Ryan Reynolds Wrexham ownership was in the spotlight as he and co-owner Rob Mac made the trip to north Wales to watch the club hold second-placed Wolves to a 1-1 draw, one day after the club announced that chief executive Michael Williamson had left. Harvey, who stepped into the role on an interim basis, was greeted with a standing ovation from home supporters.

## A sudden change at the top

According to [The Leader](https://www.leaderlive.co.uk/news/26708781.ryan-reynolds-sees-wrexham-afc-fans-rise-new-acting-ceo/), Williamson left on Friday after just over two years at Wrexham. Harvey was named acting chief executive the same day. [TribalFootball](https://www.tribalfootball.com/article/soccer-championship-rob-mcelhenney-and-ryan-reynolds-fly-in-for-wrexham-clash-after-ceo-departure-this-week-7797c9df-152b-47d1-9afe-5a9e74ef11d7) reports that the club is searching for a permanent replacement.

In a joint statement quoted by both outlets, Reynolds and Rob Mac said Harvey had been "an integral part" of the club's journey. They added that they knew he would "give every support to Phil Parkinson and the lads on the pitch".

Harvey is a familiar face. The Leader says he became a strategic adviser to the owners in 2021 after consulting for them during the takeover, and joined the Wrexham board in December 2023. He also spent almost six years as chief executive of the English Football League and held senior roles at Leeds United and Bradford City.

The timing caught even the club's own people out. Sports Illustrated notes that Williamson had already written his programme notes for the Wolves match, in which he urged supporters to keep up their backing after a midweek win over Middlesbrough.

## What the owners saw on the pitch

Sports Illustrated says it was the first match either owner had attended since the home draw with Watford in August. They watched from the Betty Buzz suite in the Mold Road Stand alongside Harvey. The Leader adds that Reynolds was in attendance with members of the Allyn family.

Harvey's ovation came as he returned to his seat after laying a wreath as part of the club's Remembrance commemorations. Supporters in the Macron Stand stood to applaud him and sang his name, according to The Leader.

On the pitch, Wolves took the lead when Adam Armstrong finished after a defensive mix-up. Wrexham levelled 33 seconds after the restart, with Lewis O'Brien heading in from an Issa Kaboré cross, per Sports Illustrated. Wolves would have gone top of the table with a win, but Wrexham held on through some late pressure and a series of saves from goalkeeper Anthony Patterson.

Parkinson was booked for protesting a foul on Jacques Ekomié, an episode Sports Illustrated says helped turn up the volume at the Racecourse Ground.

## Ryan Reynolds Wrexham project: the league picture

The result does not change a difficult season. TribalFootball says three of Wrexham's four Championship wins after 14 games have come at home, and the side sits 15th. Sports Illustrated points out that the club has spent about US$94 million on signings since promotion to the Championship last year, so the "underdog" label is wearing thin.

The same report says Wrexham had won only one of eight Championship games against sides relegated from the Premier League, losing three. A point against a team of that calibre, and with the fight Parkinson had questioned only a week or so earlier, was a welcome change in tone.

## The stadium project in the background

Away from the football, the owners' bigger investment is taking shape. [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/uk/in-2021-ryan-reynolds-took-control-of-wrexham-as-its-historic-kop-stand-lay-derelict-five-years-later-a-70-million-rebuilding-project-has-approval-for-up-to-7750-seats-with-opening-planned-for-2027/articleshow/135578107.cms), citing Football Stadium Management and Place North West, reports that Wrexham County Borough Council's planning committee backed a larger Kop Stand on 5 October 2026. The stand would hold up to 7,750 seats, taking the ground to just over 18,000.

The paper puts the wider project at £70 million, with construction starting in December 2025 and the stand expected to be fully operational in early 2027.

## What happens next

Wrexham now need a permanent chief executive, and Harvey will fill the gap until one is found. The club has not named a timeline. The festive schedule is close, and Parkinson's side will look to build on the draw.
