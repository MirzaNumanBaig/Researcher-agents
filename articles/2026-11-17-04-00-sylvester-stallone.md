**Quick summary**

- A collection of letters, contracts and other papers from [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone)'s early career is going to auction as *Rocky* turns 50.
- Heritage Auctions is selling it, and bidding is scheduled to close on 1 December.
- The papers belonged to Jane Oliver, Stallone's first manager, who died in 1977.
- A new film, *I Play Rocky*, opens nationwide in the US on 20 November.

A set of documents from the years before Sylvester Stallone became a star is heading to auction, timed to the 50th anniversary of *Rocky*. According to [Billy Penn at WHYY](https://billypenn.com/2026/11/16/rocky-50th-anniversary-stallone-memorabilia-auction/), the archive is listed by Heritage Auctions, with bidding due to close on 1 December. For anyone searching for the Sylvester Stallone Rocky auction, here is what is on offer and where it came from.

## What is in the Sylvester Stallone Rocky auction

The collection belonged to Jane Oliver, who managed Stallone while he was still a struggling actor. Billy Penn reports that it includes an agreement dated 8 October 1973 setting out her representation of him, his early acting résumé and headshots, handwritten notes, and letters from his attorneys during the development and production of *Rocky* in 1975 and 1976.

Heritage has put a value on three lots. The Oliver-Stallone management agreement and its accompanying archive carries an estimate of US$4,000. A separate set of six contracts with the [William Morris Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Morris_Agency) is estimated at US$3,000. A revised draft of the original *Rocky* screenplay is estimated at US$1,000.

Brian Chanes, a senior director at Heritage, told Billy Penn that opening bids are normally set at half the minimum estimate to draw in bidders. He expects the Oliver material and the script to sell well above those figures, pointing to the link with Oliver and the fact that none of it has been offered publicly before.

## Who was Jane Oliver?

Oliver represented Stallone before his breakthrough as [Rocky Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Balboa). She died in 1977, shortly after the film's success made him a Hollywood name. Billy Penn notes that Stallone later dedicated several of the *Rocky* sequels to her memory.

After her death, her belongings passed to the grandfather of the man now selling them, a television producer, and sat in family storage for decades.

## How the papers came to light

The seller is Nicolai Feldman, a Brooklyn-based independent filmmaker whose grandparents were close friends with Oliver and her partner. He told Billy Penn he first found some of the materials after his grandmother died in 2021. Returning to the storage unit last year, he turned up more letters, contracts, headshots and unproduced screenplays.

Feldman said Stallone's drive to get *Rocky* made spoke to his own ambitions. After years of pitching an animated series to networks, he decided to build his own platform, a free streaming service called BrainZap TV. He said any money from the sale would go towards his own films and filmmaking events, and that he has no set price in mind.

## Why Rocky still draws a crowd

Michael Vitez, a Pulitzer Prize-winning former *Philadelphia Inquirer* reporter, co-wrote *Rocky Stories*, a book about visitors who recreate Rocky's run up the Philadelphia Museum of Art steps. He spent a year documenting them. In his view, those runners are the film's greatest tribute and proof of its lasting power.

The auction also lands in the same week as a new film about the making of the original. *I Play Rocky* stars [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) as a young Stallone, and is playing in select theatres before its nationwide US release on 20 November, Billy Penn reports. RELEVANT Magazine has also published a piece praising Ippolito's performance, though its full text could not be read for this article. ## What happens next

Bidding in Heritage's Hollywood/Entertainment Signature Auction is scheduled to close on 1 December. *I Play Rocky* opens nationwide in the US on 20 November. No Australian release date was given in the sources.
