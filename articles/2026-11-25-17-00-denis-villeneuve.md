**Quick summary**

- Ida Brooke, 18, plays Ghanima, the daughter of Chani and Paul Atreides, in Denis Villeneuve's *Dune: Part Three*, according to a Vogue interview published this week.
- Villeneuve called her himself while she was at the Brit School in London, after a self-taped audition she did not know was for Dune.
- Nakoa-Wolf Momoa plays her twin brother, Leto II.
- Brooke also filmed the live-action Gundam movie in Australia and will star in and executive produce the indie drama *Grass Roots*, due to shoot next summer.

[Ida Brooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Brooke) was a film student a few months ago. Now she is one of the newest faces in [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve)'s *Dune: Part Three*, cast as the daughter of Zendaya's Chani and Timothée Chalamet's Paul Atreides. In a profile for [Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/ida-brooke-dune-3-interview), the 18-year-old from west London describes how the role arrived, and it began with a phone at five percent battery.

## A self-tape and a stairwell call

Brooke told Vogue she first sent a self-tape without knowing which film it was for. When her agent named the project, she closed the curtains on a sunny day and watched the first two Dune films back to back. "This is going to really hurt if I don't get this," she remembers thinking.

By the final audition she had talked herself into believing another girl waiting outside had the edge. She gave herself a pep talk in the bathroom: she did not have to win, only make the decision a little harder for the filmmakers.

The next day, at school, a message from an unknown number said Villeneuve wanted to ring in 20 minutes. Her phone was nearly flat. She searched the building for a charger and took the call in a stairwell. Villeneuve warned her about the heat and physical demands of the shoot, then asked if she wanted to join. A friend found her in tears and took a photograph, telling her she might want it one day.

## What the role is

Brooke plays Ghanima, with Nakoa-Wolf Momoa as her twin brother, Leto II, according to Vogue. It is the third film in Villeneuve's Dune series.

She said Zendaya, who had already worked through the desert shoots on the earlier films, taught her how to settle her body enough to perform in difficult conditions. Of the director, she said his imagination is so vivid that you can step into his vision, as long as you are willing to "be a kid".

One keepsake has stayed with her. At the end of filming, silver used in the production was melted down and recast as wrap gifts for the cast. Brooke wears hers daily, and told Vogue it has turned her from a gold person into a silver one.

## Beyond Arrakis

The profile paints Brooke as a cinephile more than a newcomer chasing fame. She watched films on a portable DVD player at six and travelled across London for Saturday acting classes as a teenager. She shares a love of [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) with her father, and says she would like to work with him.

She chose not to attend university, preferring what she calls self-education: meeting writers, reading about psychology and screenwriting, and watching widely. She is writing a screenplay about Vietnam veterans and the pharmaceutical industry.

Her schedule has already moved beyond Dune. Vogue reports she missed her Brit School graduation, and the screening of her final student film, *The Operator*, which won an award, because she was in Australia filming the live-action Gundam movie. Her co-stars, including [Noah Centineo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Centineo) and Sydney Sweeney, improvised a graduation by piling onto a sofa and watching the film with her.

## What happens next

Brooke's next announced project after the Dune shoot and the Gundam film is *Grass Roots*, an indie drama she will star in and executive produce, due to shoot next summer.
