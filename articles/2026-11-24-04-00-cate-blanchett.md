**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) made her first public appearance as Oxford's Cameron Mackintosh Visiting Professor of Contemporary Theatre on Saturday 21 November.
- The conversation took place at the Sheldonian Theatre, which was lit in purple and blue for the occasion.
- Separately, the Displacement Film Fund, which Blanchett supports, has released its first slate of five short films.
- The films mix documentary and narrative work by directors who have experienced displacement.

Cate Blanchett has begun her new life as an academic. On Saturday night, 21 November, the Oscar winner appeared at the Sheldonian Theatre in Oxford for her first public event as the university's visiting professor of contemporary theatre, according to [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/hollywood-star-cate-blanchett-spotted-130000098.html). The same week brought news of her work behind the camera: the first films backed by the Displacement Film Fund are now out.

## Cate Blanchett's Oxford professorship begins

Blanchett was seen leaving the Sheldonian in Broad Street after an on-stage conversation with Jude Kelly CBE, head of St Catherine's College. Yahoo News UK reports that it was her first public appearance in the role, and that the historic theatre was lit in purple and blue for the evening.

The post is the Cameron Mackintosh Visiting Professor of Contemporary Theatre, based at St Catherine's College. Blanchett was named as the next holder in June. She follows [Sir Ian McKellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_McKellen), [Dame Meera Syal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meera_Syal), [Sir Tom Stoppard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Stoppard), Adjoa Andoh and Sir Stephen Fry, all listed by the outlet as earlier holders.

When the appointment was announced, Blanchett said her years of creative practice had let her sharpen feelings into ideas. She called the professorship an "electrifying opportunity" to work in direct creative dialogue with the next generation. Kelly described the appointment as a hugely exciting next chapter for the post, and Sir Cameron Mackintosh, whose 1990 gift founded the role, said he was thrilled.

## The Displacement Film Fund's first five films

Away from Oxford, Blanchett's backing of the Displacement Film Fund has produced its first results. Awards site [The Contending](https://thecontending.com/cate-blanchett-filmmakers-interview/) reports that the inaugural slate debuts this season with five short films. It says the films centre on friendship, kinship, found family and shared belonging.

The group includes both documentary and narrative work, and the directors come from different parts of the world. According to The Contending, the five are:

- **Allies in Exile** by Hasan Kattan, which follows Kattan and his creative partner Fadi Al-Halabi as they wait in a UK asylum hotel.
- **Rotation** by Maryna Er Gorbach, a narrative short about a Ukrainian woman, built around movement and sound.
- **Sense of Water** by Mohammad Rasoulef, about an exiled Iranian writer learning German.
- **Super Afghan Gym** by [Shahrbanoo Sadat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahrbanoo_Sadat), set among women exercising together in a gym.
- **Whispers of a Burning Scent** by Mo Harawe, about a musician put before a courtroom after an accusation involving his wife's age.

The site says some of the Oscar-qualified films are available to watch through The New Yorker.

## Why Blanchett backs the fund

Blanchett framed the project around scale. "With one in seventy people on earth displaced by conflict, war and persecution," she said, storytelling that crosses divides matters, and short films by gifted directors who have experienced displacement are one way to start conversations and "build bridges back to our common humanity", as quoted by The Contending.

### What is Cate Blanchett's role at Oxford?

She is the Cameron Mackintosh Visiting Professor of Contemporary Theatre at St Catherine's College, University of Oxford. She was announced in June and gave her first public event on 21 November.
