**Quick summary**

- [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) says he first feared viewers would "crucify" him for joking about Parkinson's on *Curb Your Enthusiasm*.
- He made the comments in an interview with Esquire journalist Michael Sebastian, reported by People on 8 November 2026.
- The cameo aired in season 8 of the HBO series in 2011.
- Fox now calls the audience reaction "a big breakthrough" for how people see Parkinson's.

The *Michael J. Fox Curb Your Enthusiasm* story has a new chapter. According to a People report syndicated by Yahoo, Fox has said he was sure the 2011 episode would draw fierce criticism when Larry David first pitched it, and he has now explained why he said yes anyway.

## The phone call that started it

Fox appeared in season 8 of [*Curb Your Enthusiasm*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curb_Your_Enthusiasm), playing himself as Larry David's upstairs neighbour. In the episode, David's fictional version of himself becomes convinced that Fox is deliberately making his life miserable. Fox stomps around in heavy boots and shakes soda before handing it over.

Sebastian described the storyline as one in which Larry accuses Fox of using his Parkinson's "to basically get sympathy". Fox told him that when David rang with the idea, his first reaction was that "they'll crucify us" and "they're gonna kill us if we do this", as quoted by People.

Fox, now 65, was diagnosed with early-onset Parkinson's in 1991 and kept it private until he disclosed it publicly in 1998. That history explains why a comedy built on suspicion about his symptoms felt risky.

## Why he changed his mind

Fox said the idea soon appealed to him. He called the shoot "a great time". He described the work as "pure improv", with a central theme set out in advance, and said that because he was playing himself he "can't screw it up". He also praised David as "so cool and so funny", adding that David would do things he did not expect.

## A real-life moment behind the joke

According to the report, the plot refers to a real incident. The late Rush Limbaugh had believed Fox deliberately shook a soda before handing it to him. Fox joked about the accusation, asking why he would invent a serious neurological disease just to ruin someone's day. He said the experience taught him "to fight for what I believed in", and summed up his position as "research freedom".

## How the episode landed

Fox said "people really loved it" and called the response "a big breakthrough". In his telling, the episode helped audiences accept that people with Parkinson's should reach out for what they need. That is Fox's own assessment, and the People report does not cite audience figures to back it up.
