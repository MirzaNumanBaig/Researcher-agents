**Quick summary**
- RoseBerry Media will recut old *Neighbours* episodes featuring [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie) into vertical microdramas for its Epis app, using AI.
- Two Donna Freedman storylines will become micro-episodes running about 150 minutes in total, according to Variety.
- Fremantle, the show's producer, is a partner in the project.
- Former cast member Stefan Dennis has called the plan "unbelievably invasive, morally and legally wrong", and the actors' union MEAA wants legislation on consent and pay.
- Robbie had not publicly responded at the time of the reports.

Margot Robbie's early career is about to be reshaped for the phone screen. Variety Australia reported on 29 September that RoseBerry Media will turn episodes of *Neighbours* in which she played Donna Freedman into short vertical episodes, with AI doing the editing. The idea has since drawn sharp criticism from a former co-star and the industry's union.

## What is being made

According to [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2026/tv/news/margot-robbie-neighbours-microdrama-episodes-roseberry-40792/), two storylines featuring Robbie's character, who appeared in the soap from 2008 to 2011, will be edited into micro-episodes totalling around 150 minutes. They will launch on Epis, RoseBerry's vertical-video platform.

The edits rely on RedSnapper, RoseBerry's own workflow, which uses AI to convert TV and film libraries into vertical series. Co-founder and CEO Guy Hameiri called the show "an iconic piece of television history" and said it was the first time the tool had been used on a long-running soap.

Fremantle is the series producer. Its commercial and international chief executive, Jens Richter, said the deal lets the company explore its archive "in an entirely new way".

## Why a microdrama

Microdramas began in China as long series cut into one to three minute vertical clips for smartphones, according to [Now To Love](https://www.nowtolove.com.au/entertainment/neighbours-microdramas-margot-robbie/). They lean on soap-opera and telenovela plots, cliffhangers and romance, and have often been dismissed for cheesy writing and low production values.

The business case is large. Now To Love cites Owl & Co's Vertical Economy Report, which expects vertical video to generate $150 billion in revenue in 2026 outside China, and says Chinese microdrama revenue passed the traditional film box office in 2025.

*Neighbours* suits the format on paper. It premiered in 1985 and ran for about 9,363 episodes. It ended in 2025, after a short revival on Amazon Freevee, so there is a deep library to mine. Ensemble storylines would be reworked to follow single characters.

## The backlash

The strongest reaction came from Stefan Dennis, who played Paul Robinson. Speaking to the Herald Sun, as reported by [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/neighbours-star-stefan-dennis-slams-unbelievably-invasive-ai-assisted-microdrama-revival), he called the project "unbelievably invasive, morally and legally wrong". He said his agent was looking into it and that he assumed other cast members were doing the same.

Asked whether it could be stopped, Dennis said: "We're dealing with a very big machine."

Adam Portelli, acting CEO of the Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance, urged the Federal Government to legislate for consent and compensation for performers affected by AI. The union also fears the work of TV and digital editors could be devalued.

Fans have weighed in too. Mediaweek reports that users on the r/Neighbours subreddit described the idea as "slop".

## Where Robbie stands

Robbie, who runs the production company LuckyChamp Entertainment, had not responded to the plan when Mediaweek published. No reply from Fremantle or RoseBerry to the criticism was reported either. Whether the actors' contracts cover this kind of reuse has not been addressed in the coverage we have read.

## What happens next

The micro-episodes are expected to arrive on Epis, though the sources give no release date. Hameiri said the *Neighbours* deal is "just the beginning". RoseBerry also has arrangements with Banijay Rights, All3Media International, A+E Global Media and Cineflix Rights to explore adapting their catalogues, so more classic shows could follow.
