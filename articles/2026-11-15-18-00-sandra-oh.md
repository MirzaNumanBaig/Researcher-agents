**Quick summary**

- [Sandra Oh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandra_Oh) stars in *The Misanthrope*, her National Theatre debut, filmed live from the Lyttelton stage.
- National Theatre Live brings the production to cinemas from 22 September 2026, according to Theatre Weekly.
- The stage run played at the Lyttelton from 16 June to 1 August 2026.
- London Box Office's roundup lists seven reviews, all three stars, with praise for Oh and doubts about the adaptation.
- Cineworld is promoting the screening with a "five reasons to book" feature.

Sandra Oh's turn in *The Misanthrope* is about to reach a far bigger audience than the Lyttelton's seats allow. National Theatre Live will show the production in cinemas from 22 September 2026, and Cineworld has started promoting it as a reason to see the actor on stage.

## What is The Misanthrope?

The play is a new version of Molière's comedy, adapted by Martin Crimp and directed by Indhu Rubasingham, the National Theatre's artistic director. [LondonTheatre.co.uk](https://www.londontheatre.co.uk/theatre-news/news/sandra-oh-to-make-national-theatre-debut-in-the-misanthrope) described the adaptation as a "biting" take that will interrogate 21st-century private relationships and public debate. Molière's grumpy lead is reimagined in this version, with Oh in the central part.

Paul Chahidi and Abigail Cruttenden join Oh in the cast, according to the announcement. Theatre Weekly's cinema-release report names a larger company: Imogen Elliott, Rina Fatania, Freddie MacBruce, Tom Mison and Jemima Rooper.

## Why this is a big moment for Sandra Oh

The production marks Oh's National Theatre debut. In the cinema announcement, quoted by Theatre Weekly, she called it "incredibly special" to make that debut and work with Rubasingham. She added that the National is one of the great homes of theatre and that NT Live lets its work reach audiences worldwide.

Oh is best known on screen, so a lead role at the Lyttelton drew attention before opening night. The cinema release is aimed at people who could not get a ticket, or could not travel to London.

## What the critics said

The reviews were remarkably even. London Box Office's roundup of seven reviews shows every one at three stars, though the comments differ.

- **Praise for Oh:** The Guardian's Arifa Akbar wrote that Oh "brings heart, fire, vulnerability and comic timing". London Box Office's Stuart King called her "on flying form".
- **Doubts about the play:** Time Out's Andrzej Lukowski found it "a chore at times", but said Oh, some good jokes and a wild ending carry it through.
- **More mixed views:** WhatsOnStage's Sarah Crompton felt Oh "can't quite find that range", and The Telegraph's Dominic Cavendish wrote that it often feels like there is an "Oh-shaped hole" in the production.

The Independent's Annabel Nugent said Oh is best in the quieter moments and that the supporting cast does well. The Standard's Nick Curtis called her performance the standout of a "mixed-bag Molière".

The pattern is clear: critics broadly liked the lead and were divided on Crimp's adaptation. A camera may suit that kind of production, since it can stay close on the performances that reviewers singled out.
