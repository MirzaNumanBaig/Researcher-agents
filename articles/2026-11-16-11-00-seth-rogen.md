**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) says [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway) was first cast in the lead female role of *Knocked Up* and left the project.
- He told the A24 podcast the sticking point was a baby "crowning" shot, though he added that he can only go by what she said.
- Rehearsals had already begun when she walked away, according to Rogen.
- [Katherine Heigl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Heigl) played the role, and the film grossed $219.9 million, as reported by Far Out Magazine.

Seth Rogen has explained why Anne Hathaway quit [*Knocked Up*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up) in 2007, saying she did not want the film's graphic birth imagery to be part of her work. Speaking on the A24 podcast, Rogen said he took her stated reason at face value, according to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/in-2007-anne-hathaway-quit-a-seth-rogen-movie-because-of-a-graphic-birthing-scene/).

## What Seth Rogen said on the A24 podcast

The topic came up when [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde) asked Rogen whether it was true that Hathaway left over the crowning scene, the moment a baby's head appears during delivery. Rogen confirmed that Hathaway "quit the movie" and that crowning was what he remembers being told.

He was careful not to claim more than he knew. "It could have been a hundred million things," he said, adding that the crowning was the reason given to him. He also pointed out that the shot would not have involved her directly, and that it was clearly not real. Even so, he said, she felt it was "not her brand".

Rogen then offered a second possibility, without pushing it. The team had started rehearsing, and he wondered whether she may simply have concluded the film was not for her. He said he would accept her explanation as given.

## How Knocked Up turned out

[*Knocked Up*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up) was directed by [Judd Apatow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judd_Apatow). It follows Allison, a put-together woman on the rise in her career, and Ben, a slacker played by Rogen, after a drunken night leads to an unplanned pregnancy. Allison decides to keep the baby, and the mismatched pair try to build something resembling a family.

Far Out Magazine notes that the film had a mixed critical reception on one point in particular: some reviewers felt its female characters were drawn as uptight next to the carefree men. It still appeared on many top ten lists and made $219.9 million, a strong result for a comedy.

The role went to Heigl, who Rogen praised on the podcast. "Katie Heigl was great," he said, and he added that she knew what was right for her. The crowning shot that reportedly caused the exit was not used in the final film, according to Far Out.

## Where Hathaway's career went next

At the time, Hathaway was building a high-profile run that included *The Devil Wears Prada* and [*Brokeback Mountain*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokeback_Mountain). Instead of Apatow's comedy, she played [Jane Austen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Austen) in [*Becoming Jane*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becoming_Jane), also released in 2007.

Far Out Magazine suggests the decision may have suited her, as she may have worried about being typecast in a different kind of film. That is the outlet's reading, not something Hathaway has said in the sources reviewed here. Rogen was generous about the call. "She has been right about a lot more things than I have over the years," he said.
