**Quick summary**

- More than 400 UK entertainment professionals have signed an open letter calling for an end to a "culture of open secrets", according to The Hollywood Reporter.
- It follows the sentencing of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) to 10 and a half years in prison for rape and sexual assault.
- The letter was written by Ben Fletcher and co-organised by Alexa Morden. Signatories include Natalie Dormer and Richard Armitage.
- Separate allegations against David Walliams, which he denies, are also named in the letter as a reason for urgency.

More than 400 people who work in British film, television and theatre have put their names to an open letter demanding that studios, broadcasters and unions stop protecting powerful figures. [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/russell-brand-open-letter-400-industry-figures-1236756889/) says the letter was released on Monday, days after Brand was sentenced for rape and sexual assault.

## What the open letter says

The letter is billed as an "open letter to the creative industry, from the creative industry" and sits on its own website, makethisaturningpoint.com. Writer-actor-producer Ben Fletcher wrote it, and Alexa Morden co-organised it, according to The Hollywood Reporter. Among the signatories are actors, directors and producers, including Natalie Dormer and Richard Armitage.

It asks those in charge to "review your processes, protect those who speak out" and to be less offended by being named for inaction than by the actions they are asked to recognise. It also tells the government to "act, not debate", by supporting victims, fully resourcing police forces and forcing accountability on those who "know but choose convenient ignorance".

The opening lines resist the idea that this is a turning point. Accepting that label, the authors write, would ignore the fact that it took some 17 years for justice. They add that one witness in the trial received death threats online, and that Brand's lawyer threatened the reporter who uncovered his crimes during the trial.

## The sentence behind it

The BBC describes Brand, 51, as a convicted rapist serving a sentence of more than 10 years for one count of rape and three of sexual assault. He started his sentence at HMP Wandsworth in south-west London and was then moved to HMP Littlehey, a Category C prison in Cambridgeshire, according to the BBC. Our earlier report covers [the move to HMP Littlehey](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-prison-littlehey/) in more detail.

The Hollywood Reporter says Brand testified in his own defence at a high-profile London trial. On Monday, a statement from Brand was read out by his wife, Laura Brand, in a video posted to YouTube, in which he maintained his innocence. Justice Secretary Alex Norris called the broadcast "completely unacceptable" and said it was being urgently investigated. We have also covered [the prison message investigation](https://moduscarry.com/russell-brand-prison-message-investigation/).

## "Open secrets" and a wider reckoning

The letter argues that the sector runs on "a belief that fame is a shield and status is a reason to look the other way". It says the lack of formal complaints should not be mistaken for an absence of systemic abuse, because many victims share their stories only quietly. It also calls fear of losing work "a tool of control".

That argument echoes an opinion piece in [The Sydney Morning Herald](https://www.smh.com.au/culture/tv-and-radio/shocked-by-russell-brand-and-david-walliams-you-shouldn-t-be-20261117-p61dyf.html). The Australian comedy and TV writer says the rumours about Brand were long known in the industry's "whisper network", and points to resurfaced clips in which colleagues joked about him and others on air. The piece says that [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) told The Guardian he was "horrified" and "genuinely unaware" of the crimes, and that [Noel Fielding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Fielding) told The Sun he regretted jokes he made alongside Brand. [Lily Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lily_Allen) is quoted saying she laughed along at the time because "it was the culture".

## The David Walliams allegations

The letter says the moment cannot be dismissed as a one-off, because allegations against another person had become public in the same week. That refers to [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams). The Hollywood Reporter says a joint investigation by The Guardian and ITV News includes accounts from two women who say he groomed them after meeting them at 15. Walliams denies wrongdoing. His lawyers told The Guardian that jokes referred to in resurfaced clips "are not statements of fact", according to the SMH piece.

## What happens next

The letter does not set a deadline. It names unions, companies and industry bodies and asks each to act. Whether any will respond publicly is not yet known. The Justice Ministry's investigation into Brand's prison statement is still open, and Norris has said he expects action if rules were broken.
