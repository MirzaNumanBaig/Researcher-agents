[Hudson Williams](https://moduscarry.com/tag/hudson-williams/) has spoken plainly about the intense online attention that followed *Heated Rivalry*. In a preview clip of the second season of CBC's *The Assembly*, reported by [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/entertainment/heated-rivalry-hudson-williams-parasocial-fans) on 30 October, the actor said he can't relate to fans who live through fictional stories, but understands why the show matters to them.

## What he was asked on The Assembly

*The Assembly* is a Canadian program in which a group of autistic and neurodivergent people interview a celebrity guest, according to Bustle. Ahead of the season's return on 1 November, a preview shows one member asking Williams how he views fans becoming emotionally invested in his characters, to the point of forming parasocial relationships with him.

Williams called the question "super interesting" and said he hadn't grasped how far it could go until the show came out. In his words, some people are living so vicariously through a fictional story that it feels more real to them than anything else in their lives. He added that he can't relate, because he has never held on to something that tightly.

The appearance follows an earlier announcement we covered: [Hudson Williams Joins CBC's The Assembly for Season 2](https://moduscarry.com/hudson-williams-joins-cbcs-the-assembly-for-season-2/).

## On fans who ship co-stars

Williams also addressed viewers who believe actors who play a couple should be together off screen. He pointed to well-known screen pairings, naming *Twilight*, *The Notebook* and *Titanic*, and said audiences asked the same thing about those casts. His answer was short: they are actors.

According to Bustle, he has said something similar before about readings of his friendship with co-star [Connor Storrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Storrie). On Threads he once urged fans to stop "RPFing", a fandom term for "real people fiction".

He was not dismissive, though. Williams said he understands where fans are coming from, because the show and its characters mean a lot to them. Even so, he said, "you kind of have to separate art from artists."

## Quoting the showrunner

To explain his position, Williams cited a line from *Heated Rivalry* showrunner [Jacob Tierney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Tierney): "Shane and Ilya are for everyone. Hudson and Connor are for themselves." Shane and Ilya are the two lead characters of the series.

Williams said how much Storrie chooses to show of himself is up to Storrie. It is not for anyone else to ask for more, or to decide it is too little or too much. On what an individual chooses to share, his advice to fans was simple: "Not my business."
