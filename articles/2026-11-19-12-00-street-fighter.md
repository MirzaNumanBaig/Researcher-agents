**Quick summary**

- WWE star Cody Rhodes says The Rock texted him right after his casting meeting for the live-action *Street Fighter* film.
- Rhodes played Guile, and told The Nikki & Brie Show that Legendary Entertainment producer Mary Parent pitched the role to him.
- He says his part grew while he was on set, with extra scenes added.
- The film has had a mixed critical reception, with a 42% Rotten Tomatoes score.

[Cody Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody_Rhodes) has revealed that The Rock sent him a text within minutes of leaving his casting meeting for *Street Fighter*, as if the actor already knew how it had gone. The former three-time Undisputed WWE Champion shared the story on The Nikki & Brie Show with the Bella Twins, according to [Wrestlezone](https://www.wrestlezone.com/news/1672936-cody-rhodes-the-rock-text-street-fighter-casting-meeting).

## What Cody Rhodes said about the meeting

Rhodes met [Mary Parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Parent), a producer at [Legendary Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legendary_Entertainment), who told him the studio wanted him for Guile. By his account she was direct about it and pointed to traits he shares with the character, calling it a good pairing.

He says he walked out feeling he had been set up by a couple of people. Then the message arrived from The Rock, whom Rhodes calls "DJ". In Rhodes' telling, it read: "Good meeting, huh?" The Rock, he said, "clearly knew" what had been discussed.

Wrestlezone reports that Rhodes was excited but also nervous about one conversation in particular. He worried that WWE boss Triple H would not welcome him taking time away for a film. He says it went far better than he feared.

## A small role that grew on set

Rhodes suggested the studio may have felt comfortable making the offer because the part was small at first. That changed during production. He stayed on set longer than planned, a few extra scenes were written, and Guile became a bigger piece of the ensemble.

He said he tried to give it everything and to approach the character seriously. That matches how the performance was received. Wrestlezone says many reviewers singled him out as one of the film's strongest elements and noted that his wrestling background carried over naturally to the screen. Roman Reigns also appears in the film as Akuma, according to the same report.

## How the film was received

*Street Fighter* was directed by [Kitao Sakurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitao_Sakurai) and stars [Andrew Koji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Koji) as Ryu, [Noah Centineo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Centineo) as Ken Masters and Callina Liang as Chun-Li. Rhodes and Orville Peck are also in the cast. According to [Wikipedia's entry on the film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(2026_film)), it opened on 12 October 2026.

Critics were split. The same entry lists a 42% score on Rotten Tomatoes and 43 out of 100 on Metacritic from 28 critics. The Rotten Tomatoes summary credits the film with affection for the games and plenty of style, but faults its incoherent storytelling and juvenile humour.

Not every reviewer agreed. [The Clarion](https://www.theonlineclarion.com/arts-culture/2026/11/17/street-fighter-delivers-fast-action-for-video-game-enthusiasts/) called it fast-paced and faithful to the franchise, praised the fight choreography, and compared its philosophical streak to the martial arts films of [Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee). The reviewer also said 50 Cent does well as Balrog and that Rhodes does a great job as Guile. The story is set in 1993, a nod to fans who grew up with the arcade games, which began with the original *Street Fighter* in 1987.

## What happens next

Wrestlezone's report carries no word on a sequel or on Rhodes returning as Guile. For now, the story is more about a wrestler's route into Hollywood than about the film's future. Rhodes' account suggests that a producer's pitch, a friend's nudge and a boss's blessing were enough to get him there.
