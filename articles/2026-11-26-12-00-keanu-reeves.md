**Quick summary**

- Director [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) says the planned Constantine 2 will be an R-rated film.
- He made the comments on Kevin McCarthy's On Film podcast, as reported by ScreenRant on 25 November 2026.
- The 2005 original was made as a PG-13 film and still received an R from the ratings board, Lawrence says.
- The sequel has no confirmed release date, and its future is tied to changes at DC Studios.

The Constantine 2 sequel will be a properly R-rated film, according to the director who made the first one. [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence) told Kevin McCarthy's On Film podcast that he has wanted to make the R-rated version since 2005, and that he is still talking with [Keanu Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keanu_Reeves) and others about a sequel, [ScreenRant reports](https://screenrant.com/keanu-reeves-constantine-2-r-rated-update-francis-lawrence/).

## Why the first film got an R anyway

Lawrence says the 2005 film was built to earn a PG-13. He told the podcast the team "followed all the rules" on violence, language, nudity and sexuality, partly because the film was expensive. He put the budget at US$96 million, according to ScreenRant.

The [Motion Picture Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_Picture_Association), then called the MPAA, still handed it an R. Lawrence said the board's reasoning was that it "stopped taking notes five minutes in". He added that every Hunger Games film he has made also came back from the board with an R at first, which started a round of negotiation.

His case was that the film sat in a grey zone. He says he pointed to [Peter Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Jackson)'s Lord of the Rings films, which he described as far more violent. The board's answer, as he tells it, was that elves and orcs are not real, while many people do believe in angels and demons. That mix of intensity and religious imagery was enough for the R.

## What Lawrence wants to do differently

Asked whether he went back and added the content that normally earns an R, Lawrence said no. His comment on the podcast was that if he had known the film would get an R, he would have made an R-rated movie. That is the reason, he says, he is still developing a sequel with Reeves, writer [Akiva Goldsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiva_Goldsman) and producer J.J. Abrams.

ScreenRant notes that the original kept most of its action among demons and angels, rather than showing graphic gore on human characters. Several human deaths in the film, including a fall from a building and a collision with a speeding car, were handled without much blood.

## Where the Constantine 2 sequel stands

The original, co-led by [Rachel Weisz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Weisz), had mixed reviews and a modest box office result, according to ScreenRant. A cult following grew afterwards, and in 2022 a sequel was confirmed with Reeves, Lawrence and Goldsman returning.

Progress has been slow since. ScreenRant says Reeves and Lawrence have described several pitches to DC Studios, and that a script was finished by 2024. A separate new take on the character, from filmmaker [Francis Galluppi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Galluppi), is also in development. After the departure of [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn) and Peter Safran as the studio's chief executives, Lawrence appeared to confirm that their wider DC Universe plans often held the sequel up, and that talks were still going. ScreenRant describes the film's fate as in limbo.

## What happens next

No start date, release window or studio green light has been announced. For now the update is a statement of intent from the director rather than a production decision. Fans of the comics may note that Reeves' dark hair split opinion in 2005, as ScreenRant recalls, while his performance was widely praised. [Matt Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Ryan_(actor)) played a more adult version of the character in animation.
