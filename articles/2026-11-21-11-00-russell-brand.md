**Quick summary**

- Transcripts of a 2025 deposition by [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) in a New York civil case were unsealed on 20 November 2026, according to the Daily Mail, as reported by Page Six.
- The civil claim was brought by a woman identified only as Jane Doe, who worked as an extra on the 2011 film Arthur. Brand says any sexual encounters were consensual.
- He said he could not "concretely and absolutely rule out" having exposed himself, though he does not remember it.
- Brand is serving 10 years and six months in HMP Littlehey after being convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

Newly unsealed court papers show what [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) told lawyers in a New York civil case, with the transcripts reaching the public on 20 November 2026. Page Six, citing documents first obtained by the Daily Mail, reports that the 51-year-old could not say whether he was married to [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) when the alleged incident took place. The claims in the civil case are allegations, and Brand denies any non-consensual conduct.

## What the civil lawsuit alleges

The case was filed by a woman known only as Jane Doe. According to [Yahoo Lifestyle Canada](https://ca.style.yahoo.com/russell-brands-katy-perry-confession-234444328.html), she worked as a film extra on the 2011 remake of Arthur. She claims Brand exposed himself in front of cast and crew, then later forced himself on her in a bathroom.

The alleged incident dates to July 2010, during filming at Le Cirque in Manhattan. That was three months before Brand married Perry in India in October 2010, [Page Six reports](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/20/celebrity-news/russell-brands-bizarre-admission-about-katy-perry-marriage-revealed-in-newly-unsealed-court-docs/). The pair divorced in 2012.

## What Brand said in the deposition

Asked whether he had ever exposed himself to anyone without consent, Brand did not give a flat denial. "The truth is, I feel like I must have done, even though I don't really remember it," he said, adding that he found the idea embarrassing and could not rule it out, as quoted by Yahoo Lifestyle Canada.

On the Perry question, he told the questioner it would be easy to look up whether he was married at the time. What he did know, he said, was that he was in a relationship with "a very high-profile woman" and that even in marriage he was "sort of drowning in love", according to the [Yahoo and Page Six reports](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/russell-brand-bizarre-admission-katy-212526916.html).

The transcripts also include an exchange about the word he uses for male anatomy. Brand described it as ordinary British usage and said he objected to the idea of mixing an erotic request with that vocabulary. Throughout, he maintained that every sexual encounter he has had was consensual.

He also said his behaviour reflected a culture of "celebrated male behavior" that ran from the 1960s to the Me Too movement in 2017, according to Yahoo Lifestyle Canada.

## How this fits with the criminal case

The deposition took place in Manhattan in 2025, well before the UK trial. Page Six notes that any mention of the Brand-Perry marriage was kept out of the recent criminal trial.

That trial lasted nearly four weeks. Brand denied wrongdoing throughout, but was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, covering incidents from the 2000s. He was sentenced to a total of 10 years and six months.

He was moved to HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire on 13 November, a prison that mainly holds male sex offenders, Page Six reports. A statement he wrote there, read out by his wife Laura Gallacher, again said he was innocent. Page Six says that message is being "urgently investigated" and that he reportedly faces punishment over it. Since the verdict, six more women have made allegations about the same period, according to the same report. Those claims have not been tested in court.

The wider reaction continues. In [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/20/russell-brand-rape-verdict-shame-men-women), columnist Jonathan Freedland argued that the debate sparked by the Brand verdict, which focused on famous men and the comedy industry, risks missing a broader problem.
