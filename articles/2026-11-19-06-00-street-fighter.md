- The [Street Fighter movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Fighter_(2026_film)) has reached about US$100 million worldwide, against a reported budget of US$82 million, according to Collider.
- Collider says a film this size usually needs roughly twice its budget to break even in cinemas.

The Street Fighter movie box office has passed US$100 million worldwide, but that is unlikely to be enough to cover the cost of the film. Collider reported on 18 November that the Capcom adaptation has probably crossed its last major milestone, with the studio now relying on home and streaming revenue to make up the gap. Meanwhile, Cody Rhodes has been explaining how close he came to turning down the Hollywood detour.

## How the Street Fighter movie box office stacks up

According to [Collider](https://collider.com/street-fighter-box-office-100-million-removed-from-2700-theaters/), the film has now earned almost exactly US$100 million globally on a reported US$82 million budget. That is a return of about 1.2 times the cost. The site notes that a release of this scale typically needs close to double its budget to turn a theatrical profit.

The theatre count tells the same story. Street Fighter opened in nearly 3,600 US cinemas. Collider says it now plays in a little more than 800, after being removed from about 2,700 screens.

Collider also draws a comparison with other game adaptations this year. Zach Cregger's Resident Evil generated more than US$410 million worldwide, while Street Fighter has taken roughly a quarter of that. Mortal Kombat II made a little over US$80 million, which puts Street Fighter slightly ahead of it but well behind the year's winners.

Director Kitao Sakurai leads a cast that includes [Noah Centineo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Centineo) and [Andrew Koji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Koji), with Callina Liang, Orville Peck and [Jason Momoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Momoa) also on the bill. Collider points out that neither lead has had much luck with big franchises so far.

## Why critics are divided

Reviews have been mixed. The film holds 42% on Rotten Tomatoes and 43 out of 100 on Metacritic, based on 28 critics. The critics' summary on its Wikipedia page says it shows real affection for the games and plenty of visual flair, but that muddled storytelling and juvenile humour let it down.

Not everyone agrees. A review in [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/campus/news/street-fighter-unapologetically-fun-and-faithful-love-letter-the-games-4327186) calls it an "unapologetically fun" and faithful tribute, praising the fast, exaggerated fight scenes, the 1993 setting and the anime-style 2D flashbacks. The same review says M Bison is the weakest part of the film and that Chun-Li fades near the end.

The story follows Ryu and Ken as they enter the second World Warrior tournament, run by the dictator M Bison, while Chun-Li works undercover for Interpol. It mostly draws on Street Fighter II.

## Cody Rhodes and the call to Triple H

The WWE champion plays Guile in the film. Speaking on The Nikki & Brie Show, as reported by [Sacnilk](https://www.sacnilk.com/news/cody-rhodes-admits-he-was-terrified-to-tell-triple-h-about-his-street-fighter-movie-role), Rhodes said he was thrilled to land the part but dreaded breaking the news to Triple H, WWE's Chief Content Officer. He said the conversation went far better than he feared and that Triple H was never the kind of boss who would be angry.

Rhodes believes he was offered the role in part because it looked small. It grew during production. He stayed on set longer than planned and picked up extra scenes that helped flesh out the ensemble. Sacnilk adds that co-star [Andrew Schulz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Schulz), who plays Dan Hibiki, said on the Flagrant podcast in October that the filmmakers added Rhodes to more scenes after seeing his work.

[Roman Reigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Reigns) also appears in the film as Akuma, so two of WWE's biggest names share the cast. Earlier in October, Rhodes credited Triple H and WWE President Nick Khan for helping him balance filming with wrestling.

## What happens next

The theatrical run is winding down, so the final worldwide total is unlikely to move much from here. Collider expects the studio to lean on downstream avenues such as home entertainment and streaming to recover costs. No home release date appears in the sources we reviewed.
