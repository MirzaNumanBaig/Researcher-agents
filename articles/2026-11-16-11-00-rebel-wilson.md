## Rebel Wilson joins an artists' call to save Scott Reef

Rebel Wilson is one of more than 150 Australian artists who have put their names to an open letter urging the federal government to protect Scott Reef from gas drilling. [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/hugh-jackman-rebel-wilson-among-150-plus-australian-artists-calling-for-famous-reef-to-be-saved-42614/) reported the letter on 16 November 2026, with Hugh Jackman also among the signatories.

The letter is addressed to Environment Minister Murray Watt. It asks him to stop plans by energy company Woodside to drill in and around the reef, a remote system off Western Australia's north-west.

## Who else signed

The list reaches well beyond film. According to Variety Australia, the signatories include actor Simon Baker, the band 5 Seconds of Summer, and musicians Missy Higgins, Ninajirachi, Jessica Mauboy, Amy Shark, Tim Minchin and Darren Hayes. Peter Garrett is also named.

That spread of film, pop and music names is what gives the letter its reach. Variety Australia's report puts the number at more than 150, and the full text of the letter is available through Greenpeace's website.

## What the campaign says about the reef

Scott Reef lies roughly 270 km off the Kimberley coast. Woodside plans to drill up to 57 wells in and around it, according to the report. A release from the campaigners says the project is forecast to emit 1.6 billion tonnes of climate pollution. That figure comes from the campaign, not from an independent assessment, and Woodside's response was not part of the coverage we could read.

Missy Higgins described the site as an ancient coral reef that is home to endangered whales, vulnerable turtles and hundreds of other marine species. Hannah Schuch of Greenpeace Australia Pacific said she was heartened by the artists' involvement and urged Watt and the Albanese Government to stop Woodside's plans.

## A wider pattern of protest

The letter follows earlier artist pushback against the company. Variety Australia notes that Tame Impala's Kevin Parker protested against Woodside earlier this year by wearing a Fremantle Dockers jersey with the Woodside logo covered in black tape during a show in the United States. Woodside has sponsored the Dockers since 2010 and in 2024 renewed the deal until at least 2027.

The report does not give a timeline for a government decision on the drilling or describe the approval process. At this stage the letter is a public appeal, and the minister has not been quoted in the coverage we read. We will update this story if Watt or Woodside responds.
