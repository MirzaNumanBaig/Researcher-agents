**Quick summary**

- [Christopher Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Lloyd) has described his "selfish" first reaction to learning that [Michael J. Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Fox) would replace [Eric Stoltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Stoltz) in *Back to the Future*.
- The two actors spoke for [Interview Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_(magazine)), ahead of Lloyd's memoir *In My Own Time*, according to People.
- Fox, 65, said Lloyd made him feel welcome when others on set doubted a television actor.
- Lloyd is 88. The film was made in 1985.

Christopher Lloyd has told his old co-star Michael J. Fox that the news of Fox taking over as Marty McFly stunned him, and that his first thought was about his own work. The conversation, published by Interview Magazine and reported by [People](https://people.com/christopher-lloyd-reaction-michael-j-fox-joining-back-to-the-future-12194373) on 26 October, is a rare chance to hear both actors describe the same turning point from the inside.

## The announcement in the car park

Stoltz was originally cast as Marty, the time-travelling teenager in the 1985 sci-fi classic. According to People, the change came a few weeks into filming. Lloyd recalled that the crew had been shooting overnight at a shopping mall for several days, so lunch fell around one in the morning.

During that break, he said, someone told the cast an announcement would follow the meal. Everyone gathered around a trailer, and Lloyd believes Steven Spielberg stood up to say Fox would take over the role the next morning.

## Why Lloyd felt "selfish"

Lloyd said the news was "pretty stunning". He explained that, thinking only of himself, he felt he had given 99 per cent in every moment shot over six weeks, and was still settling into the character of Doc Brown. Having to start again with a new Marty was a lot to take in, he said, before adding that "it all worked out".

He had warm words for Fox as well. Lloyd told him that when he arrived, "it was a whole different game", and praised his spontaneity, humour and intelligence.

## Fox on being the outsider from TV

Fox recalled that people on set were openly sceptical. In his telling, the line going around was that Stoltz was a real movie star and the production now had someone from television. Lloyd, he said, never gave him that attitude, in part because Lloyd also had a television background.

Fox said he understood that the relationship with Doc was the one that mattered most. If that worked, he thought, he would be safe. He credited Lloyd with being welcoming even though Lloyd had to redo his own work and find a new approach to the character.

He added that Lloyd immerses himself in a role without damaging his relationships with other actors, and does not treat his own process as more important than anyone else's.

## What Fox has said about Stoltz

People notes that Fox wrote about meeting Stoltz, and easing any tension between them, in his 2025 memoir *Future Boy*, 40 years after the role changed hands. Speaking to the magazine about that section, Fox rejected the idea of a villain: he said there was a lot of mythology around the episode, but it was simply something that happened, and that people had different experiences of the same situation before moving on.
