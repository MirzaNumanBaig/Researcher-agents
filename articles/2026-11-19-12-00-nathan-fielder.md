**Quick summary**

- *Love and Cameras in America*, a 2008 documentary by [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder), went online on YouTube on 18 November 2026.
- The rights holder, WildBrain, digitised the film because of renewed interest in Fielder, according to The Globe and Mail.
- The film follows the 2008 US presidential race and was never widely distributed after a Halifax festival screening.
- Interest has surged since Fielder's new film, *You Can See Everything*, which Wikipedia lists as a 97% Rotten Tomatoes hit.

Nathan Fielder's first feature-length documentary, *Love and Cameras in America*, can now be watched for free. The 2008 film was posted on YouTube on 18 November 2026, nearly two decades after it screened at a Halifax film festival and then disappeared from view. For years it was a sort of holy grail for the comedian's most devoted fans.

## How the lost film surfaced

The film's return traces back to reporter Tom Cardoso of The Globe and Mail. Early in 2025 he wrote about his hunt for the documentary, and he says he came close to finding a copy without ever managing to. [Gold Derby](https://www.goldderby.com/film/2026/watch-nathan-fielder-love-and-cameras-in-america-streaming/) reports that the search began in February 2025.

According to [The Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/culture/film-and-tv/film/article-long-lost-nathan-fielder-movie-posted-online-after-nearly-20-years/), the company holding the rights, WildBrain, has now put the film online. A WildBrain spokesperson, Shaun Smith, told Cardoso that the company chose to digitise it because of the increased interest in Fielder following *You Can See Everything*.

Gold Derby says the upload appeared on the Full Length Documentaries channel at about 8am Pacific time on 18 November, and describes the version as remastered. Cardoso announced the news on X, noting that it came 18 months after his original piece.

## What the documentary is about

Fielder was a young talent on the Canadian satire show *This Hour Has 22 Minutes* when he made the film, according to The Globe and Mail. The YouTube description, quoted by Gold Derby, frames it as the story of a comedian with almost no filmmaking experience and no interest in politics who is sent to document the 2008 US presidential election.

The film follows him through political rallies, street interviews and focus groups as he travels the United States. That was the contest between [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and John McCain, as Gold Derby notes.

It also predates his television breakthrough by about five years. Gold Derby points out that the film was shot before *[Nathan For You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_for_You)*, the satirical series on [Comedy Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Central) in which he advised struggling businesses. That show ran from 2013 to 2017.

## Why now: the *You Can See Everything* effect

The timing is no accident. *You Can See Everything*, Fielder's second feature, was co-directed with [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim) and centres on [Elizabeth Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Holmes_(writer)), the former Theranos chief executive. [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) plays Holmes, according to Gold Derby.

Wikipedia lists a 97% Rotten Tomatoes score from 229 reviews and a Metacritic score of 97 from 43 critics. The same page puts its worldwide gross at about US$93 million against a budget of roughly US$7 million. Gold Derby gives a slightly higher figure of US$94 million.

Gold Derby also describes the film as a favourite for several Oscar nominations, with Best Picture, Best Director and Best Supporting Actress for Seyfried among them. The Globe and Mail says the film has propelled Fielder into the Hollywood spotlight, which explains why a rights holder would suddenly care about a forgotten 2008 title.

Closer to home, Australian trade site Mediaweek ran an opinion piece by Guy Marshall of Bashful Group. He argued that Fielder's work is a lesson in authenticity for brands heading into 2027, and called the new film the most culturally attuned release of 2026. It is an opinion column, not a review, but it shows how far his profile has travelled.

## What happens next

Fans can watch *Love and Cameras in America* now. Whether WildBrain plans any further release is not stated in the sources. Cardoso ends his piece hoping Fielder will not make fans wait another two decades for his next feature.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where can I watch Love and Cameras in America?

It is on YouTube, posted on 18 November 2026 on the Full Length Documentaries channel, according to Gold Derby. The Globe and Mail says rights holder WildBrain made the digitised copy available.

### When was Love and Cameras in America made?

It dates from 2008 and covers that year's US presidential election. It screened at a Halifax film festival but was never distributed, per The Globe and Mail.

### Is it Nathan Fielder's first film?

It is described as his debut feature-length documentary. *You Can See Everything* is his second.
