**Quick summary**

- [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie) says she has not used her natural Australian accent on screen since leaving Neighbours in 2011.
- Director Kogonada asked whether she wanted to use it in A Big Bold Beautiful Journey, according to her comments to the Daily Mirror.
- She declined, saying she feared she no longer knew how to do it.
- She added that a day when she does it again may be close.

Margot Robbie has admitted that the accent she grew up with is now the one she is most nervous about. The Australian actor told the Daily Mirror she turned down the chance to speak in her natural voice in A Big Bold Beautiful Journey, as reported by [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/2011-margot-robbie-used-her-real-accent-last-time/) on 7 November 2026.

## What Robbie said about the offer

According to Far Out, the film's director, Kogonada, asked her directly whether she would like to play the part with an Aussie accent. Robbie said she gave it a moment's thought and then decided against it.

"It's been so long since I've acted in my Aussie accent, I'm scared I don't know how to do it now," she told the Daily Mirror. She said she last did it on Neighbours, and that she now uses accents "to kind of hide myself in a character".

Her reasoning was simple: the idea felt like too much. "I did contemplate it for a second and then thought, 'Oh, it's too much,'" she said.

She did not rule it out for good. "There will be a day, and I think I'm getting close to it," she added.

## The last time she used it

Far Out reports that Robbie's final appearance on Neighbours came in 2011, and that this was the last time her own accent was heard in a role. She has described the soap as "perfect training for Hollywood", according to the same report. Neighbours gave her early screen experience and the discipline to build on it.

Since then, the publication notes, she has worked almost entirely in other voices. That includes the title role in Greta Gerwig's Barbie and the anti-hero Harley Quinn in Suicide Squad, both played with American accents. Far Out describes this as a common requirement for non-American actors trying to work in the United States.

## Why it matters to her career

The magazine frames the comments as a rare moment of self-doubt from one of Hollywood's most sought-after leads. It also points to a risk Robbie appears to be aware of: that returning to her natural voice might cost her roles built around an American sound.

The article also recalls the story of her Wolf of Wall Street audition, in which she famously slapped Leonardo DiCaprio. It uses that episode to illustrate the nerve that has marked her career. That anecdote is background in the piece rather than new reporting.
