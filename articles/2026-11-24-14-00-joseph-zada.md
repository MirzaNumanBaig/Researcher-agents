**Quick summary**

- *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* opened to $12.9 million from 518,000 admissions in Australia, according to B&T.
- That is the biggest opening in the [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) franchise locally, beating a record that stood for 13 years.
- Sydney-based [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) plays a young Haymitch Abernathy in the film.
- RUSSH has listed the roles fans can watch next, including *We Were Liars* season two, due in 2027.

Joseph Zada's first turn as the face of a blockbuster has produced a record at home. *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* took $12.9 million across its opening weekend in Australian cinemas, the best debut the franchise has had here, B&T reported on 23 November.

## A record that stood for 13 years

The result tops the $12.5 million opening of 2013's *The Hunger Games: Catching Fire*. It is also more than two and a half times the $5.09 million that the previous instalment, 2023's *The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes*, made in its first weekend. That film went on to gross about $20 million in Australia, according to B&T.

Sunrise on the Reaping is now the third-biggest opening of any film in the country this year. It also helped lift total admissions across cinemas by 48 per cent on the previous weekend, alongside *Ramayana* and *You Can See Everything*.

Val Morgan Cinema managing director Guy Burbidge said that seeing a "homegrown star" lead the franchise to its biggest opening was a fantastic result heading into summer. Younger audiences drove much of it: 71 per cent of the opening weekend crowd was aged 14 to 39.

## Where the film sits in a big year for adaptations

The film is set 24 years before the first *Hunger Games* story and opens on the morning of the reaping for the 50th games, the Second Quarter Quell. Francis Lawrence directs. Zada leads a cast that includes McKenna Grace, Jesse Plemons, Elle Fanning, Ralph Fiennes, Glenn Close, Maya Hawke and Kieran Culkin.

B&T places it in a strong year for book-to-screen titles. *The Odyssey* opened to $13.5 million, *Project Hail Mary* to $6.2 million and *Wuthering Heights* to $6.1 million. Next on the calendar is *Dune: Part Three*, which reaches Australian cinemas on 17 December.

## Where to see Zada next

Zada is 21 and, as RUSSH puts it, the Bondi local has gone from a niche talent to a recognised star in a single year. The magazine has pulled together the roles to chase if Haymitch won you over.

His newest is Cal Trask in Netflix's adaptation of John Steinbeck's *East of Eden*, a part first played on screen by [James Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dean) in 1955. Zada told The Hollywood Reporter the director asked him repeatedly not to watch the original. The story comes from Steinbeck's [1952 novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_Eden_(novel)).

In *We Were Liars* he plays Johnny Sinclair, one of four cousins who spend summers on a private island. He told Polyester that he took up tennis again to prepare, studied John McEnroe for Johnny's energy and read [F. Scott Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._Scott_Fitzgerald)'s *The Great Gatsby*. Season two is due in 2027 and his character returns, according to RUSSH.

Closer to home, he played Charlie Roth in [*Invisible Boys*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invisible_Boys), set in Western Australia in 2017 during the national same-sex marriage debate. He also has a small part in *Total Control* opposite [Deborah Mailman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deborah_Mailman) and [Rachel Griffiths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Griffiths).
