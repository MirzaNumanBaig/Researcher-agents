**Quick summary**

- Irving Azoff's Music Artists Coalition has joined a court filing in the New York Times' copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft, according to Music Business Worldwide.
- The group signed on with the Human Consent Foundation, a nonprofit co-founded by [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett), and talent agency CAA.
- On October 30 they asked the court for permission to file a brief on whether rights to creative works can be licensed for AI use at scale.
- The brief takes no position on how the judge should rule on the pending motions.

Cate Blanchett's AI campaign has picked up a notable ally from the music business. Irving Azoff's Music Artists Coalition (MAC) has joined a proposed brief in the New York Times' copyright case against OpenAI and Microsoft, [Music Business Worldwide reported](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/irving-azoffs-music-artists-coalition-joins-cate-blanchett-and-caa-on-ai-licensing-brief-in-new-york-times-case-against-openai/). The group signed alongside Blanchett's Human Consent Foundation (HCF) and CAA, and the request to file went to the court on October 30.

## What the Cate Blanchett AI licensing brief asks

The case combines the Times' lawsuit with others against OpenAI and Microsoft, including claims from book authors. Both sides have asked Judge Sidney Stein to decide, without a trial, whether training AI models on copyrighted works without a licence counts as "fair use". That is the US copyright exception allowing some reuse without permission.

The HCF-led group is not choosing a side. Its filing says it takes "no position on the ultimate disposition" of the motions. Instead, it raises a narrower question: whether rights for AI training can practically be identified, cleared and licensed at scale, and whether a working market for those uses exists.

HCF publishes a machine-readable standard that lets creators and rightsholders state how AI systems may use their work and likeness, according to the filing. MBW reports the group says it draws on experience in creative-rights licensing, representation and consent infrastructure.

## Who has signed on

According to MBW, the filing's other signatories include the National Association of Voice Actors, enterprise AI company Writer, the Creators Coalition on Artificial Intelligence, Blanchett herself, filmmaker [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh) and 311 frontman [Nick Hexum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Hexum). Soderbergh is a founding board member of HCF, and Hexum, like Blanchett, co-authored its consent standard.

TheWrap, which obtained a copy of the brief, reports that it answers the tech companies' "dispersed owner" argument by pointing to collective licensing, citing CISAC and BMI as examples. It also argues that creative industries already have "a proven infrastructure and workflows to clear rights for dispersed works at scale". TheWrap adds that an appendix lists supporters including Madonna, [Bette Midler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bette_Midler), [Anne Hathaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hathaway) and [Emma Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Thompson).

For earlier coverage of the group's involvement, see our report on [Cate Blanchett's consent group backing the NYT in the OpenAI case](https://moduscarry.com/cate-blanchetts-consent-group-backs-nyt-in-openai-case/).

## The music industry's separate brief

The same day, the RIAA, NMPA, ASCAP, BMI and five other music organisations filed their own proposed brief, which formally backs the plaintiffs. They ask Judge Stein to rule that OpenAI and Microsoft's training on the plaintiffs' works is not fair use. The other co-signers are A2IM, Songwriters of North America, the Black Music Action Coalition, the Recording Academy and the Artist Rights Alliance.

These groups are not parties to the case, and MBW notes their briefs do not seek damages. Even so, the stakes reach beyond the Times. A ruling for the tech companies could hand AI firms already sued by the music business, including Suno, Udio and Anthropic, a New York federal court decision to cite in their defence. Sony Music has two suits against Udio pending in the same court.

The RIAA brief argues that AI models are trained on works such as a John Grisham novel or a [Stevie Wonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stevie_Wonder) song to produce close substitutes, not to learn about language in general. It also says the US Justice Department has compared AI training to [Joan Didion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Didion) drawing on [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway) as an influence.

The court has yet to rule on whether the HCF group may file its brief, and Judge Stein still has the fair use motions to decide. Everything published on the case so far is gathered on our [Cate Blanchett](https://moduscarry.com/tag/cate-blanchett/) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the Human Consent Foundation?

It is a nonprofit co-founded by Cate Blanchett. It publishes a machine-readable standard that lets creators and rightsholders declare how AI systems may use their work and likeness, according to the filing.

### Is Cate Blanchett suing OpenAI?

No. Her group's brief takes no position on the final outcome of the motions. It addresses whether AI training rights can be cleared and licensed at scale.

### Who is Irving Azoff's Music Artists Coalition?

Quoting its own website, the filing describes MAC as focused on protecting the rights, financial security and creative freedoms of musicians.
