**Quick summary**

- [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson), 59, shared a new video this week of her estate in Ladysmith, British Columbia, according to the New York Post.
- The roughly 6-acre property was bought by her grandparents in the 1950s, and she bought it from them in the early 1990s.
- The New York Post says her Malibu house sold for $11.8 million in 2021, after being listed at $14.9 million.
- Harper's Bazaar UK separately reports that she has a new "soft wolf" haircut.

Pamela Anderson has given fans another look at her Vancouver Island estate, and the video this week is a gentle one: a sweater pulled on in the sunroom, a few blooms cut from the garden, a walk down the dock at sunrise. The [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/06/real-estate/inside-pamela-andersons-new-life-of-solitude-in-canada/) describes the 59-year-old as living a "cottage-core" life in her native Canada.

## What the new video shows

The clip opens in the sunroom, where Anderson puts a cosy sweater over her dress and heads outside with her dog. She snips flowers, then sits down with a well-thumbed copy of "Amor and Psyche: The Psychic Development of the Feminine".

"I've read this book so many times," she says in the video. A small card tucked between the pages reads "There's nothing to worry about."

Her woven garden basket holds pruning shears, her glasses and a jar of moisturiser from Sonsie, her vegan skincare brand. "I love the garden after it's rained," she says. Fans responded warmly. One commenter compared the scene to a [Nora Ephron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nora_Ephron) film, according to the Post. We covered an earlier clip in [Pamela Anderson Shares Autumn Video From Her Island Home](https://moduscarry.com/pamela-anderson-vancouver-island-home/).

## A property with family history

The estate sits in Ladysmith, the small coastal town where Anderson was born. Her grandparents bought the parcel in the 1950s, the Post reports, citing the Vancouver Sun, and she bought it from them in the early 1990s. She did not move back permanently until 2019.

Today it includes a main house called "The Roadhouse", the cabin where she spent her childhood, rose and vegetable beds, a large porch and a long private pier.

"I always knew that I would come back here," she told the Vancouver Sun, as quoted by the Post. She added that having the place would help her keep "a little bit of my sanity and a little bit of my feet on the ground."

## The Malibu sale behind the move

The Post ties the homecoming to the sale of her Malibu beachfront house. Anderson paid about $1.8 million for the lot in 2000 and spent roughly ten years building on it. She listed the home in March 2021 at $14.9 million with Compass agent Tomer Fridman, and it closed six months later at $11.8 million.

The Post puts the gap between the land price and the sale price at roughly $10 million, before construction costs. That figure is the outlet's own calculation and does not account for what the build cost her.

## From renovation to TV series

The Canadian renovation became the subject of her Hulu series "Pamela's Garden of Eden", which follows her move away from Hollywood and her work to restore the land her grandmother once held. "It became like an art project," she told Today, as quoted by the Post.

The project was led by Vancouver designer Francesca Albertazzi. One of the builders, Dan Hayhurst, married Anderson on the property on Christmas Eve 2020. The Post says they split a little over a year later and she filed for divorce in Canada.

Anderson shares two sons, Brandon and Dylan, with her ex-husband [Tommy Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lee). The Post notes she has said she misses her sons, who still live in the United States. This year, she said, she was happy to spend time "just on my own with my dogs and my vegetables and the trees and the ocean."

## The new "soft wolf" haircut

Away from the garden, Anderson's hair is getting attention. [Harper's Bazaar UK](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/uk/beauty/hair/a74356030/soft-wolf-cut-pamela-anderson/) calls her latest look a "soft wolf" cut, a gentler take on the 1970s-inspired wolf cut with choppy layers and face-framing pieces.

Hairdresser Anita Rice, co-founder of Buller and Rice salons, told the magazine the shape stays "feminine and ageless" because the edges are softened. She advises keeping the hair around the temples longer, since cutting it shorter can look harder. For fine hair, she warns against over-texturing, which can make it look thinner.
