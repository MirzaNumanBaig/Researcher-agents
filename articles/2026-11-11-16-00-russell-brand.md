**Quick summary**

- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) was sentenced on Monday 9 November to 10 years and six months in prison at Southwark Crown Court in London.
- He was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, covering four women between 2004 and 2009.
- Brand pleaded not guilty. He must serve two-thirds of the term before he can be considered for parole, according to People.
- The judge also criticised the "enablers" who watched some of the assaults happen.
- Comments by his ex-wife [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) have resurfaced, and [Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) has issued a statement.

The Russell Brand sentencing ended on Monday with a prison term of 10 years and six months, handed down at London's Southwark Crown Court. The 51-year-old comedian and actor had been convicted on 5 November of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three others, after a trial that ran for several weeks.

## What the court heard and decided

According to [Yahoo Entertainment](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/katy-perry-very-controlling-comments-203044207.html), the Crown Prosecution Service confirmed the convictions related to crimes against four women committed between 2004 and 2009. [People](https://people.com/katy-perry-hasnt-followed-russell-brand-life-since-split-exclusive-source-12247437) reports that the allegations put to the jury stretched from 1999 to 2009, and that Brand denied all of them.

Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy said after the hearing that Brand "used his influence, status and fame to exploit and sexually offend against women". He added that he hoped the conviction sent "a clear message that no one is above the law, no matter how famous or powerful they may be."

The sentence is long by any measure, yet the severity is not the only thing people are discussing. The judge's own words have drawn as much attention.

## The judge's message on rape myths and enablers

Mr Justice Bennathan told Brand that, whatever women wear, wherever they go and whatever they drink, they keep the right to say no. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2026/nov/10/judge-sentenced-russell-brand-praised-rape-myths-justice-bennathan) reports that the Criminal Bar Association praised those remarks for their "piercing clarity", noting that rape myths had played a part in the trial.

Ciara Bergman, chief executive of Rape Crisis England & Wales, called the remarks "hugely important". She contrasted them with what she described as the myths put forward by Brand's defence barrister, such as the idea that a credible victim must have fought, screamed or reported immediately. Labour's deputy leader, Lucy Powell, also called the sentencing remarks "incredibly powerful".

## What the Russell Brand sentencing says about enablers

Bennathan reserved some of his sharpest language for the people around Brand. He said two of the assaults took place in front of others, one at a radio station and one in a TV studio. Of the radio incident he said companions "no doubt" saw it as outrageous laddish behaviour, but it was also a criminal sexual offence. The studio assault, he said, showed Brand's "staggering arrogance" and how his entourage indulged him.

Bergman told The Guardian the case was "about more than just one man". In her view it concerned everyone who laughed along, stayed quiet or looked away, and so contributed to a culture in which he kept harming women without consequence for years.

## Katy Perry's old comments resurface

Brand married Perry in India on 23 October 2010 and filed for divorce in December 2011. As Yahoo notes, Perry told Vogue in 2013 that he ended the marriage by text, and that she had first blamed herself until she learned the "real truth", which she did not explain. She also described him as having struggled with her being "the boss on tour".

Those remarks, along with a 2010 clip in which Brand holds up a badge reading "Obey", have circulated widely since the verdict. They are old comments from a personal relationship, not evidence in the case.

An unnamed source told People that Perry has not followed Brand's life since the split. "She knows their divorce was ultimately for the best," the source said. People says it asked Perry's representative for comment. The claim is a single anonymous account and has not been confirmed on the record.

## Jonathan Ross responds

Broadcaster Jonathan Ross said through his lawyer that he was "horrified" by the "crimes of Russell Brand" and by separate allegations about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), according to [Nine](https://www.nine.com.au/entertainment/celebrity/jonathan-ross-breaks-silence-david-walliams-russell-brand-20261111-p61d6j.html). Ross said he had been on friendly terms with both men and was unaware of the conduct at the time. The allegations against Walliams come from a joint ITV News and Guardian investigation; they are allegations only, and nothing in that report establishes guilt.

## What happens next

Under the terms reported by People, Brand must serve two-thirds of his sentence before parole can be considered. Campaigners quoted by The Guardian say the focus should now include the institutions and individuals around him, not only the man himself.
