**Quick summary**

- UK ministers are considering changing the law to force the entertainment industry to better protect women and children, [The Guardian reports](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2026/nov/11/tv-entertainment-industry-protect-women-david-walliams-allegations-nandy).
- The move follows a joint Guardian and ITV News investigation into [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), 55, and the jailing of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand).
- Walliams denies the allegations and says he is "not a sexual predator".
- His cameo in the Australian film Fing! has been cut, and Stan and Sky have dropped the film. It was due in Australian cinemas on 14 January.

Culture Secretary [Lisa Nandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisa_Nandy) is expected to meet leaders from television, film and publishing next week, and potentially sport, as the UK government weighs legislation on abuse and harassment protections. The talks come in the wake of the allegations against David Walliams, which he denies, and the conviction of Russell Brand.

## What ministers are considering

According to The Guardian, the government would prefer the industries to strengthen protections voluntarily, but it could act if it judges the response too weak. One official told the paper the instinct was to let industries sort it out themselves, "but we are not squeamish about stepping in if that does not happen".

One option floated is making it mandatory for companies to join the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority, which handles bullying and harassment complaints in the sector through confidential whistleblowing. It is not yet known which organisations will attend or what ministers will ask of them.

Justice Secretary Alex Norris told a House of Lords committee that there was "a lot of concern" about powerful men using injunctions or non-disclosure agreements to silence accusers. [Jess Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jess_Phillips), until May the Home Office minister responsible for tackling violence against women and girls, said the Walliams case was "an outlier" because the injunction attempt failed. Baroness [Louise Casey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Casey%2C_Baroness_Casey_of_Blackstock), who leads an inquiry into grooming gangs, also called for changes to how sex with young teenagers is charged.

## What the investigation alleges

The investigation, published after a court dismissed an attempt by Walliams to stop it, includes accounts from multiple women. [The Guardian's explainer](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/nov/11/what-is-david-walliams-accused-of) says dozens more people raised concerns about his conduct, including young fans and models messaged out of the blue on social media, and people who described inappropriate behaviour in workplaces.

Two women say they were 15 when they first met him. Others, including a woman who says she met him on the Britain's Got Talent set when she was 18, say he used his fame to pursue them. His lawyers deny the allegations from the four women named in the report, though he does not deny having sex with one of them, who says it was consensual but that she never felt in control.

Production companies behind Britain's Got Talent and broadcaster ITV said they were not aware of any complaints. HarperCollins dropped Walliams in December 2025 after an earlier inquiry into alleged inappropriate behaviour towards young women at work.

## His response

Speaking to ITV News, as quoted by [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/david-walliams-jonathan-ross-statement-comedy-awards-joke-1237220082/), Walliams said: "I am not a sexual predator… I have not broken the law." He said he regretted behaving thoughtlessly at times and not always considering people's feelings, but that abusing a power imbalance "is something that I never did". Deadline also reports that his lawyers told the court he had twice tried to take his own life because of the allegations.

## Fing! and the industry fallout

Transmission Films confirmed Walliams will not take part in the Australian publicity campaign for Fing!, which he co-wrote with Kevin Cecil. His cameo has been removed. The film stars [Taika Waititi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taika_Waititi), [Mia Wasikowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Wasikowska) and [Blake Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Harrison), and is directed by [Jeffrey Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Walker_(director)).

Stan will no longer stream it, and Sky is exiting its UK and Ireland licence. US distributor Angel Studios has also removed the cameo. For more, see our earlier report on [Fing! being dropped by Stan and Sky](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-allegations-fing/).

[Jonathan Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Ross) said he was "horrified" and called a 2004 British Comedy Awards joke about Walliams "tasteless". He said he believed it referred to Walliams publicly dating younger women. Deadline notes there is no suggestion Ross knew of the alleged behaviour. Walliams has also been dropped by his agent, and Little Britain has been removed from BBC iPlayer, according to the same report. Our [fallout coverage](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-allegations-fallout/) tracks the wider response.

## What happens next

The ministerial meeting is expected next week. Whether the government legislates will depend on how the industries respond. Any decision on Fing! outside Australia, the US and the UK is unclear, as it is not known whether the cast and crew will want it shown.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are ministers considering after the David Walliams allegations?

They are weighing legal changes to force entertainment bodies to protect women and children better, possibly by making membership of a standards authority mandatory. Voluntary action is the preferred first step.

### Has David Walliams responded?

Yes. He denies all the allegations, says he is not a sexual predator and has not broken the law, and says he regrets some of his past behaviour.

### What is happening to the film Fing!?

His cameo has been cut, and he is out of the publicity campaign. Stan will not stream it and Sky is exiting its UK licence. The Australian release was scheduled for 14 January.
