**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) has said he could tell during the making of 2012's The Guilt Trip that the comedy was not working, according to Far Out Magazine.
- He blames the film's PG-13 rating, saying he cannot be as funny in that kind of universe.
- The film, co-starring Barbra Streisand, barely recouped its budget, and Streisand earned a Golden Raspberry nomination for Worst Actress.
- Rogen bounced back in 2013 with This Is the End, which Far Out says made almost $100 million in profit.

Seth Rogen has spoken candidly about a film he believed was heading for trouble before it was even finished. [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-2012-movie-seth-rogen-knew-was-doomed/) reported on 31 October 2026 that the actor sensed on set that 2012's The Guilt Trip was not landing, even though he thought the script was strong.

## What Rogen said about The Guilt Trip

The Guilt Trip is a road-trip comedy in which a travelling salesman takes his mother across the country, secretly hoping to reunite her with an old boyfriend. [Barbra Streisand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbra_Streisand) played the mother opposite Rogen.

According to Far Out, Rogen has said that the script was "really great", but that during filming there was a sense that "this is not making us laugh really hard". He pointed to a shared trait in the PG-13 films he made in that period and said he tried for a year or two to be funny in that space, but concluded he cannot do it. In his words, what makes him and his friends laugh is "really dirty, sick, edgy" material.

## How the film fared

Far Out says audiences and critics agreed with his instinct. The film barely made back what it cost to produce, and Streisand was nominated for [Worst Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Raspberry_Award_for_Worst_Actress) at the Golden Raspberry Awards.

That sits awkwardly against the run that came before it. Far Out notes that 2007's Superbad, which Rogen co-wrote with [Evan Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evan_Goldberg), cost under $20 million and took about $170 million. It also introduced [Jonah Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Hill), [Emma Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Stone) and [Michael Cera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cera) to wide audiences, alongside [Bill Hader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Hader) as one of a pair of hapless cops. [Knocked Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up), made with producer [Judd Apatow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judd_Apatow) the same year, is described by the outlet as generating around $200 million in profit.

## A superhero detour before the dip

The outlet also revisits The Green Hornet, the 2011 superhero film directed by [Michel Gondry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Gondry). Far Out calls it far too expensive and says it struggled to break even. It quotes Rogen telling The Guardian that the shoot was such a nightmare that any presentable result would have been a miracle.

The article places The Guilt Trip as the second misstep, arriving while Apatow was still making similar comedies to those of five years earlier, such as This Is 40 with [Paul Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Rudd).

## The recovery

Rogen did not have to wait long for a win. In 2013 he reunited with Hill and Cera, plus [James Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franco), for This Is the End, an apocalyptic comedy packed with cameos, including [Kevin Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Hart). Far Out says it made almost $100 million in profit and was well reviewed.
