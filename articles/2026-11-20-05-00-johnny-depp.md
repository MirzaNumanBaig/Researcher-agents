**Quick summary**

- [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp), 63, says he now lives mostly on his private island in the Bahamas, Little Hall's Pond Cay.
- He made the comments in a November 10 interview with The Wall Street Journal, according to Yahoo.
- His new film Ebenezer opened on November 13 and took the top spot at the North American box office with about $19 million.
- Worldwide, the opening came to roughly $42 million, per Box Office Mojo figures cited by Yahoo.
- His next studio release, Day Drinker, is scheduled for March 26, 2027.

Johnny Depp says the Bahamas is now home. The 63-year-old actor told The Wall Street Journal on November 10 that he spends most of his time on Little Hall's Pond Cay, the private island he owns, where "nobody's pointing a camera" at him. The remarks, reported by [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/interviews/articles/johnny-depp-says-lives-mostly-203052650.html), land in the same week his Christmas film Ebenezer debuted at No. 1 in North America.

## A quiet life away from the cameras

Depp bought the wooden house on the island in 2004. It dates from the 1940s, and he has since added a yurt and a painting studio. He described a routine that is far from red carpets: sitting on the couch with four or five books, or playing guitar in his music studio. He also said art gives him a way to make sense of the world.

The island is not new to the public record. In 2009, historian [Douglas Brinkley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Brinkley) visited the 45-acre property for Vanity Fair. He described six beaches, including two named for Depp's children, Lily-Rose and Jack, and others honouring writer [Hunter S. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_S._Thompson) and actor [Marlon Brando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlon_Brando). Depp told Brinkley he was worn out after playing John Dillinger in Public Enemies, and that boarding his yacht helped him recover.

That same year, at the sixth Bahamas International Film Festival, [Sean Connery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Connery) presented Depp with a career achievement award on December 13. Depp told The Bahamas Weekly he planned to spend Christmas on his island in the Exumas, calling it "as close to paradise as anywhere you can get."

## From a restless childhood to Hollywood

The WSJ interview also looked back at his early years. According to PEOPLE, as quoted by Yahoo, his family moved from Kentucky to Florida and then lived in upwards of 40 places, which left him feeling like an outsider.

His mother gave him an acoustic guitar when he was 12, and he went on to form a band. By late 1983 he had moved to Los Angeles with his bandmates to chase a music career. There he met [Nicolas Cage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Cage), who came to several of his shows and suggested he try acting. Cage introduced him to his agent, and Depp made his film debut in Wes Craven's 1984 horror film A Nightmare on Elm Street.

## Ebenezer opens at No. 1

Depp's latest film is Ebenezer, a new take on [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens)' A Christmas Carol directed by [Ti West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ti_West). Depp plays Ebenezer Scrooge opposite [Rupert Grint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Grint), [Daisy Ridley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daisy_Ridley), [Sam Claflin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Claflin), Andrea Riseborough and Ian McKellen.

According to Box Office Mojo, the film earned about $19 million in North America over its opening weekend and a further $23 million overseas, for a worldwide start of around $42 million. The Associated Press describes it as his first major Hollywood studio film since Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald in 2018. In the years between, he kept working, starring in the French historical drama Jeanne du Barry and directing Modi: Three Days on the Wing of Madness.

In interviews published by Paramount, Depp said he saw Scrooge as a man who had pulled away from others because caring had become painful. He let some of the character's gestures and sounds develop on set.
