[Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook) has spent the week talking about the lowest point of his career, and the audience seems to be listening. His Netflix documentary Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story arrived on 6 November 2026, and according to a friend of his, it has reached number one on the service.

The film is directed by Brett Harvey, as [ComingSoon.net](https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/news/2220666-alan-ritchson-dane-cook-documentary-reaction) reports. It follows Cook's comedy success in the 2000s and the betrayal that followed. What did that betrayal involve?

## What is Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story about?

The documentary traces Cook's rise to fame and what [People](https://people.com/where-is-dane-cook-brother-now-12246857) describes as his crash at the hands of the person he trusted most. That person was his older half-brother, Darryl McCauley.

Cook grew up in Arlington, Massachusetts, outside Boston, with five sisters and McCauley. People says McCauley once worked as a corrections officer at a maximum-security facility near Boston. Cook told Rolling Stone, as quoted by People, that he asked his brother to quit and come to work for him after McCauley was attacked by an inmate.

McCauley became his assistant and later his business manager, running Great Dane Enterprises, Cook's company, including its day-to-day operations and finances. The detail that matters most came next.

## How much was taken, and what happened to McCauley?

According to People, Cook learned in 2008 that McCauley had been drawing money out of his accounts for years. Cook estimates the theft began slowly. Around 2004 McCauley was writing cheques to himself for between $22,000 and $31,000, and by two years later single withdrawals had grown to as much as $995,000.

McCauley fled but was arrested later in 2008. People reports he pleaded guilty to larceny, embezzlement and forgery in 2010, received a six-year prison sentence and was ordered to repay $12 million. Other outlets use a higher estimate: AOL's report on Cook's podcast appearance puts the total at about $15 million.

Cook told Deadline, as quoted by People, that looking back he saw red flags, many of them in his own insecurity about asking to see the books. He wished he had trusted his instincts.

## What did Dane Cook say about recovering?

Speaking to Jesse Tyler Ferguson on the Dinner's On Me podcast on 9 November, Cook said the film lets him show people that the worst fall can be survived. "The worst mountain you could plummet straight down... you can come back from," he said, according to [AOL.ca](https://www.aol.ca/articles/comedian-dane-cook-reveals-recovered-165227000.html).

He also said the theft came right after the deaths of both his parents, and that he had not yet grieved them. When he and Ferguson first met, he said, he had only just started real therapy.

By 2011, Cook told Ferguson, he barely recognised himself and wondered who he was without the fame. He said he found renewed purpose through performing.

## How have friends reacted?

Alan Ritchson, the star of Reacher, posted on Instagram Stories that Cook sent him the film before release and that he "had no idea his story was so intense", ComingSoon.net reports. Ritchson called it a phenomenal documentary and said it was a reminder that you never know what somebody is going through.

Not every outlet that covered the film could be checked for this article, and some reports go further into family detail than the sources above. Only what People, AOL and ComingSoon.net reported is used here.
