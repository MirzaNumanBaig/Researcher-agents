**Quick summary**

- The Displacement Film Fund, led by [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett), is releasing five short films by displaced directors.
- Variety reports the films are rolling out weekly on The New Yorker's website and YouTube channel.
- Each film received a €100,000 production grant, according to Variety.
- Austrian-Somali director [Mo Harawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Harawe) discussed his entry, "Whispers of a Burning Scent", with Blanchett on WNYC this week.

The Cate Blanchett Displacement Film Fund, which backs filmmakers forced from their homelands, is now reaching a wider audience. Its first five short films are being released online through The New Yorker, and Blanchett and Harawe talked about the project on WNYC's culture show on 17 November.

## What the Displacement Film Fund is

WNYC [describes the fund](https://wnyc.org/browse/shows/episode/simplecast/e76b763b-37a2-422a-83b7-2401916227be) as an initiative led by Blanchett that provides money to displaced filmmakers around the world. Its first slate is five short films from displaced directors.

According to [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/film/news/cate-blanchett-displacement-film-fund-new-yorker-41841/), each of those projects received a €100,000 production grant. The fund's management partner is the Hubert Bals Fund, part of the International Film Festival Rotterdam (IFFR), where the films premiered in 2026. Founding partners include Master Mind, UNIQLO, Droom en Daad, the Tamer Family Foundation and Amahoro Coalition, while the SP Lohia Foundation joined as a major partner this year.

Blanchett framed the need in numbers. Variety quotes her saying that one in 70 people on earth is displaced by conflict, war and persecution, which is why she wants audiences to have ways to connect with experiences unlike their own.

## The five films

Variety lists the slate as follows:

- *Allies in Exile*, a documentary by Hasan Kattan and Fadi Al Halabi, follows two Syrian journalists whose 14-year friendship has survived war and displacement.
- *Rotation*, by Maryna Er Gorbach, is a scripted story of a young Ukrainian woman who undergoes therapeutic hypnosis as she adjusts to military service.
- *Sense of Water*, by Mohammad Rasoulof, centres on an Iranian writer in exile who struggles to find his voice in a foreign language.
- *Super Afghan Gym*, by Shahrbanoo Sadat, is set in a Kabul gym during the single hour reserved for women.
- *Whispers of a Burning Scent*, by Mo Harawe, follows a musician preparing to play at a wedding while he waits for a court hearing that could expose his private life.

## Why Harawe's film is in focus

On WNYC, Blanchett and Harawe spoke about the fund and about his short. WNYC identifies him as an Austrian-Somali filmmaker. The conversation aired on the station's culture programme *All Of It*, hosted by [Alison Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Stewart), in the same week as the DOC NYC festival, which runs through 29 November.

## The New Yorker release

Variety reports that the films are being released weekly, starting with *Allies in Exile* on 9 November. Paul Moakley, The New Yorker's executive producer of video, called the collection especially urgent. IFFR managing director Clare Stewart and Hubert Bals Fund head Tamara Tatishvili described the magazine as an ideal partner for reaching more viewers.

Variety also reports that a screening of the slate at Film Forum in New York was set for 11 November, with Blanchett in conversation alongside Er Gorbach and Harawe. The films have since played at the BFI London Film Festival.

## What happens next

The weekly online releases continue until all five films are out. Variety says the slate is also scheduled for the São Paulo International Film Festival and the Tokyo International Film Festival.

A second group of recipients has been chosen: Mohammed Amer, Annemarie Jacir, Akuol de Mabior, Bao Nguyen and Rithy Panh. Their films are due to premiere at IFFR in January, according to Variety.
