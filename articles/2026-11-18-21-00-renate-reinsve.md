**Quick summary**

- Awards Radar published an interview with [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) about her lead role in *Fjord* on 18 November 2026.
- She says part of the appeal was her own experience as a mother, along with the debate at the heart of the film.
- Like director [Cristian Mungiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Mungiu), she hopes audiences watch the film on its own terms and make up their own minds.
- *Fjord* is in cinemas now, according to Awards Radar, and is being positioned for awards attention.

The Renate Reinsve Fjord interview, published by [Awards Radar](https://awardsradar.com/2026/11/18/interview-renate-reinsve-discusses-the-appeal-of-starring-in-fjord/) on 18 November, gives the Norwegian actress a chance to explain why she took on one of the most difficult roles of her career. She plays a gentle but unyielding mother in a film built around an argument that many viewers will find uncomfortable.

## What drew Renate Reinsve to Fjord

According to the interviewer, the pair discussed what appealed to Reinsve about the part. The answer, as the outlet describes it, has a lot to do with her being a mother herself. That personal angle matters because the film turns on questions of parenting and the limits of what a family may do in the name of its beliefs.

The site's write-up does not quote her answers in full, as the conversation itself is presented as a video. What it does make clear is that she also talked about the debate that sits at the centre of *Fjord*, and about how she wants viewers to approach it.

## A film that asks audiences to decide for themselves

Reinsve's stance echoes that of Mungiu. Awards Radar says she hopes people see the film, engage with it on its own terms and form their own view. The same writer spoke to Mungiu earlier in the week, and reports that both sides of the project take the same line.

The outlet also argues that a film with a contested subject can easily lose its artistry to the argument around it. In its view, that does not happen here because of Reinsve's central performance, which it describes as hard to pull off.

## Reviewer's verdict on her performance

The interviewer reprinted a passage from his review at the Toronto International Film Festival. In it he praises both Reinsve and her co-star [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan), saying each plays a very tricky role.

He describes Reinsve's character as gentle and kind, yet unable to stop slipping religion into her work even when asked not to. He calls the performance subtle, noting that the material could easily have tipped into melodrama. Stan, he writes, plays a more rigid man who believes in his own way of doing things, and even when he defends behaviour viewers may dislike, his love for his children is never in doubt.

This is one critic's opinion, and it reads more warmly than some other reaction. Our earlier coverage of the film's UK release is in [Renate Reinsve Named a Bond Dark Horse as Fjord Opens in UK](https://moduscarry.com/renate-reinsve-bond-fjord/).

## An Oscar nominee in the thick of the race

Awards Radar frames the interview as part of the awards conversation. It notes that Reinsve was passed over for her breakthrough performance in *The Worst Person in the World*, and that Academy voters later recognised her with a first nomination for [*Sentimental Value*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentimental_Value) in the [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actress) category. The outlet says she is now back in the Oscar race with *Fjord*.

## What happens next

Awards Radar says *Fjord* is currently showing in cinemas and is angling for awards attention. No nominations or release dates beyond that are given in the piece. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is Fjord about?

Awards Radar describes it as a drama built around a central debate about family and parenting, directed by Cristian Mungiu. The outlet does not set out the full plot in this piece.

### Who stars in Fjord?

Renate Reinsve and Sebastian Stan lead the cast, according to Awards Radar's review and interview.

Yes. The outlet says she received her first Academy nomination, for Best Actress, for *Sentimental Value*.
