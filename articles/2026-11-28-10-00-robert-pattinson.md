**Quick summary**

- [Robert Pattinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Pattinson) has been announced by Amazon MGM Studios as the next [James Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Bond), succeeding [Daniel Craig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Craig).
- The Robert Pattinson Bond casting breaks a rule that lasted about 64 years: that Bond should be played by a relatively unknown actor.
- Bond historian Steven Jay Rubin told The New York Times that choosing an unknown would have been a tactical error today.
- Denis Villeneuve will direct the 26th Bond film. No release date has been announced.

The Robert Pattinson Bond casting has ended one of the franchise's longest-running habits, and a leading Bond expert says it was the right move. According to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/james-bond-casting-tradition-robert-pattinson-broke-justified-steven-jay-rubin/), film historian Steven Jay Rubin believes a famous face gives the series its best shot at a new audience.

## The rule Pattinson broke

ScreenRant reports that producer [Albert R. Broccoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_R._Broccoli) believed Bond should be played by a lesser-known actor, and that his children carried the idea forward. Amazon has now taken full creative control of the brand, and its first choice goes the other way.

Pattinson is far from an unknown. He is a former [Edward Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Cullen) in Twilight, a veteran of the [Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter) films, a cast member of Dune and the current Batman. ScreenRant notes that some viewers have called the casting a safe option because of that résumé.

## Why a historian calls it justified

Rubin, who wrote The James Bond Movie Encyclopedia, took the opposite view in comments to The New York Times. He said picking a little-known actor "would probably have been a tactical error" because the world has changed a great deal since Bond arrived in the 1960s.

His argument is about reach. A younger audience, he suggested, will recognise the Batman and Dune actor, and that familiarity adds intrigue to the new Bond.

Rubin also dismissed the idea that an actor cannot carry two big roles. Pattinson is due to return as Batman in The Batman: Part II. "There's precedent all over the place," Rubin said, pointing to [Harrison Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Ford) moving from [Han Solo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Solo) to [Indiana Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Jones), and Sylvester Stallone playing both Rocky and Rambo.

## A mixed reaction

Not everyone agreed. ScreenRant says the announcement drew a mixed response, partly because names such as [Jack Lowden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lowden) and [Callum Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callum_Turner) fitted the older profile of rising actors who had not yet led a major franchise. The search reportedly ran close to five years.

Reports also said Pattinson and [Nicholas Hoult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Hoult) were tested late in the process, while filming on The Batman: Part II was paused for personal reasons affecting director Matt Reeves. Another report claimed Pattinson was offered a three-film deal worth close to US$100 million. These details come from reports cited by ScreenRant, not from Amazon.

For more on how the story has developed, see our coverage of [Villeneuve's promise of a traditional 007](https://moduscarry.com/robert-pattinson-bond-villeneuve-traditional/) and the [Twilight director's claim that Pattinson foretold the role](https://moduscarry.com/robert-pattinson-bond-twilight-line/).

## What happens next

Pattinson will join Sean Connery, Timothy Dalton and Pierce Brosnan among the actors who have played Bond. He reunites with Villeneuve, who directed him in Dune: Part Three, which is due out on 18 December. Peaky Blinders creator Steven Knight is writing the script. Plot details are still under wraps and Amazon has not given a release date.
