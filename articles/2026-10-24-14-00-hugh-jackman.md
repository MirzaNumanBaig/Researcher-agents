**Quick summary**

- [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) was offered the part of Billy Flynn in the film Chicago in 2001, according to Far Out Magazine.
- He turned it down because he felt too young for the role. He was 30 at the time.
- [Richard Gere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gere) played Flynn instead and won a Golden Globe for it.
- Jackman named it as a career regret in a 2012 interview with Movies, while promoting Les Misérables.

Hugh Jackman has said that his one big career regret is saying no to Chicago. Far Out Magazine revisited the story on 24 October, recalling that the Australian actor was offered the role of lawyer Billy Flynn in 2001 and declined, a part that later won Richard Gere a Golden Globe.

## What did Hugh Jackman say about turning down Chicago?

Jackman raised the subject himself in 2012. According to Far Out, he was asked by Movies whether he had any regrets while promoting Les Misérables, directed by Tom Hooper, and answered straight away: "More than anything, I was offered a role in Chicago, playing Richard Gere's part."

His reason was age. Far Out quotes him saying he was 30 and kept stumbling on the line "I've seen it all, kid". Delivering that to Renée Zellweger, he felt, would simply not be believable.

It is a modest explanation, and a fair one. Flynn is a world-weary courtroom operator, and Jackman was still early in his screen career.

## How the casting played out

The film was directed by [Rob Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Marshall), who adapted Bob Fosse's stage production for the screen in 2002. Chicago won six Oscars, according to Far Out, and Gere took the Golden Globe for best actor in a motion picture, musical or comedy.

The numbers explain Jackman's hesitation. Far Out reports that John Travolta, then 47, had also turned the part down before it went to Gere, who was 52. Against those two, a 30-year-old was the youngest name in the conversation by a wide margin.

Far Out's view is that Jackman could probably have handled the role. Jackman, by his own account, did not think so at the time.

## What he did instead

Far Out is blunt about what came next. Jackman went on to make the thriller Swordfish and the romantic comedy Kate & Leopold, and the magazine describes both as disappointing. That is the outlet's opinion, not a settled verdict, but it frames why the Chicago decision still stings.

Jackman had arrived in film by an unusual route. Far Out notes he first took a drama class at university for extra credit, then enrolled in a one-year course at the Actors' Centre in Sydney. Early work included the short-lived Australian series Corelli and a Melbourne production of Beauty and the Beast, where he played Gaston.

He then won a Laurence Olivier Award for playing Curly in the Royal National Theatre's Oklahoma! in London. That musical-theatre background is why the Chicago offer made sense in the first place.

The X-Men break came when Dougray Scott left the role of Wolverine. [Russell Crowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Crowe), who had declined the part, suggested Jackman, according to Far Out. He has since played the character nine times.

## What happens next

The Chicago decision cannot be undone, and Far Out reports no new offer or project tied to it. What the record does show is that Jackman did eventually get his big-screen musical: Les Misérables arrived in 2012, the film he was promoting when he named his regret.
