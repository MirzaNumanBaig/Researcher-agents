**Quick summary**

- [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman) is the cover star of Vogue Australia's November issue, according to Nine.com.au.
- Asked by [Joey King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joey_King) what she is proudest of, Kidman said "Surviving", having started in the industry at 14.
- She says she feels more comfortable in her own skin at 59.
- She also shared her daughters' access to her couture and vintage wardrobes, and a McDonald's commercial she never landed.

Nicole Kidman says the thing she is proudest of after more than four decades on screen is simply still being here. The 59-year-old Australian actress made the comment in her Vogue Australia November cover interview, reported by [Nine.com.au](https://www.nine.com.au/entertainment/nicole-kidman-vogue-australia-november-cover-star-interview-20261024-p6189z.html) on 24 October 2026.

## "Surviving" is the answer

The interview was built around questions from other well-known names. When King asked what she was most proud of, Kidman didn't reach for an award. "Surviving," she said. "Literally starting out in this industry at 14 and still being here."

She added that she is proud of her current ability to "support people and get things financed", though she qualified it with "who knows when that will go". That is a candid note from an Oscar winner, and it puts her pride in staying power and in helping others make work, not in her own trophies.

Kidman also said she is "more comfortable in my own skin right now", calling it her "new thing". "Weird to get that at 59, but I'm glad to say I somehow got there," she told the magazine.

## The job she never got

Margot Robbie asked Kidman about her favourite Australian television advertisement, and the answer was a surprise. Kidman said she always wanted to appear in a McDonald's commercial, because it was "it" when she was growing up. Her motive was the Quarter Pounder, which she said she wanted to eat on camera "over and over again". "I never got cast," she said.

She was also asked about the viral song that carries her name. According to Nine.com.au, it has passed 180 million streams. Kidman said she never heard Adéla's track before approving it. The singer sent her the lyrics, and she replied, in her words, "Go for it, girl." ## Family, wardrobe and work ethic

Kidman has separate couture and vintage archives, which she said were curated with help from "beautiful Dolly", the late [Dolly Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_Parton). Her daughters Sunday Rose, 18, and Faith, 15, regularly raid them. "Sunday has the best boots," Kidman said, adding: "I don't buy for me, I buy for us."

Sunday Rose now walks international runways and has her own Vogue cover, Nine.com.au reports. Kidman said the lesson she repeats to her daughters is practical: be grateful, have "incredibly good manners", be on time, and "be the hardest worker".

She also said she has never hit the snooze button. Years of waiting around film sets taught her to rest almost anywhere. "I can literally just zone out and have minimal thoughts," she said. "And that's rest in itself."
