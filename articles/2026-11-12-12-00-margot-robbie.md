- [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie), [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) and [Greta Gerwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Gerwig) are said to have sought $25 million each, plus bigger profit shares.
- Talks reportedly collapsed under Warner Bros.' previous management, and the matter now sits with Skydance chief [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison).

The Barbie sequel is reportedly stuck on money. A Variety report, summarised by the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/11/business/barbie-2-talks-hit-snag-over-25m-paydays-for-margot-robbie-ryan-gosling-and-greta-gerwig-in-new-test-for-david-ellison/) on 11 November, says Margot Robbie, Ryan Gosling and director Greta Gerwig each asked for $25 million, along with larger shares of the profits, and the Barbie 2 pay dispute is now the first big creative test for Ellison since his company took over Warner Bros. Discovery.

## What the Barbie 2 pay dispute involves

According to the report, the demands came after the success of the first film, which ComicBook.com describes as the biggest movie of 2023. The creative team reportedly wanted richer deals for a follow-up.

[Noah Baumbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Baumbach), Gerwig's co-screenwriter, would reportedly split a writing fee with her under the proposal. The talks were said to be led by CAA agent [Bryan Lourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Lourd), who represents the team. A CAA spokesperson declined to comment to the Post, and Lourd could not immediately be reached.

None of the figures has been confirmed by the people named. They come from Variety's unnamed sources, so treat them as reported, not settled.

## Why the talks stalled before

Variety reported that talks broke down under Warner Bros.' earlier leadership. Former Warner Bros. Discovery chief David Zaslav reportedly balked at the profit-sharing terms before Ellison bought the company.

The timing was awkward. The studio was reportedly facing a deadline to renew its licensing rights to the Barbie franchise while the negotiations ran. Gerwig and Baumbach had also pitched a sequel to studio film chiefs [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca) and [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy), who were ousted days before Skydance completed its takeover in October.

## The money behind the decision

Skydance closed its $111 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery on 6 October, the Post reports. The company now carries roughly $80 billion in net debt and faces more than $6 billion in interest expenses in 2027, though it reported about $10 billion in cash on Monday.

At the same time it has pledged to spend more than $30 billion a year on content, a figure the Post says is higher than the individual budgets of Netflix and Disney. Ellison is also targeting at least $6 billion in annual cost savings over three years. A 9 November shareholder letter named corporate overhead, offices and facilities as the biggest target, with at least $2.2 billion in projected yearly savings.

That is the tension in this story: a studio that wants more films but is cutting costs, weighing $25 million cheques for a franchise that proved enormously lucrative. Variety also reported that layoffs are expected by the end of 2026 and into early 2027.
