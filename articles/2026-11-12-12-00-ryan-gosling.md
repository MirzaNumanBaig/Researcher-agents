**Quick summary**

- [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling), Margot Robbie and Greta Gerwig reportedly each sought $25 million for a Barbie sequel, plus bigger profit shares.
- Variety reported the talks collapsed under Warner Bros.' previous management, according to the New York Post.
- Skydance chief David Ellison now has to resolve the standoff after completing his Warner Bros. Discovery takeover on 6 October.
- Two sources told Variety that all sides hope to reach a deal by the end of the year.

The Barbie sequel has reportedly run into a money problem. [Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling), [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie) and director [Greta Gerwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Gerwig) each sought $25 million for a follow-up, along with larger shares of the profit, according to a Variety report relayed by the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/11/business/barbie-2-talks-hit-snag-over-25m-paydays-for-margot-robbie-ryan-gosling-and-greta-gerwig-in-new-test-for-david-ellison/) on Wednesday. None of the parties has publicly confirmed the figures. A spokesperson for the talent agency CAA declined to comment to the Post.

## Ryan Gosling Barbie 2 deal: what was reportedly asked

The New York Post says the negotiations fell apart under Warner Bros.' former leadership, after the creative team pushed for richer terms following the success of the original film. Former Warner Bros. Discovery chief David Zaslav reportedly balked at the profit-sharing proposal before the company changed hands.

Gerwig's co-writer, [Noah Baumbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Baumbach), would reportedly split a writing fee with her under the proposal. The talks were said to be led by CAA agent [Bryan Lourd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Lourd), who represents the group.

The timing was awkward. Per the report, Warner Bros. was also facing a deadline to renew its licensing rights to the Barbie franchise while the discussions were under way. Gerwig and Baumbach had earlier pitched a sequel to studio film chiefs [Michael De Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_De_Luca) and [Pamela Abdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Abdy), who were ousted days before the takeover closed.

## A new boss with a tight budget

The decision now sits with [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison), whose Skydance completed a $111 billion takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery on 6 October. The Post reports that Ellison, Lourd and Mattel's former chief executive, Kreiz, hope to land an agreement by year's end, citing two sources in Variety's story.

Ellison is negotiating from a stretched position. The Post puts Skydance's net debt at roughly $80 billion, with more than $6 billion in interest expenses due in 2027. The company reported about $10 billion in cash on Monday, and it has pledged to spend more than $30 billion a year on content, which the Post says is more than Netflix or Disney spends individually.

## The cost-cutting backdrop

A 9 November shareholder letter set a target of at least $6 billion in annual savings over three years, with corporate overhead, offices and facilities the largest piece at $2.2 billion. Technology and outside vendors are each tagged for at least $1.4 billion, and marketing and advertising for another $1 billion. The company says more than 20 initiatives are running, aiming for $1.8 billion in annualised savings by the end of the first year.

Variety also reported, per the Post, that layoffs are expected by the end of 2026 and into the first quarter of 2027. That makes a trio of $25 million asks a pointed early test of how Ellison balances expansion with restraint.

## What happens next

No deal has been announced. Based on the reporting, the next milestone is the end-of-year target, though that is an aspiration shared by two anonymous sources rather than a firm deadline.
