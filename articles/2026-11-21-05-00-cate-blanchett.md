**Quick summary**

- The feature documentary *Maverick: The Epic Adventures of David Lean*, narrated by Cate Blanchett, has sold to distributors in France, Germany, Spain, Australia and New Zealand.
- MetFilm Distribution releases it in cinemas in the UK and Ireland from 4 December, according to Deadline.
- Sky holds UK broadcast rights and will air it after the theatrical run.
- The film premiered in the Cannes Classics program and was an official selection at Telluride.

The [Cate Blanchett David Lean documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) is heading to screens around the world. *Maverick: The Epic Adventures of David Lean* has sold to key territories ahead of its UK release on 4 December, [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/11/david-lean-film-sky-uk-release-cate-blanchett-deals-1237229966/) on 20 November. For Australian audiences, Limelight has picked up rights for Australia and New Zealand.

## Where the film has sold

Sales were handled by [Embankment Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embankment_Films). According to Deadline, ARTE ZDF has taken France and Germany, A Contracorriente has Spain, and Limelight covers Australia and New Zealand. MetFilm Distribution handles the UK and Ireland, where the film opens in select cinemas on 4 December. Sky has the UK broadcast rights and will show it after the cinema run.

The Chenab Times, which said it relied on details it received, also reported the deals and added that further agreements for other territories were reportedly under discussion. Deadline did not name any additional territories, so that remains unconfirmed.

Producer Jack Heller told Deadline the team always wanted a David Lean documentary "worthy of the big screen". He thanked Sky for backing the project. The film is produced by Embankment Films, Fragile Films and Assemble Media.

## Who is behind the film

[Barnaby Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnaby_Thompson) wrote and directed it. Blanchett narrates, while [Kenneth Branagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Branagh) supplies the voice of [David Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lean) himself.

The list of contributors is long and weighty. Deadline names [Steven Spielberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Spielberg), [Wes Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Anderson), [Celine Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celine_Song), [Paul Greengrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Greengrass), [Joe Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Wright), Nia Da Costa, [Alfonso Cuaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n), [Autumn Durald Arkapaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_Durald_Arkapaw), [Francis Ford Coppola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ford_Coppola), [Brady Corbet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brady_Corbet), [Steven Soderbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Soderbergh) and [Denis Villeneuve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Villeneuve). That is a dozen filmmakers lining up to talk about a director who died in 1991.

## The story of a famously obsessive director

Lean began in news editing and went on to cut films for Michael Powell and Emeric Pressburger, and to collaborate with Noel Coward. He then built a legacy of 17 films of his own, among them *Brief Encounter*, *Oliver Twist*, *The Bridge on the River Kwai*, *Lawrence of Arabia*, *Doctor Zhivago*, *Ryan's Daughter* and *A Passage to India*.

Deadline describes him as an obsessive maverick with a dogged determination to film the impossible. The same report says his private life was messy, rootless and painful, a contrast with the scale of his screen epics. An earlier Deadline story on the Cannes screening, linked from the report, framed the film around how he overcame his father's scorn. We have not read that piece in full, so we leave it as a pointer only.

The Chenab Times says the documentary draws on archival footage and interviews, and that it looks at his creative process and his struggles to realise big visions.

## Why it matters for Blanchett fans

Blanchett, an Oscar winner, is lending her voice to the film's framing narrative rather than appearing on screen. The Chenab Times suggests her name should help the film reach a global audience. That is its own assessment, not a figure or a confirmed marketing plan. The film's festival record is the firmer evidence: a Cannes Classics world premiere, then Telluride.

## What happens next

The UK and Ireland cinema release starts on 4 December through MetFilm Distribution, in select cinemas. Sky's broadcast premiere follows, though no date has been given in the reports we read. Release dates for France, Germany, Spain, Australia and New Zealand have not been announced. We will update when they are.
