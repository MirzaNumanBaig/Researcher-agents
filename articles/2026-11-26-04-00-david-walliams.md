**Quick summary**

- A law firm, legacy Allen & Overy, has been named as the firm HarperCollins brought in during 2023 to look into complaints about [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams), according to Legal Business.
- Former publisher staff say that inquiry amounted to a "cover-up". Walliams denies any wrongdoing.
- The UK Government said on Tuesday that plans to curb non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) will be brought forward to January, Metro reports.
- Campaigner Zelda Perkins told ITV the Walliams and [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) cases are "the tip of the iceberg".

The law firm behind the 2023 David Walliams HarperCollins inquiry has now been identified, and the allegations of a cover-up around that inquiry are feeding a wider push to limit NDAs in Britain. Walliams denies wrongdoing.

## Who ran the David Walliams HarperCollins inquiry?

Legal Business reports that legacy Allen & Overy, a "magic circle" firm, was brought in by HarperCollins in 2023 to conduct an inquiry after female employees raised concerns about Walliams's behaviour towards women. The trade title says the detail comes from reporting by The Guardian and ITV News.

Former employees of the publisher have told those outlets the investigation amounted to a cover-up. Legal Business presents that as a claim from former staff, not an established finding, and the pages we read do not include a response from the firm. Our earlier coverage looked at [questions over the HarperCollins inquiry](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-harpercollins-inquiry-2/) and what [ex-HarperCollins staff allege](https://moduscarry.com/david-walliams-harpercollins-cover-up/).

## What the author and campaigner are saying

Metro reports that one author currently represented by the publisher told the news organisations: "I certainly believe that there has been a cover-up." The author added that women were pushed to stay quiet and keep their accounts under wraps.

Metro also quotes Zelda Perkins, who said there were "so many red flags" about Walliams's behaviour and that other authors had raised concerns. She said "we knew that he was being protected because he was a valuable asset". These are the views of people speaking to the media. HarperCollins's side is not set out in the sources we read.

## Why the NDA debate is heating up

[Zelda Perkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Weinstein_sexual_abuse_cases) CBE, a campaigner who was Harvey Weinstein's assistant, signed an NDA at 24 that barred her from discussing his behaviour. She broke it in 2017, and Weinstein was later jailed in 2020, as [Metro explains](https://metro.co.uk/2026/11/25/david-walliams-russell-brand-tip-iceberg-pandoras-box-perpetrators-29818094/). Speaking to ITV, she said "the law is not for sale" and that people's silence should not be something money can buy.

She described the Walliams and Brand cases as "the beginning of the reckoning" in the UK creative industries, and said she knows of many more perpetrators "across all industries". Metro reports that Brand has been jailed for 10-and-a-half years.

Metro says the Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, called for a "watershed moment" in a Guardian article, and that the Government said on Tuesday its NDA measures would arrive in January.

## The legal position so far

Earlier this month Walliams lost a High Court bid for an injunction to stop media organisations publishing the allegations, Metro says. He denies wrongdoing. Metro also reports that the Metropolitan Police has received a number of non-recent allegations against him, separate from the Guardian and ITV News reporting. These are allegations, not findings, and no charge has been reported in the sources we read.
