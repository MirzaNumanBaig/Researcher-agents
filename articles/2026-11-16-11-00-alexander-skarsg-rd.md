**Quick summary**

- [Alexander Skarsgård](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Skarsg%C3%A5rd) appeared at the Deadline Contenders Film event at the Directors Guild of America theatre to promote Wicker, according to Harper's BAZAAR.
- He wore a mint-green JW Anderson rugby shirt embroidered with the word "Baby" and hot-pink silk shorts from the same label.
- Wicker, in which he plays a man made of wicker, reached cinemas last month.
- Separately, MovieWeb reports that The Legend of Tarzan (2016) ranked No. 4 on HBO Max's US most-watched movies chart as of 15 November.

Alexander Skarsgård has had a busy weekend in two very different ways. The Swedish actor turned up at an awards-season panel for Wicker in one of the brightest outfits of the circuit, and one of his older films, The Legend of Tarzan, has re-entered the streaming charts almost a decade after its release.

## Skarsgård's Wicker panel at Deadline Contenders

[Harper's BAZAAR](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/celebrity/latest/a74433389/alexander-skarsgard-jw-anderson-mint-green-bubblegum-pink-photos/) reports that Skarsgård attended the all-day Deadline Contenders Film event at the [Directors Guild of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directors_Guild_of_America) theatre. The programme put the cast and crew of 15 major films from the past year on stage, and he was there for Wicker.

He sat on a panel with the film's co-writers and co-directors, Eleanor Wilson and Alex Huston, and prosthetic makeup designer Joe Dunckley. Prosthetics matter to this project: Skarsgård plays a man literally made of wicker, and the film opened in cinemas last month, according to the same report.

Awards panels like this one are how studios and distributors keep a film in front of voters and critics in the months after release. A prosthetics-heavy horror project has an obvious talking point in the makeup team, which helps explain why Dunckley was on the stage.

You can read more about the film in our earlier post, [Alexander Skarsgård on Wicker, the Suit and a Corduroy Sequel](https://moduscarry.com/alexander-skarsgard-wicker-suit-sequel/).

## The mint and pink JW Anderson look

His co-panellists kept to jeans and neutral colours. Skarsgård did the opposite. Harper's BAZAAR describes a mint-green JW Anderson rugby shirt with a white collar and an elongated placket, cut oversize and embroidered in satin with the word "Baby" in a font inspired by text from vintage magazines.

The hot-pink shorts came from the same label and were made with Humphries Weaving. The company was founded in Sudbury, England, in 1972 and is known for bespoke silk fabrics woven with historically accurate techniques. For this piece, working with creative director Jonathan Anderson, it produced a 100 per cent silk damask with a dotted-line motif. Black socks and black loafers finished the outfit.

It is not the first time he has made a splash. The magazine points to a kilt and a gold suit worn with transparent Oxford shoes as earlier examples. Readers who follow his style can browse our [fashion coverage](https://moduscarry.com/category/fashion/) or see his [pink COS appearance at the Hamilton Awards](https://moduscarry.com/alexander-skarsgard-hamilton-awards/).

## The Legend of Tarzan finds a streaming audience

The second story is about a film from 2016. MovieWeb reports that The Legend of Tarzan, directed by David Yates, stood at No. 4 on HBO Max's most-watched movies chart in the US as of 15 November. It sat ahead of The Hunger Games and behind titles including Matilda, The Pout-Pout Fish and The End of Oak Street.

The numbers behind the film are mixed. MovieWeb says it earned roughly US$356 million worldwide against a reported production budget of US$180 million, a result widely regarded as disappointing at the time. Critics were lukewarm, with a Rotten Tomatoes score in the mid-30s, although the audience score was noticeably higher.

The cast includes Skarsgård as John Clayton, also known as Tarzan, [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie) as Jane, Christoph Waltz as the villain and Samuel L. Jackson as George Washington Williams. MovieWeb argues the pairing of Skarsgård and Robbie looks far stronger now than it did in 2016, given Robbie's profile after Barbie and Skarsgård's work on The Northman, Succession and Murderbot. It also notes that the film reached Netflix's global top ten in 2021, which it reads as steady recurring demand.
