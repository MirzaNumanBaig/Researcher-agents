**Quick summary**

- UK Prime Minister [Andy Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Burnham) has urged Google to remove a YouTube video that carries a message from [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), according to the ABC.
- Brand, sentenced to 10 and a half years in prison, protests his innocence in the statement, which was read out by his wife and uploaded on Sunday.
- Google has reportedly refused, saying its rules were not broken, while UK officials investigate whether prison rules were breached.
- A Netflix documentary on the case, *Russell Brand: Open Secret*, reached No. 3 on the US chart on 18 November.

The British government has asked Google to take down a YouTube video in which a message from Russell Brand, now serving a prison sentence, protests his innocence. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on Friday that victims "shouldn't be hearing from him", according to [the Australian Broadcasting Corporation](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-11-20/burnham-urges-google-to-remove-russell-brand-youtube-statement/107407936).

## What Burnham asked Google to do

Burnham called on social media companies to "take these interviews down" and to "show some decency". Asked whether he would change the law, he stopped short, saying he would not "leap to that". He told platforms to think of the victims first.

Government minister [Matthew Pennycook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Pennycook) told the BBC that UK authorities have no power to force a removal. He said the government remained in "close dialogue" with the company and had made the request because victims should not have to see the content. A spokesperson for Burnham called the video "clearly appalling".

## Google's response

The BBC has reported that Google refused, on the basis that its rules had not been broken. A company spokesperson told AFP that its teams were aware of the new content and were investigating. The ABC reported that as of Friday morning AEST the video was still online on the couple's channel, which has more than 6.6 million subscribers, and had been viewed more than 950,000 times.

Brand's channel was removed from YouTube's monetisation program when allegations first surfaced in September 2023, so it has not earned money on the platform since. His Facebook and Instagram accounts appear to be unavailable. His X account, with 10 million followers, is still visible but has not posted since 11 November.

## The prison-rules question

The statement was read by Brand's wife, Laura Brand. UK officials are looking into whether it broke laws that stop prisoners from broadcasting messages. According to the ABC, prisoners are barred from making statements that distress victims, speaking to the media is allowed only in exceptional circumstances, and inmates cannot get around the rules by having someone else publish their words.

## A victim responds

Mia Latter, who was known as "Alice" in earlier media investigations and was not part of the recent criminal trial, told the BBC the video was "appalling" and "upsetting to watch". She said pairing a Christian message with what she called "absolute lies" was a contradiction. She alleges she was raped by Brand when she was 16 and he was 30. Brand was not convicted on her allegation, and the ABC does not report any ruling on it.

British police have confirmed they are investigating additional allegations against him.

## The documentary behind the headlines

Interest in the case is also showing up on streaming. According to [ComingSoon.net](https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/news/2227093-russell-brand-open-secret-new-movie-documentary?amp), *Russell Brand: Open Secret* sat at No. 3 on Netflix's US Top 10 on 18 November, two days after its debut. The film premiered on 13 November.

Directed by Olivia Isaacs and produced by 72 Films, the Fremantle company behind *Jimmy Savile: A British Horror Story*, it traces Brand's path from comedian and film star to online commentator, ending with the trial. The report says it draws on reporting by The Times and The Sunday Times. Isaacs said the team began filming before charges were laid and avoided anything that could prejudice the trial.

The court convicted Brand on 5 November of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He was acquitted of two other rape counts and one count of attempted indecent assault. He was sentenced on 9 November.

## What happens next

The investigation into whether the video breached prison rules is still under way, and the government says it will keep talking to Google. Whether the video stays up will depend on the platform's own terms, as a ministry spokesman put it. Police inquiries into further allegations also continue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Has Google removed the Russell Brand video?

Not as of Friday morning AEST, according to the ABC. The BBC reported that Google refused the UK request because its rules had not been broken.

### Can the UK government force YouTube to take it down?

No. Pennycook told the BBC that UK authorities have no power to force removal, so the government has made a request instead.

A London court convicted him of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, and he received a sentence of 10 years and six months, according to ComingSoon.net.
