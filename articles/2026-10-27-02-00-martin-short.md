**Quick summary**

- [Martin Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Short) told the podcast Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend that he gave himself one year to succeed in show business.
- If it failed, he planned to return to university for a master's in social work, according to a Parade interview report published on 25 October 2026.
- The year paid off in 1972, when he was cast in a Toronto production of Godspell that ran for 488 performances.
- A Netflix documentary on SCTV, the show that followed, begins streaming on 30 October.

Martin Short has revealed that his career began as a time-limited bet. Speaking to Conan O'Brien on the podcast Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend, the comedian said he set himself exactly one year to make it in show business, with social work waiting if it didn't work, as [reported by Parade](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/celebrity/articles/martin-shorts-says-countdown-showbiz-213900146.html) and syndicated on Yahoo on 25 October.

## The one-year contract

O'Brien had asked whether Short would have found another path without his big break. Short's answer was a plain one. He had finished four years of university and was about to start a master's in social work. He holds a social work degree from McMaster University, according to the report.

The timing was shaped by loss. Short said his eldest brother, David, died in a car accident when Short was 12. His mother died of cancer in 1968 and his father two years later. "I definitely gave myself a one-year contract to be in show business because my parents weren't living at this point. I was 20 and my father had just died," he said.

His father's likely view mattered too. "I had an inheritance, but I knew that he wouldn't support me being an actor ... he'd support education," Short said. He added that he didn't want to be a waiter at 37.

The deadline was real. "Had I not worked in that year, I definitely would have gone back to school," he said. He framed the plan as protection against regret rather than a bet on fame, imagining a later conversation with himself in which he could say that no one had hired him.

## How Godspell settled it

Someone did hire him. In 1972, composer Stephen Schwartz cast Short in a new Toronto production of Godspell. It opened at the Royal Alexandra Theatre and ran for 488 performances, according to Parade.

Short said hundreds of hopefuls turned up for callbacks and the role came down to him and "this other very talented guy named Andrew." He recalled Schwartz settling it from the stage: "You are fabulous. I think we're going to go with Martin." Short put it down partly to luck: "I was just lucky that that show was happening."

The cast around him was remarkable. It included [Gilda Radner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilda_Radner), [Eugene Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Levy), [Andrea Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Martin) and [Victor Garber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Garber). That group is the subject of the documentary You Had to Be There, which reached US cinemas on 18 September.

## From SCTV to Only Murders

Short went on to SCTV and Saturday Night Live, then a long film career. Newsday's [SCTV retrospective](https://www.newsday.com/entertainment/tv/sctv-top-moments-glhefz4d) lists his SCTV characters as the neurotic Ed Grimley and the lounge singer Jackie Rogers Jr. It points to Mars Attacks!, Three Amigos! and Father of the Bride among his film hits, the last two alongside Steve Martin.

The pair now co-star in the Hulu series Only Murders in the Building. Parade reports that the show has taken its sixth season to London. For more on the shows and films behind the stories, see our [TV & Dramas](https://moduscarry.com/category/tv-dramas/) coverage.

O'Brien closed the conversation with his own verdict. "I can't think of another name of someone who's brought me as much joy as you have," he told Short.

## A joke from Barack Obama

Short also turned up in an unexpected place over the weekend. According to CTV News, as relayed by [The News International](https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1421303-obama-takes-aim-at-trumps-canada-trade-war-with-jokes-about-maple-syrup-and-martin-short), former US president [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) used him as a punchline at a Democratic rally in Detroit on Saturday. Criticising [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump)'s tariffs on Canadian goods, Obama joked that Canadians "gave us Martin Short ... and maple syrup." The line was about Canada's reputation for friendliness, not about Short himself.
