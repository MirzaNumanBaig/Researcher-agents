**Quick summary**

- ESPN Films has released *Coach Osh*, a new short in its 30 for 30 series, with a trailer unveiled on 5 November 2026.
- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) and [Lauren Miller Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Miller_Rogen) are executive producers.
- The film follows Stanley Cup champion T.J. Oshie and his father, Tim "Coach" Oshie, after Tim's Alzheimer's diagnosis.
- It is available to stream on the ESPN App, according to ComingSoon via AOL.

Seth Rogen and his wife, Lauren Miller Rogen, have lent their names as executive producers to *Coach Osh*, a new ESPN 30 for 30 short about hockey star T.J. Oshie and his father's fight with Alzheimer's disease. The trailer was revealed exclusively by ComingSoon, in a report [carried by AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/seth-rogen-t-j-oshie-160000000.html), and the film is available on the ESPN App.

## What Coach Osh is about

According to the AOL report, the documentary tells the story of the relationship between Oshie, a former right winger and Stanley Cup champion, and his father Tim, known as "Coach". The film picks up after Tim received an Alzheimer's diagnosis.

ESPN Films describes it through a logline quoted in the report: an intimate look at the effect of Alzheimer's on a family, the lasting bond between a father and son, and what it means to hold on to the moments that matter most.

Oshie said in a statement that his father was there for him at every step growing up and that he would not have had his career without him. He added that sharing the Stanley Cup with his dad is something he will always be grateful for. "This film is about a lot more than hockey," he said, describing it as a story about his father, their family and the memories they made together.

## What the trailer shows

The trailer opens with home video of a young Oshie on the ice with his father. It then moves through archive footage of highlights from his career with the Washington Capitals.

One clip shows a tearful Oshie hugging Tim after the 2018 Stanley Cup win. The report notes the film celebrates a man who stood beside his son throughout, even though he now struggles to remember his greatest achievements.

In a voiceover, Oshie reflects on what the disease takes away. He says a person's memories and identity are held in the brain, and that losing control of them, forgetting where you came from and who your loved ones are, sounds like torture to him.
