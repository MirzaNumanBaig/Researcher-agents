**Quick summary**

- Ebenezer, [Johnny Depp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Depp)'s take on A Christmas Carol, fell 59.5% in its second weekend and slid to third place at the US box office, according to MovieWeb.
- It took $7.79 million over the three days, and I Play Rocky outgrossed it with $9.32 million in 1,000 fewer theatres.
- Ebenezer still leads overall, with $32.5 million domestically and $78.4 million worldwide against a $38 million spend.
- Mark Seal's unauthorised biography, The Many Faces of Johnny Depp, reached shelves this week.

Johnny Depp's Ebenezer has lost its early lead at the US box office. The gothic Christmas Carol opened at number one on 13 November, then dropped sharply in its second weekend and was overtaken by the sports drama I Play Rocky, according to MovieWeb. The slide lands in the same week a new biography puts Depp's whole career back under the spotlight.

## Ebenezer's second weekend

Ebenezer opened with $19 million, in line with studio projections of $18 million to $20 million, and took first place. Week two was harder. Facing The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, it fell 59.5% to $7.79 million and finished third, MovieWeb reported.

For context, that is a drop of more than half for a film that had met expectations seven days earlier. It now heads into the Thanksgiving period with new family competition, including Disney's animated Hexed and the comedy sequel Focker-In-Law.

## The Rocky film that overtook it

I Play Rocky tells the story of the making of the 1976 classic. Sylvester Stallone does not appear in it. [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) plays the young, struggling writer, per MovieWeb.

The film opened in seven theatres on 6 November, grew to 29, then went nationwide on 20 November. It beat its $7 million projection with $9.32 million, backed by an A+ CinemaScore. It averaged $3,763 per theatre, against $2,197 for Ebenezer, which is more than 70% higher.

## Who is still ahead overall

The headline numbers still favour Depp. Ebenezer has earned $32.5 million in the US and $78.4 million worldwide, MovieWeb said, against its $38 million spend. I Play Rocky sits at $10.4 million domestically and $15.1 million globally, with a $30 million budget still to recoup.

So this is a change in momentum, not a verdict on which film has made more money. ## A new biography arrives

Seal, a Vanity Fair contributing editor, wrote The Many Faces of Johnny Depp, published by Gallery Books. USA Today reports it draws on interviews with nearly 100 people who know the actor. Seal did not interview Depp, and relied on earlier interviews and court transcripts. Slate describes it as unauthorised.

Slate's review argues that Ebenezer is the kind of safe, familiar project an actor would choose to win back audiences and executives after years of litigation. That is the critic's view, not a fact about Depp's intentions.

According to the book, as reported by Entertainment Weekly and USA Today, Depp tried to leave 21 Jump Street, where he played Officer Tom Hanson from 1987 to 1990. The claims include arriving on set dressed as Mahatma Gandhi and setting his underwear alight to protest an uncleaned trailer. The book also says he was arrested and charged with assault after a confrontation with a hotel security guard in Vancouver. He pleaded guilty and received an absolute discharge, Entertainment Weekly said.

Entertainment Weekly said it had asked Depp's team for comment. These are claims made in the book, and Depp has not been quoted responding to them in the sources we read.

USA Today also reports that the book credits Nicolas Cage with pushing Depp towards acting, which led to his role in A Nightmare on Elm Street. Depp is quoted as calling that first job luck, done to pay the rent.

## What happens next

Ebenezer faces a crowded Thanksgiving corridor, and its holiday run will show whether the second-week fall was a blip. USA Today also names Day Drinker as an upcoming Depp film.
