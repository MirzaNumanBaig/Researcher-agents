**Quick summary**

- [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) is among more than 150 Australian artists backing a Greenpeace-led campaign against Woodside Energy's Browse gas proposal, The Guardian reported on 18 November.
- The open letter asks federal environment minister [Murray Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Watt) not to approve drilling near Scott Reef, about 300km off the Kimberley coast.
- Woodside says the project is still under detailed public assessment and that Scott Reef itself will not be built on, drilled or mined.
- The federal assessment "decision clock" is paused while the national EPA waits for further technical advice.

## Rebel Wilson signs open letter on Scott Reef

Rebel Wilson has added her name to an open letter opposing a major gas development off Western Australia. According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/18/hugh-jackman-rebel-wilson-australian-artists-greenpeace-campaign-woodside-energy-scott-reef), more than 150 artists have backed the Greenpeace-led campaign against Woodside Energy's plan to develop the Browse basin, roughly 300km off the Kimberley coast.

Fellow actors [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman), [Richard Roxburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Roxburgh) and [Mia Wasikowska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mia_Wasikowska) are also on the list. The letter asks Murray Watt, the federal environment minister, to refuse approval. It argues the project would turn Scott Reef into an industrial gas zone, with years of loud seismic blasting and carbon dumping beneath the seabed.

The signatories cross several creative fields. Musicians include [Jimmy Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Barnes), Paul Kelly, Tame Impala's Kevin Parker, [Tina Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Arena), [Jessica Mauboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Mauboy), G Flip, [Vance Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vance_Joy), [Missy Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missy_Higgins) and [Baker Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Boy). Authors JM Coetzee, [Helen Garner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Garner), [Anna Funder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Funder) and Diana Reid signed as well.

## What Woodside wants to build

Woodside, which The Guardian describes as Australia's largest fossil fuel company, calls Browse the country's biggest undeveloped offshore gas resource. It hopes to win federal approval to drill more than 50 gas wells about 3km from Scott Reef. The reef is home to more than 1,500 species, including endangered pygmy blue whales.

Woodside first proposed Browse in 2018. Under the plan, gas would travel about 900km by pipeline to the company's ageing North West Shelf export plant. That site sits next to the world heritage-listed Murujuga rock art.

## The environmental and cost concerns

The letter warns that drilling at the reef could cause a spill up to 30 times bigger than the 2009 Montara disaster, with effects reaching the Kimberley coastline and possibly Indonesia. Woodside disputes that framing. A company spokesperson told The Guardian that Browse is a natural gas and condensate development, not an oil development, and that references to an "oil spill" are inaccurate. They added that the longest credible well blowout, while highly unlikely, would last around 12 days.

Last month Guardian Australia revealed that Western Australia's independent environmental watchdog had sharply criticised the proposal. A draft Environmental Protection Authority letter written in 2023 said plans to protect turtles and blue whales near the reef would be ineffective, and that it might be impossible to cut the risk of a spill to an acceptable level. Greenpeace has also nominated Scott Reef for national heritage listing, citing its ecological and scientific values.

There is a climate and cost argument too. Modelling by the Institute for Energy Economics and Financial Analysis suggests Browse could add up to 6.8 million tonnes of carbon dioxide a year at peak production. That equals between 2.9% and 3.7% of Australia's emissions in 2035. The institute also found last year that Browse gas is likely four times more expensive than existing domestic gas.

Matt Roberts, executive director of the Conservation Council of Western Australia, said the state has enough gas to support the move to renewables and that regional demand for LNG is declining.

## What happens next

A spokesperson for the national EPA told Guardian Australia the project is being assessed under the national Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act. The decision clock has been paused while the agency waits on further technical advice, so no approval date has been set.

Woodside says the proposal remains subject to a detailed, public and transparent assessment by state and Commonwealth regulators. It says feedback has already reduced the infrastructure footprint around sensitive areas such as Scott Reef.

The open letter follows a musical push last week, when more than 20 artists including King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, Jimmy Barnes, John Butler and Montaigne released the song If Not Now. It reworks King Gizzard's 2020 track and was made with Greenpeace Australia Pacific, Green Music Australia and p(doom) Records.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who has signed the Scott Reef letter?
More than 150 Australian artists, including Rebel Wilson, Hugh Jackman, Richard Roxburgh, Mia Wasikowska, Jessica Mauboy and Jimmy Barnes, plus authors such as JM Coetzee and Helen Garner.

### What is the Browse project?
It is Woodside Energy's proposal to develop gas in the Browse basin off the Kimberley coast, with more than 50 wells planned about 3km from Scott Reef and gas piped about 900km to the North West Shelf plant.

### Who decides whether Browse goes ahead?
The federal environment minister, Murray Watt, makes the decision under national environment law. The assessment clock is currently paused for more technical advice.
