**Quick summary**

- Audiences have given [The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games%3A_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping) a 95% Popcornmeter score, which [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) labels "Verified Hot".
- Critics were far cooler: the film sits at 68% on the Tomatometer and 55/100 on Metacritic, according to recorded film data.
- Fans praise the visuals and the emotional hit, but some say the story feels rushed and too tame next to the book.
- The film opened on 28 October 2026 and has taken about US$237.2 million against a US$150 million budget.

Fans are far more generous than critics to the latest [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) prequel. According to a Rotten Tomatoes editorial published on 20 November 2026, audiences have lifted *Sunrise on the Reaping* to a 95% Popcornmeter score, even though the film debuted to lukewarm reviews from critics.

## Why audiences are so far ahead of critics

The film follows [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada), the Australian actor, as a teenage Haymitch Abernathy competing in the 50th Hunger Games, known as the Second Quarter Quell. Rotten Tomatoes says readers voted it the second-most anticipated movie of November.

The editorial lists Zada, [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons), [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace) and Ralph Fiennes as the stars. Francis Lawrence directed, according to Wikipedia's record of the production.

The gap between the two scores is wide. Wikipedia puts the Tomatometer at 68% and Metacritic at 55 out of 100 from 43 critics. The critics' consensus describes a prequel that gains a lot from spotlighting well-loved characters but retreads familiar ground. In a negative review quoted there, Brandon Yu of The New York Times called the film both bloated and limited.

## What fans are saying

The most common reaction in the Rotten Tomatoes roundup is emotional. One viewer said they sobbed for ages and called it heartbreaking. Another said the person beside them cried through the whole film. A third gave it a 10 out of 10 but said they would not watch it again "for my mental health".

Several reviewers single out the look of the film. One calls the visuals best experienced in IMAX, and another simply tells people to see it on the biggest screen possible. The strongest endorsement in the piece comes from a viewer who calls it the best film of the whole franchise, apart from the wigs near the end.

## Where the complaints sit

Not every viewer is won over. The most frequent complaints are about pacing and depth. One reviewer felt a lot had been cut and wanted the story to slow down. Another wished it ran longer so the characters' relationships had time to build.

There is also a debate over how closely it follows the book. Some readers say the film is PG-rated while the source material is far bloodier, and that the plot and characters feel rushed as a result. Another viewer, who read the book, said the director stuck to it closely. A couple said it was more violent and less focused on relationships than the earlier films, and rated it "just ok".

## The numbers behind the release

Based on the recorded figures, the film opened on 28 October 2026 and has grossed roughly US$237.2 million worldwide. Against a budget of about US$150 million, that is around 1.6 times its cost. It is a solid return for a blockbuster, but a modest one by the standard of the franchise's biggest entries. These figures come from Wikipedia and Wikidata and may change as the run continues.

## What happens next

The film is still in cinemas, so both the audience and critic scores can move. Rotten Tomatoes is inviting viewers to add their own ratings, and the Popcornmeter will keep updating as more people see it. Whether the sentiment holds as general audiences, rather than early fans, fill the seats is the next thing to watch.
