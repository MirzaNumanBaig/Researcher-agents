**Quick summary**

- [Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook) spoke to Canadian outlet Original-Cin on 16 November 2026 about his documentary, Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story.
- The film covers how his business manager, his older half-brother, took millions from his career, according to Original-Cin.
- Cook says he waited years to tell the story and credits therapy and humour with helping him recover.
- The documentary is streaming on Super Channel and Super Channel+ in Canada.

Dane Cook says he only felt ready to put his family betrayal on screen once he could see his career as a finished chapter. In an interview published by Original-Cin on 16 November, the comedian explained why he made Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story and how he got through what he calls a "biblical" ordeal.

## Why he waited to tell the story

Original-Cin describes the film as a raw and personal documentary about discovering that his business manager brother had embezzled millions from his career. Cook told the interviewer that he had already begun to feel he had built a lasting legacy, one he describes as growing up alongside a generation of comedy fans over more than a decade.

That sense of perspective, he said, changed how he saw the later events. He talked about pioneering the internet age of comedy and building an audience at a time when, in his words, the industry had turned him away. The family story arrived years after those highs.

He also passed on a reaction from comedian [Robby Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_Hoffman), who told him backstage at the Comedy Store that the tale sounded biblical. Cook said everything he does, comedy included, aims for heart and humour, and that the two finally came together in this project.

## Humour as an escape hatch

Asked how he avoided sliding back into the insecurities of his shy childhood, Cook said the first instinct in comedy is to keep going. He recalled watching the famous funeral episode of The Mary Tyler Moore Show as a child and learning that laughter can be a way out of pain.

He was candid that fame had not healed everything. Success, he said, had been sitting on top of unresolved sadness. Therapy, honest self-examination and what he called owning his own faults helped him work through it, and he described the crime as something that forced all of it to the surface. He said he knew it would be a long road, which is why the film took so long to arrive.

## A football lesson on staying calm

Cook said the documentary was hard to watch back, even if it felt cathartic. He linked his calm to his parents: a mother who pushed through intense fear to stay cheerful, and an athletic father who wanted wins. He then used a coaching analogy about Bill Belichick, saying he lives by moving forward rather than defending against loss. The source text ends partway through his answer.
