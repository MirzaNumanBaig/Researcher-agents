**Quick summary**

- [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman)'s thriller *The Death of Robin Hood* passed 30 days in HBO Max's US Top 10, according to data from 18 November 2026.
- The film arrived on the platform on 16 October 2026, so it has stayed in the chart for almost its whole run there.
- In cinemas it took US$9.5 million worldwide against a US$20 million budget.
- Separately, Jackman is one of more than 150 Australian artists who signed an open letter about Scott Reef.

*The Death of Robin Hood* did poorly at the box office, but it has now spent 30 days in HBO Max's US Top 10. ScreenRant reported the milestone on 18 November. The film starring Hugh Jackman reached the platform on 16 October, and the outlet says it has stayed in the chart for nearly all of the time since.

## A streaming second life

The numbers show how far the film's fortunes have turned. According to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/hugh-jackman-death-of-robin-hood-hbo-max-streaming-milestone-november-2026/), the thriller made US$9.5 million at the global box office. Its budget was US$20 million. In theatres it recovered less than half its cost.

Five months after its cinema release, it is among the most-watched films on HBO Max in the United States. A full month in the Top 10 is a long stay for a film that was not a hit on release. ScreenRant describes it as growing into a cult favourite among viewers who have found it.

Reviews were mixed from both critics and audiences, the outlet notes. The streaming run suggests word of mouth has been kinder than the first reception.

## What the film is about

Jackman plays an ageing Robin Hood who has withdrawn from the world, weighed down by guilt over the people he killed when he was younger. After he is attacked and badly hurt, he finds shelter in a priory. There he meets a woman and an orphaned girl, and the story turns on whether he can find some redemption.

ScreenRant says this is a darker take than most Robin Hood films, and that Jackman gives the role a weary, Logan-style intensity. The comparison is the outlet's own, and it points back to the actor's final run as Wolverine. For more on that career, see our [Hugh Jackman archive](https://moduscarry.com/tag/hugh-jackman/).

## Why the milestone matters

ScreenRant frames the run as a quiet redemption for the film. It notes that Jackman is better known for the X-Men films, *The Greatest Showman* and *The Prestige*, and that this smaller historical thriller has now reached a wide audience in a different way.

Streaming charts reward films that are easy to find and keep people watching. A longer chart run does not change the theatrical figures, which stay at US$9.5 million against US$20 million. It does change how many people have now seen the film. For a story about a hero questioning his own past, the late attention is a fitting turn. ## Jackman also joins the Scott Reef letter

Jackman's name also appears in a very different story this week. The Times of India reports that he and more than 150 other Australian artists signed an open letter to Federal Environment Minister [Murray Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Watt). It asks him to reject Woodside Energy's plan to drill for gas near Scott Reef, off Western Australia's Kimberley coast.

Other signatories include Kevin Parker of [Tame Impala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tame_Impala), [Tkay Maidza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tkay_Maidza), actor [Simon Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Baker) and singer [Tina Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Arena).

According to Greenpeace, whose campaign the report draws on, Woodside plans to drill up to 57 wells for its Browse project, about 270 kilometres from the coast. The campaign says the project is forecast to produce 1.6 billion tonnes of climate pollution and carries a risk of an oil spill along whale migration routes. The Times of India says it has not independently verified those figures. We reported the letter earlier in the week in our [Scott Reef story](https://moduscarry.com/tag/hugh-jackman/).

## What happens next

ScreenRant says it remains to be seen how long *The Death of Robin Hood* holds its place on HBO Max. The chart data will show whether the run continues past the 30-day mark. On Scott Reef, the open letter now sits with Minister Watt, and the sources do not say when a decision is due.
