**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) is helping Uniqlo launch "Stories She Tells", the brand's first-ever literary magazine, according to WWD.
- She will chair the selection committee for essays chosen through an open call.
- The first issue, themed "Connection", is scheduled for autumn 2027.
- The project launches in partnership with the University of Tokyo, with plans to add universities worldwide.

Cate Blanchett has taken on a new role as Uniqlo's global brand ambassador: chair of the selection committee for "Stories She Tells", a [literary magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_magazine) of essays on women's experiences. [WWD reported](https://wwd.com/fashion-news/fashion-features/uniqlo-cate-blanchett-literary-magazine-launch-1239390854/) the launch from Tokyo, where the Australian actress spoke at a press conference at the University of Tokyo.

## What "Stories She Tells" will be

According to Uniqlo's release, quoted by WWD, the magazine aims to share diverse voices across genders and generations and to foster new dialogue and connections. It is the retailer's first literary magazine.

The inaugural issue is due in autumn 2027, with the theme "Connection": links between people, shared experience and each person's place in society. Essays will be picked through an open call, and Blanchett will lead the committee that chooses them.

Selected contributors will also receive support for their writing and mentorship. Uniqlo plans to include academic research papers and literary works on the theme alongside the essays.

## Why Blanchett says she got involved

Blanchett told WWD the idea grew from conversations about a year ago with Koji Yanai, a group senior executive officer at Uniqlo's parent company Fast Retailing and the son of its founder, Tadashi Yanai. They began by talking about shared passions and different forms of expression.

She said women make up more than half the world's population, yet their stories are often kept out of the mainstream. With Uniqlo's global reach, she argued, the brand could help build confidence in female literary expression. She also noted that Uniqlo already edits its own magazine, so the format is not new to it.

## The University of Tokyo link

The project launches through a partnership with the University of Tokyo and aims to expand through universities around the world. On stage with Blanchett were two students, Kaori Hayashi, the university's executive vice president and a professor, and writer and poet Mieko Kawakami.

Blanchett read passages from Kawakami's novel "Breasts and Eggs" and from "Belonging" by Clarice Lispector, whom she named as two of her favourite writers. Eiko Sherba, Uniqlo's sustainability marketing director, said the university tie-up gives the programme an academic grounding in women's empowerment and gender issues, and brings in young people's energy.

Blanchett also described how she collects language. She keeps notes on her phone of things she reads, and recalled that her late friend, the Australian author Frank Moorhouse, kept a card catalogue of ideas he drew on when writing. That analogue habit, she said, is part of the appeal of a printed magazine that invites readers to slow down.
