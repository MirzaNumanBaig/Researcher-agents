**Quick summary**

- [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) and Ryan Reynolds raced F50 catamarans before the Spain Sail Grand Prix in Valencia on 5 September 2026.
- Reynolds sailed for Canada's NorthStar team and won; Jackman sailed for the BONDS Flying Roos.
- The two co-own the Flying Roos, Australia's SailGP team, with CEO Tom Slingsby.
- Disney+ has announced a premium docuseries that follows the team through a racing season, with no release date yet.
- The New York Times has since listed the Reynolds v Jackman race among nine stories that defined the 2026 SailGP season.

The Deadpool and Wolverine rivalry has found a new stage: the water. Hugh Jackman and Ryan Reynolds went head to head in foiling catamarans in Valencia, and the Canadian came out ahead, according to [Euro Weekly News](https://euroweeklynews.com/2026/09/06/hollywood-icons-hugh-jackman-and-ryan-reynolds-race-across-spains-waters/).

The challenge took place on Saturday 5 September 2026, ahead of the Spain Sail Grand Prix. It has since earned a place in The New York Times' round-up of the season's defining stories, although the paper's page could not be opened for this report, so its details are not used here.

## The Valencia race

Both actors sailed as guest "sixth sailors". Reynolds joined Canada's NorthStar SailGP team, while Jackman crewed for the BONDS Flying Roos. The boats are F50 foiling catamarans that can pass 100 km/h, according to Euro Weekly News.

Reynolds won. He joked that beating Jackman was "a national pastime in Canada". Jackman, for his part, praised the professional sailors for their speed and skill.

Fans gathered at Marina Port Valencia to watch. SailGP is making its debut in the city under a three-year agreement, and the report says the event is expected to lift tourism and showcase the waterfront to a global television audience.

## Why they are racing at all

Jackman and Reynolds are not casual visitors. They co-own the BONDS Flying Roos with team CEO and Olympic champion Tom Slingsby. They are far from the only famous backers: the same report names Anne Hathaway, Kylian Mbappé, Sebastian Vettel, Lindsey Vonn and DJ Khaled among celebrity investors, and says all 13 national teams now have outside investors.

Reynolds had earlier taken a practice ride at about 45 knots, coached by Slingsby as part of SailGP's Sixth Sailor Experience, according to [Yachting World](https://www.yachtingworld.com/news/sailgp-gets-the-star-treatment-new-disney-docuseries-announced-with-ryan-reynolds-and-hugh-jackman-163158). He described it as a highly memorable experience.

## The Disney+ docuseries

Disney+ announced around early June 2026 that it is making a premium docuseries about the Flying Roos, with both co-owners involved. The pair called it their first collaboration since *Deadpool & Wolverine*, promising action, comedy and heart "with a lot more water".

Eric Schrier, a Disney executive, said following the team offers an inside look at what it takes to compete at the top of the sport. Yachting World notes the series had no title or release date at the time, and points out that SailGP's earlier docuseries, *Racing on the Edge*, drew little mainstream attention. A Hollywood-backed America's Cup documentary was announced and later quietly dropped.

## Where the season stood

On the Valencia weekend, the Flying Roos led Group A after day one, while Spain's Los Gallos topped Group B with two race wins. Euro Weekly News described the season as entering its final stages, with 13 national teams competing. That report does not give the overall championship table, so this article does not either.
