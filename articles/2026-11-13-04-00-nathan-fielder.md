**Quick summary**

- [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) told Wired he still cannot say whether Elizabeth Holmes knows when she is lying, according to CinemaBlend.
- He spent weeks living with Holmes and her partner before she reported to prison in 2023.
- The A24 documentary *You Can See Everything*, which he co-directed with Lance Oppenheim, has grossed about US$86.5 million on a US$7 million budget, per Wikipedia.
- Critics have rated it highly: 96% on Rotten Tomatoes and 97/100 on Metacritic.

Nathan Fielder says the central puzzle of his new documentary is one he has not solved. In a recent interview with Wired, quoted by [CinemaBlend](https://www.cinemablend.com/movies/thing-nathan-fielder-found-interesting-elizabeth-holmes-wasnt-in-you-can-see-everything), the filmmaker said he still cannot work out whether Elizabeth Holmes knows she is lying when she makes her claims.

## The question Fielder cannot answer

*You Can See Everything* is built around a simple problem for the audience: who is telling the truth, and what is the truth at all? CinemaBlend's Hugh Scott notes that Fielder spent weeks living with Holmes and her partner just before the former Theranos founder went to prison in 2023, and still has no firm grip on what she actually believes.

The film is designed that way. Fielder rarely challenges Holmes directly, with a couple of exceptions, even when she says she has never lied about anything in her life. He also does not define truth for the viewer, and he never settles whether Holmes herself knows when she is not telling it.

## What he told Wired

Fielder's answer to Wired, as quoted by CinemaBlend, is that the usual idea of a lie does not quite fit. A classic lie, he said, is a person consciously saying something they know is untrue in that moment. With Holmes, he suggested, it is less clear-cut, because it is not just her words but the design of every part of her life that seems to be involved.

He added that what interests him about her goes beyond any single statement. He said he cannot piece it together: she makes claims that make you wonder how anyone could believe them, and he is left asking whether something is a lie if she believes it to be true. These are Fielder's views as reported, not findings of fact about what Holmes knows or intends.

## A film that keeps people talking

CinemaBlend's writer says the documentary stays in the mind for days or weeks, and that he saw it a month ago and still wants to talk about it. He compares it to peeling an onion, where every thread pulled reveals another layer.

The numbers suggest plenty of others are talking too. According to the film's [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything), the documentary was released on 6 September 2026 and has earned about US$86.5 million against a US$7 million budget, roughly 12 times its cost. That is a strong result for a low-budget documentary.

Reviews match the box office. Wikipedia lists a 96% Rotten Tomatoes score from 226 reviews and a Metacritic score of 97 from 42 critics. It also reports a 98% positive score from PostTrak audiences, with 91% saying they would definitely recommend it. The critics' consensus on Rotten Tomatoes calls it an absorbing and haunting test of Holmes's psyche that pushes Fielder's commitment to the bit to its limit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is You Can See Everything about?

It is an A24 documentary directed by Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, centred on Elizabeth Holmes. Fielder lived with Holmes and her partner for weeks before she went to prison in 2023.

### Does Nathan Fielder think Elizabeth Holmes is lying?

He told Wired it is not that black and white. He said he cannot tell whether she is consciously saying untrue things or whether her claims are part of how she sees her whole life.

### How well is the film doing?

According to Wikipedia, it has grossed about US$86.5 million on a US$7 million budget. It holds 96% on Rotten Tomatoes and 97/100 on Metacritic.
