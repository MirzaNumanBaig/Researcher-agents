The [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson) strength routine puts strength ahead of flexibility, and she says her body needs it. In an interview published by Grazia on 26 November 2026, the 59-year-old explained how she manages joint hypermobility, and the details were reported by [Limapagi](https://www.limapagi.id/en/anderson-strength-baywatch-reboot) and [Bsinews](https://bsinews.id/en/pamela-anderson-strength-hypermobility).

## Why Pamela Anderson's strength routine matters to her

Joint hypermobility means joints move beyond the usual range. Some people notice nothing. Others deal with joint pain, recurring sprains, muscle fatigue or instability, according to the reports on the Grazia interview.

Anderson put it plainly. "I have always trained my body, I have joint hypermobility and I need to maintain my strength," she said. She also described herself as "more flexible than strong", which is why resistance work sits at the centre of what she does.

The reasoning is simple. Stronger muscles around a loose joint give it extra support. The coverage lists controlled resistance exercises, walking, swimming and cycling among the activities commonly linked with managing the condition.

## A day built around walking and gardening

Anderson does not rely on punishing gym sessions. "I walk every morning; I have to get fresh air, no matter the weather," she said, according to the Grazia interview as relayed by [Bsinews](https://bsinews.id/en/pamela-anderson-strength-hypermobility).

Gardening counts as training too. [Demokratis](https://demokratis.co.id/en/anderson-strength-baywatch-return) reports she sees it as far more than a "cute hobby", because digging, lifting, bending and carrying all make the body work. Grazia also mentions controlled bodyweight movements, possibly Pilates-style, and regular medical check-ins as part of how she looks after her joints.

That sits a long way from the high-profile fitness image tied to her 1990s fame.
