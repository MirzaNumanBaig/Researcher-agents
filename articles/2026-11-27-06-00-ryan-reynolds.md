**Quick summary**

- [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and co-chairman [Rob Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney) posted tributes to Wrexham assistant manager Steve Parkin on Instagram.
- Parkin, 61, announced he is stepping away from football and leaves the club on Monday, 30 November.
- His last game is Saturday's Championship match against Portsmouth.
- Separately, The Hollywood Reporter says Reynolds is developing another Deadpool film, according to LRMonline.

Ryan Reynolds has thanked Wrexham AFC assistant manager Steve Parkin in a public message after the long-serving coach announced he is leaving football. The Wrexham co-chairman shared the club's tribute on Instagram on 26 November, as Parkin prepares to depart at the end of the month.

## What Reynolds and Rob Mac said

According to [The Leader](https://www.leaderlive.co.uk/news/26766101.ryan-reynolds-rob-mac-wrexhams-steve-parkin-exit/), Reynolds reposted a club tribute that carried pictures from Parkin's time at Wrexham, along with the Welsh word "Diolch" and the words "Thank you, Steve". His own caption read: "Love you, Steve. Thank you for everything you've done for Wrexham on and off the pitch."

Rob Mac, the club's other Hollywood co-chairman, shared the farewell message from Wrexham's account too. He wrote "good game sir good game" and then "thank you Steve", the paper reports.

Parkin returned the thanks in his own statement. He credited the two owners for their "incredible support, vision and wisdom in driving the transformation of our football club", according to The Leader.

## Why Parkin's exit matters at Wrexham

Parkin arrived in July 2021 with manager Phil Parkinson, a partnership that had already taken in Bradford City, Bolton Wanderers and Sunderland. Together they have worked 14 years and more than 762 matches, and the pair count five promotions between them.

At Wrexham the record is hard to ignore. The club won the National League in 2022/23, then went up from League Two the following season, and completed a third successive promotion by climbing out of League One. Last season Wrexham finished seventh in the Championship, the club's highest finish on record.

Parkin said the decision followed "much thought and careful consideration" and that he wants more time with his family and close friends. He singled out the relationships he made over the three promotions, saying the people of Wrexham had made his family feel welcome.

Parkinson said the two had been discussing the move for a few weeks and that the club fully respects it. Parkin's reply to his long-time boss was brief: "Fourteen years, 762 games and five promotions, thank you Phil, it's been some journey."
