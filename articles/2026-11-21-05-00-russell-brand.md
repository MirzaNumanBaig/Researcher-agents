**Quick summary**

- [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) was sentenced to 10 years and six months after a jury convicted him at Southwark Crown Court on 5 November, according to Heatworld.
- He was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women, and cleared of two further counts of rape and one of attempted indecent assault.
- Since the sentence, comedians and entertainers have described warnings they gave or heard over many years.
- The Irish Times says the Netflix documentary on his rise and fall keeps returning to one phrase: "nobody wanted to know".

Years of private whispers and a few public insults now sit alongside a criminal conviction. After Brand's sentencing, several people who worked near him have said the warning signs were visible long before the 2023 investigation that led to his arrest, and that few in the industry acted on them.

## What the court decided

Brand, 51, was convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, [Heatworld reports](https://heatworld.com/celebrity/news/all-of-these-stars-warned-us-about-russell-brand-why-didnt-we-listen/). He was cleared of two other counts of rape and one of attempted indecent assault. He denied all the allegations.

The case began with a joint investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches in 2023, which prompted a Metropolitan Police inquiry. He was formally charged in April 2025, with more charges added that December. His lawyers argued the women only went to police after the documentary aired.

Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy said Brand used his influence, status and fame to exploit women. He called the outcome a testament to the courage and determination of those who came forward.

## The warnings made in public

Some of the earliest criticism was on the record. Singer [Dannii Minogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dannii_Minogue) called Brand "a bit of a vile predator" in 2006, after appearing on his short-lived MTV show, and said he would not take no for an answer.

In 2013, his former wife [Katy Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Perry) told Vogue he had been "very controlling" and "really hurtful". The pair were married for 13 months in 2010. The late comedian Sean Lock criticised him on 8 Out of 10 Cats in 2014, saying his own fear as a father was behind his strong feelings.

Comedian Katherine Ryan says she told Brand to his face, in front of an audience, that he was a predator while filming Comedy Central's Roast Battle in 2023. Heatworld reports that the remark was cut from the broadcast. She later discussed it with [Louis Theroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Theroux).

## The warnings passed around quietly

Others describe a private network of caution. Comedian [Daniel Sloss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Sloss) told Dispatches that groups set up by female comedians to warn each other discussed Brand, and that the stories came from many different people.

Writer and comedian [Katy Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katy_Brand), who is no relation, says that in 2019 she asked producers on a TV comedy whether they had done due diligence on him and said she would quit if he was cast. According to Heatworld, the show was scrapped rather than recast. Campaigner Gina Martin says she first heard about his behaviour in a Soho comedy club in 2018, and urged others in the industry to "use your platform" after the verdict.

[Georgina Baillie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgina_Baillie), whose late grandfather [Andrew Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Sachs) was the target of Brand's 2008 Radio 2 answerphone messages, said Brand should now apologise to the women he was convicted of attacking.

## Why it went on

In The Irish Times, Ed Power writes that Brand kept shifting persona, from misogynist comedian to left-wing campaigner to commentator on the right and then a born-again Christian, depending on who was watching. He recalls a 2007 phone interview in which Brand insisted his stage character was an act.

Power notes that comedian Nish Kumar describes a culture in which objecting was dismissed as being a "party pooper". Kumar also says that being treated as "the talent" enabled Brand and prolonged the situation, something he thinks the industry should be ashamed of. Power adds that Brand had money for top lawyers and kept working even after his conduct was widely discussed.

## What happens next

Brand is serving his sentence. The coverage so far points to continuing debate about how entertainment companies handle complaints about powerful performers, though neither source reports any formal industry change yet. We will update this story as confirmed developments arrive.

## Frequently asked questions

### What was Russell Brand convicted of?

A jury at Southwark Crown Court convicted him on 5 November of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault involving four women. He was cleared of two further rape counts and one of attempted indecent assault.

### How long is Russell Brand's sentence?

He was sentenced to 10 years and six months, according to Heatworld and The Irish Times.

### How did the case begin?

A 2023 joint investigation by The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches led the Metropolitan Police to open an inquiry. He was charged in April 2025.
