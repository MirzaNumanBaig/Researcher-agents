**Quick summary**

- [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) spoke at length about the Elizabeth Holmes documentary *You Can See Everything* in a Rolling Stone interview published on 11 November.
- She says the project carried a constant sense of risk and she wondered whether it might "destroy my career".
- [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) first reached her through a phone number from his friend [Emma Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Stone).
- Seyfried did not tell her agent or lawyer about the film until after she had shot her part, according to USA Today.
- The documentary is now in cinemas in the United States.

Amanda Seyfried has finally explained why she was so reluctant to discuss *You Can See Everything*, the documentary in which she revisits Elizabeth Holmes, the role that won her an Emmy. In a Rolling Stone interview on 11 November, reported by [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/movies/2026/11/12/amanda-seyfried-elizabeth-holmes-interview/92739145007/), she said making it felt dangerous and that she feared it could damage her career.

## How Emma Stone set it in motion

According to USA Today, Seyfried had been comically cagey about the film for about a month, telling one journalist she would rather talk about emus, olives and hard ciders. In the Rolling Stone conversation, she opened up alongside codirectors Nathan Fielder and [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim).

Fielder got her phone number from Stone, a friend and collaborator. A 30-minute Zoom followed, and then he visited her apartment to show her footage from the film. In it, he had embedded himself with the former Theranos chief for a month in 2023, before her prison sentence began.

Seyfried first assumed he wanted advice on playing Holmes in Hulu's *The Dropout*. "After the Zoom, I was totally game," she told Rolling Stone, saying the chance to join the journey in any capacity appealed to her.

## "Am I going to destroy my career?"

The risk was part of the appeal and part of the worry. Seyfried said there was "always this vibration of risk", and that she asked herself whether she would get tangled in a "wasp's nest" or say something that fed negativity towards Holmes.

USA Today reports that she did not tell her agent or lawyer she was filming until the work was done. In the film she plays Holmes in a series of reenactments, and she also helps look for answers about Holmes's fiancé, Billy Evans, who allegedly sought to profit from the documentary without his partner's knowledge.

What drove her was curiosity. The footage of Evans gave her strong negative feelings, she said, and she became what she called a "weird Billy detective". She wanted to meet him, calling the prospect exciting, scary and questionable.

## The dinner that became a talking point

One of the film's most talked-about scenes has Seyfried and Fielder visiting Evans at home. While she plays with Holmes's young children, Evans takes a phone call from Holmes in prison, and Seyfried makes small talk with her.

"We didn't know she was gonna call right then," Seyfried recalled, describing herself cuddling Holmes's dog and sitting with her children while Holmes was in prison. When people ask whether it was really Holmes on the line, she answers that it is a documentary and asks who else it would be.

A tense dinner follows. Seyfried questions Evans about how he controls Holmes's social media accounts. He responds by attacking her over earlier negative comments about the activist [Charlie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Kirk), who USA Today notes was killed last year.

Seyfried told Rolling Stone she was trying to connect, but that Evans became needlessly aggressive. She said she disliked how he uses Holmes as "a tool" while describing the relationship as "building monuments of love". Maybe he respects her, Seyfried added, but his behaviour suggests otherwise. These are Seyfried's views, and the reported claims about Evans come from the interview and the documentary's account.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is You Can See Everything?
It is a documentary about former Theranos chief Elizabeth Holmes, directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim. Fielder spent a month with Holmes in 2023, before her prison sentence started.

### What is Amanda Seyfried's role in it?
She reprises her portrayal of Holmes in reenactments and takes part in scenes with Billy Evans, Holmes's fiancé. She previously won an Emmy for playing Holmes in *The Dropout*.

### How did Seyfried get involved?
Fielder obtained her number from Emma Stone, held a 30-minute Zoom, then showed her footage at her apartment.

### Why did she think it could hurt her career?
She told Rolling Stone the project carried constant risk, including the chance of saying the wrong thing or being drawn into a messy situation with Evans.
