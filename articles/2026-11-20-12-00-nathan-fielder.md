**Quick summary**

- [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) and [Lance Oppenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Oppenheim) are one of four directing teams in this year's Oscar conversation, according to Variety on 20 November 2026.
- The Academy's directors branch has given best director to a duo only three times in 98 years.
- Variety says Fielder and Oppenheim compete in a documentary lane the branch has always kept separate.
- Their film, *You Can See Everything*, is rated 97% on Rotten Tomatoes, according to Wikipedia.

Nathan Fielder's first serious run at Oscar attention comes with an awkward piece of arithmetic. He shares the director's credit on *You Can See Everything* with Lance Oppenheim, and [Variety's awards column](https://variety.com/2026/film/awards/directing-duos-lord-miller-javis-fielder-oppenheim-oscars-1236952486/) argues that this season's four directing duos are up against one of the Academy's longest-running habits: it does not like to split the statuette.

## Four duos, one stubborn rule

Variety lists Fielder and Oppenheim alongside Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi, the Spanish pair known as Los Javis, who made *[La Bola Negra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_bola_negra)*. The others are [Phil Lord and Chris Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Lord_and_Christopher_Miller), who directed *[Project Hail Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Hail_Mary)* for Amazon MGM, and the twin brothers Arie and Chuko Esiri, who made *Clarissa* for Neon.

The column puts the history bluntly. Over 98 years, only four directing duos have been nominated for best director, one of them twice, and only three have won. One prominent member of the directors branch told Variety that needing two people to make a film is "a problem". A veteran strategist said the first question other directors will ask is why two people were needed.

## What history rewards

According to Variety, the pairs that got through were long-standing partnerships. Robert Wise and Jerome Robbins won for *West Side Story*. The Coen brothers won for *No Country for Old Men* and were nominated again for *True Grit*. Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert, known as the Daniels, took the prize most recently for *Everything Everywhere All at Once* in 2022. They met making music videos.

The column places Fielder and Oppenheim, and Lord and Miller, in that professional-partnership mould. It places the Esiris on the Coens' sibling path and calls Los Javis the wild card.

Plenty of duos have missed out too. Variety notes that Jonathan Dayton and Valerie Faris won Screen Actors Guild and Producers Guild honours for *Little Miss Sunshine* and still missed a directing nomination. The Safdie brothers were overlooked for *Uncut Gems*, though Josh Safdie was nominated for his solo debut, *Marty Supreme*.

## Where Fielder stands

Variety is clear that none of the four teams is a conventional frontrunner. Fielder and Oppenheim turned the story of Elizabeth Holmes into what the column calls a slippery A24 documentary. The directors branch has always treated documentary as its own territory, which is the hurdle Variety highlights for them.

The film itself has been strong with critics. According to [Wikipedia's entry on the film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/You_Can_See_Everything), it holds 97% from 229 reviews on Rotten Tomatoes and 97 out of 100 on Metacritic from 43 critics. The same page lists a US$7 million budget and a worldwide gross of about US$93 million, roughly 13 times its cost. Wikipedia's summary of critics describes a film that is both absorbing and haunting. Those figures come from a crowd-edited reference page, not an audited tracker, so treat them with some care.

## The Australian angle

The film has not reached every market at the same pace. Writing in [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/nov/20/watch-you-can-see-everything-for-the-culture-cringe-comedy-film), Australian columnist Rebecca Shaw says she has long avoided cringe comedy, including Fielder's earlier work such as *Nathan For You* and *The Rehearsal*. She has now decided she must see this one anyway, because the film is "everywhere" and she wants to join the conversation. She also notes that films often reach Australia a few months after the rest of the world.

Shaw's piece is opinion, not a review. It does show how far the film has travelled beyond Fielder's existing fans. ## Frequently asked questions

### Who directed *You Can See Everything*?

Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim share the director's credit, according to Variety and Wikipedia. The film centres on Elizabeth Holmes and is released by A24.

### How often has a directing duo won the Oscar for best director?

Variety says the Academy has given the prize to a duo only three times in 98 years. The most recent winners were the Daniels for *Everything Everywhere All at Once*.

### Is Fielder expected to be nominated for best director?

Variety does not call him a frontrunner. The column says he and Oppenheim compete in a documentary lane the directors branch has always kept separate, and no nomination has been announced.
