**Quick summary**

- The Wild Horse Nine box office picture: Searchlight has widened [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh)'s film to 2,175 US theatres this weekend, 1,075 more than its opening run, according to [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/wild-horse-nine-box-office-update/).
- Deadline's figures, as cited by MovieWeb, project about $4.6 million for the second weekend, down 22% from a $5.9 million debut.
- The film holds 94% from critics on Rotten Tomatoes and a Verified Hot 92% from audiences.
- Worldwide takings reached $28 million after ten days, against a reported budget of $13 million to $15 million.

The Wild Horse Nine box office story is one of word of mouth. Searchlight Pictures has nearly doubled the film's US theatre count to 2,175 this weekend, according to [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/wild-horse-nine-box-office-update/), after strong audience scores followed a modest platform opening for [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh)'s first film since *The Banshees of Inisherin*.

## A wider release built on reviews

The expansion adds 1,075 screens to the opening run. McDonagh's fifth feature is a dark comedy set in 1973, in which [Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell) and John Malkovich play two CIA men sent to Easter Island in the days before the Chilean coup. It follows the pattern of McDonagh's earlier releases: a festival premiere, a limited start and a slow build towards awards season.

The numbers behind the decision are strong. MovieWeb reports a 94% critics' score on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) and a [Verified Hot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) 92% audience rating. PostTrak exit polling gave the film a 5 out of 5 average, with 72% of viewers saying they would definitely recommend it.

Those scores sit alongside a festival win. Malkovich took the [Volpi Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Film_Festival) for [Best Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Actor) at Venice, after a long standing ovation for the film.

## What the Wild Horse Nine box office shows so far

The takings are more measured than the reviews. Deadline's figures, quoted by MovieWeb, put the second weekend at roughly $4.6 million, a 22% drop from the $5.9 million opening. The per-theatre average fell from $5,369 to $2,115, which is expected when a film moves from a handful of screens to more than two thousand.

The worldwide total stands at $28 million after ten days. MovieWeb notes a reported production budget of $13 million to $15 million, and says that leaves the film within a few weeks of outright profitability. That is a budget-and-gross comparison from one outlet, not a studio statement, so treat the timing as an estimate.

## Awards season and the performances

MovieWeb points to the awards angle as part of the logic. It says McDonagh's last two features together drew seven acting nominations and two wins, and that the loudest praise this time goes to his leads. It quotes The Hollywood Reporter's David Rooney calling Malkovich's turn "the real revelation". Empire's Ian Freer wrote that "Malkovich and Rockwell make a double act for the ages."

A wide release through November keeps the film in front of voters and on year-end lists at the moment they begin to pay attention. Searchlight has played that game before, according to MovieWeb.

## What happens next

The next test is the third weekend, when the film's hold on those 2,175 screens will show whether audience goodwill turns into steady business. None of the sources reviewed sets out Searchlight's longer plan, so the studio's next move is unconfirmed. Watch for the worldwide total to pass the film's reported budget, and for the first awards-season nominations that would extend its run.
