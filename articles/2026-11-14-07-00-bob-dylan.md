**Quick summary**

- Far Out Magazine reports that "Like a Rolling Stone" drew on the "rollin' stone" line in Hank Williams' 1949 hit "Lost Highway".
- The song reached number two on the US Billboard chart in 1965 and, by Bob Dylan's own account in 1966, pulled him back from thinking about quitting.
- On 7 November, 17 performers played a Dylan tribute concert at Tantra in San Marcos, Texas, according to The University Star.
- American Songwriter says Dylan's rhyming style helped inspire Sara Evans' 2004 country hit "Suds in the Bucket".

[Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) wrote "Like a Rolling Stone" in 1965, and a new feature from Far Out Magazine argues it began with a borrowed lyric. The magazine traces the song to [Hank Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hank_Williams) and his 1949 country hit "Lost Highway". The story arrives in the same week a Texas student concert and a country-music anecdote showed how far his writing still reaches.

## How "Like a Rolling Stone" began

According to [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/bob-dylan-greatest-song-began-accidentally-with-a-stolen-lyric/), Williams sang "I'm a rollin' stone, all alone and lost" on "Lost Highway", a song that reached number 12 on the US country chart. Dylan's 1965 line, "How does it feel to be on your own? Like a complete unknown. Like a rolling stone", sits close to it. The publication also notes that the album carrying the song is called *Highway 61 Revisited*, which it reads as another possible nod. That link is the writer's interpretation, not something Dylan has confirmed in the article.

The magazine adds that Williams was not even the first to record "Lost Highway". Leon Payne wrote and recorded it a year earlier. Williams died in 1953, aged 29. Dylan later wrote in his autobiography *Chronicles* that news of Williams' death hit him squarely on the shoulder.

## A song that began as ten pages of verse

Dylan described the origin himself. Far Out reports that the piece started as a long stretch of verse, reportedly ten pages, with no title. "It wasn't called anything, just a rhythm thing on paper," he later recalled. He said it only became a song when he sat at the piano and the words "How does it feel?" began to sing in slow motion.

The result ran about six minutes, which made radio stations hesitant at first. It still peaked at number two on the US Billboard chart. Speaking in 1966, Dylan said that before it he had been close to quitting singing, and that this was the song that changed things. He later called it the best he had written so far in a conversation with the critic Ralph Gleason.

## Tribute night in San Marcos

The music is also being played live by people who were not born when it came out. The University Star reports that the third annual "May Your Song Always Be Sung: The Music of Bob Dylan" concert took place on Saturday, 7 November, at Tantra in San Marcos. Seventeen performers took part, and organisers planned the night from August, with the first rehearsal in mid-October.

Producer Mason Moreno, a history senior at Texas State University, mixed deep cuts with "All Along the Watchtower". The show ran acoustic for an hour and then went electric, a deliberate echo of Dylan's 1965 shift. Bassist and vocalist Mia Youngblood said she joined as a favour. Dylan had covered "Fixin' to Die Blues" by her great-grandfather, [Bukka White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukka_White).

## The country hit with a Dylan fingerprint

American Songwriter adds a smaller story with the same theme. Billy Montana, who co-wrote "Suds in the Bucket" with Tammy Wagoner, told Songfacts that Dylan's habit of bending words to fit a rhyme gave him confidence. He pointed to a line about something Dylan "never knowed", rhymed with "road". Sara Evans' recording became a 2004 country hit, even though her label was reluctant at first. It ended up as the closing track on her album *Restless*.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which song inspired "Like a Rolling Stone"?
Far Out Magazine points to Hank Williams' 1949 song "Lost Highway", which contains the line "I'm a rollin' stone". Leon Payne wrote and first recorded that song a year earlier.

### How high did "Like a Rolling Stone" chart?
It peaked at number two on the US Billboard chart, according to Far Out, despite radio doubts about its six-minute length.

### Which Dylan tribute concert took place this month?
"May Your Song Always Be Sung" ran on 7 November at Tantra in San Marcos, Texas, with 17 performers, The University Star reports.
