**Quick summary**

- Wrexham AFC, co-owned by [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob McElhenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney), has been fined £75,000 by the English Football League (EFL).
- £25,000 of the fine is suspended, so £50,000 is payable now.
- The club agreed the decision with the EFL over two breaches of player eligibility rules, one on 28 August and one on 2 September.
- Wrexham says the paperwork errors were not the fault of manager Phil Parkinson, his coaching staff or sporting director Steve Nickson.
- The club sits 17th in the Championship and plays Sheffield United away at 8pm on Friday, 30 October.

Wrexham have been fined £75,000 by the EFL after fielding one player who was not eligible and naming another in a matchday squad before he was registered. The club, owned by Hollywood stars Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney, confirmed an "Agreed Decision" with the league on 29 October, according to reports from [North Wales Live](https://www.dailypost.co.uk/sport/ryan-reynolds-wrexham-fined-75000-34834819) and the [Daily Star](https://www.dailystar.co.uk/sport/football/wrexham-ryan-reynolds-fined-efl-37822996).

## What the EFL found

The fine totals £75,000, but £25,000 is suspended. In practice that means the club pays £50,000 now, with the remainder hanging over it as a deterrent.

Wrexham's own statement describes two separate breaches. The first involves [Issa Kabore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_Kabor%C3%A9), who came off the bench in the Championship defeat to Birmingham City on Friday, 28 August 2026. The club says it held the correct right-to-work documentation for him but did not submit it properly to the EFL, which made him ineligible for that match. Reports put the scoreline at 2-1 to Birmingham.

The second concerns Callum O'Hare, who was named in the matchday squad for the Championship win over Millwall on Wednesday, 2 September 2026. In the club's words, he "had not been registered in time to be eligible for the fixture". He did not play in the game.

## A paperwork problem, not a coaching one

Both breaches are administrative. The club says it had Kabore's documents and simply failed to lodge them correctly, while O'Hare's registration was not completed before kick-off.

A club spokesperson told BBC Sport, as relayed by the Daily Star and [AOL](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/ryan-reynolds-forced-fork-wrexham-082300000.html), that the mistakes were not down to manager [Phil Parkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Parkinson), his coaching staff or the recently appointed sporting director, Steve Nickson. Wrexham added that it is "taking the appropriate steps to prevent such issues occurring in the future".

## A hard start to the season

The fine lands during a difficult stretch on the pitch. Wrexham finished seventh last season, missing a playoff place by two points, and supporters expected Parkinson to build on that. Instead, according to the reports, the club has won only three of its first 12 league games and sits 17th in the table.

It was also knocked out of the Carabao Cup in the first round by Middlesbrough. The contrast with the club's rise under Reynolds and McElhenney, which was documented in the series [Welcome to Wrexham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Wrexham), is a theme running through the coverage. Some outlets have described the slump as "second-season syndrome" in the Championship, which is a journalist's label rather than a finding.

None of the three sources quotes Reynolds or McElhenney on the fine. The statement came from the club.

## What happens next

Wrexham travel to Sheffield United for an 8pm kick-off on Friday, 30 October, a chance to start climbing from 17th. Off the field, the suspended £25,000 gives the club a clear reason to tighten its registration process. The EFL has not announced any further action in the sources reviewed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much was Wrexham fined by the EFL?

The fine is £75,000, of which £25,000 is suspended. That leaves £50,000 payable now, according to the club's statement as reported by North Wales Live and the Daily Star.

### Why was Wrexham fined?

The club agreed to two breaches of EFL player eligibility rules. Issa Kabore played as a substitute against Birmingham City without his right-to-work documents being properly submitted, and Callum O'Hare was in the squad for the Millwall game without being registered in time.

### Was Ryan Reynolds personally involved?

The sources do not say so. Reynolds and Rob McElhenney are the club's co-owners, and the fine was issued to the club. The statement was issued by Wrexham, not by either owner.
