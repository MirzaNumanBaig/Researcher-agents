**Quick summary**

- Environment minister Murray Watt says [Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) and other campaigners are "not wrong to be concerned" about Woodside's Browse gas project, according to The Guardian.
- The project would sit near Scott Reef, about 300km off the Kimberley coast in Western Australia.
- The federal government is expected to decide under national environment laws early next year.
- More than 150 Australian artists backed a Greenpeace-led open letter against the project last week.
- Separately, Jackman's name is back in Marvel conversation ahead of Avengers: Doomsday, though he has not been announced for it.

Hugh Jackman has won a measured nod from the federal government in his campaign against a major gas development in Western Australia. Environment minister [Murray Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Watt) said he "absolutely understands" why the actor and other campaigners fear the Browse project could have "very serious impacts" on nature, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/27/hugh-jackman-woodside-gas-project-wa-reef-murray-watt).

## What the minister said

Watt told reporters that concerns had existed "from day one" of the proposal, and that the national environment agency still has to assess whether the impacts can be managed. He noted that the former government decided in 2019 that Browse was likely to have significant impacts on several environmental matters.

He did not signal how he will rule. What he did say is that the Environment Protection Authority needs to go through the scientific evidence in far more detail before any decision is made.

## Why Scott Reef matters

The Guardian reports that scientists and Western Australian state regulators have documented the risk of leakage and oil spills near the reef. Scott Reef is home to more than 1,500 species, many found only in that area, including endangered green turtles and migratory whales.

Browse was first proposed by Woodside in 2018. Gas would be piped about 900km to the North West Shelf export plant, which had its life extended to 2070 last year despite concerns about its possible effect on the Murujuga rock art, which is world heritage listed.

In 2023, WA's independent environmental watchdog sharply criticised the proposal in a draft letter. It warned that reducing the risk of an oil spill to an acceptable level might be impossible, and that plans to protect marine life near the reef would be ineffective, according to the same report.

## The celebrity campaign

More than 150 Australian artists, including Jackman, [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) and Jessica Mauboy, signed an open letter last week backing the Greenpeace-led campaign. They asked the government to block the project.

There is a timing detail worth knowing. Watt said any project assessed before 1 December falls under the previous rules, and that includes Browse. The new laws set a higher bar for fossil fuel proposals lodged after that date, though they do not count climate effects of emissions as grounds to refuse a development. The Guardian notes that no fossil fuel proposal has been rejected since the reforms passed.
