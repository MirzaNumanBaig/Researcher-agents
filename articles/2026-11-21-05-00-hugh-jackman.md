**Quick summary**

- More than 150 Australian entertainers, musicians and writers, including Hugh Jackman, signed an open letter in mid-November 2026.
- It asks Federal Environment Minister Murray Watt to reject Woodside Energy's Browse gas development near Scott Reef.
- The plan involves drilling up to 57 wells in and around the coral system, off Western Australia's Kimberley coast.
- Greenpeace Australia Pacific and Green Music Australia backed the campaign; Woodside says it has already changed parts of its plan.

[Hugh Jackman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Jackman) has put his name to a public appeal against a gas project near Scott Reef. In mid-November 2026, more than 150 Australian creatives signed an open letter urging Environment Minister [Murray Watt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Watt) to turn down Woodside Energy's Browse development, according to [Zoom TV](https://www.zoomtventertainment.com/hollywood/hugh-jackman-rebel-wilson-scott-reef-gas-drilling-protest-article-156543315).

## Who signed the Scott Reef letter

Jackman is joined by actors [Rebel Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebel_Wilson) and [Simon Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Baker). The music list is long: [Kevin Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Parker) of [Tame Impala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tame_Impala), [Missy Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missy_Higgins), Paul Kelly, [Tina Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Arena), [Jimmy Barnes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Barnes), [Jessica Mauboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Mauboy), G Flip, [Tkay Maidza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tkay_Maidza), [Amy Shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Shark), members of 5 Seconds of Summer, [Peter Garrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Garrett) and [Tim Minchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Minchin).

The campaign was driven by environmental groups including Greenpeace Australia Pacific and Green Music Australia. The Times of India, citing a report in The Guardian, says the push is a clear escalation of public pressure on Canberra.

Several signatories added their own words. Higgins warned that drilling up to 57 wells under and around the ancient coral system puts an extraordinary place at risk, adding: "We need to preserve these beautiful places for our kids. If not now, then when?" Mauboy said caring for Country means caring for the oceans and everything living in them, and that places like Scott Reef must be protected for future generations, [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/entertainment/english/hollywood/news/in-2026-hugh-jackman-rebel-wilson-and-more-celebrities-sign-an-open-letter-to-save-australias-historic-scott-reef/articleshow/136024089.cms) reported. Neither outlet quotes Jackman directly on the issue.

## Why Scott Reef matters

The reef sits about 270 kilometres north-west of the Kimberley coast and is one of Australia's largest standalone oceanic coral systems. It lies directly above the underwater Browse gas basin. Both outlets say it supports more than 1,500 marine species and serves as a critical habitat for threatened animals, including endangered whales and sea turtles.

That scale is the core of the argument. The signatories are not objecting to a distant, abstract project; they are pointing at a single, enclosed ecosystem and asking what a field of up to 57 wells would do to it.

## The climate and regulatory concerns

The Times of India, citing reports from The Point, says the Browse project is forecast to produce 1.6 billion tonnes of climate pollution over its lifetime. It also reports on a previously undisclosed letter from Western Australia's Environmental Protection Authority, which warned that the wider development could cause "significant, serious, and irreversible" environmental impacts.

For comparison, those are figures attributed to media reports and campaigners, not a final government finding. The federal decision is still to be made.

## What Woodside has changed

Woodside has already adjusted its plans in response to regulatory concerns. According to both outlets, the company will not drill within 20 kilometres of Sandy Islet, a move aimed at protecting turtle nesting sites. It has also committed to restricting gas flaring, because the bright light can disorient turtle hatchlings.

Woodside maintains the project is a necessary part of natural gas production. The coalition argues those changes do not remove the risk, so the dispute between developer and campaigners remains open.

## What happens next

The letter now sits with Murray Watt, who will decide whether the Browse development goes ahead. The sources do not give a decision date.
