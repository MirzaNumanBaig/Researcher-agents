**Quick summary**

- [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) recently met Pope Leo at the Apostolic Palace in Rome, according to The Age.
- The Pope gave Dylan a rosary, and Dylan gave the Pope a copy of his memoir, Chronicles: Volume One.
- Pope Leo had already quoted an unreleased 1963 Dylan lyric in the preface to a Vatican-published book on peace.
- Far Out Magazine revisits how Dylan's 1965 album Bringing It All Back Home won over Jerry Garcia.
- Guitar World reports that Memphis guitarist Jad Tariq joined Dylan's band partway through the current tour.

Bob Dylan and Pope Leo have met in Rome, and the exchange of gifts has become one of the more unusual music stories of the week. The Age's Warwick McFadyen reports that the songwriter and the Pope swapped a rosary and a book at the Apostolic Palace, a meeting that made news around the world.

## A rosary for a memoir

The two men are both Americans from the Midwest. The Age notes that the Pope is from Chicago and Dylan from Duluth. At the meeting, the Pope handed Dylan a rosary. Dylan gave him a copy of Chronicles: Volume One, his book Chronicles: Volume One.

The Age's column describes the encounter as one between two "shepherds" with very different flocks. Pope Leo leads a Catholic Church of more than a billion followers. Dylan, the paper says, would never describe himself as tending a flock at all.

## The lyric the Pope chose

The link is not new. According to The Age, Pope Leo quoted Dylan in the preface to Disarmed and Disarming: Peace Is a Gift, a book published by the Vatican Publishing House. He did not pick a famous song such as Blowin' in the Wind. He chose a line from Go Away You Bomb, a 1963 song that was never released: "I hate you cause yer man-made and man-owned, an' man-handled."

The columnist suggests it is easy to see why the song stayed in the archive. It is still an unusual choice for a Pope, and it shows a close reading of the catalogue.

## Dylan's long conversation with faith

The Age traces Dylan's spiritual thread from hints in his 1960s lyrics to his late-1970s conversion to Christianity. That period produced the albums Slow Train Coming, Saved and Shot of Love. The column says the open display of faith has faded since, though the thread remains.

There is a precedent for a papal meeting. In 1997 Dylan performed at the National Eucharistic Congress in Bologna and shook hands with Pope John Paul II. That pope quoted Blowin' in the Wind to the crowd and answered its famous question about how many roads a man must walk down with "only one", the road of Jesus Christ, The Age recounts.

## Why Jerry Garcia came round

Away from Rome, Far Out Magazine has looked back at a different kind of conversion. [Jerry Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Garcia) did not always rate Dylan. In a 1987 interview he said he had been "too much of a folkie" to appreciate him, disliked the sound of his voice and had little interest in his topical songs.

That changed with Bringing It All Back Home in 1965. Garcia called It's All Over Now, Baby Blue "one of the prettiest things I'd ever heard" and wanted to play it as soon as he heard it. He said Dylan's songs began speaking to "what the freak on the street was experiencing".

The respect became mutual. Far Out notes the Grateful Dead covered Dylan's songs until Garcia's death in 1995, and Dylan said two years later that the band "understood these songs better than I did". The two acts toured together in 1987 and released the album Dylan and the Dead.

## A new guitarist on the road

Dylan's touring band is also changing. Guitar World reports that Jad Tariq, a Memphis R&B player, joined mid-tour in support of 2020's Rough and Rowdy Ways. He got the call while on the road with blues harmonica veteran Charlie Musselwhite.

Tariq says there was no dramatic backstory. He was available for the dates and played them. He describes the stage as fluid, with Dylan switching keys and arrangements, and space for a guitar lick whenever Dylan looks his way. Tariq released his album Precious Company in September.

## What happens next

Neither the Vatican nor Dylan's team has said, in the sources reviewed, whether the meeting will lead to anything further. The Age's account is a column rather than a news report, so details of the visit come from that piece alone. Dylan's tour continues, and more band changes are possible given how many players have come and gone on this leg, according to Guitar World.
