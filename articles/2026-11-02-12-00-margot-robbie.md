**Quick summary**

- Fremantle and RoseBerry Media will recut [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie)'s Neighbours episodes into vertical, phone-first microdramas, according to Variety.
- The first release covers two Donna Freedman storylines, edited down to about 150 minutes in total.
- Fremantle says it will use only previously broadcast footage and will not generate new scenes or alter faces or voices.
- Former cast and crew, and the actors' union, have raised concerns about consent and compensation.
- Robbie has not commented publicly, according to the reports.

Margot Robbie's early career on Australian television is about to get a second life on phone screens. [Fremantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremantle_(company)) and the production company RoseBerry Media have announced a plan to turn her Neighbours episodes into short vertical series, a move that has already drawn criticism from people who worked on the soap.

## What is being made

Robbie played teenager Donna Freedman on Neighbours from 2008 to 2011. [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/neighbours-margot-robbie-new-release/) notes that although she was 18 when she joined, her storylines included a wedding, an affair and a same-sex kiss, which was a notable moment for the show at the time.

According to [Variety Australia](https://au.variety.com/2026/tv/news/margot-robbie-neighbours-microdrama-episodes-roseberry-40792/), RoseBerry will recut the episodes for its Epis platform. The launch focuses on two of Donna's storylines, which will be edited into vertical micro-episodes running to about 150 minutes combined. The soap itself premiered in 1985 and ended in 2025 after roughly 9,363 episodes, so this is a small slice of a very large archive.

## How the AI part works

The recut uses RedSnapper, RoseBerry's own production workflow, which uses AI to turn film and television libraries into vertical series. RoseBerry co-founder and chief executive Guy Hameiri told Variety that Neighbours is "an iconic piece of television history" and that this is the first time RedSnapper has been applied to a long-running soap. Jens Richter, Fremantle's commercial and international chief executive, said the deal lets the company explore its archive "in an entirely new way."

Fremantle has tried to set limits. A spokesperson told the BBC, as reported by MovieWeb, that the episodes will use "original, previously broadcast footage, dialogue and performances." The company said it will not generate new scenes or performances, alter actors' faces or likenesses, or use synthetic voices, and that a human editor will oversee every stage.

## Pushback from the Neighbours family

Not everyone is reassured. Former cast member Matt Werkmeister, who appeared alongside Robbie, told the BBC he believes consent is needed for the use of actors' image and voice, as well as for the writers. [Sam Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Clark), who played the character who marries Donna, said he does not mind his work getting a "second life" but dislikes the idea of footage being "manipulated into new storylines." Sound recordist Bruce Findlay compared the process to "cutting up a Picasso and rebuilding it."

Stefan Dennis, who played Paul Robinson, was blunter. [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/neighbours-star-stefan-dennis-slams-unbelievably-invasive-ai-assisted-microdrama-revival), citing the Herald Sun, reports he called the project "unbelievably invasive, morally and legally wrong." He said his agent was looking into it and that he expected other cast members to do the same.

The Media, Entertainment and Arts Alliance has also weighed in. Its acting chief executive, Adam Portelli, called on the federal government to "legislate to protect our creative and media sectors" and said performers should receive consent and compensation when AI is involved.

## Robbie's silence, and her history on AI

Robbie, who owns the production company LuckyChamp Entertainment, has not responded publicly to the plan. MovieWeb points out that she joined the SAG-AFTRA picket line in 2023, when the threat AI posed to industry workers was a central issue in the strike. That context is background, not a statement about this project.

## What happens next

The first batch of micro-episodes is the test case. Hameiri described the deal as "a powerful proof point" for RoseBerry's technology, and the company has similar arrangements with other rights holders, so the reaction to Neighbours may shape what follows. Whether the cast and unions secure consent or compensation terms is still unclear.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Margot Robbie in a new Neighbours series?
No. The microdrama reuses footage from her original 2008 to 2011 run as Donna Freedman. Fremantle says it will not generate new performances.

### Who is making the Neighbours microdrama?
Neighbours producer Fremantle is working with RoseBerry Media, which will release the vertical episodes on its Epis platform, according to Variety.

### Has Margot Robbie commented?
Not publicly, according to MovieWeb and Mediaweek.
