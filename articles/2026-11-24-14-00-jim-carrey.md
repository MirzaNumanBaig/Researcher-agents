**Quick summary**

- Makeup artist Kazuhiro Tsuji left the set of *How the Grinch Stole Christmas* because he was worn out by how [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey) treated the crew, according to his own account.
- Carrey called a week later and Tsuji did not pick up. Director Ron Howard then relayed a promise from the star to change.
- Tsuji's condition for returning was help from the filmmakers with a US green card.
- The 2000 film ranked seventh on Netflix's global English-language film chart for 9 to 15 November, with 3.1 million views.

A Christmas film still pulling millions of viewers has a behind-the-scenes story that is getting fresh attention. Kazuhiro Tsuji, one of the people who built the Grinch's face, walked off the production over Carrey's behaviour and agreed to return only after the filmmakers promised to back his immigration application, as Startefacts [retells](https://startefacts.com/news/grinch-makeup-artist-walked-out-on-jim-carrey-then-made-one-demand) from his 2017 interview with Vulture.

## Why the makeup artist left

[Kazuhiro Tsuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazu_Hiro) stepped away from the 2000 film because he was exhausted by the way Carrey treated the crew, according to the account Startefacts summarises. One example he gave: Carrey stood up, studied his chin in the mirror and insisted it was a different shade of green from the day before. Tsuji said he had used exactly the same colour, but went through the motions of correcting it so work could continue.

The pressure was not one-sided. Carrey had his own complaints about the costume, including fake snow getting into his enormous contact lenses. Even so, Tsuji counted about three days of scheduled work across two weeks on set, and said an actor could vanish and return with the makeup in pieces, leaving someone to rebuild the face.

## The phone call he let ring

Tsuji took the problem to makeup chief [Rick Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Baker) and a producer. The three agreed he would stay away long enough for Carrey to feel the loss. About a week later Carrey phoned. Tsuji let it ring.

Next, [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard) left a message saying Carrey had promised to change. Friends told Tsuji to ask for a raise. He asked for something else: letters from the filmmakers supporting his application for permanent residency in the United States. In his words, the idea came to him as a simple question: why not ask them to help him get a green card?

## What happened after

The filmmakers wrote the letters and Tsuji returned. In his telling, Carrey kept his temper in check for the rest of the shoot. The green card application came through after awards season.

The credit is worth getting right. BAFTA lists Tsuji among the five winners for the film's makeup and hair. The Academy's makeup Oscar went to Baker and [Gail Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gail_Ryan). Tsuji later moved away from film to work as a sculptor, and returned when [Gary Oldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Oldman) wanted his help to play [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) in *Darkest Hour*. That work did earn Tsuji an Oscar of his own.
