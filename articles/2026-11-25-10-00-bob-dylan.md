**Quick summary**

- Far Out Magazine published three Bob Dylan stories on 24 November 2026, each looking at his reach across other musicians.
- Lou Reed's Dylan impression, heard on a demo of "I Found a Reason", is described as strikingly close to the real thing.
- Dylan is reported to have called Jimmy Webb's "Wichita Lineman" the greatest song ever written, a view Linda Ronstadt's own praise of Webb echoes.
- George Harrison took the title "Handle With Care" from a box in Dylan's garage in 1988.

The Bob Dylan influence story is usually told through his own songs. Three new pieces from [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/) tell it from the other side: what [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan) did to the people around him, from a rival's voice to a stray cardboard box. These are retrospective features rather than breaking news, but together they make a neat portrait.

## Lou Reed and the Dylan voice

[Lou Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lou_Reed) was a noted Dylan fan, according to [Far Out's feature on the pair](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/lou-reed-set-out-to-channel-bob-dylan-he-ended-up-sounding-like-he-was-taking-the-piss/). The writer traces it back to early demos made before the Velvet Underground, including a cover of "Don't Think Twice, It's Alright" with altered lyrics. That early impression, Far Out says, was not especially faithful.

By 1970 it had sharpened. On the Velvet Underground's "I Found a Reason", from the album Loaded, Reed shares vocals with [Doug Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Yule) on a doo-wop-flavoured track. The demo is different: Reed sings alone, adds Dylan-style harmonica, and the piece calls his imitation much closer to his hero. The writer reads it as affectionate, though with a hint of a jibe at Dylan's famously odd phrasing.

The article also notes that borrowing ran both ways. It points to John Lennon echoing Dylan on "Norwegian Wood" and Dylan answering with ["Fourth Time Around"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Time_Around). It also mentions the Timothée Chalamet biopic A Complete Unknown, which keeps debate about Dylan impressions alive.

## Linda Ronstadt, Jimmy Webb and a big verdict

Far Out's [second piece](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/in-1974-linda-ronstadt-met-the-greatest-pop-composer-of-her-time/) is mainly about [Linda Ronstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Ronstadt) and her taste for songs written by others. She has long preferred to find the right song rather than write her own. The article quotes her praising Jimmy Webb, whose record she produced, as one of the great pop writers of the second half of the 20th century.

Dylan enters as the heavyweight witness. Far Out says that even he took time to describe Webb's ["Wichita Lineman"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_Lineman) as "the greatest song ever written". The article adds that Glen Campbell's recording remains one of the most beautiful pop versions ever made, with its bass solo holding it together. Far Out does not give a date or venue for Dylan's remark, so treat the context as unconfirmed.

## The box in Dylan's garage

The third story concerns [George Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Harrison) in 1988. Far Out recounts that Harrison, working with producer Jeff Lynne after the Cloud Nine sessions, needed one more B-side. With Tom Petty and Roy Orbison involved and Dylan's home studio on offer, the group still had no lyrics or title.

Harrison is quoted saying he spotted a box in Dylan's garage marked "Handle With Care". That became the song and, per the article, the seed of the Wilburys, who went on to make two albums together. It is a small detail, but a telling one: a title that came from the clutter of Dylan's property rather than from a notebook.

None of the three pieces announces new Dylan material or an event. They are archive-minded features, and their value is context. Expect the usual cycle of anniversary and biopic-driven retrospectives to continue. We will update this page if Dylan or the artists involved add anything on the record.

## Frequently asked questions

### Did Lou Reed really imitate Bob Dylan?
According to Far Out Magazine, yes. A demo of "I Found a Reason" features Reed alone, with Dylan-like harmonica and phrasing, and the writer calls it quite accurate.

### What did Bob Dylan say about "Wichita Lineman"?
Far Out reports that Dylan called it "the greatest song ever written". The article gives no date or interview for the remark.

### Where did the title "Handle With Care" come from?
George Harrison said he saw a box marked "Handle With Care" in the garage of Dylan's house while the group was finishing the song, as quoted by Far Out.
