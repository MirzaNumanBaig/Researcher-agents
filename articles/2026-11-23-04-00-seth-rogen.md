**Quick summary**

- Seth Rogen Dawson's Creek: the actor says he "hated" his single guest appearance on the show in 2003.
- He spoke on the 16 November episode of the Happy Sad Confused podcast, hosted by [Josh Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Horowitz).
- Rogen described resentment between the showrunners and the cast, and a stay in a hotel he believed was haunted.
- The report comes from [Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly), which published the interview highlights.

The [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) [Dawson's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawson's_Creek) story is a blunt one. The actor and comedian, now 44, told a podcast this month that his 2003 guest spot on the show was an experience he disliked, according to [Entertainment Weekly](https://ew.com/seth-rogen-hated-playing-bob-on-dawsons-creek-12261946).

## What Seth Rogen said about Dawson's Creek

Rogen appeared on the 16 November episode of Happy Sad Confused, where Horowitz asked whether landing the part had felt like a big deal at the time. Rogen answered straight away. "No, I hated that," he said, laughing.

He played Bob in a sixth-season episode called "Rock Bottom". Bob is a laid-back stoner who hooks up with Audrey Liddell while she is on a road trip to Los Angeles. It was a short appearance, and by Rogen's account it was not a happy one.

"It was so weird," he said. He described "a lot of resentment between, like, the showrunners and the cast" and called the atmosphere "a very bad dynamic". Entertainment Weekly reports that he was unhappy with the tension behind the scenes. Rogen did not name anyone in particular in the comments quoted by the magazine.

## How a friend talked him into the part

Rogen said [Busy Philipps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busy_Philipps), who played Audrey, was the reason he took the job. The two had already worked together on Freaks and Geeks and on an episode of Undeclared, both projects linked to [Judd Apatow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judd_Apatow).

He remembered her telling him that now she was on the show, he had to do it too. He laughed it off at the time. About six months later he was on set, and he recalls wondering how it had happened.

That detail puts the episode early in his career. A few years after it aired, Rogen landed the lead in Apatow's Knocked Up, when he was 25, as Entertainment Weekly notes. Today he is better known as the creator of The Studio, which the magazine names as his latest high-profile project.

## The haunted hotel and the "nervous breakdown"

The most striking part of the story came near the end. Rogen said he was staying in what he described as a haunted hotel while filming, and that he had a nervous breakdown. He gave no further detail in the portion of the interview that Entertainment Weekly quoted.

Horowitz joked that he was glad he had raised the subject, then moved the conversation on. Because Rogen was speaking lightly and in a comic tone, it is not clear how literally to take the phrase "nervous breakdown". The only account so far is his own, and nobody involved with the show has been quoted in response.

## A show that launched other careers

Dawson's Creek ran for six seasons between 1998 and 2003 on The WB. It followed a group of friends growing up in the fictional town of Capeside, Massachusetts. [Kevin Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Williamson_(screenwriter)), the writer behind Scream, created it. It helped launch the careers of [Katie Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Holmes), [Michelle Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Williams_(actress)) and [Joshua Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Jackson), among others.

Rogen's episode aired in the final season, so his recollection of tension fits a production that was nearing its end. That is a reading of the dates, not something he said.
