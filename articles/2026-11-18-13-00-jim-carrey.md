**Quick summary**

- Director [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard) says a sequel to "How the Grinch Stole Christmas" will only happen if it has a chance to be great, according to Variety.
- Howard says he and [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey) are moving "cautiously", months after the follow-up was announced.
- Carrey would return as the Grinch, Variety reports.
- A headline from Inquisitr on the same remarks says the pair "don't even have a script" yet.

Plans for a second Grinch film are moving slower than the announcement suggested. Ron Howard has said that a sequel starring Jim Carrey will not be made unless it has a real chance of being great, and that he and the actor are proceeding with care, according to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/ron-howard-jim-carrey-grinch-sequel-1236951121/).

## What Howard said about the sequel

Variety reports that the comments come months after a "How the Grinch Stole Christmas" follow-up was first announced. Howard used the interview to set out what it would take for the project to become a real film.

He did not close the door. "We're gonna move cautiously but hopefully toward another movie because we both believe in the premise," Howard said, adding that "with a little luck, we'll get to revisit that world." Inquisitr's headline on the same story puts it more bluntly, saying the two "don't even have a script". Its article could not be read for this report, so that wording is taken from the headline alone.

## Why the original still matters to them

Howard said he and Carrey feel proud of the 2000 film. He described how much luck it takes for a movie to come together and become an evergreen, and said it means a great deal to both of them that it has become a tradition in many households.

That helps explain the caution. Variety notes the original was a box office hit, earning more than US$350 million. A sequel would arrive with high expectations from an audience that returns to the first film each December.

## The cost of the green makeup

Carrey would reprise his role, Variety says, and the makeup is part of the story. The actor has long spoken about the toll the effects work took on him.

Howard told Vulture last year that Carrey "started having panic attacks" in the early days of filming because of how uncomfortable the transformation was. Howard recalled seeing him lying on the floor between setups with a brown paper bag, and said Carrey was ready to give back his US$20 million salary, according to Variety's account of that interview.

## What happens next

No release date, script, cast list or studio timeline appears in the Variety report, so none can be confirmed. For now the project is an intention rather than a scheduled film.
