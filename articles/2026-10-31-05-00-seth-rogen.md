**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) said at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival this week that Season 2 of *The Studio* leans harder into guest stars, according to Gold Derby.
- Season 2 is expected to premiere early next year.
- Rogen called Madonna's storyline "wonderful and meta" and said she is the hardest-working person he has met.
- Separately, Martin Scorsese told Tribeca's "Crafty" series that the 2014 Sony hack derailed his planned *Cleopatra*, and he joked "Tell Seth Rogen", per The Playlist.

[Seth Rogen](https://moduscarry.com/tag/seth-rogen/) is back in the headlines for two very different reasons. The Emmy-winning comedy *The Studio* is heading into a second season built on bigger ideas and bigger names, and Martin Scorsese has pointed a decade-old grievance at the film that put Rogen at the centre of a Hollywood scandal.

## What Rogen said about Season 2 of The Studio

Speaking at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival, Rogen told an audience he was nervous during production of the first season, [Gold Derby reports](https://www.goldderby.com/tv/2026/seth-rogen-interview-the-studio-season-2-madonna-storyline/). His reason was simple. Everything he had learned about comedy said you shoot with several cameras and build the timing in the edit. The show's heavy use of one-shot takes removed that safety net completely.

The gamble paid off. "The first season taught us that our ambition and the specificity of our vision could work," Rogen said. It encouraged the team to try what they found hard or scary, and to approach people they assumed would never say yes. Season 2 is expected to premiere early next year.

## More guest stars, written deeper into the story

Rogen said the new episodes lean even further on guest stars and weave them more tightly into the show's narrative. That brings its own cost. "That makes things more difficult because we're asking more of people we often don't know, who may have much better things to do," he said.

For readers following the show's growing roster of cameos, that is a notable shift. Guests are no longer a punchline that arrives and leaves. They carry plot.

## Madonna's storyline

The most talked-about name is Madonna. Paparazzi photos from September 2025 showed her on set in Venice with Julia Garner and Michael Keaton, according to Gold Derby. She also posted pictures on Instagram of herself in a gondola holding a script for the Apple TV series, captioned "The Italian job."

Rogen said the shoot is demanding. "The show is physically hard to make. We do a lot of takes and run around on our feet; it's not a normal process," he said. Madonna met that standard. "Not surprisingly, Madonna is the hardest-working person I've ever met," he said, adding that she was dedicated to delivering a great performance and played "our game."

He also explained why she was needed. The storyline, he said, is "really wonderful and meta in a lot of ways," and the team had a vision that required her. Without her, "a big chunk of what we were developing wouldn't work." Rogen admitted it was scary to attach big ideas to specific people, but said that is what the show hopes to do. Plot details were not given.

## Scorsese's Cleopatra complaint

The second story has a Rogen connection through *The Interview*, the North Korea comedy he made. [The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/martin-scorsese-angelina-jolie-cleopatra-sony-hack-20261030/) reports that Scorsese spoke with his daughter Francesca Scorsese for Tribeca's "Crafty" interview series about a *Cleopatra* project at Sony that Angelina Jolie was attached to.

Scorsese said the film was far closer to happening than most people knew. "We were going to do it," he said, and he told his daughter that screenwriter Eric Roth had been ready. The Playlist notes that James Cameron, David Fincher, Paul Greengrass and Ang Lee were among the directors linked to the project over the years.

He blames the 2014 Sony email hack, which followed the controversy around *The Interview*, for the project's collapse. His line, as quoted by the outlet, was "Tell Seth Rogen; They Ruined Everything." The Playlist describes him as still angry more than a decade later. Rogen has not been reported as responding. Readers should treat the remark as Scorsese's own account of events.
