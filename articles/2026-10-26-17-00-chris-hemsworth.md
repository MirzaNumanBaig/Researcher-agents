**Quick summary**

- [Chris Hemsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Hemsworth) has described the 2015 film In the Heart of the Sea as a "brutal" shoot, according to Far Out Magazine.
- He told ABC7 the cast were exhausted, wet, hungry and "it was not fun".
- He lost a large amount of weight to play 1820 whaleman Owen Chase.
- The film was a box office disappointment, and he was back to his Thor build by 2017's Thor: Ragnarok.

Chris Hemsworth has a long list of physically demanding roles, but he singles out one as close to unbearable. In recalling Ron Howard's 2015 drama In the Heart of the Sea, the Australian actor has said the shoot left the cast drained and short-tempered, as [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/brutal-2015-chris-hemsworth-movie-that-was-brutal-to-make/) reported this week.

## What Hemsworth said about the shoot

In an interview with ABC7, Hemsworth listed the conditions plainly. "We were exhausted. We were uncomfortable. We were wet. We were tired. We were hungry," he said, adding that it was "brutal" and "not fun". He also said the mood swings and sensitivity to trivial things were probably harder on the people around the cast than on the cast themselves.

The film tells the story of the whaling expedition that inspired Herman Melville's Moby-Dick. It is set in 1820, and Hemsworth plays Owen Chase, an experienced whaleman fighting to survive. [Cillian Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_Murphy) and a young [Tom Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Holland) were in the supporting cast.

## Why it was so tough to make

According to Far Out, [Ron Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Howard) could not put a real ship on cold, rough water with a real whale, so the film leaned on CGI. That was familiar territory for Hemsworth after years of acting against green screens. Parts of the ship were still built in a studio water tank, though, and the actors had hundreds of gallons of cold water thrown over them day after day.

Howard backed up Hemsworth's account of the commitment. The cast, he told the interviewer, all wanted to rise to the occasion because they were playing real people, and he said he would never forget the level of commitment they gave.

## The weight loss

The role changed Hemsworth's body as well. A man in 1820 was not meant to look like a superhero, so he lost a lot of weight and went from granite-like muscle to a much smaller, lean frame, Far Out reports. It was a striking contrast with the Thor build audiences knew.

By 2015 he had played the Norse god four times in four years, twice in his own films and twice in The Avengers. He had also taken on other parts to avoid being typecast, including the Huntsman in Snow White and the Huntsman and a Formula One driver in Howard's earlier film Rush.

## A box office miss, and a return to Thor

Far Out describes In the Heart of the Sea as a box office disappointment. Even so, it showed what Hemsworth could do without the muscle and the weight of a franchise behind him. By 2017 he was back in his Thor physique for Thor: Ragnarok, and the outlet says he swore he would never go through such a drastic transformation again.
