**Quick summary**

- [Quentin Tarantino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quentin_Tarantino) says [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie) played a second, little-noticed part in *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood*.
- Besides Sharon Tate, she reprised her flight attendant role from the TV series *Pan Am*, with a few lines opposite a character named Rick.
- Tarantino revealed this at an AFI Fest screening in Los Angeles on Friday, according to Yahoo's report of the event.
- He also showed the first 17 minutes of *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*, due in cinemas on 25 November.

Margot Robbie had a second job on the set of *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* that most viewers never spotted. Quentin Tarantino told an AFI Fest crowd in Los Angeles on Friday that the Australian actor also played a flight attendant, the same type of character she played on the short-lived TV series *Pan Am*, [according to Yahoo's report](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/quentin-tarantino-screens-opening-scene-081500072.html) from the night.

## How the Pan Am cameo came about

Robbie's main role in the 2019 film was [Sharon Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Tate). Tarantino said the extra part came from a simple observation: a Pan Am flight belongs to the same era as the film's story. So he asked Robbie why she shouldn't play the character she had played on television.

"And she was like, 'Yeah, OK, cool,'" Tarantino recalled. He added that Robbie rewatched an episode of the series to find the character again.

The part is small. Tarantino said she has a few lines with Rick, and that viewers can see her make an entire Bloody Mary in one go. Much of her dialogue is hard to hear in the final cut, he said, and if he played the full takes, the whole character would come through.

For fans of Robbie, it is a reminder of how far her career has travelled since *Pan Am*. ## A first long look at Cliff Booth

The main event was the footage. After a screening of *Once Upon a Time in Hollywood* at the Chinese Theatre and a Q&A, Tarantino played the opening 17 minutes of *The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth*. It is a sequel written by Tarantino and directed by [David Fincher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fincher). Yahoo, which attended the event, avoided spoilers but described the set-up.

The scene is set in 1977, eight years after the first film. [Brad Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Pitt) returns as Booth, no longer a stuntman. He works as a projectionist at the New Beverly Cinema, the real Los Angeles theatre owned by Tarantino, which is showing a fictional Blaxploitation film called *Soul Brothers Die Hard*. Booth also has a side career as a Hollywood fixer.

Until now, the public had seen only a one-shot Super Bowl spot and a trailer released last month. The film is produced by Netflix and opens in cinemas on 25 November, before it reaches the streamer on 23 December. Fincher attended the screening but did not speak to the crowd or reporters.

## Why Tarantino went back to Booth

Tarantino said he loved the character and the way Pitt played him, describing him as something like a paperback hero. He imagined a series of paperback adventures and, as time passed, started thinking about a misadventure of his own for Booth.

The reveal arrives a week after a Page Six report, cited by Yahoo, claiming Fincher clashed with recently fired Netflix movie boss [Dan Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Lin) over budget cuts and creative control. That report is unconfirmed in the material we reviewed, and neither Fincher nor Netflix is quoted on it.

## Casting stories from the original film

Tarantino also looked back at the 2019 casting. He said he and casting director Victoria Thomas saw "pretty much every young person in town" for the Manson Family roles. They hired relative unknowns including [Margaret Qualley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Qualley), [Austin Butler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_Butler), Sydney Sweeney, [Mikey Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikey_Madison) and [Maya Hawke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Hawke).

The casting that meant most to him, he said, is not in the finished film. [Burt Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burt_Reynolds) had agreed to play [George Spahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Spahn) but died before shooting began. Tarantino said Reynolds rehearsed and took part in a script reading, which was his last performance. [Bruce Dern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Dern) played Spahn in the film.
