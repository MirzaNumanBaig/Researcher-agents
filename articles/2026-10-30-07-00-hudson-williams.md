**Quick summary**

- [Hudson Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Williams) wore a cardigan by Indigenous label Section 35 to an event in New York on Sept. 30, Orange Shirt Day.
- Designer Justin Jacob Louis says the piece sold out in just under two hours.
- Louis told Alberta Native News that the label opened a pre-sale after fans asked for a restock.
- A fall-winter collection is expected in late November or early December, according to Louis.

Hudson Williams, the star of [Heated Rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heated_Rivalry), has sent a small Indigenous fashion label into overdrive. After he wore a Section 35 cardigan to an event in New York on Orange Shirt Day, designer Justin Jacob Louis says every piece in stock disappeared within about two hours, according to [Alberta Native News](https://www.albertanativenews.com/section-35-cardigan-go-viral-after-hudson-williams-wears-it-on-orange-shirt-day/).

## How the cardigan reached Williams

Louis, originally from Maskwacis, Alberta, and now based in Vancouver, said Williams's team found him and asked to buy the cardigan for the New York event. He happened to have one in his studio. The team collected it and passed it on to one of the actor's agents.

Louis said Williams chose Orange Shirt Day on purpose, because he wanted to represent an Indigenous designer. "Luckily happened to be me, I guess," Louis said.

## Sold out in under two hours

Louis was with family on the day and not focused on marketing. When he came back to his phone, he said, it was full of sales alerts and social media was "lighting up". He said the cardigan sold out in just under two hours, every piece the label had.

Fans then kept asking for more, so the business opened a pre-sale. Louis described the fan pages as "insane" and said people reposted the look widely. He also recalled coverage from Vogue, and said the whole episode "basically went viral and just took off for a week". That Vogue coverage comes from Louis's account, and we have not read it ourselves.

## Where Section 35 started

The label has a modest origin. Louis said he began by designing T-shirts that recreated old hockey logos from back home, including the Hawks and the Oilers. At the time, he said, few brands spoke for communities like his own and for life on the rez. That gap led to Section 35.

He says his culture guides his work, from language and politics to humour and the land he grew up on. "Everything has a story or some type of inspiration from where I come from," he said.

Building a label independently has been hard, Louis added. He called the fashion industry difficult to enter and expensive to run, and credited investors who backed his vision. He believes Indigenous storytelling helps designers stand out in a crowded market, and he wants them to share space with the biggest brands. "Our work deserves to be seen," he said.

## What happens next

Louis said a fall-winter collection is planned for late November or early December. He is also working on an archive collection of one-off pieces and designs saved from ten years of Section 35. A Montreal fashion show is planned for November, with dates still to be announced, and some collaborations are in development. None of these plans has been confirmed beyond Louis's comments.
