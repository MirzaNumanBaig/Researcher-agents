**Quick summary**

- *The Wolf of Wall Street*, starring [Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie), joins Pluto TV's free, ad-supported lineup on 1 November, according to MovieWeb.
- The film runs 179 minutes and is rated R.
- Pluto TV has not said how long it will stay; MovieWeb notes the service usually works on one-month contracts.
- The film grossed more than $406 million worldwide and holds a 79% Rotten Tomatoes score.

[Margot Robbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margot_Robbie)'s breakout film, *The Wolf of Wall Street*, arrives on Pluto TV on 1 November, giving viewers a free, ad-supported way to watch the three-hour comedy. The news comes from [MovieWeb](https://movieweb.com/wolf-of-wall-street-stream-free-pluto-november-2026/), which says the platform has added the title to its November movie lineup.

## What Pluto TV has confirmed

MovieWeb reports that Pluto TV acquired the film as part of its November slate, with a start date of 1 November. No end date has been announced. The outlet points out that Pluto TV tends to licence titles for about a month, so it expects the film to leave around the end of November. That is an expectation, not an announcement, so viewers who want to catch it should not leave it too late.

The report also does not say which countries get the film on the service. Australian readers should check the Pluto TV app for their region before planning a night around it.

## A role that changed Robbie's career

The film is a useful reminder of how quickly Robbie's career moved. MovieWeb notes she came from the Australian soap *Neighbours* before [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) picked her for the film. Today she is better known for Harley Quinn and *Barbie*, which makes it easy to forget this was her step up to Hollywood stardom.

She plays Naomi Lapaglia, the second wife of stockbroker Jordan Belfort, who is played by Leonardo DiCaprio. The story is a biopic based on Belfort's best-selling memoir, following a former Wall Street broker who built his own firm by questionable means and then spent the money on an extravagant lifestyle.

## The cast, the money and the reception

The supporting cast is large. MovieWeb lists [Jonah Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Hill), [Matthew McConaughey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_McConaughey), [Cristin Milioti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristin_Milioti), [Kyle Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Chandler), [Jon Favreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Favreau), [Jon Bernthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Bernthal), [Aya Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aya_Cash) and the late [Rob Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Reiner).

The numbers back up its standing. According to MovieWeb, the film earned five Oscar nominations and holds a 79% critics' score on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), with an 83% audience rating. It took more than $406 million at the worldwide box office.

MovieWeb also argues the film is often misread 13 years after release. In its view it is a cautionary tale about extreme wealth and power, and Belfort was never meant to be admired. That is the outlet's opinion, but it explains why the film keeps getting rediscovered.
