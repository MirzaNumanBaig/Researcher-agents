**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) spoke to The Irish Times at the London Film Festival about her rapid rise.
- She says Norway has its own "tallest poppy syndrome", which can make it hard to feel proud of success.
- She stars in *Fjord*, Cristian Mungiu's film that won the 2026 Cannes Palme d'Or.
- She says she is hearing criticism of the film from both liberal and conservative sides, and that Mungiu wanted to spark debate.

Renate Reinsve says the hardest part of sudden success is giving herself permission to enjoy it. In a profile published on 9 November, the Norwegian actor told [The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/film/2026/11/09/renate-reinsve-we-too-have-tallest-poppy-syndrome-ireland-has-that-right/) that her home country shares the habit Australians know well, and she spoke about *Fjord*, the Palme d'Or winner now drawing heated reactions.

## Why Reinsve says Norway has tall poppies too

The interviewer asked whether theatre life in Norway resembles that in Ireland, given the two countries have similar populations. Reinsve answered that Norway also has "tallest-poppy syndrome". Australian readers will recognise the idea: a culture that nudges high achievers back towards the pack.

She did not describe it as purely negative. According to The Irish Times, she said a strong insistence that everyone is the same has benefits, and that she learned this in the theatre, where it creates a sense of equality on set and a team spirit. The drawback, she said, is that in a culture that urges everyone to be similar, it can be challenging to give yourself the space to be proud when success arrives.

## A rise that did not come overnight

The profile traces a steady climb. Reinsve won best actress at Cannes in 2021 for Joachim Trier's *The Worst Person in the World*, after what the paper calls a busy early career at home. She made her American debut opposite [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) in *A Different Man* in 2024. A year later she returned to Cannes with Trier's *Sentimental Value*, which earned her a first Oscar nomination for playing the stressed actor daughter of Stellan Skarsgård's director.

This year she appeared opposite [Chiwetel Ejiofor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiwetel_Ejiofor) in the horror film *Backrooms*, and reunited with Stan in *Fjord*. The Irish Times notes that these were three Cannes appearances in total, and that she handled them with confidence.

Asked whether she could have coped with this at 20, the 38-year-old said no. By the time fame arrived, she told the paper, she already knew who she was and who her friends were, and she was settled in a life she loved. She believes that earlier success, before her life was set, would have been very hard to navigate.

## What *Fjord* is about, and why it divides

Mungiu, who won his second Palme d'Or with the film, follows a Christian couple who move from Romania, the husband's homeland, to a small town in Norway, the wife's country of origin. After rumours that they have spanked their children, and concerns that the husband has been proselytising, child-welfare authorities place the children in foster care. Stan and Reinsve play the couple.

The Irish Times says the film echoes the case of a Pentecostal couple, also from Romania, who clashed with Norwegian authorities in 2015. It says the film's sympathies appear to lie with the parents, who are portrayed as sober and largely decent, and that online unease is building as a wider release approaches.

Reinsve was careful on the subject. She said her impression was of "heat from both the liberal side and the conservative side". She explained that she had been working on a shoot and had only just come up for air to attend the London Film Festival, so she had not talked it through with anyone and had only heard parts of the reaction. Mungiu, she said, wanted to spark debate, as his films always do.
