**Quick summary**

- [Troye Sivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troye_Sivan)'s fourth album, *She's the Best*, was released on 9 October.
- A review in [New University](https://newuniversity.org/2026/10/23/troye-sivan-yearns-and-parties-on-shes-the-best/), the UC Irvine paper, says it swings between dance-floor hedonism and a longing for something real.
- The title track's video features several women, including [Nicole Kidman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Kidman).
- The reviewer rates the opening run highly, finds the fourth track "redundant", and singles out "Le Boy" as a standout.

Troye Sivan's new album is being read as two records in one. According to a review published on 23 October by New University, *She's the Best*, released on 9 October, moves from the club to the quiet of an empty apartment, and tells a story of joy, friendship, love and loneliness along the way.

## A tribute to women who inspire him

The reviewer says the title track opens the album as a tribute to the women who inspire Sivan. He describes them in a spoken-word style, as people who contain multitudes and move through life with chaos and confidence. The music video features many women in their element, including Kidman.

One line the review highlights calls its subject a "night owl and somehow an early bird" who "stays perpetually tired". The reviewer credits the album with specific, vivid imagery, and that detail is the example given.

## Party, Canada Dry and the dance-floor run

The next track, "Party", keeps the experimental approach. According to the review, Sivan plays a knight in a campy, medieval-inspired video, and the word "party" repeats for most of the song over a beat that is hard to resist.

Sivan gave Rolling Stone a statement about the idea behind it. "Nightlife is often the first place a queer person finds themselves in a room where they're not the minority," he said, adding that it becomes a formative and liberating space for many. The review says the video imagines partying in another era, with fight scenes interrupted by dancing and romance to show that joy and love have persisted through dark times.

"Canada Dry" carries the momentum on, in the reviewer's words a celebration of the heat of the moment and the desire born on a dance floor.

## Where the review pushes back

Not everything lands. By the fourth track, "I Do This For You", the reviewer finds the theme of desire worn out, noting it is the fourth song in a row on the subject. The song also leans on repetition, but its mid-tempo, jazzy production does not give that choice the high energy it needs, and the review calls it out of place.

The album shifts, the reviewer says, on "Cowboy". Here Sivan addresses a specific love interest and, in this reading, drops the confident front he shows on "Canada Dry".

## Le Boy and the search for "the one"

The review names "Le Boy" a standout. It describes the subject as an imagined love, a man somewhere in the unseen future, with no name because he is pure fantasy. Despite what the reviewer calls an objectively great life, the narrator feels something is missing. The verses are calm, the choruses suddenly loud, and the track fades into a dreamy outro with the line "I was born in love with you".

The reviewer says the mood turns melancholy on "Love So Big It Hurts", where guitar and strings frame a portrait of loneliness and routine, such as folding sheets and cooking and cleaning. The source text available to us cuts off partway through this track, so we cannot report the review's verdict on the rest of the album.
