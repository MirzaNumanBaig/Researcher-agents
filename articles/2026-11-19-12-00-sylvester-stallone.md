**Quick summary**

- *I Play Rocky*, the biopic about the making of the original *Rocky*, is set to expand to about 2,000 US theatres this weekend, according to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/sylvester-stallone-i-play-rocky-box-office-theater-count-expansion/), citing Deadline.
- It opened on November 6 in seven theatres and took US$337,465 over three days.
- A three-day gross of about US$12 million is projected for the wide weekend.
- Audience reviews on Rotten Tomatoes sit at 98%, against 88% from critics.

The film that tells how [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) wrote his way into Hollywood is about to find out how many people want to see it. *I Play Rocky* goes from a handful of screens to roughly 2,000 across the United States this weekend, the one before American Thanksgiving, and early projections point to a US$12 million opening.

## A small start, then a very large step

The biopic was directed by [Peter Farrelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrelly) and stars [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito) as the young Stallone. The cast also includes AnnaSophia Robb, [Matt Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Dillon), [Jay Duplass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Duplass), P. J. Byrne and [Tracy Letts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Letts).

According to ScreenRant, it opened on November 6 in only seven theatres. Its US$337,465 weekend works out at a per-screen average of US$48,209. That ranks 196th among domestic debuts of all time and 12th for 2026 so far.

The release then stayed small. Only 22 theatres were added in its second weekend. Per Deadline, the third weekend adds 1,971 screens in one move, which is how a seven-screen release ends up near 2,000.

## What the numbers say

The film had earned US$1.8 million worldwide at the time ScreenRant wrote. A US$12 million wide weekend would lift that to about US$15 million, before any international ticket sales are counted.

Its cost helps. ScreenRant reports, citing Variety, that the production spent US$27 million shooting in New Jersey and received US$10 million in incentives. Page Six puts the overall budget in the low US$30 millions, and says Amazon MGM Studios agreed to pay US$10 million for US distribution. ScreenRant's own estimate is that, because cinemas keep about half of ticket sales, the film probably needs between US$20 million and US$25 million domestically, depending on marketing spend. That is an estimate, not a studio figure.

## Reviews and awards talk

On Rotten Tomatoes the film holds a Certified Fresh 88% from critics and 98% from verified audience members, [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/sylvester-stallone-i-play-rocky-box-office-theater-count-expansion/) says. If that enthusiasm holds as more reviews arrive, it would earn the site's Verified Hot label.

The same article says Gold Derby currently ranks the film as the 12th most likely Best Picture nominee, and Ippolito as the seventh most likely Best Actor nominee. Those are predictions from one forecasting site, not nominations. Still, a run of awards attention could stretch the film's time in cinemas into early 2027.

## The competition

The wide weekend is not an empty field. *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, the sixth film in that franchise, opens at the same time. Thanksgiving week then brings the A24 drama *Club Kid* from Jordan Firstman, which had the fifth-best per-screen average of the year and goes wide on Thanksgiving Day. The holiday weekend usually fills cinemas across the board, though, and that gives a word-of-mouth title room to grow.

## What happens next

The first test is the wide weekend itself, when the US$12 million projection will be checked against real ticket sales. Then Thanksgiving week and the new releases will show whether the film holds its screens. Whether awards voters take it up is the longer question, and it will not be answered until nominations are announced.
