## What Wicker is about

According to [Mashable](https://mashable.com/entertainment/wicker-review), the film adapts Ursula Wills-Jones' short story "The Wicker Husband". The set-up is simple. A fisherwoman, played by [Olivia Colman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Colman), asks a local basket weaver to make her a husband. She does it to get the townspeople off her back.

The basket weaver is [Peter Dinklage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Dinklage), whom the reviewer calls "positively Shakespearean". The wicker husband is Skarsgård. Mashable compares the idea to a Frankenstein story for craft lovers, and notes that writers have long been drawn to building a partner from things around the house, from [Mary Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley) onwards.

## The village that wants her married

The story is set in a medieval village where patriarchy is treated as divine. Marriage there literally means putting a collar on the bride. The fisherwoman is happy living alone in her crumbling cottage, and she smells of her trade. She has no wish to marry, and her independence annoys the neighbours.

Chief among them is the Tailor's Wife, played by [Elizabeth Debicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Debicki). Mashable describes her as "diabolical" and says she is oppressed in her own marriage to the Tailor. That role goes to [Nabhaan Rizwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabhaan_Rizwan), whom the review calls "superb". Once the husband is made, the review says, the whole town can hear the couple's happiness echo across the meadows.

## How the reviewer rates the cast

Mashable is warm about nearly everyone. Colman is "impeccable" as the fisherwoman, and Skarsgård is "stoically sweet" as the husband. The reviewer says directors Alex Huston Fischer and Eleanor Wilson deliver a "hilarious and heartfelt" script and a cast that is "incredibly talented". The magical production design, in the review's words, drew real gasps and cackles from the audience at the BFI London Film Festival.
