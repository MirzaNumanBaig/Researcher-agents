**Quick summary**

- A documentary, *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story*, retells how the comedian's half-brother and business manager stole about $12 million from him.
- Part of the cash was reportedly hidden in frozen pasta sauce bags, according to the film's coverage.
- The film premiered in Los Angeles on 18 September 2026 and reached digital platforms on 29 September.
- It began streaming on Netflix in the US on 6 November 2026, according to What's on Netflix.
- At least $1 million is still unaccounted for, according to WLOS.

[Dane Cook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dane_Cook) has turned the worst financial betrayal of his career into a feature documentary, and the detail people keep repeating is the pasta sauce. *Funny Money: The Dane Cook Story* tells how his half-brother Darryl McCauley, who managed his money, took around $12 million and stashed some of it in frozen sauce bags. The film reached Netflix in the US on 6 November 2026, per [What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/funny-money-dane-cook-netflix-release-date/).

## What the Dane Cook Funny Money film covers

The documentary was directed and written by Brett Harvey, who also made *Breaking Olympia: The Phil Heath Story*, according to What's on Netflix. Super Channel first commissioned it under the title *Brace for Impact: The Dane Cook Story*, with Cook as an executive producer.

It premiered at the Aero Theatre in Los Angeles on 18 September 2026, followed by a Q&A with Cook, then moved to digital storefronts on 29 September. The Netflix run is a licensed release distributed by UpStreamFlix, not a Netflix Original, the outlet reports.

Cook, 54, described the film to [WLOS](https://wlos.com/news/entertainment/dane-cook-can-laugh-at-the-humiliating-frustrating-and-disgusting-theft-by-his-brother) as a "love letter" to stand-up comedy and said it felt like a victory lap.

## How the theft worked

McCauley became Cook's business manager after being seriously injured while working as a corrections officer. WLOS reports that Cook offered him the job partly so he could leave a dangerous line of work.

What's on Netflix says McCauley withdrew sums of $20,000 to $100,000 at a time without Cook noticing. The scheme came apart in 2008, after Cook hired a new business manager and started asking questions about his finances. That is a useful comparison: the amounts were small enough individually to pass unseen, yet they added up to roughly $12 million.

## The pasta sauce and the missing money

WLOS reports that McCauley and his second wife, Erika, hid cash in potted plants, in boxes of bagel bites and in shrink-wrapped bundles inside frozen pasta sauce bags. [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2026/tv/news/dane-cook-brother-stole-12-million-cash-pasta-sauce-39548/) ran the pasta sauce detail in its headline. The exact amounts hidden this way come from the documentary's account, and I could not confirm them independently.

According to WLOS, at least $1 million remains unaccounted for. Cook joked that he would split it 50-50 with anyone who finds it.

## The sentence and Cook's reaction

WLOS says McCauley pleaded guilty in 2010 to larceny, embezzlement and forgery, and received five to six years in prison, plus an order to repay the $12 million. Erika was sentenced to two and a half years. What's on Netflix says the pair were ordered to repay the money and that Cook believes the true total may be higher. It adds that McCauley later served his sentence and then seemingly vanished.

Cook called the experience "humiliating, frustrating, and disgusting" and said he can now laugh at parts of it while still feeling disgust, particularly because he had worked to build their relationship. He told WLOS he has recovered financially.

## What happens next

Cook hinted to WLOS at a possible sequel, saying the story's "tentacles" continue. No second film has been announced. Whether the missing money or McCauley himself turns up is unknown.
