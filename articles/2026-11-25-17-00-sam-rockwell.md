**Quick summary**

- Casting director Sarah Finn spoke to The HoloFiles about building the cast of *Wild Horse Nine*, published on 24 November 2026.
- [Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell) plays CIA operative Lee opposite John Malkovich's Chris.
- Finn says stars of that standing were approached through conversations and scene reels, not standard auditions.
- The film is set on Easter Island in 1973 and, according to The HoloFiles, is now in cinemas via Searchlight Pictures.

[Sam Rockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Rockwell) did not audition for [*Wild Horse Nine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Horse_Nine) in the usual sense. According to [The HoloFiles](https://theholofiles.com/2026/11/24/wild-horse-nine-sarah-finn-interview/), casting director [Sarah Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Halley_Finn) said the process for the film's leads was built around meetings and discussion with director [Martin McDonagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_McDonagh), a different route from the one used for newer faces.

## What Wild Horse Nine is about

The HoloFiles describes the film as a story of two CIA operatives sent to a remote Chilean island, Easter Island, in 1973, shortly before the Chilean coup. Rockwell plays Lee and John Malkovich plays Chris. The outlet frames it as typical McDonagh territory: dark comedy, sharp dialogue and characters whose tensions drive the plot.

Finn told the interviewer that the film puts unusual weight on its performances. She contrasted it with *[Seven Psychopaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Psychopaths)*, which had a much bigger cast. In *Wild Horse Nine*, she said, every part has to be "an absolute gem", because the actors carry the story.

## Why the stars did not audition

Finn, who has worked with McDonagh since *Seven Psychopaths*, said the process for performers of Rockwell's and Malkovich's experience was different. As summarised by The HoloFiles, the team skipped formal auditions. They used readings, meetings and conversation instead. Finn also put together collections of scenes from across each actor's career to show what a role might draw on, and McDonagh then sat down with them to talk it through.

She also spoke about Rockwell's screen presence, pointing to his ability to say a great deal through silence and expression. That is a useful trait in a two-hander about men stuck together in an isolated place. Finn added that casting even one pivotal part, such as Malkovich's Chris, takes as much time and thought as assembling a large ensemble.

## A shared theatre background

Both Finn and McDonagh come from the theatre, and Finn said they run auditions like rehearsals, open to what an actor might discover in a character. That openness extended beyond the usual submissions. By Finn's account, it led them to an actress from a Chicago theatre company who was not signed to mainstream representation.

The role of Monica was the biggest search. Finn said she felt early on that the part should go to a Chilean actress and that McDonagh agreed. The team held an early table read and began casting in earnest the summer before production, hoping to introduce a performer new to audiences. The source text available to us does not name who won the role, so we will not guess.

## A nod to casting directors

Finn also said the Academy's decision to give casting its own award category was long overdue. She cited Robert Downey Jr.'s casting as Iron Man in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) as the kind of choice that looks obvious only in hindsight, even though it took careful work. Her own résumé includes much of that franchise, as The HoloFiles notes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who does Sam Rockwell play in Wild Horse Nine?
He plays Lee, one of two CIA operatives sent to Easter Island in 1973. John Malkovich plays the other, Chris.

### Who directed Wild Horse Nine?
Martin McDonagh directed the film. Sarah Finn has cast his projects since Seven Psychopaths.

### Did Sam Rockwell audition for the role?
Not in a traditional way, according to Finn's interview. For actors of his standing, the team used conversations and scene collections rather than formal auditions.
