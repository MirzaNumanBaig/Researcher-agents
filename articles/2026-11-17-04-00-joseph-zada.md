**Quick summary**

- Australian actor [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) used a vulgar London slang word during a "British test" video for the outlet Joe, according to The Tab.
- His co-stars [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace) and [Whitney Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Peak_(actress)) reacted with visible shock.
- Zada said he picked up the phrase from watching "heaps" of [Top Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Boy) while growing up in Australia.
- Fans online are split between calling the press tour unserious and defending Zada as refreshingly unscripted.

The Hunger Games press tour has produced its first viral awkward moment. In a video for the outlet Joe, Joseph Zada let slip a piece of London slang that the interviewer asked him to repeat, and his co-stars made their discomfort plain, [The Tab reported on 16 November](https://thetab.com/filmshrine/2026/11/16/hunger-games-joseph-zada-nsfw-comment-leaves-co-stars-stunned-awkward-interview/).

## What happened in the interview

Zada plays the young Haymitch Abernathy in *Sunrise on the Reaping*, the prequel in the [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) series. The role was played in the original films by [Woody Harrelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woody_Harrelson). Grace appears as Maysilee Donner and Peak as Lenore Dove Baird.

The three took a "British test" for Joe, in which they were asked which UK words and phrases they had found odd. According to The Tab, Peak went first with "fanny", a word that means something quite different in Britain than elsewhere. Zada, who is originally from Australia, said many mannerisms are similar in the UK, and Grace began a story about the six months she spent living in London.

Mid-story, Zada said a slang term of the kind often heard in Multicultural London English. The Tab describes it as a vulgar word, usually used to call someone weak or cowardly. The interviewer stopped Grace and asked him to say it again, then asked when and where he would ever use it.

## How the cast reacted

Grace was blunt. "I don't want to be here for this," she said, adding that she did not want to be in the same clip. She joked that she fears for her life whenever she shares an interview with Zada, and that she could watch "15 years of my media training" spiral away.

Zada explained that he had watched "heaps of Top Boy" growing up. "I thought I was a roadman but I was in Bondi Beach," he said, according to The Tab.

Much of the online attention landed on Peak, whose expression during the segment viewers described as exasperated. One viewer on X wrote that she "looks so done", while a TikTok comment said the pair were "so annoyed with him". The Tab also points to a second clip, shared on X on 14 November, in which fans again focused on Peak's reactions while Zada told a story.

## Fans are divided

Reaction is far from one-sided. One commenter quoted by The Tab said Zada had better be "Oscar worthy" as Haymitch because they could not take more videos from an "unserious" press tour. Others see the moments as harmless. One fan hoped he is never media trained because he is "so unintentionally funny", and another asked whether people were really trying to cancel him over the word.

It is worth keeping the scale in view. This is a light moment from a promotional interview, not a scandal, and the reports so far come from a single outlet describing a video and social media posts. A second video, a quiz featuring the same three actors on YouTube, was also published on 16 November, though its content could not be checked for this report.
