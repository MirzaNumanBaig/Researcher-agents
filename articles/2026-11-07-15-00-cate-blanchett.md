**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) and her husband [Andrew Upton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Upton) have had landscaping, lighting and shutter details approved for their Cornwall home.
- The decision was issued on 14 July 2026 under planning application PA26/03634.
- Revised plans sent in May 2026 switched to bat-friendly, warm-white lighting after an ecological assessment raised concerns.
- The approval covers three conditions on a 2022 permission, not a new permission to build.

The Oscar-winning Australian actor has secured sign-off from [Cornwall Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall_Council) for the garden around her coastal eco-home in Mawgan Porth, according to a report in [The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/world/rest-of-world/cate-blanchett-wins-approval-for-a-luxury-garden-at-her-cornwall-eco-home-after-neighbours-raised-concerns-locals-had-earlier-accused-the-actor-of-encroaching-on-environmentally-important-land/articleshow/135575817.cms). The approved details include a grass driveway, hardwood decking and Cornish granite steps. It follows months of scrutiny of the project, which has drawn complaints from some neighbours. Readers can find more coverage of the actor on our [Cate Blanchett tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/cate-blanchett/).

## What the Cate Blanchett Cornwall garden plans include

The house, known as Bob Tails, sits in Mawgan Porth near Newquay. Blanchett and Upton, an Australian playwright, bought the original cottage for about £1.6 million in 2020, the report says. They later won permission to replace it with a five-bedroom sustainable home.

The newly approved landscaping adds local plants, trees and ornamental grasses, plus Cornish boulders chosen to suit the coastal setting. Alongside the grass driveway, decking beside the house and granite steps, the decision also covers outdoor lighting and shutters.

It is worth being clear about what was decided. According to The Times of India, the 14 July ruling discharges three conditions attached to the original September 2022 permission. It does not grant a new permission to build the house.

## Why bats changed the lighting design

Lighting became the main sticking point. The first landscaping proposals included pole-mounted path lights, spike lights in trees, low-level step lights and wall lights. An ecological assessment found that some of those fixtures did not meet the bat-protection guidance referred to in the property's planning conditions.

A 2021 survey found no active bat roosts in the building, but it did find that bats forage across the wider site. That led to advice to keep artificial light to a minimum: low-level lights pointed downwards, warm-spectrum bulbs and timers.

The couple submitted revised plans in May 2026 using warm-white LED lighting, in line with Bat Conservation Trust guidance. A representative for the couple told the Daily Mail the changes made the lighting more suitable for bats, as relayed by The Times of India. The council accepted the revised arrangements.

## How the project drew neighbours' complaints

The build has not been without friction. In February 2024, The Guardian reported complaints from Karen Burgess, who owned a nearby holiday rental. She said construction noise disrupted guests during 2023 and cost her business more than £60,000 in lost revenue. The couple's architects, Watershedd, disputed this, saying the noise must have come from other developments nearby and that they had kept in touch with neighbours.

A separate application also caused argument. The couple had bought an adjoining plot in 2021 and proposed a new entrance and parking for four cars on part of it. Neighbour Helen John called the plan "a blatant attempt to erode an environmentally important piece of land by stealth and incorporate it". Their planning representatives, Situ8, argued about 86% of the meadow would stay untouched. That application was withdrawn in June 2024, and the letter to the council did not publicly give a reason.

## What happens next

Based on the report, the approval clears the way for the outdoor works to proceed as described. The sources do not say when the landscaping will be built or when the couple expect to move in, so no timetable can be confirmed. Blanchett has not been quoted in the coverage we reviewed.
