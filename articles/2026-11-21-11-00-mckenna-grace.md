**Quick summary**

- The Hunger Games prequel *Sunrise on the Reaping* has a 95% Rotten Tomatoes audience score, according to ScreenRant.
- That beats the franchise's previous best of 89%, shared by *Catching Fire* and *The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes*.
- Critics are less warm, with a 68% rating, the second-lowest in the series.
- [Mckenna Grace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mckenna_Grace) stars alongside [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada) in the film, which is in cinemas now.

Audiences have given the new [Hunger Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games) prequel its best score yet. *Sunrise on the Reaping*, which stars Mckenna Grace, sits at 95% on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes) from viewers, according to [ScreenRant](https://screenrant.com/the-hunger-games-sunrise-on-the-reaping-rotten-tomatoes-audience-score-record/), and the figure is a record for the franchise.

## The audience score in numbers

The 95% rating rests on more than 500 verified ratings, ScreenRant reports. The old franchise high was 89%, a tie between *Catching Fire* and *The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes*. That puts the new film six points clear of both.

The score is also a career best for director [Francis Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Lawrence), who has directed every Hunger Games film except the first. For Grace, it ties her highest audience rating, which belongs to the animated *[Spirit Untamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_Untamed)*.

The film follows Haymitch Abernathy, played by Zada, in the 50th Hunger Games, the Second Quarter Quell, where the number of tributes is doubled. Because the score is still based on a few hundred ratings, ScreenRant notes it may move as more viewers weigh in.

## Why critics and viewers disagree

The critics' rating is 68%, the second-lowest in the franchise, so the gap between reviewers and audiences is the widest in the series, according to ScreenRant. Reviews praised the production design, the visuals and the emotional weight of Haymitch's origin story. Zada, Grace, [Elle Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elle_Fanning) and [Jesse Plemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Plemons) were singled out for their performances.

The complaints were mostly about pacing. At two hours and 38 minutes, it is the longest film in the series, and some reviewers felt it rushed through much of the novel and left little room to connect with the characters. Others said the PG-13 rating softened the violence and lowered the stakes.

Viewers appear more forgiving. ScreenRant points to the final act, which links Haymitch's story back to the original trilogy, as the main reason.

## Box office and the franchise's future

The report says the film has had the franchise's second-best opening weekend, with US$21 million, behind *Catching Fire* at US$25.2 million. ScreenRant adds that projections for a much bigger opening of around US$100 million look solid, which would be a clear step up from *The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes*, said to have made about half that. These figures come from one outlet, so treat them as early estimates.

Whether more films follow is unclear. Lawrence and producer [Nina Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Jacobson) have said they would not make another unless author [Suzanne Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzanne_Collins) writes a new book, and Lionsgate chairman Adam Fogelson repeated that view at the premiere, according to ScreenRant.

## What happens next

Audience scores tend to shift as more people rate a film, so the next few weeks will show whether 95% holds. Weekend box office results will also show whether the strong word of mouth turns into ticket sales.
