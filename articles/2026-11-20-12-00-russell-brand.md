**Quick summary**

- A UK Ministry of Justice inquiry is examining whether a YouTube message from jailed comedian [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) broke prison rules on distress to victims, according to The Guardian.
- Brand was convicted on 5 November of one count of rape and three sexual assaults and is serving 10 years and six months.
- The message, protesting his innocence, was read out by his wife, Laura Gallacher, on his YouTube channel.
- Google says its teams are investigating, and the government says it cannot force the video down.

UK prison authorities are weighing whether Russell Brand broke the rules that govern what inmates can say publicly, after a message from his cell was read out on YouTube. The Guardian reported on 19 November that a continuing Ministry of Justice inquiry is focused on whether the statement risks causing "serious distress" to the victims of his crimes.

## What the inquiry is looking at

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/news/2026/nov/19/prison-authorities-consider-russell-brand-youtube-message-rules-victim-distress), the video shows Brand's wife, Laura Gallacher, reading a message in which he says he is not guilty of the offences that put him in prison. It stayed online for days after ministers asked for its removal.

Official guidance says a prison governor must consider whether broadcast material is likely to cause serious distress to victims or outrage to public sensibilities. The guidance says this is likely when a prisoner's crimes are recent, notorious or horrific. Correspondence likely to be published must also avoid material about the prisoner's own offences, unless it consists of serious representations about conviction or sentence.

The Guardian reports that the inquiry will weigh the "notoriety" of the crimes and the gap between the verdict and the statement. It is also understood to be taking account of a prisoner's right to protest innocence. A strict protocol covers how such statements are issued, including whether permission was sought first.

## What Brand was convicted of

Brand was convicted at Southwark crown court on 5 November of one count of rape and three sexual assaults, The Guardian reports. [Nine.com.au](https://www.nine.com.au/world-news/google-refuses-to-remove-russell-brand-video-20261120-p61fta.html) says he was sentenced to 10 years and six months in jail.

Nine reports that the message was read out on his YouTube channel, titled Sunday Service, on a Sunday UK time. In it, Brand wrote that he is not guilty of the offences and that "something strange is happening". He has maintained his innocence, and Nine notes he is allowed to keep doing so despite the conviction.

## Where Google and the government stand

The Guardian says the government has no power to compel Google, which owns YouTube, to remove the video, but has been in "dialogue" with the company. A Downing Street spokesperson said the prime minister's position is that Brand's victims "deserve peace" and that people have heard enough from him.

The prime minister, Andy Burnham, said he "wouldn't leap" to changing the law to force platforms to act, but called on social media companies to take such interviews down. Asked whether the channel itself should be disabled, No 10 said removal depends on YouTube and its terms and conditions.

A Google spokesperson said the company is aware of the new content and its teams are investigating. It also pointed out that it suspended his channel from the YouTube Partner Program in September 2023, so the channel has not been able to earn money since. Nine's headline says Google has refused to take the video down, while The Guardian's account describes an investigation still under way, so the position may still change.

Opposition leader [Kemi Badenoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemi_Badenoch) took a different line on BBC Radio 4's Today programme. She said Brand has been convicted, that his wife is a separate person free to make her own protestations, and that demanding private platforms remove content without checking whether a law has been broken is not where she would start.

## Where Brand is being held

The Guardian reports that Brand is in HMP Littlehey in Cambridgeshire, a category C prison and a specialist prison for sex offenders. Recent reports claimed he might be moved to a category A or B prison after protesting his innocence, but it is understood no such proposal exists and he is likely to stay at Littlehey. Nine says he was recently moved there from the higher-security Wandsworth prison, reportedly over safety fears.

## What happens next

The Ministry of Justice has not announced when the inquiry will conclude. The outcome will turn on the prison rules above and on whether permission was sought before the message went out. Google's own review of the video is the other open question. We will update this story as either decision is made.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is the Ministry of Justice looking at Russell Brand's YouTube message?

It is checking whether the message broke prison rules that aim to prevent serious distress to the victims of crime. The Guardian says factors include how notorious the crimes are and how soon after the verdict the statement came.

### Can the UK government make YouTube remove the video?

No. The Guardian reports the government has no power to compel Google to remove it. Ministers have asked for it to come down and say they are in dialogue with the company.

### Is Russell Brand allowed to say he is innocent?

Yes, to a point. The Guardian says the inquiry is considering prisoners' right to protest their innocence, but there is a strict protocol for how such statements are issued.
