**Quick summary**

- A review published on 25 November by The Diamondback calls [Joseph Zada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Zada)'s performance as young Haymitch "exceptional".
- The same review argues the film leans on nostalgia from the original movies instead of its own story.
- The film opened on 28 October and has taken about US$237.2 million against a US$150 million budget, according to Wikipedia.
- Rotten Tomatoes lists a 68% score, and Metacritic gives it 55/100 from 43 critics.

Joseph Zada's turn as a young Haymitch is the strongest part of *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping*, according to a review published this week by the University of Maryland's student paper, The Diamondback. The same review is far less kind to the film around him.

## What the review says about Zada

Reviewer Justin Mehlbrech calls the performance "exceptional" in [The Diamondback](https://dbknews.com/2026/11/25/sunrise-reaping-movie-review/). The praise centres on how Zada plays Haymitch's anxiety before the games begin. The review singles out the scene where he rides into the arena, saying his shaking body and twitching face show the character's fear without needing dialogue.

It also says the film gives audiences a clear sense of who Haymitch was before he became the mentor seen in the original trilogy, and that Zada builds on that earlier version of the character. For readers who want more on the actor's path to the role, our earlier piece on [Joseph Zada's road to Haymitch](https://moduscarry.com/joseph-zada-haymitch-invisible-boys/) covers his career before the franchise.

## Where the review finds fault

The film is the sixth in the series, directed by Francis Lawrence and based on Suzanne Collins's novel. It is set 24 years before Katniss and Peeta's story and follows Haymitch through the 50th Hunger Games, a Quarter Quell with 48 tributes, double the usual number.

Mehlbrech argues the script skips relationship building. In his view the deaths of Haymitch's friends and family carry little emotional weight because the audience has not spent enough time with them. He also finds the nods to the original films heavy-handed, naming a scene with the Everdeen parents and a baby Katniss, and says they make the plot feel artificial.

## How the film is faring overall

The Diamondback is one voice among many. According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hunger_Games:_Sunrise_on_the_Reaping), the film has a 68% Rotten Tomatoes score and a Metacritic average of 55/100 from 43 critics. The critics' consensus there describes a prequel that retreads familiar territory, but sturdily enough to keep the franchise's odds favourable. The New York Times review, quoted in the same entry, was negative.

Wikipedia puts the worldwide gross at about US$237.2 million on a US$150 million budget. That is roughly 1.6 times the cost, which sits in the "modest" range for a blockbuster of this size.

Zada's co-stars include Whitney Peak, Mckenna Grace and Ben Wang. More coverage is gathered on our [Joseph Zada tag page](https://moduscarry.com/tag/joseph-zada/), including [his reviews as young Haymitch](https://moduscarry.com/joseph-zada-haymitch-review/).

## What happens next

The film is still in its post-release run, so the box office figure will keep moving. For Zada, the steady stream of praise for Haymitch keeps his name attached to one of the franchise's best-loved characters. No new project for him has been confirmed in the sources we read.
