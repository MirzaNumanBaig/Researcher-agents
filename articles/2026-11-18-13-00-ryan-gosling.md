## The Ghost Rider post that took off

[Ryan Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gosling) is officially Ghost Rider, and a single post on X has turned the casting into an argument about his head shape. According to [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/movies/articles/ryan-gosling-officially-ghost-rider-200815803.html), an account called Marvel Mania asked whether the actor's "cranial asymmetry" would be addressed in the 2028 film. It drew more than 39,000 likes in nine days.

Yahoo's writer says it is unclear whether the post was sincere or simply engagement bait. Either way, it spread quickly, and the casting itself is not in doubt: Marvel confirmed it in July.

## Why the debate has legs

The article ties the chatter to the rise of "looksmaxxing", an online trend that obsesses over facial symmetry. It links that talk to manosphere personalities such as Clavicular.

Medical guidance cuts against the panic. Yahoo quotes Penn Medicine, which says most people have some degree of facial asymmetry and that this is completely normal.

Some users agreed. One asked why people were "skullshaming" Gosling, and another said he was being bullied. There is also a practical point that Yahoo raises. Ghost Rider is a flaming demon skeleton, and earlier versions, including Nicolas Cage's 2007 take, leaned on digital effects and stunt doubles. On that evidence, an actor's real skull is unlikely to be on screen for long.

## What we know about the film

Yahoo reports the film will be directed by Shawn Levy and is scheduled for cinemas on 28 July 2028. It also notes that Gosling has openly wanted the part for years. No further plot or cast detail appeared in the pieces we could read, so anything beyond that remains speculation.

## A very different Gosling moment

Elsewhere, [Far Out Magazine](https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/2011-scene-that-made-ryan-gosling-feel-like-a-knucklehead/) looked back at Crazy, Stupid, Love. Gosling played Jacob, a smooth seduction expert who coaches Steve Carell's newly single Cal on clothes, drinks and pick-up lines.

The magazine says the bar scenes were filmed at eight in the morning with no music playing, so the actors could hear one another. In a 2011 interview with Tribute, Gosling said he felt like a "knucklehead" yelling lines at strangers. Far Out argues the editing hides that discomfort, and that Jacob's polished act sets up the film's emotional payoff when Emma Stone's Hannah talks to him as a person rather than a target.
