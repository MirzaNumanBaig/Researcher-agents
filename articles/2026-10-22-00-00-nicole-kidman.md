Nicole Kidman has spoken candidly about her career, women's place in the film industry and the lessons she wishes she had learned sooner during a public conversation with students at the University of Southern California (USC). The Sydney-raised, Oscar-winning actor appeared at Bing Theatre on Monday for an instalment of the School of Dramatic Arts' Spotlight@SDA series, according to a [Daily Trojan report](https://dailytrojan.com/2026/10/21/nicole-kidman-gets-candid-with-usc-students/).

## "I'm a working actor"

The student newspaper reports that the dean of the School of Dramatic Arts, Emily Roxworthy, opened the session by asking Kidman how it felt to be called iconic, pointing to pop singer ADÉLA's 2026 song "Nicole Kidman", which the Daily Trojan says has spent six weeks on the Billboard Hot 100.

Kidman brushed off the label. "I don't feel iconic at all. I feel like I'm a working actor," she said, adding that, at 59, she still loves the work passionately.

## From a Sydney theatre school to producing

Kidman described how her path began in Sydney, where she spent weekends at a small theatre school her father had found. She told the audience she found her people there, including teachers and film students, and said being around emerging directors matters for actors because those peers may later cast them.

She also addressed the setbacks in a career spanning more than four decades. "You don't want to get mired in the failures. Keep moving forward," she said, according to the Daily Trojan. The paper links that determination to her move into producing, noting she produced and starred in the 2010 film *Rabbit Hole*, a performance that earned her an Academy Award nomination for Best Actress.

## Women on screen and behind the camera

Roxworthy noted Kidman has long championed women directors. Kidman said it is important that women's stories are heard, arguing that if they are told only through a male lens, audiences are not truly getting access to women's experiences.

She also criticised the industry's treatment of older women, recalling being told that a woman's career is over by 40. "It's not true, and it needed to be changed," she said. She spoke warmly of support among female colleagues, including Meryl Streep, the report says.

During the audience questions, Kidman referred to heartbreak and what she called her "devastating divorce", saying she felt there were too few places on screen exploring that experience for women at this stage of life. The comments were reported in her own words; the Daily Trojan did not go into further detail.

## Advice for young actors

Kidman urged the acting students to try every medium, from theatre to film and television. "Don't be an artistic snob," she said, explaining that each job tends to lead to the next and that waiting only for the perfect role rarely works out.

Her closing advice was the lesson she wished she had followed when younger: "Don't let anyone break your spirit, and protect your talent vigilantly."

Students quoted by the paper praised her generosity and focus on craft over glamour. Senior acting student Lara Botha said Kidman showed "a real appreciation for the craft itself, not all the glitz and glamour around it."
