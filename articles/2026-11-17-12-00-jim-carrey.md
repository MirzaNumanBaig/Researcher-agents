**Quick summary**

- Director Joel Schumacher first thought Jim Carrey was too young to play the Riddler in Batman Forever, according to a 1994 interview with Comics Scene.
- Schumacher changed his mind after seeing the trailer for The Mask.
- Carrey's Ace Ventura: Pet Detective and Dumb and Dumber both opened in 1994, shortly before Batman Forever arrived in 1995.
- The story was retold this week by SlashFilm.

The Jim Carrey Riddler nearly never happened. [Jim Carrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Carrey) was almost passed over for the part. According to a 1994 interview with Comics Scene, revisited by [SlashFilm](https://www.slashfilm.com/2313491/batman-forever-director-cast-jim-carrey-the-riddler-the-mask/) this week, director [Joel Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Schumacher) believed the actor was too young for the part until a trailer changed his view.

## Why Schumacher had doubts

By 1994, Carrey was one of Hollywood's fastest-rising comedy stars. [Ace Ventura: Pet Detective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ace_Ventura%3A_Pet_Detective) and Dumb and Dumber both reached cinemas that year, according to SlashFilm, and they made him a household name.

Even so, Carrey told Comics Scene that Schumacher "thought I was a little too young." The Riddler is usually played as an established villain, so the concern is easy to understand. The film that followed, [Batman Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_Forever), became the franchise's biggest casting coup in the eyes of SlashFilm, which also notes that Robin Williams had been considered a favourite for the role beforehand.

## The Mask changed the picture

The turning point was a trailer. Carrey said that once Schumacher saw the promo for The Mask, he reversed his decision. In that film Carrey plays a mild-mannered man whose life is upended by a magical mask, and the performance showed he could turn cartoonish chaos into a convincing screen presence.

The Mask began as a comic book by writer John Arcudi and artist [Doug Mahnke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Mahnke). SlashFilm says director [Chuck Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Russell) first saw it as something closer to horror, and that the film toned down the violence and added a lot of swing dancing. Carrey's exaggerated style, which first made his name on [In Living Color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Living_Color), suited that version well.

## A comic Riddler for a campy Batman

Schumacher's take on the [Caped Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman) leaned on the 1960s television series, so a comedian was a logical choice. [Frank Gorshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Gorshin), who played the Riddler on that show, was himself a comedian and impressionist, SlashFilm points out.

The result was a very loud performance. [Tommy Lee Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lee_Jones), who played Two-Face, reportedly told Carrey on meeting him, "I cannot sanction your buffoonery." SlashFilm still finds things to praise, including the way Carrey plays Edward Nygma as an obsessive admirer of Val Kilmer's Bruce Wayne, even dressing as him in one scene.

## What happens next

This is a look back rather than new casting news, and nothing in the sources points to a return to the role. For readers, it is a reminder of how a single trailer can reshape a casting decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why did Joel Schumacher doubt Jim Carrey as the Riddler?

Carrey told Comics Scene in 1994 that the director thought he was a little too young for the part.

### What changed his mind?

Schumacher saw the trailer for The Mask, in which Carrey played another comic-book character, and decided to cast him.

### When did Batman Forever come out?

It was released in 1995, the year after Carrey's Ace Ventura: Pet Detective and Dumb and Dumber.
