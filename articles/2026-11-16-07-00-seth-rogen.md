- She said she felt "so alone" in her 20s when her mother was diagnosed with Alzheimer's at 55.
- She later found a large community of families facing the same illness.
- The couple founded the charity Hilarity for Charity in 2012 and produced "Tangles".

[Lauren Miller Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauren_Miller_Rogen), the filmmaker married to Seth Rogen, has described how isolated she felt as a young woman when her mother was diagnosed with Alzheimer's at just 55. She made the comments to Page Six at a special screening of "Tangles" earlier this week, in a story the outlet published on 15 November.

## "I was sure I was the youngest person"

According to [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/15/celebrity-news/seth-rogens-wife-lauren-miller-recalls-feeling-so-alone-during-moms-heartbreaking-alzheimers-battle/), Miller Rogen said she was in her 20s when the diagnosis came. "I was sure I was the youngest person who had experienced this, and no one could understand," she said.

That belief did not last. She told the outlet she came to see that she was neither alone nor the youngest, and that she belonged, in her words, to "a huge community that did understand". For families facing early-onset illness, that kind of discovery is often the first sign that support exists.

## From personal loss to a charity

In 2012, Miller Rogen and her husband founded Hilarity for Charity, a nonprofit that raises awareness of Alzheimer's disease and supports affected families, Page Six reported. The couple, both 44, met on a blind date in 2004 and married in 2011, so the charity began a year after their wedding.

Miller Rogen said the work has given them a sense of optimism, even though there is no cure for the progressive brain disorder. "We've learned so much about brain health," she told Page Six, adding that the charity offers education on how people can keep their brains healthy. "It's not as dark as it was," she said, while stressing that "there's still a lot more work to be done".

## The film behind the screening

The couple also produced "Tangles", an animated film about a woman whose mother is diagnosed with Alzheimer's. Page Six lists a voice cast that includes [Julia Louis-Dreyfus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Louis-Dreyfus), [Abbi Jacobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbi_Jacobson), [Bryan Cranston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Cranston) and [Beanie Feldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beanie_Feldstein).

Rogen was also at the event. When the reporter suggested his wife was the real star of the family, the "Superbad" actor agreed: "Very much so." He added, "We've been together a long time. I've seen it all."
