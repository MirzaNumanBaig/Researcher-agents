**Quick summary**

- [Cate Blanchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cate_Blanchett) and [Nina Hoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Hoss) starred in *Electra/Persona* at London's National Theatre from August 19 to October 10, according to Interview Magazine.
- A coin toss decided which of the two played which role at each performance.
- Castmate [Ella Lily Hyland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Lily_Hyland) told the magazine she stayed onstage with both leads for the full two-hour running time.
- The play was written by its director, Benedict Andrews, and the interview was recorded in the final week of the run.

When a coin toss decides who plays what, the person standing next to the two stars needs steady nerves. That was Ella Lily Hyland's job in *Electra/Persona*, the Benedict Andrews play that ran at London's National Theatre with Cate Blanchett and Nina Hoss, and she has now described what it was like in an [Interview Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interview_(magazine)) conversation published on October 20.

## A coin toss decides the roles

According to Interview Magazine, Blanchett and Hoss played together almost every night from August 19 to October 10. Hyland says the pair switched roles each evening, so the production could not be rehearsed in the usual way. Scenes were run both ways, then stopped and restarted, and she told the magazine it felt as though the show had not properly been run until previews began.

Even then, things kept moving. Hyland said the performance shifted from day to day during previews, and that she was learning in front of a full house of about 800 people. She called that nerve-wracking, but added that it showed her what her place in the piece was.

## The anchor between two big energies

Hyland, an Irish actor known for the Netflix series *Black Doves*, plays two characters: the young, nervous Chrysothemis in the first half and a doctor named Anna in the second. The interviewer, Harald Smart, described the doctor as the voice that helps the audience settle into what feels like a different play.

Hyland described her job as being an anchor for "two extraordinary energies". She said she is naturally an observer, was a little shy at the start, and chose to stay sturdy rather than make bold choices, given how much the two leads had to absorb. She also said the doctor required restraint, even when she was emotional as Chrysothemis, and that moving energy into the right place was a skill she took from the run.

On the text itself, Hyland said the shifts between characters were clear on the page and that the actor's task was to give in to the words, not impose on them. She said the director steered her away from pushing the doctor too hard.

## A different kind of schooling

Hyland said she expects to understand much of what she learned later, much as a reader keeps thinking about a book's characters after finishing it. She also said holding the energy of the piece with the audience every night was new to her. The interview notes she had just finished a BBC drama, *Tall Tales & Murder*, before taking on the stage work.

## What happens next

The run ended on October 10, and the interview was conducted in its last week, so no further performances were mentioned in the source. The article does not say whether the production will return. Hyland's BBC drama *Tall Tales & Murder* is described as upcoming.
