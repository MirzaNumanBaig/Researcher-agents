**Quick summary**

- [Olivia Wilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Wilde) told The New York Times in an interview published on 14 November 2026 that she and ex-fiancé [Jason Sudeikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Sudeikis) have reached a "healthy equilibrium" as co-parents.
- She was candid that it is "so hard" and called the idea that exes simply find a way "BS".
- Wilde described the decision to separate as the most painful of her life.
- Sudeikis spoke about the relationship on a podcast on 18 October, according to InStyle.

Olivia Wilde says she and Jason Sudeikis have found a steady rhythm raising their two children, but she is not pretending the road there was smooth. In a long interview with The New York Times published on 14 November, the actress and director said she takes pride in how well the pair co-parent, and that the result took effort. [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/11/14/olivia-wilde-rare-comments-co-parenting-jason-sudeikis/) and [Just Jared](https://www.justjared.com/2026/11/14/olivia-wilde-shares-update-on-co-parenting-relationship-with-ex-jason-sudeikis/) were among the outlets to report her comments.

## What Olivia Wilde said about co-parenting

According to the quotes carried by Just Jared, Wilde said: "I really take pride in how good we are at co-parenting our kids in my family. We have reached this healthy equilibrium. It's so hard."

She was blunt about the popular idea that separation can be tidy. "Anyone who pretends it's easy, like, 'Oh, you just consciously uncouple, and then you find a way', that's BS," she said. She added that she feels relieved to be able to give her children a home with "a real sense of authenticity" about how everyone is feeling.

TMZ called the comments rare. The pair share a son aged 12 and a daughter aged 10.

## Why she chose honesty over performance

Wilde told the paper that children notice more than adults assume. "When you're trying to hide it from your kids, they sense it," she said. "They're the most intuitive people in the house." She said that when a parent hides what is going on, children start to wonder what is real, because they can tell something is being performed for them. "I wouldn't wish that on anyone," she said.

[InStyle](https://www.instyle.com/olivia-wilde-hard-journey-co-parenting-kids-with-ex-fiance-jason-sudeikis-12254583) reported that she sees pretending as more damaging to her children than being honest about the ups and downs of sharing their care.

She also spoke about what the end of the relationship cost her. "It feels like a failure when you can't make a relationship work, particularly if it's with the person you have children with," she said. She called it "by far the most difficult, painful decision I've ever made in my life", explaining that she came to understand it was not better for her children to live in a house where their parents were not aligned.

## A timeline of the split

Wilde and Sudeikis ended their engagement in 2020, according to TMZ, after nearly a decade together. Their separation became public in a different way in 2022, when TMZ notes that Wilde was served custody papers on stage at CinemaCon. [BollywoodShaadis](https://www.bollywoodshaadis.com/articles/olivia-wilde-admits-co-parenting-with-ex-fiance-jason-sudeikis-is-so-hard-after-their-bitter-split-86655) reports that the legal dispute ended about a year later, in 2023.

There are signs of a calmer chapter since. TMZ reported earlier this year that the two reunited to celebrate their daughter's graduation in June, a detail that fits the picture Wilde is now describing.

## Sudeikis has been reflecting too

Wilde's interview follows comments from Sudeikis himself. On an 18 October appearance on Sam Fragoso's "Talk Easy" podcast, the 51-year-old said, "I think it's just flat-out communication. I could have communicated better [with] how I felt, how things were making me feel," according to InStyle.

He added that he had regrets about a tendency to hold things in before sharing them with a partner. "One thing I've learned is that [I] waited a little too long," he said, "and it's coming out in different ways."

Taken together, the two sets of remarks show both parents describing the same lesson from different sides: honest communication, offered late, is harder to repair.
