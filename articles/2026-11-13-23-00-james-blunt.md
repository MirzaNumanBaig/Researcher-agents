James Blunt has been talking about the city he once lived in and now mostly visits for work. In a "My London" interview with the London Evening Standard, published on 13 November, the singer-songwriter covers his two pubs, his years as a soldier in Hyde Park and the world tour he starts in February.

## Two pubs and a life split between countries

Blunt tells the Standard that he now moves between Ibiza and Verbier in Switzerland, but still works in London often. The reason is the Fox & Pheasant, the Chelsea pub he owns. He adds that he has just bought another, the Carpenter's Arms, on the border of Hammersmith and Chiswick.

The paper introduces him as a [Celebrity Traitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Celebrity_Traitors) star. Blunt himself describes the show as a social experiment that shows an uninformed majority will, on the whole, lose to an informed minority.

## From Fulham Palace Road to Hyde Park Barracks

Blunt says he lived on Fulham Palace Road around 1997, above a dry cleaner and opposite a Sainsbury's Local. He joined the army in 1996 and stayed about six years. After serving in Kosovo in 1999, he worked as the Queen's ceremonial bodyguard, based at Hyde Park Barracks.

He calls it the most amazing job. It also left him evenings free to play small bars and clubs in north London.

Hyde Park comes up again when the Standard asks about his favourite place. He says he used to get up at 5.30am to ride around the park on army horses, and enjoyed watching people head home from clubs as the sun came up.

## Restaurants, clubs and a missing portrait

The interview is full of local picks. Blunt names La Famiglia in Chelsea for its ragu, Il Portico on Kensington High Street and Azteca, a Mexican place on the King's Road. He says he read that neighbours are trying to close La Famiglia and thinks it deserves protecting.

For a romantic night out he suggests his own Fox & Pheasant, or the London Eye after dark. He recalls a charity dinner there hosted by [Gordon Ramsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Ramsay), with a new course served on each rotation.

He says London lost something when the Boujis nightclub closed. He also keeps visiting the National Portrait Gallery in the hope of finding his own portrait, without luck so far.

## The Elton John connection

Asked about a London hero, Blunt picks Sir Elton John, who signed him to his management company early on and took him on tour around the UK and South America. His first gig with a band was to 50 people, he says, and the next night he supported Elton in front of something like 50,000.

Some 20 years later he supported [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran) at the same venue. Sheeran was also signed to Elton's management firm. Blunt's verdict: two decades on, he was still the support act.
