**Quick summary**

- Netflix has released *Russell Brand: Open Secret*, a documentary of just under 90 minutes, within days of Brand's sentencing, according to IndieWire.
- Brand was convicted of rape and sexual assault and sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison.
- Meta disabled his Facebook and Instagram accounts, with Brand notified on 11 November, NZ Herald reports.
- His X account, with more than 10 million followers, is still active and posted a cryptic "stay tuned" message.

[Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand) is out of the public eye behind bars, but the fallout from his conviction keeps landing. In the past week Netflix put a documentary about him on its service and Meta shut down his two biggest social media profiles, according to IndieWire and the NZ Herald.

## Netflix releases Russell Brand: Open Secret

The documentary is directed by Olivia Isaacs and runs just under 90 minutes. IndieWire's commentary describes it as slim and unfocused, and says it arrived roughly a week after Brand's conviction and two days after his sentence. The outlet called the timing distasteful.

The project was not a rushed job in the sense of being started this month. IndieWire points to Deadline's earlier report that the film was mentioned at the trial. Former friend Mark Pinheiro told Southwark Crown Court that a producer had approached him about the Netflix show.

The conviction only appears at the very end. According to IndieWire, title cards in the last few minutes note the verdict, say the allegations inside the film pre-date those that led to the conviction, and state that Brand's legal team was contacted after the conviction and chose not to respond.

## What the film covers

IndieWire says the documentary follows a familiar rise-and-fall pattern, from Brand's early career to his fame and later turn to right-wing politics. Interviewees include comedians, former girlfriends and several accusers. One accuser who didn't want to appear on camera is represented by an actress.

The reviewer singles out journalists Rosamund Urwin and Charlotte Wace of The Times as the strongest part. Urwin began looking into Brand in January 2019, and the pair describe what they could and couldn't report and how their work was nearly stopped. Writer [George Monbiot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Monbiot) also appears, and IndieWire says comedian [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) also appears in the film. The film also mentions Brand's brushes with fame, including meeting [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise).

IndieWire's verdict is harsh on style. It cites captions such as "[unsettling music playing]" and slowed-down old footage of Brand, and argues the accusers' interviews are often drowned out by those techniques. The Times has also reviewed the film, calling it "bleak but instructive", but that review sits behind a paywall and we couldn't read it.

## Meta removes his accounts

Separately, Meta disabled Brand's Facebook and Instagram accounts after his sentencing. The NZ Herald reports that screenshots shared by his X account show he was told on 11 November that his Meta account was disabled for not following "community standards". Meta confirmed the removal to TMZ, according to the Herald.

Brand's X account then posted: "There never was a Russell Brand, according to @Meta. Thank God for @elonmusk." The Herald says it is unclear whether Brand or someone on his team wrote it. The account also carried a "stay tuned" message, and the Herald notes it isn't clear what that means given he has just begun his sentence. Replies were largely hostile.

The Herald also reports that Brand is being investigated over further allegations of rape from two other women. That is an investigation, not a charge or a finding.

## What happens next

The Herald says police are looking at the allegations from two further women. Whether anything comes of that hasn't been reported. Brand's X account remains live for now, and what, if anything, follows the "stay tuned" post is unknown.

It's a Netflix documentary directed by Olivia Isaacs that runs just under 90 minutes. It covers Brand's career and the allegations against him, with the conviction added in closing title cards, according to IndieWire.

### What was Russell Brand sentenced to?

He was sentenced to 10 years and six months in prison after being convicted of rape and sexual assault, IndieWire and the NZ Herald report.

Meta disabled his Facebook and Instagram accounts. His X account, with more than 10 million followers, is still active, according to the NZ Herald.
