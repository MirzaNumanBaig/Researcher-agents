**Quick summary**

- Awards site Gold Derby says *I Play Rocky*, the story of how [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone) got his first big film made, is within reach of two categories at the 99th Academy Awards.
- The film is directed by Peter Farrelly and widens its release this weekend.
- Its combined odds put it at No. 6 for Best Casting, one place outside nomination range, though Gold Derby's experts pick it for the fifth slot.
- Best Actor and Best Picture look harder: Anthony Ippolito sits at No. 7 and the film at No. 12 in the combined odds.

*I Play Rocky* has a ready-made awards story, and as it reaches more cinemas, the early Oscar numbers say its best chance lies in a category that is only in its second year. That is the reading from [Gold Derby](https://www.goldderby.com/film/2026/i-play-rocky-oscars-odds-analysis/), which ranks the film's prospects ahead of the 99th Academy Awards.

## Why the film looks made for Oscar voters

Gold Derby's argument starts with the premise. *I Play Rocky* is about the making of a Best Picture winner, and its central figure is an actor who backed himself and refused to sell out along the way. That is a story the Academy's acting branch tends to like.

The timing also fits. The film widens its release this weekend, just as Stallone's original *Rocky* is being marked for its 50th anniversary. Men's Journal ran a piece headlined as a look back at the film's release 50 years ago, though that page could not be read in full, so no detail from it is used here. For more on the anniversary, see our report on [Stallone's fan tribute for 50 years of Rocky](https://moduscarry.com/sylvester-stallone-rocky-50th-anniversary/).

## Best Actor: a performance that has to beat a crowded field

The film's headline awards prospect is [Anthony Ippolito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ippolito), who plays Stallone. Gold Derby describes the work as far more than an impression and credits it with giving the film emotional realism, which matters when a film must recreate a screen presence audiences already know well.

The difficulty is the category itself. Gold Derby calls Best Actor probably the most competitive race of the year. Ippolito currently sits just outside nomination range at No. 7 in the combined odds, behind [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise) for his role in *Digger*. If you want to know how the performance came together, our piece on [Ippolito becoming Stallone](https://moduscarry.com/anthony-ippolito-stallone-transformation/) has the background.

## Best Casting: the closest call

The second category is Best Casting, which is in only its second year at the Oscars. Gold Derby argues that the film's main achievement is a feat of casting, which would put casting director Rick Montgomery in line for a first nomination.

The numbers are close. The combined odds have *I Play Rocky* at No. 6, one place outside nomination range. The site's experts, taken on their own, have it in the fifth and final nomination slot. That split is why Gold Derby describes the film as within reach rather than a lock.

## Best Picture: a fight for the last places

Best Picture is the toughest of the three. The combined odds place the film at No. 12, and the experts have it at No. 11. Gold Derby lists several perceived locks, including *The Odyssey*, [*La Bola Negra*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_bola_negra), *You Can See Everything* and *Project Hail Mary*. A group of other films, among them *Obsession*, *All of a Sudden* and *Artificial*, are also chasing the last spots. The site expects those standings to move as more of them reach cinemas, and it calls the tail end of the top 10 a likely fistfight.

## What happens next

The first test is this weekend's wider release, when more audiences and critics will see the film. Gold Derby says the standings of rival films could shift as they open, so these odds are a snapshot, not a forecast. Nominations for the 99th Academy Awards are still ahead. Not yet. Gold Derby's combined odds have it at No. 12 and its experts at No. 11, outside the top 10.
