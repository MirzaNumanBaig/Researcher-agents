**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) and [Sebastian Stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Stan) discussed their film Fjord in a joint interview published by The Independent on 10 November 2026.
- Fjord won the Palme d'Or at this year's Cannes Film Festival and was directed by [Cristian Mungiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Mungiu).
- It is loosely inspired by the 2015 Bodnariu case, in which a Romanian-Norwegian couple's children were taken into care in Norway.
- The film deliberately declines to side with either the Norwegian authorities or the strict religious parents.

## Renate Reinsve and Fjord: what the stars said

In a joint interview with The Independent, Reinsve and Stan describe Fjord as a drama about two cultures colliding over how to raise children. Reinsve, the Norwegian star of The Worst Person in the World and [Sentimental Value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentimental_Value), plays opposite Stan as a devout couple, the Gheorghius, who are investigated by Norwegian authorities.

The interview opens lightly. According to The Independent, Stan shaved his head for the film and kept it that way for his role in a sequel to The Batman, which he is shooting now. Reinsve joked that she once cut her own hair very short in a life crisis a week before being cast in The Worst Person in the World, so the production fitted her with a wig. Stan, the paper reports, had never realised it was a wig.

## The real case behind the story

The Independent says Fjord draws on the story of Ruth and Marius Bodnariu, a Romanian-Norwegian couple whose children were taken into care in Norway in 2015 after allegations that they had hit them. The couple were also investigated over claims of religious indoctrination. After a public seven-month investigation the children were returned, and the family moved back to Romania.

The case became an international row. Conservative politicians and Christian groups argued that the family had been targeted for their faith, pointing to Norway's largely secular culture and liberal approach to child-rearing. The film also borrows from other immigrant families in Norway who faced similar scrutiny.

## A film that refuses to pick a side

The paper describes the Norwegians in the film as hypervigilant and unwilling to see grey areas, while the Gheorghius are shown as severe and puritanical, with their children caught between the two. Stan told The Independent the film is "not pro-God, or pro-discipline", and that it asks parents whether they raise a child for the child or for who they want that child to become.

Stan was born in Constanța, Romania, and moved to Vienna at eight and New York at 12. He said that background helped him understand the family's patriarchal, old-school world. He also argued that people have grown less able to acknowledge their own faults because they feel constantly under attack.

The ambiguity has had a cost. The Independent notes that some early reactions from Cannes labelled Fjord a "MAGA movie" on X. The headline quote has the pair saying they knew people would get riled up by it.
