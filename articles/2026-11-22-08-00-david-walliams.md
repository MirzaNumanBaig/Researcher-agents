**Quick summary**

- Suffolk County Council says it has no plans to remove books by [David Walliams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walliams) from its libraries, according to the Ipswich Star.
- Neighbouring Norfolk has also decided to keep his titles on shelves.
- Monmouthshire and Denbighshire councils in Wales have withdrawn his books.
- The debate follows allegations of sexual misconduct and grooming reported by The Guardian and ITV News. Walliams denies them.

Library services in England have split over whether to keep the children's author's books on their shelves. [Suffolk County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County_Council) confirmed this week that his titles will stay, according to the [Ipswich Star](https://www.ipswichstar.co.uk/news/26747463.david-walliams-books-will-remain-suffolk-libraries/), even as councils in Wales have pulled them.

## Why Suffolk is keeping the books

The council said its decisions about stock follow guidance from the Chartered Institute of Library and Information Professionals and its duty to support freedom of speech and access to information. A spokesman said books are judged on their contents, not on the actions of their author. A book would only be removed or refused if there were legal or safety grounds.

That does not mean every copy will stay forever. The Ipswich Star reports that Walliams' books will go through the library service's usual stock-management process, so copies could gradually disappear once they are worn out, out of date or no longer borrowed. The council also said it does not currently plan to buy extra copies.

## Where other councils stand

Not every authority has taken the same view. According to the same report, [Monmouthshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monmouthshire_County_Council) and [Denbighshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denbighshire_County_Council) have withdrawn his books from their libraries. Norfolk is among the councils that have chosen to keep them.

The Times has also reported on Irish libraries letting parents decide, but that article sits behind a paywall and its details could not be confirmed here.

## The allegations and the publisher

The decisions follow a joint investigation by The Guardian and ITV News that reported allegations of sexual misconduct and grooming against Walliams. He denies them. The Ipswich Star notes that his bestselling titles include Gangsta Granny, The Boy in the Dress, Mr Stink and Billionaire Boy.

HarperCollins said in December 2025 that it would stop publishing new Walliams titles, according to the Ipswich Star, and in September 2026 confirmed that his existing back catalogue would be phased out.

## A wider conversation

The story has landed in a month of intense public debate in Britain. The Christian Science Monitor, in a piece on the conviction of [Russell Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Brand), describes the Walliams exposé alongside that case as part of a long shift away from so-called "lad culture". It reports that Brand was jailed on 9 November for 10 and a half years after being found guilty of rape and sexual assault. The two cases are separate, and the Monitor's piece is commentary on culture rather than a finding about Walliams.

Other library services are likely to face the same question. Suffolk says its approach is unchanged for now, and stock will be reviewed through the normal process. Walliams continues to deny the allegations.
