**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) says his guest role on Dawson's Creek in 2003 was "not fun", according to E! News.
- He described "a lot of resentment" between the showrunners and the cast and called it "a very bad dynamic".
- He made the remarks on the Happy Sad Confused podcast, in an episode dated November 16.
- Page Six reports he also said the experience left him with a nervous breakdown.

[Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) has opened up about a Seth Rogen Dawson's Creek chapter he clearly does not remember fondly. Speaking on the Happy Sad Confused podcast, the actor said his early guest spot on the teen drama was an unhappy experience, with tension running between the show's cast and its leadership, E! News reported on November 23.

## What Seth Rogen said about Dawson's Creek

According to [E! News](https://www.eonline.com/news/1438354/seth-rogen-says-dawsons-creek-cast-had-feud-tension-with-showrunners), Rogen told the podcast on November 16 that he did not enjoy his time on the series. He called the set "not fun" and said he picked up on friction between the actors and the crew.

He went further, describing "a lot of resentment" between the showrunners and the cast. In his words, it was "a very bad dynamic". E! also reports that he mentioned staying at a haunted hotel during the shoot.

Page Six, in a [separate report](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/23/entertainment/seth-rogen-says-starring-in-hit-90s-show-was-so-bad-he-had-a-nervous-breakdown/), headlined the story around his claim that the job left him with a nervous breakdown. We could not open that page, so that detail rests on its headline and on E!'s summary of the interview.

## How he ended up on the show

Rogen appeared in 2003, in the sixth-season episode "Rock Bottom". E! says he took the part because Busy Philipps persuaded him. Philipps was his former Freaks and Geeks co-star and was a Dawson's Creek cast member at the time. She had earlier appeared on his series Undeclared.

Dawson's Creek ran from 1998 to 2003, so his episode landed in the show's final run.

## Others have described a tough set

Rogen's account is not the first to describe a demanding workplace. E! points to earlier comments from people tied to the show.

Tom Kapinos, one of the showrunners Rogen's remarks touch on, said at a 2009 Emmys panel that the experience was miserable but worked as a "four-year boot camp" in running a TV show. Michelle Williams told Variety in 2019, in a conversation with Patricia Clarkson, that the heavy workload and lack of creative input made the job feel like a "factory job".

E! says it contacted creator Kevin Williamson and showrunners Greg Berlanti and Tom Kapinos for comment. It had not heard back when the article was published.

## What happens next

No response from the show's creators had been reported at the time of writing. Rogen was 44 when the interview coverage appeared, and he has spoken freely about his early career before. If Williamson, Berlanti or Kapinos reply, their account would add the other side of the story.
