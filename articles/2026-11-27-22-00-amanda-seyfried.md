**Quick summary**

- The team behind *You Can See Everything* has explained how [Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) ended up in the documentary, according to HuffPost UK.
- Nathan Fielder says he got her number through [Emma Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Stone) and they spoke for half an hour.
- Seyfried only agreed to watch the first half of the film and talk about it.
- Her recreations of Elizabeth Holmes scenes have made her a surprise favourite for Best Supporting Actress at next year's Oscars, per HuffPost UK.

[Amanda Seyfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Seyfried) did not join the Elizabeth Holmes documentary *You Can See Everything* through an agent. According to [HuffPost UK](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/you-can-see-everything-amanda-seyfried-how_uk_6b094904e4b01f494a0380c4), which drew on a Rolling Stone interview, co-director [Nathan Fielder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Fielder) tracked down her number through a mutual friend, rang her, and she invited him over.

## A number passed on by Emma Stone

Fielder told Rolling Stone that after two years living alongside Holmes, he and his collaborators asked themselves where the film should go next. The answer kept returning to *The Dropout*, the miniseries in which Seyfried played the Theranos founder. He said Holmes's parents felt strongly about it and saw it as one of the main ways the public came to know their daughter, so speaking to Seyfried seemed worthwhile.

Stone, her *[Mean Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_Girls)* co-star, supplied the number. The two talked on the phone for about half an hour, HuffPost UK reports.

## "There was no protection"

The offer was modest. Fielder said they wanted to show her what became the first half of the film and then discuss it, and that this was all she agreed to. He was struck that she bypassed talent representatives entirely and simply said to come over.

Seyfried agreed with that account. She said nobody was protecting her interests, but she was keen because she was invested in the project and already trusted the filmmakers.

## Muscle memory and the recreations

Co-director Lance Oppenheim noted that Seyfried had previously said she would never play Holmes again. Even so, in that first meeting with Fielder, he saw her slip into Holmes's mannerisms.

Seyfried put it down to muscle memory, the result of playing a character so intensely and for so long. She described something "almost uncontrollable" about it and said it was already surfacing while she spoke to Fielder.

That moment shaped the film. Fielder said the team had assumed Holmes's early off-camera meetings could not appear on screen, and at first thought recreations were a little lame. After shooting at Seyfried's apartment, though, he felt she wanted to revisit the material. HuffPost UK says she starts by commenting on existing footage and becomes steadily more central, eventually appearing in reconstructed scenes the cameras never caught.

## An unusual awards case

HuffPost UK reports that this work has made her a surprise favourite for Best Supporting Actress at next year's Oscars. It adds that a win would be the first time anyone has taken both an Emmy and an Oscar for playing the same role. Seyfried won her Emmy for *The Dropout* and is already an Oscar nominee.

## What happens next

The awards talk is for now only that. HuffPost UK describes Seyfried as a favourite, not a nominee, and next year's Oscar season will decide whether the Emmy-and-Oscar milestone becomes real. The full conversation between Seyfried, Fielder and Oppenheim is in Rolling Stone, which HuffPost UK links to.

## Frequently asked questions

### How did Amanda Seyfried get involved in You Can See Everything?
Nathan Fielder says he got her number through Emma Stone, called her, and she invited the team to her home to show her the first half of the film.

### Did Seyfried say she would play Elizabeth Holmes again?
According to co-director Lance Oppenheim, she had said she would never play Holmes again. She later appeared in recreations in the film regardless.

### Could she win an Oscar for this?
HuffPost UK calls her a surprise favourite for Best Supporting Actress, and says a win would be the first Emmy and Oscar for the same role. Nominations are not yet announced.
