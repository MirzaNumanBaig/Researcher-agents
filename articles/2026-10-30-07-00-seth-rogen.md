**Quick summary**

- [Seth Rogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seth_Rogen) told The New York Times' oral history of *Donnie Darko* that he doubted [Jake Gyllenhaal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jake_Gyllenhaal)'s performance would land, according to HuffPost.
- Rogen says he came around and admired the thought Gyllenhaal put into the role.
- At the SCAD Savannah Film Festival, he told Gold Derby how improvisation changed the ending and tone of *The Invite*.
- *The Invite* is streaming on HBO Max and will receive the Vanguard Award at the Palm Springs International Film Awards in January 2027, MovieWeb reports, citing Variety.

Two Seth Rogen stories landed on the same day, and they pair neatly. One looks back at his first film role, a teenage turn in a cult classic. The other looks at how a very different set let him rewrite a comedy as it was being shot.

## What Rogen said about Donnie Darko

In The New York Times' oral history of [*Donnie Darko*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Darko), published earlier this week, Rogen recalled watching Gyllenhaal work and wondering how it would play. "I don't know how this is going to land," he said, according to [HuffPost](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/seth-rogen-jack-gyllenhaal-donnie-darko_n_6ae36222e4b0a2cccd46c4f8). He added that Gyllenhaal "wasn't playing this charming version of it."

His view shifted. Rogen said he came to see that Gyllenhaal had put a level of thought into the role that he, as an actor, had not realised was an option. Rogen played the bully Ricky Danforth in the 2001 film, which was his first screen role. He was a teenager at the time.

He also admitted wider doubts about the finished product. "This will either be really good, or it'll be like the weird B-movie of itself," he remembered thinking.

## From box office miss to cult classic

HuffPost notes the film was not a success on first release but found an audience after its UK release the following year, and is now regarded as a cult classic. Rogen's explanation is simple: "If a thing is good and resonates, it will find its people."

This is not the first time he has spoken about the set. HuffPost points to a 2022 interview with Wired in which he said Gyllenhaal was in one of his first films and that he figured the actor would be far more successful than he would be. He added, "And I was right." Rogen has also joked that his first film line was a crude one, a story he has told before.

For readers who know Rogen mainly from [*Knocked Up*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knocked_Up) and his later comedies, it is a useful reminder of where he started.

## Why acting still unsettles him

The second story comes from a Q&A at the SCAD Savannah Film Festival, reported by [Gold Derby](https://www.goldderby.com/film/2026/seth-rogen-the-invite-olivia-wilde-improvising-on-set/). Rogen said he is more comfortable behind the camera as a writer and director, and that taking a role in Olivia Wilde's *The Invite* frightened him.

"Acting is more mysterious to me," he said. On most sets, with a new director and new co-stars, he says he does not know what the dynamic will be. Writing and directing his own projects feels more predictable because he knows the process and the people.

*The Invite* raised the stakes. Rogen said he had never met Penélope Cruz, and although he has known Edward Norton for more than 20 years, the two had never acted together. He had also never worked under Wilde as a director to this extent.

## How improvisation reshaped The Invite

Rogen praised Wilde's collaborative approach. Many directors say they want ideas, he said, but she seemed to mean it, which for an actor is like "all the green lights" turning on. Because the film was shot in sequence, the team could adjust as they went.

According to Gold Derby, the changes were substantial:

- The ending is completely different from what was first written, and it came out of conversations in the weeks before filming.
- Rogen's character was not originally called Hawk, had no firefighter backstory, and none of the perimenopause material was in the script.
- Rogen credits Cruz with the idea of the characters pairing off, which the cast chose to push close to the edge so the audience believed it might happen.
- The image of Rogen at the piano with Cruz beside him grew out of rehearsals.

He also described a scene in which Norton's character delivers an emotional backstory. Rogen said he expected his character to be moved, but in the moment decided he wanted to make fun of it. He described Norton's reaction as genuine shock and betrayal, and said it changed the tone of a scene designed as a heavy one.

## The Invite's run so far

MovieWeb reports that the comedy premiered at Sundance, sold to A24, did well at the box office over the summer and reached HBO Max last week, where it still holds the top spot on the platform. It cites Variety for the Vanguard Award, which goes to the whole cast and director. The site lists a 96% critic score and an 89% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes.
