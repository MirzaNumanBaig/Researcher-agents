**Quick summary**

- [Renate Reinsve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renate_Reinsve) stars in "Butterfly", directed by Itonje Søimer Guttormsen, in the feature competition at the 44th Torino Film Festival, according to Variety.
- Ten of the 16 films in Torino's feature competition are directed by women, the second year running.
- In a BBC interview published on 3 November, Reinsve said "The Worst Person in the World" stopped her from quitting acting.
- The BBC also reports she plays Lisbet in Cristian Mungiu's "Fjord", which won the Palme d'Or.

Norwegian actress Renate Reinsve is one of the best-known faces in this year's Torino Film Festival line-up, and the Renate Reinsve Butterfly project is a headline title in a competition built around women directors. Variety reported on 6 November that she leads "Butterfly", the work of emerging filmmaker Itonje Søimer Guttormsen. The same week, Reinsve told the BBC that her breakthrough role came a day after she had decided to leave acting for good.

## Renate Reinsve Butterfly: a star lending her name to a new director

Variety's report on the festival's female talent describes Torino as a showcase for first and second features. Within that mission, it says the competition has more star power than most. Reinsve's "Butterfly" sits beside "When She Hears the Bells" from Chabname Zariab, starring [Golshifteh Farahani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golshifteh_Farahani), and Rachel Rose's "The Last Day", led by [Alicia Vikander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicia_Vikander) and [Wagner Moura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner_Moura).

Variety's reading is that established arthouse names are lending their profiles to emerging female directors. The sources do not detail the plot of "Butterfly", so this article does not describe it.

Festival chief Base told Variety the gender balance is not a quota exercise. "I only care about good films, and there are plenty of them," he said. The festival's selection committee, which sifted through more than 5,000 submissions, is gender-balanced, and every member was recruited before turning 30.

## A festival leaning into women-led programming

Torino's 44th edition goes further than the competition slate. According to Variety, three juries are led by women: director Francesca Archibugi chairs the feature competition, [Stefania Casini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefania_Casini) oversees documentaries, and Chiara Francini handles shorts.

The festival's Marilyn Monroe retrospective will also be introduced by an all-female roster of 24 journalists and academics. Base said the aim was to show that Monroe "was so much more" than the sensual object she was often seen as. The wider programme runs to 120 titles across six sections, and Variety's coverage places [Pamela Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamela_Anderson) and [Isabelle Huppert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabelle_Huppert) among the festival's other names.

## The call that stopped her quitting

Reinsve's profile has risen sharply since 2021. In its Close Up Film series, the BBC recalls that she walked the Cannes red carpet for Joachim Trier's "The Worst Person in the World" believing it would be her first and last, because she planned to give up acting.

She told the BBC that she was 32, had years of theatre work behind her and could not land film roles, and was considering other careers, including training as a carpenter in Italy. Trier, who had directed her in "Oslo, August 31st" in 2011, phoned about the part the day after she decided to quit. "It changed my life completely," she said.

The numbers that followed are worth setting side by side. The BBC reports that she won the Cannes Best Actress prize for the film, and that it earned two Oscar nominations. She later received her own Best Actress nomination for playing Nora in Trier's "Sentimental Value".

## Fjord, English-language work and her favourite shoot

The BBC lists her recent work as including English-language films such as Kane Parsons' horror title "Backrooms", and "Fjord" from Romanian director Cristian Mungiu, which won the Palme d'Or. She plays Lisbet, an evangelical Christian whose children are removed on suspicion of abuse.

Even so, she called "The Worst Person in the World" the most significant film of her career, her first leading role and her favourite shoot. She described a long summer of running around Oslo in 2020, with a playful collaboration. She said Julie, the restless 30-year-old she plays, is more familiar to her than other characters. "I've lived a similar life to her," she told the BBC.
