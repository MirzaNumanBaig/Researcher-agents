# Humanizer (editing pass)

Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing". Run this on your draft before saving. Do not remove facts or attributions; remove the tells.

## Process
1. Write the draft. 2. Scan it for the patterns below and rewrite each hit. 3. Ask yourself: "What still makes this obviously AI generated?" List the tells briefly (in your head, not in the file). 4. Fix them. 5. Save only the final text.

## Patterns to remove
- **Inflated significance**: "a testament to", "pivotal moment", "marks a shift", "evolving landscape", "underscores", "indelible mark". Say what happened instead.
- **Tacked-on "-ing" analysis**: "..., highlighting/showcasing/reflecting/fostering...". Cut the participle phrase or make it its own sourced sentence.
- **Promotional words**: stunning, breathtaking, vibrant, renowned, groundbreaking, nestled, must-see, iconic (unless a source calls it that, and then attribute it).
- **Vague attribution**: "experts say", "observers note", "critics argue". Name the outlet or person, or drop the claim.
- **AI vocabulary**: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, intricate, interplay, landscape (abstract), foster, garner, additionally, align with, enduring.
- **Copula avoidance**: "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features" where "is" or "has" works.
- **Negative parallelism**: "It's not just X, it's Y". Tailing negations ("no fuss", "no guesswork").
- **Rule of three**: stop forcing lists of three. Use the number of items there actually are.
- **Synonym cycling**: do not rename the same person or thing in every sentence ("the actor", "the star", "the Oscar winner"). Use the name or "she/he/they" as the person is known to use.
- **False ranges**: "from X to Y" where X and Y are not on a scale.
- **Em dashes**: use commas, full stops or brackets. At most one em dash in the whole article, preferably none.
- **Bold, emojis, title-case headings, curly quotes**: no emojis; sentence-case headings; straight quotes; bold only for the key-facts label if used.
- **Chatbot artefacts**: "Here is", "I hope this helps", "Certainly".
- **Filler**: "it is important to note", "in order to", "at this point in time", "due to the fact that".
- **Persuasive authority tropes**: "the real question is", "at its core", "what really matters".
- **Signposting**: "let's dive in", "here's what you need to know", "without further ado".
- **Generic upbeat endings**: "exciting times lie ahead". End on the next dated, sourced fact instead.
- **Fragment headers**: a heading followed by a one-line sentence that only repeats the heading.

## Voice (news-feature voice, not opinion)
- Vary sentence length. Short sentence after a long one. Real rhythm.
- Be specific: dates, places, figures, names of outlets and people. Specific beats impressive.
- Use contractions the way an Australian magazine writer would ("she's", "doesn't").
- A journalist may show mild reaction ("a striking contrast", "an unusual choice") but never invents feelings, motives or private facts about a real person.
- Let a little texture in: a scene-setting detail from a source, a direct quote, an aside in brackets.
- Never invent quotes, statistics, people or studies to sound human. Facts come only from the sources you read.
